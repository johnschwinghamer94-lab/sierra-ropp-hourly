What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He asked, and it worked the same day. Yesterday: five calls, nothing booked. Today: an equipment specialist signed onto Thursday afternoon, a thermostat sold and paid for, and a second lead created. That is the coaching landing.
  • The most complete expectations block on the board. All six beats verbatim, plus a realistic time estimate and permission to be interrupted: my goal is just to deliver a five star experience. So if I'm not meeting that, you stop me.
  • The only complete question set of the day. Filters with the teaching behind them, the power-bill follow-up, and the hot-and-cold-rooms question — all on the same call. Nobody else got all three.
  • He split the objection instead of losing it. The customer would not have an installer at ten at night. Thermostat work order for tomorrow, specialist for Thursday afternoon. Two signatures instead of a callback.
  • He read the household. On WYLAND, a husband in hospital and a brother sleeping upstairs — so he resequenced the entire visit around it: I'll do the outdoor system first, then the garage furnace. That'll give time for him to sleep.

6

This week's drill — ask early, then keep talking

The signature comes before the stories

Trigger: the findings are explained and the customer understands them.

Do this before the conversation gets comfortable:

"So here's where we are. Here's what I'd fix today, and here's the specialist visit for the replacement number — that one costs you nothing. Which do you want me to set up first?"

Then sign it, and then enjoy the conversation. Robert's warmth is a genuine asset; it just needs to sit on the far side of the ask instead of in front of it.
