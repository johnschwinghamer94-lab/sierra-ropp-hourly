1

The one-line verdict

Why all three flipped anyway

He earns trust fast, then never tells them what happens next.

What actually happened

On the DEWEES call he walked in, got to the filters and the hot rooms inside a few minutes, listened to a customer describe six weeks in ICU and answered it like a human — I'm very sorry to hear that… at least you're walking, talking — and then went straight to work. No look, no thorough evaluation, no options promise, no approval promise, no today, no five-star seed. The same on GRUMMETT and TERRIEN.

What the FSG model calls for

The LTOAT5 block is thirty seconds and it is the only part of the FSG model that is entirely under his control. I'm going to look at the whole system, not just the one thing. I'll do a thorough evaluation, bring you back everything I find and show you your options. I won't do anything without your approval, and my goal is to take care of it today. All of that is for a five-star experience. Three for three is a great day; three for three with the frame set is a repeatable one.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • Three for three. Every job number he ran on this day exists as a lead in ServiceTitan. Nobody else on the board converted at that rate.
  • The best personal connection of the day. You guys were teachers out here in Vegas… I'm born and raised here, so I appreciate that. He finds the real thing, not the small talk.
  • He stayed human when it counted. A customer described six weeks in ICU and a coma over Christmas; he answered I'm very sorry to hear that and told her about his mother-in-law, then let her finish. That is why she trusted the rest of the visit.
  • Filter teaching that actually changes behavior. He did not just ask — he explained why washing a washable filter drives dirt inward, and offered to check his truck for the right size.
  • Warranty diligence. I just want to make sure if it's like nine years, eight months, if I can use a warranty, get you some other prices. That is a rep looking for the customer's money, not ours.

Weak

Gap 1 — Not one customer was told what the visit would look like

What happened on DEWEESDoor, thermostats, attic access, filters, work. The evaluation was thorough and the customer never heard it described first.
What was missingLook · Thorough Evaluation · Options · Approval · Today · Five-Star. All six beats, all three calls.
Why it mattersTwo of the three recordings cut off before the presentation. When the frame is set up front, the customer knows the options are coming even if the call runs long.

The fix This is the one automatic fail on his day and it is pure habit. Thirty seconds, at the door, every call: I'm going to look at the whole system, not just the one thing. I'll do a thorough evaluation and bring you back everything I find with your options. I won't do anything without your approval, and my goal is to take care of it today — all of that so you have a five-star experience. If I'm not doing that, stop me. He is already earning the trust; this is just telling them the plan.

Moderate

Gap 2 — The options are in his head, not on the customer's table

On TERRIENThorough discovery, good analogies, a real lead created — and no explicit here is option A, here is option B anywhere on tape.
How the call closedPlease don't be afraid to hit me up.

The fix He converts on relationship, which works until it doesn't. Packaging costs nothing: name the options out loud, price them out loud, and ask which direction makes sense. Option A gets the system running today at X. Option B is the advisor out to price a replacement, which costs you nothing. Which one do you want to start with? Then stop talking. A customer who can point at an option owns the decision; a customer who liked the tech has to remember to call back.

The fix He is leaving reviews on the table from customers who would obviously give them — a woman he treated with real care after she told him about her hospitalisation is a five-star review waiting to be requested. Plant it at the door, collect it at the end: I told you at the start I was going for a five-star experience. Did I earn that today? Ask it and then be quiet.

6

This week's drill — thirty seconds at the door

Say it before you touch anything

Trigger: you have the customer's name and you are about to start work.

Before you pick up a tool:

"Here's how I work. I'm going to look at the whole system, not just the one thing. I'll do a thorough evaluation, then bring you back everything I find and show you your options. I won't do a single thing without your approval, and my goal is to take care of it today. All of that is so you get a five-star experience — if I'm not doing that, stop me."

Say it out loud five times with a manager before the next shift. Three flips in one day means the hard part is already working; this is the thirty seconds that makes it repeatable.
