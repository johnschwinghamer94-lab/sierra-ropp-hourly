1

The one-line verdict

What the fourth call proved

He owns the expectations block. He just does not run it on ordinary jobs.

What actually happened

On MORALES, before he picked up a tool: just kind of go over what I'm gonna do is I'm gonna do an evaluation of both systems. Systems inside, outside… Whatever the issue is, I've built out multiple options. I don't like to tell you guys what to do. I prefer if you tell me what you want to do. Then the approval promise — I have to get your approval before anything can be done — and the five-star seed: we got 4.9 out of 5 stars… If I fall short, stop me, I'll correct it. On DEWEES, GRUMMETT and TERRIEN: none of it.

What that means for coaching

This is no longer a skill gap, and it should not be coached like one. He has the block, he delivers it cleanly, and the call where he used it is the call that produced the biggest handoff of his day. The instruction is not learn this — it is run the same open on the twenty-minute job you run on the four-hour one. That reframe matters: telling a rep to learn something he already does well is how you lose him.

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • Four for four. Every job number he ran on this day moved forward. Nobody else on the board converted at that rate.
  • The Option C handoff was textbook. He never tried to own the replacement conversation. He built the case, said that's all a conversation for him, booked the specialist into a confirmed window, got the authorisation signed at no charge and told the family how to reach him with questions before the visit. That is the primary win in this process and he executed it cleanly.
  • Decision-maker discipline under pressure. Standing in a house full of people who wanted answers, he stopped and got recorded verbal authorisation from the actual owner before he would say anything: I just got to cover our legal bases. That protects the customer and the company.
  • He taught the same thing twice without a trace of impatience. When the homeowner arrived hours in, he rebuilt the whole explanation from the thermostat forward: because I definitely want you to understand what we're talking about.
  • He caught something dangerous and fixed it for free. A melted, badly spliced high-voltage wire that fell apart when he touched it — repaired, explained, not billed.
  • The expectations block exists and it is good. This is the single most useful thing the fourth recording told us.

The three gaps

each one anchored to a real moment on tape
Moderate

Gap 1 — The frame comes out for the big job and stays in the truck for the rest

On MORALESFull block at the door: evaluation of both systems, multiple options, I have to get your approval before anything can be done, and the five-star seed.
On DEWEES, GRUMMETT and TERRIENNone of it. Door, thermostats, attic, filters, work.
Why it mattersTwo of those three recordings cut off before the presentation. When the frame is set up front, the customer knows options are coming even if the call runs long — and the short jobs are exactly where a customer is most likely to assume the tech is just here to fix one thing.

The fix Nothing to teach. Run the MORALES open on every call, including the ones that look routine, and time it — it is about thirty seconds. The manager check is simply: did the first two minutes of this recording sound like the first two minutes of MORALES?

Moderate

Gap 2 — He talks himself smaller than he is

Twice, unpromptedI'm a little new with this company. And later, in front of the decision-maker: I don't know the full cost because like I was telling you guys, I'm new with this company.
And through the closeI'm so sorry, I got gum in my mouth… I got food poisoning right now. I'm so sorry, I took 70 photos. I'm sorry, there's a lot of numbers cycling through my head right now.
What it undercutsThe same visit where he correctly said I'm a nerd, so I clearly know what I'm talking about — and he was right.

The fix Routing the replacement conversation to the specialist is the correct process and he should keep doing exactly that — but route it from expertise, not from apology. Pricing on a full replacement is the advisor's conversation, and he'll bring you real numbers tomorrow lands; I don't know, I'm new gives the family a reason to doubt everything he found. Drop the word new, and cut the apologies for photographs and thoroughness — those are the job.

6

This week's drill — his own open, every call

This is his script, not a new one

Trigger: you are inside, you have the reason for the call, you are about to pick up a tool.

Say the thing you already said on MORALES:

"Here's what I'm going to do. I'm going to do a full evaluation of the system, inside and outside, take photos of everything I find. Whatever the issue is, I'll build you multiple options — I don't like telling people what to do, I'd rather you tell me what you want to do. I have to get your approval before anything gets done. We're a 4.9 out of 5 star company and I'm going for that today — if I fall short, stop me and I'll correct it. Any questions before I get started?"

He delivered this cleanly on a four-hour job. The drill is running it on a forty-minute one.

7

3-week training plan

Week 1
Same open, short jobs too
Run the MORALES expectations block at the start of every call regardless of job size. Manager spot-checks the first two minutes of three recordings against that call.
Week 2
Required questions before rapport
All required questions — filters included — asked and answered before the visit opens up into conversation. Rapport is his strength; it should follow discovery, not replace it.
Week 3
Speak from expertise, close the five-star loop
Retire the word new and the reflex apologies; route replacement conversations to the specialist as the confident next step. Five-star ask at the end of every visit where the seed was planted.

Bottom line

Andrew moved every customer he saw and did it on warmth, real teaching and a specialist handoff executed exactly the way this process is designed to work. The fourth call changes the story: he is not a rep who cannot set expectations, he is a rep who sets them beautifully when the job feels big enough to deserve it. Give the forty-minute customer the same open the four-hour customer got, ask for the review he has already earned, and stop telling people he is new. Everything else is already working.
