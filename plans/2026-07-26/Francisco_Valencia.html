1

The one-line verdict

What separated the three flips from the two that didn't

When he picks up the phone, it flips. When he doesn't, it doesn't.

What actually happened

On PARRA he called dispatch from the customer's house: Hey, Abby, it's Francisco. Can you see if we have an available comfort advisor to come out tomorrow from 8 to 10?… can you make an OTO ticket for this call that I'm at from eight to ten? On CASIS VALDEZ, with a frozen coil and a failed damper motor, the customer said she would rather let it break and call the home warranty. He answered Yeah, cuz they'll replace it if it's broken… that's pretty much all I can do.

What the FSG model calls for

It is not pretty much all he can do — he proved that twice the same day. A home-warranty customer still needs a real number to argue with: Go ahead and use the warranty, that's smart. What I'd hate is you waiting three weeks on their tech in this heat with nothing to compare. Let me get you our written number free so you know what you're actually being offered — advisor can be here tomorrow morning. Same customer, same warranty, one appointment on the board.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He books it in the room. Hey, Abby, it's Francisco… can you make an OTO ticket for this call that I'm at from eight to ten? Called dispatch from the customer's house, got the slot, then took payment. That is the Option C handoff executed exactly as designed.
  • The best teaching sequence on the board. The full system anatomy on PARRA — return, blower, control board, burners, cooling box, line set, condenser — delivered in plain language to a customer who had never been told any of it.
  • He protects the flip even when the customer chooses the repair. On PARRA the customer said let's go with the repairs right now and Francisco still put the advisor on the calendar so both numbers would be on the table. Then he rescheduled it when the customer's shift changed rather than letting it drop.
  • Video as evidence. How do I know it's open? I took a video of it. So you see that blade? Not a claim — a demonstration.
  • He took an accusation without flinching. A customer opened by saying the system fails every time Sierra visits. He found the real cause, told his manager it wasn't Andrew's fault, and never made the customer feel foolish for saying it.

6

This week's drill — the warranty answer

A home warranty is not the end of the call

Trigger: "I'll go through my home warranty" / "let me check with my insurance first."

Agree with the customer, then keep the appointment:

"Use it — that's smart, it's what you pay for. What I'd hate is you waiting on their tech in this heat with nothing to compare it to. Let me get you our written number free so you know what you're actually being offered. Our advisor can be here tomorrow morning — does that work, or is afternoon better?"

He already ran this play successfully twice on the same day. This week the target is running it every time the warranty comes up.

What we owe Francisco

  • The dispatch call on PARRA played to the whole team. That is exactly what the Option C handoff is supposed to look like and most of the board has never heard one done properly.
  • A clear, approved line on home-warranty customers. Three techs hit this wall in one day and each improvised a different answer.
  • Backing on the CASIS VALDEZ service-recovery. He walked into a customer who blamed us for the breakdown, found the real cause, and defended a colleague. That deserves saying out loud even though the call did not flip.
Bottom line

Francisco converted three of five and did it with the cleanest mechanics on the board — tiered options with prices and warranties attached, video evidence, and a phone call to dispatch that turned understanding into a calendar slot before the customer could cool off. The two he lost were lost the same way: a customer reached for a home warranty and he let go of the call. He has already proved he knows the answer to that objection. He used it twice and forgot it once, and the once cost him a family sitting in an 81-degree house.
