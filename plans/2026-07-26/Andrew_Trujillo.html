The one-line verdict

Why two of three didn't land

He runs a masterclass and forgets to take attendance.

What actually happened

On the 127-minute consultation he explained thermal expansion, installation quality and warranty implications until the customer understood his own house better than the builder did. Then it ended on Please don't be afraid to hit me up. On BUJAZIA he diagnosed a failed compressor across 110 minutes, worked through warranty coverage and replacement paths, and the tape ends with no commitment and no date.

What the FSG model calls for

Education is the setup, not the close. The FSG move at the end of a two-hour consultation is to convert the understanding into a slot while it is still warm: You now know more about this system than anyone who has looked at it. Let's get our advisor out to put real numbers next to what I just showed you — it costs you nothing. Tomorrow morning, or is afternoon better? Then silence. He earned that ask three times and made it once.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • The warmest opening on the board. That's a pretty cool name, man. Unprompted, genuine, and it set up a close ninety minutes later.
  • He closed the hardest call of his day. A rental property, a landlord who is not the occupant, a cost he did not want to spend — and Nazareth approved the repair and thanked him for it.
  • Teaching depth nobody else reaches. Thermal expansion, installation quality, warranty mechanics — a two-hour consultation that left the customer able to explain his own house.
  • Process transparency as a closing tool. Make sure to get your approval before we do anything, with written estimates promised up front. Customers relax when they know they are in control.
  • He validates before he answers. Cost hesitation on the tenant call got acknowledgement first and value second, in that order. That sequence is exactly right.

How it endedNo explicit commitment, no scheduled next step, no approval captured.
The Critical ActionObjections — Fail. Answering a question is not handling the concern underneath it.

The fix When a customer keeps asking about warranty coverage, the concern underneath is am I about to spend money I should not have to? Answer the concern, not the question: Let's find out for certain rather than guessing. I'll write it up both ways — what it costs if the warranty covers it and what it costs if it doesn't — and I'll get the advisor out Tuesday so you have the replacement number too. Then you decide with everything in front of you. That turns an open question into a booked appointment.

6

This week's drill — the sentence after the teaching

Convert understanding into a calendar slot

Trigger: the customer has understood the problem and you are about to wrap up.

Say this before anything else:

"You know more about this system now than anyone who has looked at it. Let's get our advisor out to put real numbers next to what I just showed you — it costs you nothing and takes twenty minutes. I've got tomorrow morning or tomorrow afternoon — which one works?"

Then be quiet. Andrew earns this ask on every call he runs. This week the only target is making it three times out of three.

3-week training plan

Week 1
Nothing leaves unscheduled
Every call ends with an approved repair, a booked advisor slot or a specific callback time. Log all three outcomes with a manager daily.
Week 2
Answer the concern, not the question
When a customer asks about warranty or cost, respond with a question that surfaces the real worry, then a written option both ways.
Week 3
Call economics
Target the same quality inside a tighter window — set the frame, teach to the decision, then ask. Review two recordings for where the teaching stopped adding to the decision.
