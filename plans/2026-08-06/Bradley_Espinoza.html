1

The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

He separated the appointment from the purchase, and then asked for a day.

What actually happened

Two hard stops in a row. I need to run it by my husband, so I’m not going to do it today got a question, not a retreat: What time does your husband get home? → he comes home on Friday → it doesn’t cost a dime to have him come over, so I can have him come over Friday, and just so you go over the options together. Then the harder one — I don’t think we’re gonna get a new unit right now — answered by shrinking what he was asking for: it’s not to get a new unit, it’s just to have the options for you… By no means am I saying you need a new system. That’s not my job. Her answer: Okay, why don’t you do that?

What he still left behind

The immediate repair he had called immediate — a failing voltage switch and outdoor fan motor at $1,477 — was presented, met with No, no, no to his questions prompt, and then never returned to. And the five-star review he seeded perfectly at the door was never collected at the end.

What Bradley did right — real strength, build on it

  • He asked a question instead of accepting a delay. I need to run it by my husband → What time does your husband get home? That single question is the entire difference between this call and the no-closes on this board.
  • He made the appointment smaller than the purchase. it’s not to get a new unit, it’s just to have the options for you… By no means am I saying you need a new system. That’s not my job. Her objection was to buying, so he stopped asking her to buy.
  • He named his own incentive and gave it away. I make my money off of these components here… I’m the repair guy. Telling a customer that the free visit costs him money is why she believed the visit was free.
  • He held the SILO price line under direct pressure. What is it roughly for a new one… you had to guess, $10,000? → that’s what his forte is. Routing the number to the specialist is the process, and he did it without making it feel like a dodge.
  • The full expectations sequence, every time, on both calls. Look, thorough evaluation, options, approval, today, five-star — word for word, and it is the reason both customers relaxed inside two minutes.

Moderate

Gap 2 — The immediate repair disappeared once the specialist was booked

Bradley, presenting"for the immediate option that we have, that would be that voltage switch and the outdoor fan motor… that'll come out to $1,477."
Bradley, immediately after"Do you have any questions on any of that before we get further into it?" — Customer: "No, no, no. No."
Where it wentNowhere. The conversation moved to the future repairs, then the energy analysis, then the specialist — and the immediate never came back before he settled the invoice and left.

The fix An immediate is by definition the thing that should be decided today, and any questions? is not the same as asking for it. Close the loop before moving up the ladder: Before we look further out — the fan motor and the switch are the two that are actually failing now. Do you want me to take care of those while I’m already here today? Then wait. If the answer is no, it goes to the specialist visit as a package. Booking the flip and losing the repair should not be a trade.

Moderate

Gap 3 — A customer asked for options twice and the visit was still in diagnosis mode

Customer, Filippini"I would just love some options because maybe you don't hear it right this second… but like right where we're sleeping and it's like clicking."
Also on tape, from her"My kids are freezing. Okay. And then our master is not freezing. So I wonder if I can close some vents."
Bradley's answer"It could be the actual grill itself up there. You know, things like that sometimes tend to wear out over time." … "We can dampen it, right? We could also throw in zoning systems throughout the home."

The fix The diagnosis instinct is right and the words we can dampen it… we could also throw in zoning are already an option set — they just need to be written down and priced rather than floated in a hallway. When a customer says I would just love some options, that is not conversation, it is an order. Note it on the spot, name it back to her at the report, and put a number on it: You told me the clicking above your bed and the temperature split between the kids’ rooms and the master. Here are your two options for exactly that.
