1

The one-line verdict

Why it converts

He tells people the truth fast, and they believe him.

What actually happens

He diagnoses out loud, in the customer’s hearing, in words they can check against their own senses: the fan is spinning, but the pipe that it goes onto… it’s supposed to be really cold and yours is liquid warm, The blower is on, so it’s pushing air, but it’s pushing hot air. And when the news is bad he does not soften it: if it’s popping the breaker, it doesn’t sound very promising, it’s drawing too much or it’s grounded. That’s what we’re about to find out right now.

What it is missing

On the Vega call he ran the process properly — scope, report, approval, it just has to make sense to you. On the other two he went straight from the door to the ladder: Well, let me go get my things, uh, hop up there. No scope, no report promise, no approval line, no five-star. He is converting despite the process, not because of it — which means none of it is repeatable when the customer is a stranger.

5

What Francisco did right — real strength, build on it

  • He converted every call he ran. Whatever else is on this page, that is the number that matters and it is the best on the board this day.
  • He diagnoses out loud and the customer follows along. the fan is spinning, but the pipe that it goes onto is supposed to be really cold and yours is liquid warm. The customer can verify that with their own hand, which is why it lands.
  • He asks the qualifying question early. is this something that you guys wanted to try to get done as soon as possible, or are you just like getting some estimates? Most technicians never find out where they stand until the presentation.
  • He gives bad news straight, without theatre. if it’s popping the breaker, it doesn’t sound very promising. No hedging, no doom, and the customer trusted him for it.
  • His process, when he uses it, is genuinely good. we always take a look at everything inside and out… then it just has to make sense to you. If it does, then we can help you out. If it doesn’t, at least you guys know what’s going on. That is the whole frame in three sentences and it is his own wording.

The fix He is winning on relationship and reputation, and both of those run out the moment he meets a customer who does not already trust Sierra. The answer is to change what happens next, not to carry on: Makes sense — and with both units down you probably want this decided rather than shopped. That way you’re comparing on one visit instead of three.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: The Vega opening at every door, word for word, with the approval line and the five-star seed added.
Proof it worked: All six beats audible on every recording, including on repeat customers and on jobs where he already knows the house.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Replace gotcha with a commitment. Any time a customer describes something that went wrong before, acknowledge it and state the specific thing he will do differently.
Proof it worked: Every prior-service complaint on tape gets a named commitment in response.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Act on the intent answer. If a customer says they are gathering estimates, the specialist gets offered before the report is written, with a day attached.
Proof it worked: Every “estimates” answer is followed by an offered appointment on the same recording.

Bottom line

Francisco had the best conversion day on this board and the thinnest process behind it. He wins because customers believe him within ninety seconds — he tells them what is wrong in words they can check themselves, and he does not dress it up. That is a real and rare asset. The risk is that none of it is repeatable: on two of three calls there was no scope, no approval line, no report promise and no review ask, and the two live objections he met went unworked. He already has the right opening in his own words on one recording. Use it every time, and this stops being a good day and becomes a standard.
