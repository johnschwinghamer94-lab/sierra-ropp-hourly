The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

He builds the option beautifully when he builds it. Twice he sent the work somewhere else instead.

The call that flipped

Decision-maker not home, so he got him on the phone and framed it as a comparison rather than a sale: that way you guys can compare between repairs and replacement costs, and then make an educated decision at your own pace. So I could always set up that appointment. Then he asked for the day and let the customer set it: is there a certain time that works with you, Don? → after 4 o’clock → That’s exactly what we have. We do have a 4 to 6 time slot. Signed on the spot: this will be to speak with the equipment specialist. It’s a $0 visit.

The two that didn’t

On Taylor, the answer to a system that never keeps up was reach out to the people who put it in, see what they can do. On Barr, a customer who wanted a filter got a model number and a recommendation to buy it elsewhere: You might save more going that route than going through us. Both customers had said out loud what they wanted. Both requests left the property.

5

What Brandon did right — real strength, build on it

  • He got the absent decision-maker on the phone. Most techs on this board leave a report with whoever is home. He called the husband, delivered the whole picture in ninety seconds, and closed the appointment with him directly.
  • He let the customer set the time and then matched it. is there a certain time that works with you, Don? → after 4 o’clock → That’s exactly what we have. We do have a 4 to 6 time slot. That is how a free visit becomes a booked visit.
  • The best video and check-in work on the board. Narrating the gauges live at the roof so the customer sees what he sees is worth more than any explanation delivered afterwards.
  • His expectations sequence never slips. Three calls, three complete versions, including the approval line and the five-star seed — on a busy day, at three very different doors.
  • He frames himself as a decision aid, not a salesman. make an educated decision at your own pace is why a customer who was not even living in the affected building agreed to a specialist visit.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Nothing a customer asks for leaves the house. Any part, filter or fix a customer names is quoted by Sierra and either done today or booked before he walks out — no model numbers handed over for self-ordering, no referrals back to the installing dealer.
Proof it worked: Every call where a customer names something has a same-visit sale or a booked return on the ticket.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Every stated concern becomes a written option. The uneven-room complaint, the noise, the filter — each one comes back at the report as a priced line quoted in the customer’s own words.
Proof it worked: A manager can match every customer concern on the recording to a line on the report.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Collect the five-star he already seeds at every door, as its own sentence before leaving.
Proof it worked: The ask is audible on every recording, flips and no-closes alike.

What we owe Brandon Moreno

  • His phone call to the absent decision-maker played back to the team. Ninety seconds, the whole picture, an appointment booked into the customer’s own window — that is a repeatable play and most of this board does not have it.
  • Common accessory and filter stock, including odd sizes. I don’t have it today should end in a booked return, and right now it ends in a shopping recommendation because there is no easy alternative.
  • A clear position on proprietary and third-party-installed equipment. He was genuinely unsure whether that call was ours to keep, and defaulted to handing it back. He should not have to guess.
  • Working recordings. Two of his three files cut out during the evaluation, so two full reports and presentations went ungraded.
Bottom line

Brandon opens a call better than almost anyone here and teaches with video in a way customers plainly trust. His close rate is not a selling problem — when he asks, he asks perfectly and gets a signature. It is a reflex problem: when a customer names a want, his instinct is to be helpful by pointing at the cheapest path, and the cheapest path is always somewhere other than Sierra. One rule fixes it. If they said it out loud, it goes on the report with a price and a date on it.
