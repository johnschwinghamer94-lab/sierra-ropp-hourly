The one-line verdict

Why the Caparoso call ended cold

He was right about everything and he told the customer so.

What actually happened

The technical case was airtight — a 2019 condenser bolted to a 2007 system, an expired warranty, a compressor pulling 143 against a 127.9 rating. But it was delivered as correction: No, you need to understand this… This is what you’re not understanding, okay? and again later, That’s what you’re not understanding, and I explained it to you right here. After four refusals the questions turned pointed — so what was the purpose of you signing up for the membership?, So is it safe to say you wouldn’t be using us? — and the customer said out loud what was happening: I hate being forced to something… it sounds like you want me to do this.

What flips it

The customer told him the winning move himself: I always call someone. So you’re my second opinion… I’m not really relying to one opinion. A man who insists on second opinions will happily take a free one. That’s exactly how I’d do it too. So take the free one first — our specialist costs you nothing, and then whatever the home warranty says, you’ve got a real number to hold it against. What day works? Same appointment, framed as his method instead of against it.

5

What Joe did right — real strength, build on it

  • He held the visit open until the specialist arrived. I’m gonna hang out until he gets here. No cold hand-off, no gap for the customer to cool off in — that is why the DeCesare call converted and stayed converted.
  • His diagnostics are measurement-led and completely legible. A 14-degree differential against a required 22, a compressor at 32.18 amps against 28.8, and the filter-dryer chain that explains both. The customer can follow every step.
  • He asks the power-bill question on every call. Most of this board never does, and it is the question that turns a repair conversation into a system conversation.
  • He presses past a soft answer. How often are you changing filters? → Often → What’s often? Small, and it is the difference between an answer and information.
  • He names his own incentive and argues against it. I get paid to do the repairs. That’s how I get paid. I don’t want you to do these repairs. And when the price was genuinely too high, he said so: It’s way too much. I agree with you.

After the second no, the play is to bank the relationship and leave the door open, not to test the customer’s reasoning: Completely fair — you’ve got the report and the numbers, and that’s what I came for. If the warranty conversation goes sideways, call me directly and I’ll have someone out the same day. He walked in with a membership sale and a genuinely grateful customer and walked out with a customer who felt cornered. The offer was not the problem; the third and fourth attempt were.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: The two-ask rule. Ask, handle the first objection, ask once more — then bank the relationship and leave the door open. No third attempt, ever.
Proof it worked: No recording contains more than two asks for the same appointment, and every declined call ends warmly.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Ban “you’re not understanding” and every variant of it. Any correction gets delivered as a question the customer answers.
Proof it worked: Zero corrective phrases on tape; a manager can point to the customer stating each conclusion themselves.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Use the customer’s own frame. Whatever rule they state about how they make decisions, agree with it out loud and route the offer through it.
Proof it worked: Every stated customer principle on tape is echoed back and used, not challenged.
8

What we owe Joe Mendoza

  • His DeCesare hand-off recorded as team training. Free, signed, and the technician staying on site until the specialist walked in — that is the cleanest version of Option C anyone ran this day.
  • A home-warranty script the whole board shares. He is right about what those policies do, and he is being asked about it in living rooms with no agreed way to turn it into an appointment rather than an argument.
  • Recognition for the diagnostic work. Finding a filter-dryer restriction from a temperature differential, and mismatched-system damage from a locked-rotor reading, is high-end troubleshooting on a maintenance ticket.
  • A conversation about pace rather than about effort. He is not short of drive — he is short of a stopping rule, and that is a coaching problem, not a character one.
Bottom line

Joe is technically the strongest diagnostician on this board and he sells hard because he genuinely believes what he found. On DeCesare that combination produced the best hand-off of the day. On Caparoso the same conviction turned into correction, and a customer who had just bought a membership and thanked him for the information ended the visit telling him to stop pushing. He does not need more persuasion; he needs a stopping rule and a different set of verbs. Ask twice, agree with the customer’s own logic, and let them say the conclusion out loud — and both of these calls end the same way.
