The pattern across the day

What is working
Strong
This was a materially better day than the one before it — ten of fourteen technicians converted at least one call. The Welcome and Assessment steps are in strong shape almost everywhere: parking, permission, names and floor protection are near-universal, and the technical teaching was genuinely excellent — two refrigerant generations with the actual legislation dates, a TXV explained as a water faucet, compressor amp draw explained as blood pressure, a borescope inside a heat exchanger, and live video narration at the roof.
What produced the flips
Strong
Every confirmed flip on this board shares one behaviour: the specialist visit was named, made explicitly free, and attached to a time before the technician left — “within the hour”, “30 to 45 minutes”, “Friday evening”, “the 4 to 6 slot”. Several were signed on the spot at zero dollars. Not one flip came from a better argument; they came from a date.
The recurring miss
Moderate
Conditional language. “I can always set that up”, “all the options are all there”, “it’s up to you though”, “I can follow up with you”. In four separate calls a customer said out loud that they wanted or expected a new system — “I might as well just get a new one”, “I would love to get the other new unit”, “I could have already bought it”, “we’re going to replace them both” — and none of those four ended with an appointment.
Wants walking out the door
Weak
Three technicians routed a customer’s stated request away from Sierra on this day: a filter sent to a hardware store, a whole system referred back to the installing dealer, and a membership that was requested at the door and never sold. Separately, two customers asked in plain words for uneven room temperatures to be fixed and neither got a written option.
Objection handling
Moderate
Split cleanly. Where a question was asked before the counter-argument — “What time does your husband get home?” — the objection converted. Where the counter-argument came first, however accurate, the customer disengaged; one told his technician outright that he felt forced.
Five-star collection
Weak
Seeded on roughly half the calls and asked for on almost none. One technician built the request into his opening; nobody else asked at the door, including on calls that ended with the customer thanking them unprompted.
Recording coverage
Weak
Still the most serious problem on this page, and not a technician problem. Most of this day’s recordings stop when the technician picks up a tool. One 63-minute visit produced about four usable minutes, one 21-minute visit produced about one, and two 5-minute captures represent a technician’s entire graded day. On those calls the Decision step is unknown, not weak.
Price discipline
Strong
Worth recording explicitly: at least five technicians were asked directly what a new system costs and every one of them routed it to the specialist rather than guessing. That is the SILO process working exactly as designed, and it is never counted against anyone on this board.
Bottom line

Ten of fourteen technicians converted this day, and the difference between them and the four who did not was not knowledge, effort or honesty — every plan on this page contains real teaching and real diagnostic skill. It was a calendar. Every flip had a specific time attached and several had a zero-dollar signature; every miss had a customer who said out loud that they wanted a new system and heard “I can always set that up” in reply. Two habits are worth more than anything else on this board right now: turn a stated want into a named day before leaving the property, and stop sending customer requests — filters, memberships, whole systems — anywhere other than to us. And fix the recordings, because a large part of this day’s real work is still invisible.
