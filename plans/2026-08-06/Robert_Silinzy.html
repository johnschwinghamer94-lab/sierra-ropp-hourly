1

The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

He framed the specialist as the customer’s comparison, not ours.

What actually happened

He laid out the whole arithmetic honestly — having $1,500 in immediate repairs, and the compressor, the control board, also the heat exchanger in the future when these things fail would bring a total to $8,900 — and then handed the decision back: I just want to give you the options, depending which way you want to go is making the best decision for you and your house… We have an equipment specialist that can come by, give you all the information about possibly updating the system, and then you can compare and contrast replacement versus repair. The customer answered all right, send them over, and Robert closed the time immediately: we can have them come in about 30-45 minutes. Is that okay?

Where it went missing

On the second call he built the same case — you get to the point where it’s repairs after repairs… how long do you want to play that game? — and the customer answered it for him: it would have been a lot more… I could have already bought it. No specialist was offered on that call at all.

5

What Robert did right — real strength, build on it

  • He frames the hand-off as the customer’s comparison. compare, contrast, and see which direction you would like to go — the specialist becomes a tool the customer is using rather than a salesman we are sending.
  • He closed the time in the same breath as the yes. we can have them come in about 30-45 minutes. Is that okay? Or did you want to set it for… Offering an immediate slot and an alternative in one sentence is how an agreement becomes an appointment.
  • Best technical teaching on the board. The TXV as a faucet, the motor as an engine without oil, metal expanding and contracting into cracks — and a borescope inside the heat exchanger so the customer sees it rather than takes his word for it.
  • He asks the power-bill question on every single call. Including on a five-minute demand call where most technicians go straight to the condenser.
  • He corrects bad advice gently. You use one inch filter?… We do recommend every month, and a straight answer about cheap filters that neither shamed the customer nor sold him anything.
