5

What Noah did right — real strength, build on it

  • The complete expectations sequence, said naturally. Scope, permission, report timing, approval, today and the five-star seed — in about twenty seconds, without sounding like a script.
  • All three critical questions, plus the follow-up. He asked the power-bill question and then stayed with it through a vague answer until the customer went and got the statements. Very few people on this board get that far.
  • He explained his booties instead of taking them off. Yesterday I was up on a roof and it was covered in pigeons. I’m not bringing that into your home. Politely refusing a customer’s permission to be less professional is exactly right.
  • He asked the maintenance goal question and it paid immediately. The most useful fact of the visit — a filter untouched for up to two years — came directly out of that one question.
  • Genuine, unhurried rapport with an older customer. The family crest, the Navy career, the shared German grandparents. A retired man living alone was treated like company, not like a ticket.

And"There's no filter in that unit there… what I did was I had a custom washable one that I had put in here. It was aluminum. That you pull out and wash it."

The fix Even on his own numbers, one to two years is past the interval he just quoted — and a homemade aluminium filter is an airflow question worth looking at on a system this age. The customer raised it because he wanted it dealt with. Good that you flagged it — even on those, one to two years is past due, so I’ll pull it, show you what it looks like, and I’ve got the right one on the truck. And I’ll check what that washable one is doing to your airflow while I’m up there. A stated need is not a question to be answered; it is work to be done.

What was never mentionedThe membership, on a single-occupant four-bedroom home whose owner books us only when he remembers to.

The fix He gathered a complete picture and then moved on from it. A customer holding a house at 80 and waking up to 85 is describing a comfort problem he has stopped complaining about — that is the thread to pull: You said it’s 85 in here when you wake up even set at 80. That gap tells me something, and I’ll check it specifically while I’m up there. The questions are only worth asking if the answers change what happens next.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Anything a customer names becomes work or a written option. No customer-raised need gets answered with reassurance alone.
Proof it worked: Every customer-raised item on tape has a matching line on the ticket or the report.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Membership offered on every maintenance call where the customer is not enrolled, framed around the problem it solves for them.
Proof it worked: The offer is audible on every non-member maintenance recording.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Use the answers. Every discovery answer gets repeated back at the check-in or the report, tied to something he actually checked.
Proof it worked: A manager can trace each of the three critical-question answers to a specific line in the report.
