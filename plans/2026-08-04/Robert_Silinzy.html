The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

Customers tell him the whole problem, and the answer is a better filter.

What actually happened

On the Robinson call the customer laid out a dead thermostat, a compressor that had already needed servicing, and power bills he was tracking closely. The only thing offered back was a filter upgrade: You ever thought about using the three inch filters? Those are every six months… you don’t have to worry about getting up and down every six months. On Browne, a thermostat running hot and a filter seven months overdue produced advice about filter frequency and nothing else.

What flips it

The filter upsell is fine and it is not the problem — the problem is that it was the whole answer. The filters will help, and here’s the bigger thing: a thermostat that’s failed plus a compressor that’s already been serviced, on a system this age, is worth getting a proper number on. Let me have our advisor come out at no cost so you know what you’re looking at.

5

What Robert did right — real strength, build on it

  • People tell him things. The Robinson customer volunteered his compressor history, his thermostat failure, his power bills and his own troubleshooting attempts, unprompted. Most technicians have to dig for half of that.
  • He notices the person before the equipment. 13 years old, man. You don’t have to put those on — about the customer’s dog, before a word about the system. That is why the rest of the conversation is easy.
  • He asks the bills question. What do the power bills look like in the summer? That is the value question, asked without prompting, and it is the doorway to the whole efficiency conversation.
  • The filter recommendation is genuinely helpful. Three-inch filters at six-month intervals, framed around not having to climb up and down — sold on the customer’s convenience, which is the right instinct applied to the wrong-sized problem.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Expectations at the door on every call — scope, sequence, approval promise, and the promise of a findings review.
Proof it worked: Manager can quote the expectations line from the first minute of every call this week.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Every fault the customer names gets a path. Filters are an add-on, never the answer to a thermostat, a compressor or a bill.
Proof it worked: Every call this week where a customer names a fault ends with an advisor offer or a repair option on tape.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Seed and collect the five-star ask. His rapport already earns it; the ask is all that is missing.
Proof it worked: Review seeded on every call and asked for on every completed visit.
8

What we owe Robert Silinzy

  • A recording check — one of his three recordings was essentially silent on the customer side, which leaves a third of his day ungradeable.
  • Comfort-advisor slots he can offer on a short call, because both of his visits were under 25 minutes and the offer has to be makeable in that window.
  • Credit for the rapport. Customers hand him information most technicians never get, and that is the expensive half of the assessment step already solved.
Bottom line

Robert has the part of this job that cannot be taught. Customers tell him everything within minutes — thermostats, compressors, bills, their own repair attempts — and both of his calls were warm and easy. What is missing is what he does with it. Two customers described real, expensive faults and were offered a filter. He does not need to become a different technician; he needs to say the advisor sentence once per call, to people who are already telling him exactly why they need it.
