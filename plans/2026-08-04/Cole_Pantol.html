1

The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

A customer asked for a replacement quote before he had opened a panel.

What actually happened

On the Heraypur call the customer opened with Are they sending 2 people out, one’s repair and one’s replacement? and later spelled it out: I told the company that if I could get a quote for like repair and replacement of everything. Cole’s answer both times was a description of his own role — I’m repair, I’m on the repair side.

What flips it

The lead did get typed, so the day was not lost — but the customer had to hold the replacement conversation open by himself for seventeen minutes. The answer that closes it in the room is one sentence: You’re right, that’s two different people — I’ll handle the repair side today, and before I leave I’ll get our comfort advisor booked so you get the replacement number too. What time works for you?

5

What Cole did right — real strength, build on it

  • His expectations paragraph should be the team standard. These are just the expectations for today… I’ll then step into my thorough evaluation looking for safety, cleanliness, proper operation. Once I figure out what’s going on, is it okay to let you know about that? Sequenced, permission-based and finished with a question.
  • He seeds the review before he earns it. Above all, I’m here to give you a five star experience. If I’m not doing that, let me know. That sentence also gives the customer permission to complain early, which is why his calls stay easy.
  • The empathy is specific. I’m sorry you guys are dealing with that to a family running portable units in two bedrooms for a week — said once, meant, and then he got to work.
  • He pre-explains the report. In the event anything’s out of the ordinary, I’ll step out to the van and I’ll build a report for you. No customer of his is ever surprised by a finding.

The gaps

The customer asked for the replacement quote, twice

Customer, Heraypur"Are they sending 2 people out, one's repair and one's replacement?"
Customer, later"I told the company that if I could get a quote for like repair and replacement of everything."
Cole"I'm repair." … "I'm on the repair side, so I'll go over everything."

The fix Both answers are true and neither is an answer. The customer is not confused about who does what — he is asking to be handed to the other half. You’re right, it is two people. I’ll do the repair side today and before I leave I’ll book our comfort advisor for the replacement number, so you get both halves of what you asked for. Does later this week work? The lead was typed anyway; this makes it happen in the room, at the moment the customer wanted it.

Moderate

Here’s the repair I can do, and here’s the no-cost advisor visit for the bigger number. Which do you want to start with?

At the doorNot asked on any of the five calls, including four that produced a lead and two where work was authorised in the call.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Answer the request that was made. Any time a customer mentions replacement, a second quote or another company, book the advisor before leaving the property.
Proof it worked: Every call this week containing a replacement mention ends with an advisor slot offered in the room, and the answer logged.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Two named paths after every report. The repair, and the no-cost advisor visit — said out loud, in that order, before the van.
Proof it worked: Manager hears both paths at the end of every call; no call ends at the report.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Close the five-star loop he opens. The promise is already in every opening; the ask goes at every door.
Proof it worked: Review asked for on every completed visit; response logged either way.
