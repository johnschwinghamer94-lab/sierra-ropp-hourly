1

The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

The specialist visit was offered, and then left for the customer to arrange.

What actually happened

He did open the door, and correctly: we can always set something up for someone to come out here and just keep give you guys all your options… just to get all your options and hear what they got to say. Just because we never know how long these units really last. The call ended on I’ll give them a call — an intention, with no date, no name and no confirmation.

What flips it

Everything up to that point was right, including the reasoning. What was missing is the hand-off being completed in the room: Let me set that up for you now — I’ll call our advisor before I leave and we’ll find a time this week. You’ll get the numbers with no obligation, so when this system does go you’re not deciding in a heatwave. Book it, do not describe it.

5

What Mario did right — real strength, build on it

  • He draws the system for the customer. So I’ll draw it out right here for you. That way you can see what’s going on and where these parts are at. On a repeat maintenance visit, that is what turns ‘same as last time’ into something the customer can act on.
  • He shows rather than tells. The filter held up side by side — See the big difference? White is dirty — and the decision handed straight back: Let me know if you guys want it changed or not.
  • He names the specialist path for the right reason. we don’t want to see you guys just dumping money for, for no reason… just to get all your options and hear what they got to say. That is the SILO logic stated correctly and in his own words.
  • He respects the household. Arriving late, he asked before proceeding rather than assuming — I just wanted to make sure I’m not, you know, crossing the line with you guys.

8

What we owe Mario Castro

  • Advisor availability that can be reached from the property on an evening call — he was ready to hand off and had nobody to hand off to on the spot.
  • Credit for the drawing. Sketching the system for a customer is one of the strongest teaching moves anyone used on this day, and it should be shared with the team.
  • Visit history in his hand before he knocks, so ‘what has changed since last time’ is a fact he can quote rather than a memory.
Bottom line

One call, ninety-three careful minutes, and a technician who found everything, drew it out on paper and correctly told the customer why an advisor visit was worth having. He should not be marked down for failing to sell — he was marked down for not finishing a hand-off he had already started. The distance between this call and a flip is one phone call made in the driveway instead of promised for later.
