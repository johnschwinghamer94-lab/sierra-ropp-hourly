1

The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

Two calls lost to one path where two would have done it.

What actually happened

On the Lau call the customer could not find their warranty paperwork, and the answer was to send them a copy — a next step that depends entirely on a document they had just said might not exist. On Cannito the customer asked outright what do you think is the solution? and got a true, careful technical answer that ended at we’ll have to address that in six months to a year.

What flips it

Both moments needed a second, parallel path attached to the first. I’ll send the paperwork over — and let me also have our specialist come out and show you replacement options and warranties, in case that document never turns up. That way you’re moving either way. Never make the next step depend on something the customer has already told you they might not find.

5

What Joe did right — real strength, build on it

  • He wins back customers the company has already lost. On Jenkins he named the previous service failure out loud and then made the investment workable. That is the hardest sale there is and he made it.
  • He asks the authorisation question on rentals. You guys get permission to come out and take a look at it? — asked before touching anything, then the owner called with the findings. It protects the company and it produces the lead.
  • He reconnects instead of defending. When Saldana hesitated on price he went back to the problem being solved rather than justifying the number. That is the objection-handling standard.
  • His expectations line ends with the customer. then we’ll sit down and chat and see what makes the most sense to you. The decision is theirs before the inspection has even started.
  • Seven calls in a day and the discovery never slipped. Even the 23-minute evening call had good questions in it — the volume did not cost him the assessment.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Two paths, always. Whenever a next step depends on the customer finding, checking or waiting for something, attach a parallel path that moves without it.
Proof it worked: Every call this week with a blocker has a second path on tape; manager can quote both.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Answer ‘what’s the solution?’ with a booking. When a customer asks for the solution, the reply names the specialist visit before it names a timeline.
Proof it worked: Every call where the customer asks for a recommendation ends with a specialist visit offered and the answer logged.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Close the loop on won calls: walk the work, then ask for the review.
Proof it worked: Both happen on every call that ends in an agreement this week.
