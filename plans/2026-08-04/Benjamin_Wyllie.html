1

The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

Four flips out of five, and the decision still belongs to somebody who is not in the house.

What actually happened

On both rental calls the close is the same sentence. On the Goh call: I’m gonna go give him a call. I’m gonna write my full report out, let him know about the things I’m seeing. It’s up to him at that point of what he wants to do. On Hampton: I’ll go over it with Mary Lou. It’s of course up to her about which direction she wants to go.

What flips it

Both of those calls produced a lead, so the hand-off itself is working — that is the Option C flip and it should not be coached away. The upgrade is what travels with the report. An absent owner cannot choose between paths that were never written down: I’ll put two directions in the report for Mary Lou — the repair, and a no-cost specialist visit for the replacement number — so she has something to pick between when I call.

5

What Ben did right — real strength, build on it

  • He resets expectations the moment the situation changes. When Hampton turned out to be an absent-owner call, he stopped and re-set the frame in the room: let me just go over the expectations with you real quick, because I am in your home. Most technicians skip that once the plan has already changed.
  • The five-star promise is built into his opening. And then we do this all for five star experience. It is seeded, it is natural, and it costs him nothing to collect at the end.
  • He protects the customer’s time. Am I on any time constraints right now?… I shouldn’t be here past 6:30. Asked before committing, honoured afterwards.
  • He handles the awkward correction well. A tenant had replaced a blown fuse with the wrong part. Ben got the fact he needed without making the customer defensive — on a call that still flipped.
  • Four flips in a day, three of them on properties where the decision-maker was absent. That is the hardest version of the Option C hand-off and he is doing it repeatedly.

The fix The hand-off is right and it flipped both calls — keep it. What is missing is the content of the hand-off. 7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Two written paths for every absent decision-maker. Whenever the person who decides is not in the room, the repair path and the no-cost advisor visit both go in the report before he calls them.
Proof it worked: Every absent-owner call this week has two named directions in the notes and a stated hand-off method.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Answer the objection in the room, then route it. When a customer or tenant reports failed prior companies or an owner mid-decision, respond to it before promising to relay it.
Proof it worked: Manager can point to a specific sentence answering the objection on every call where one was raised.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Collect the five-star ask he already seeds. The promise is in his opening on most calls; the ask goes at the door on all of them.
Proof it worked: Review requested on every completed visit, and the response logged either way.
8

What we owe Benjamin Wyllie

  • Comfort-advisor availability that can be offered while he is still standing in the property — his flips depend on a lead being typeable the same evening, and on this day that worked four times.
  • His absent-owner hand-off played back to the team. Three of his four flips came from properties where the decision-maker was off site, which is the situation most technicians treat as a dead end.
  • A standard two-path report template for rentals, so the option-building does not have to be reinvented in the van on every rental call.
Bottom line

This is the strongest conversion day on the board and it is not luck — the expectations line, the discovery and the hand-off are all repeatable, and he ran them on properties where the decision-maker was not even present. He should not be coached for ‘not closing’; he flipped four out of five. The one thing to add is content in the hand-off: two written directions travelling with every report, so the person who decides is choosing rather than waiting.
