1

The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

The leads are real. The conversations that produced them stop at the diagnosis.

What actually happened

The pattern is the same on four of five calls. On Prayers: I’ll reach out to the management and they’ll reach out to you — a customer who had been fighting an 87° house for weeks, and a previous technician who had already failed to fix it, told only that somebody would be in touch. On Magana, the customer said they were keeping the property — we’re going to run it out — and the answer was oh, okay.

What flips it

The hand-off is the right move and it is producing leads — that part stays. What is missing is thirty seconds of substance before it: Based on what I’m seeing this is likely a replacement conversation, not another repair. Our specialist will call you within 24 hours with the options and the numbers, and here’s what I expect them to say. Same hand-off, and the customer is no longer waiting in the dark.

5

What Brandon did right — real strength, build on it

  • His expectations line, when he gives it, is excellent. I’m going to do a thorough evaluation… head to toe exactly what’s going on. Plain, confident, and it tells the customer exactly what they are getting.
  • He asks the ownership question. Are you guys planning on, like, moving back here or? That is the question that decides whether a system is worth investing in, and most technicians never ask it.
  • He is honest about scope. On the Pham call he told the customer straight that Sierra does not work through their home warranty rather than letting them discover it later.
  • Three leads out of five. Whatever the conversations lacked, three customers ended the day connected to somebody who could help them — and the Yacono call shows he can run the whole model when he chooses to.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Never hand off empty. Before saying anyone will call, state the suspected problem, the likely path and a specific timeframe.
Proof it worked: Every hand-off this week contains a diagnosis, an expected next step and a named deadline; manager can quote all three.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Follow the goal sentence. Whenever a customer says what they want from the system — last longer, keep the property, get through the summer — ask the confidence question and build the option out of the answer.
Proof it worked: Every call this week where a goal is stated has a follow-up question and a named path in the notes.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Set expectations on every call without exception, including the short ones, and seed the five-star ask inside it.
Proof it worked: Expectations given on every call; review seeded on all of them and asked for on every completed visit.

What we owe Brandon Moreno

  • The Yacono call played back to him alongside Prayers — same technician, same day, and the difference between them is entirely in the last five minutes.
  • A specialist response-time commitment he can actually quote. He cannot promise a 24-hour callback if nobody will confirm one.
  • Credit for the ownership question. It is the right question, asked unprompted, and the coaching is about what to do with the answer — not about asking it.
Bottom line

Three flips out of five is a genuinely good day, and the hand-off instinct behind them is right — nobody should coach that out of him. The problem is what the customer holds after he leaves. On four of five calls that was nothing: no diagnosis said out loud, no path named, no timeframe. He is one habit away from a much better day, and it is the same thirty seconds every time — here is what I think is wrong, here is what happens next, here is when.
