What Bradley did right — real strength, build on it

  • He gives customers the whole hour. Nothing on his day ran under 59 minutes. On a team where short visits correlate almost perfectly with no-closes, that is a structural advantage he already owns.
  • The five-star promise sets the standard at the door. On the Pagusara call it was part of the opening, and his card credits it directly for the customer agreeing to the estimate visit.
  • His questions do the selling. On Onda the discovery uncovered the real problem and built the credibility to present the solution — no pressure required.
  • He is patient with customers who need patience. Repeating the entry request calmly on the Crismarie call, twice, in the same friendly tone, is a small thing that decides how the next hour goes.

The gaps

each one in Bradley’s own calls
Weak

Eighty-six minutes of maintenance with nothing built at the end

The callMcKinnon · Job 670170738 — a full maintenance tune-up, expectations set, discovery questions asked, system gone over.
What was presentedNothing. No upgrade path, no repair option, no advisor visit, no horizon.
For contrastHis three flips all ended with a named next step, and two of them were shorter calls.

The fix A preventive visit is the best possible moment to talk about the future, because nothing is on fire and nobody is under pressure. Good news — it’s running fine today. Here’s what I’m watching, and here’s what I’d do about it: let’s have our advisor give you the replacement number now, at no cost, so when this system does start to go you’re choosing on your schedule instead of in a heatwave. Same visit, one extra sentence.

Moderate

The decision step depends on what kind of call it is

PatternOptions built and presented on all three diagnostic calls. Options not built at all on the maintenance call.
Why it mattersMaintenance visits are the largest single block of tape on this team’s day, and they are where the option work disappears most reliably.

The fix Make the ending identical regardless of the reason for the visit. The last two minutes of a tune-up should sound the same as the last two minutes of a no-cool call: what I found, what I’d do, which of these do you want to start with.

Moderate

The review is promised more often than it is collected

Bradley, PagusaraThe five-star experience is promised in the opening — the standard is set out loud at the door.
At the endNot collected on the maintenance calls, including a customer who got 86 unhurried minutes of his time.

The fix The promise creates the opening; the ask closes it. I told you at the start I was here to give you a five-star experience — did I? If I did, a review helps more families find us. On an 86-minute maintenance visit that answer is close to guaranteed.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Every maintenance call ends with a horizon. Before leaving a tune-up, name what he is watching and offer the no-cost advisor visit for the replacement number.
Proof it worked: Every maintenance call this week has a named next step in the notes; none end on ‘everything looks fine.’
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Same ending, every call type. The last two minutes of a tune-up must contain what I found, what I’d do, and which do you want to start with.
Proof it worked: Manager can point to those three elements at the end of every call, diagnostic or maintenance.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Collect the five-star promise he already makes, on every completed visit.
Proof it worked: Review asked for on every call where the promise was given; response logged either way.
8

What we owe Bradley Espinoza

  • The Pagusara call played back to the team — the opening promise, the systematic diagnosis and the estimate agreement are the cleanest end-to-end run of the model on this day.
  • Advisor availability for maintenance calls, not just for no-cools. He cannot offer a no-cost replacement number on a tune-up if the slot does not exist.
  • Protected call length. His visits are long because they are thorough, and three of four flipped — that is the return on the time.
Bottom line

Three flips out of four, on four long and genuinely thorough visits — Bradley is running the model properly wherever there is a problem to solve. The single gap is the maintenance call, where 86 minutes of good work ended with no path named at all. He already knows how to build the option; he just does not build it when nothing is broken. That is the one habit to change, and it is worth more than anything else on his list.
