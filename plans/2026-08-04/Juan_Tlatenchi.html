1

The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

The inspection starts before the customer has been told what it is for.

What actually happened

On both calls the sequence is greeting, a few questions, then work. The closest thing to a roadmap on either was I’ll figure out what the primary issue is… and then just kind of note down any possible things that might come up so we can prevent visits like these — said after the inspection had already begun, and never followed by a review of what was found.

What flips it

The roadmap costs ten seconds and it changes what the customer expects from the rest of the visit: Here’s how today works — I’ll check the indoor and outdoor units, then we’ll sit down and I’ll walk you through what I found and the options for it. I won’t do anything without your approval. Without it, the customer has no reason to expect a conversation at the end.

What Juan did right — real strength, build on it

  • He listens back. So it’s been kind of running under this condition for a while. That is the customer’s own answer reflected back, which is what makes people keep talking.
  • He gets names immediately and uses them. Both customers were on first-name terms with him inside a minute, on evening calls where people are tired.
  • He explains the preventive intent. note down any possible things that might come up so we can prevent visits like these — the right idea, and it belongs at the start of the call rather than in the middle.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Roadmap at the door, every call, before anything else. Scope, sequence, review conversation, approval promise.
Proof it worked: Manager can quote the roadmap from the first minute of every call this week; no inspection starts without it.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Fixed ending on every call: what I found, what I’d do, which do you want to start with.
Proof it worked: All three elements present at the end of every call, regardless of what was found.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Seed and collect the five-star ask, starting with the roadmap that now opens every call.
Proof it worked: Review seeded on every call and asked for on every completed visit.

What we owe Juan Tlatenchi

  • A written roadmap script he can carry, plus a ride-along on a call where somebody runs it properly — this is a repeatable habit to install, not a personality problem.
  • Job numbers on his recordings. Neither of his calls carried one, which makes outcome verification impossible and may be hiding results he is already getting.
  • Credit for the discovery. His questions are already right, and that is the harder half to teach.
Bottom line

Juan is doing the difficult part well — customers like him, and his onset and history questions are exactly the right ones, asked without a script showing. What is missing is the frame around them. Neither customer was told what the visit was for at the start or what it found at the end, so there was never a moment where a decision could happen. Two sentences, one at the door and one before leaving, would change both of these calls completely.
