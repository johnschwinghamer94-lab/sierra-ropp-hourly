The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

Same technician, same skill, two different answers to ‘that’s a lot of money.’

What actually happened

On the daytime call the customer balked at the investment — spend 14 grand — and the reply was a pitch: if you upgrade it, you can add… it adds equity to the home. The call ended with an emailed report: Do you have an email that I could send this? I’m going to type up a report and still send it over to you with pictures.

What flips it

On the evening call he did the opposite and it flipped. He let the customer name their own position — I’d like to at least be aware of what’s coming down the road, possibly. Or at least have the option — and answered with an action: Let me call him. Let me see if he’s still up. Cost objections need a question first, then a booking. He already knows how; he did it eight hours later.

5

What David did right — real strength, build on it

  • He proves things instead of asserting them. A megohm meter reading showing compressor damage, and side-by-side reference images of a correct plenum coil installation against the customer’s own. Nobody has to take his word for anything.
  • He turns a competitor’s miss into trust rather than a sales line. The evening customers had just paid for an inspection that missed improper plenum work, condensate faults and compressor damage. He showed them what was there and let the comparison speak.
  • He books while the customer is still warm. Let me call him. Let me see if he’s still up. Ten in the evening and he is arranging the advisor rather than promising to.
  • The numbers are translated, every time. An RLA is 25 and it’s running at 22… once it hits that 25, compressor starts to overheat and shuts off. Rated against actual, in plain language.

How it ended"Do you have an email that I could send this? I'm going to type up a report and still send it over to you with pictures."

The fix Everything before that moment was excellent and the answer is not a better pitch — it is a question. That is a real number, I get it. What would make an investment like this feel manageable — is it the total, or the timing? Then the advisor visit becomes the obvious next step instead of an emailed report. He proved this himself on the evening call, where he asked and then booked.

Moderate

Retirement and downsizing were discussed at length and never used

The contextThe daytime call covered union work, retirement timing and downsizing plans — definitely a downsize… it depends what my wife wants to do.
What happened with itNothing. The conversation stayed rapport and never came back to what it means for a $14,000 decision on a house they may be leaving.

The fix That is not small talk, it is the whole decision frame. Someone planning to downsize needs a different recommendation from someone staying twenty years, and saying so out loud makes the advice trustworthy: Given you’re looking at downsizing, let’s be honest about what makes sense — keeping this running versus what a buyer will expect to see. Let me get our advisor to price both so you can choose.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Cost objection = question first, booking second. No pitch, no benefit list, until a question has been asked and answered.
Proof it worked: Every call this week with a cost objection has a question on tape before any further explanation, and an advisor slot offered after it.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Use the life context. Whenever a customer names a plan — retirement, downsizing, selling, staying — state out loud what it means for the recommendation.
Proof it worked: Manager can point to the sentence connecting the customer’s plan to the advice on every call where a plan was mentioned.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Ask for the review in the same breath as the email address, on every completed visit.
Proof it worked: Review requested on every visit that ends with a report being sent.
