The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

Excellent openings, and then every call ends on ‘I’ll write a report.’

What actually happened

On Petrizzo — 137 minutes, a 13-year-old system, a fan motor losing oil, heat-exchanger wear and inrush stress — the findings were explained and the call defaulted to writing a report. On Martin the customer said This is going to destroy our unit for sure and the reply was to carry on inspecting.

What flips it

Both of those are open doors. The customer on Martin had already supplied the urgency; all that was needed was to come back to it: You said this is going to destroy the unit — you’re right to worry, and here’s what I found that explains it. Two ways forward: the repair I can do, or a no-cost advisor visit to look at whether replacing makes more sense at 13 years. Which do you want to start with?

5

What Nathan did right — real strength, build on it

  • His expectations line contains the permission clause and the standard. don’t do anything without your approval plus I do all this to give you a five star experience. So at any time if I’m not doing that, feel free to kick me out immediately. Confident, disarming, and it sets the bar out loud.
  • He opens on the customer’s problem. I understand you got some issues with the system today. First sentence, and it is about them.
  • He asks about the customer’s time before taking it. Checking constraints up front on a visit that ran over two hours is what kept that call comfortable.
  • His discovery is genuinely broad. Power bills, tenure, onset and prior issues — not just the symptom in front of him.
  • He gives customers the whole visit. 137 minutes across three zones on the Petrizzo call. The thoroughness is real; it just has no ending yet.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: No report without two paths. Nothing goes to the van until the repair and the no-cost advisor visit have both been said out loud.
Proof it worked: Manager hears two named paths at the end of every call this week; no call ends on ‘I’ll write a report.’
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Catch the customer’s own words. When a customer states a fear or a risk, repeat it back after the diagnosis and attach the recommendation to it.
Proof it worked: Every call where a concern is voiced has a sentence on tape linking a finding back to it.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Collect the five-star promise he already makes, on every completed visit.
Proof it worked: Review asked for on every completed call; response logged either way.
