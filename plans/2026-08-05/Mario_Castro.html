1

The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

Three customers volunteered the sale. Three reports went out with nothing on them.

What actually happened

A tenant told him the master bedroom is unliveable and asked him for written recommendations — maybe you could recommend them putting in some ceiling fans — and got No, they don't do that. An original 1991 homeowner told him one of two units will not cool and that a woman on an oxygen line lives there. A maintenance customer said I'm at eight years, unprompted, right after he explained that units last eight to ten.

What flips it

Each of those is one sentence away from an Option C hand-off. Here's what I'm putting in the report for your landlord: the duct correction, and a no-cost visit from our comfort advisor so he has both numbers in front of him. The information he gathers is already excellent — it just has to leave the house attached to a named path and a date.

What Mario did right — real strength, build on it

  • He disarmed the hardest opening of the day. A customer began by describing the last technician as You need this. You need this, you need this… I can't afford it. Don't even tell me. His answer bought him the whole visit: My job is just to give you all the information, let you know how your unit is running. That's it… it's up to you.
  • He corrected dangerous advice kindly. She had heard on the radio to hold the house at 80 when it is 110 outside. No. You can keep it at whatever temperature. If you are comfortable at 76… If you don't use it, the heat destroys these units. That is expertise delivered as a favour, not a lecture.
  • He worked past his own job description for a tenant. I can always check in the. Check the duct work. I can go up there and take a look at it… I don't mind. I mean, I'm sure if you guys are here, you guys want to be comfortable here. The tenant tried to let him off and he insisted.
  • He brought the part the customer was promised. Yes, ma'am. I brought it with me. Small thing, enormous trust value — and the opposite of what happened on several other calls this day.
  • The full bill question, both periods, without it feeling like an interview. Our last electric bill was like 250… the first two was what, like 80? That contrast is the whole Assessment step and he got it inside a conversation about Chicago winters.
  • He thought about the next tenant. whether it's you guys, the next tenants, stuff like that, we don't want them to keep having that same problem. That is the right instinct — it just needs a written path behind it.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Nothing leaves the house unnamed. Every visit ends with at least one specific path said out loud to the person in front of him — repair, duct correction or a no-cost advisor visit — even when the decision-maker is elsewhere.
Proof it worked: Every recording contains a named path in the final two minutes.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Affordability statements get a question, not a nod. Acknowledge, ask what would have to be true, then offer the no-cost look.
Proof it worked: On every call where money comes up, the recording contains a question back and an advisor offer.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Age or a comfort complaint said out loud equals the advisor offer within thirty seconds — plus the five-star seed in the opening on all calls, not just some, and the ask at the door.
Proof it worked: Manager can point to the offer inside thirty seconds of the signal on every recording that week.

Bottom line

Mario's front half is as good as anyone's on this board and better than most — he gets parking, permission, the bill, the setting, the hot rooms and the customer's real story inside ten minutes, and he does it while people are laughing. Then all of that information goes into a report and nothing comes out of his mouth. A tenant literally asked him to write a recommendation and got “no.” He does not need to sell harder; he needs to say one path out loud before he picks up his ladder. On this day he had three chances and used none of them.
