5

What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • He gave the customer a number and the line it must not cross. they're running at about 170. There's a thermal switch in them at 200… Yours is 200 degrees. Urgency built from a measurement needs no persuasion behind it.
  • He talked a finding down. It's showing burn marks. No, not a super urgent concern, if I'm being honest. That single sentence is why the urgent one was believed.
  • He separated now from later, out loud. Immediate concern on the compressor temperatures, future concern on the coil rust holding on for dear life. Customers can plan around that; they cannot plan around a list.
  • He let the customer hear the evidence. if you listen to the video and put it up to your ear, you'll be able to hear.
  • He asked for the review. I hope I gave you guys a great experience… put us in good words for us. On a board where almost nobody collected, he did.
  • He booked the specialist. The Option C hand-off is the win, and he made it on a two-hour call about a noise complaint.

Gap 1 — Objection handling was the one action that failed

What the call had in itAn immediate concern on both compressors, a future concern on the coils, a refrigerant phase-out conversation, and a replacement decision routed to a specialist — over 122 minutes.
ScorecardHandling objections: fail. The other three critical actions all passed.

The fix The FSG sequence is three beats and he already has the hardest one — credibility. When pushback comes: acknowledge without agreeing (That's completely fair), get curious (What's the part that concerns you most — the timing or the cost of being wrong?), then go back to their own words (You told me it's loudest at night when you're trying to sleep — that's what this fixes). He should not lose a call he has already diagnosed this well.

The fix That is a good inference and it belongs in the customer's mouth. Turn it into a question and wait: So the upstairs one is already running thirty degrees from its cut-out and the downstairs one is on the same age and the same schedule — what do you think happens there? Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: The three-beat objection response, rehearsed out loud with a manager five times before the next demand day — acknowledge, question, back to their words.
Proof it worked: Every recording containing pushback has an audible question back before any re-explanation.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Hand over the conclusion. After every measurement, ask the customer what they think it means rather than telling them.
Proof it worked: On every call the customer states the consequence in their own words at least once.

Week 3
