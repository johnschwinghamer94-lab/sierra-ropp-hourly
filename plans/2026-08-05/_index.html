The pattern across the day

What is working
Strong
The Welcome and Assessment steps are in genuinely good shape. Parking, permission and names are near-universal; four technicians delivered the complete expectations sequence including the five-star seed; and the technical explanation on this board is excellent — capacitor tolerances read out to the decimal, amp draw against the data tag, refrigerant pressures shown on screen with the target range beside them, compressors compared to hearts and blood pressure.
Where the day was lost
Weak
The ask. Nine of fifteen technicians produced no confirmed flip, and in almost every case the diagnosis was right and the appointment was never requested. The four confirmed flips all share one thing: a specific time and, on three of them, a signature taken before leaving the property.
The recurring miss
Moderate
Customers named their own buying signals out loud all day — “I'm at eight years”, “October, November, I want to replace it”, “how much I have to start saving”, “I might as well go for the financing”, “what do we do?” asked twice — and were answered with reassurance, lifespan facts or more diagnosis instead of an advisor visit with a date on it.
Objection handling
Weak
Failed for eleven of the fifteen technicians. The pattern is consistent: empathy is present, the question is missing. Affordability statements and absent decision-makers get agreement rather than curiosity.
Five-star collection
Weak
Seeded on roughly half the calls and collected on almost none. One technician asked for it outright.
Recording coverage
Weak
The most serious problem on this page and it is not a technician problem. Well over half of the day's recordings stop when the technician picks up a tool, and several captured only a fraction of the billed time — one 91-minute visit produced roughly ten usable minutes, and one 45-minute visit produced twenty words. On those calls the Decision step is unknown, not weak, and the close rates below understate the day.
Bottom line

This board can diagnose. Across fifteen technicians the Welcome and Assessment steps are solid to strong almost everywhere, and the technical teaching — refrigerant law, capacitor tolerances, amp draw against the plate, internal overloads, seized dampers — is better than the numbers suggest. What separated the four who converted from the eleven who did not was a single behaviour: naming the no-cost specialist visit, putting a time on it, and getting a signature before leaving the property. Three of the four flips have a zero-dollar authorisation attached. Everyone else described the service and waited to be asked. Two things need fixing above all else: give every technician the reflex of turning a stated buying signal into a booked date, and fix the recordings, because more than half of this day's real work is invisible.
