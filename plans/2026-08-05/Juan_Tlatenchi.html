The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

Perfect discovery, and he closed both doors himself before the evaluation started.

What actually happened

The customer pre-empted him — we're not gonna, like, get a new unit or anything like that, right? — and he answered No. Later, about the two rooms that never cool: I don't think there's anything we can do with our AC that can help because those two rooms just heat up, and he agreed: south facing houses always going to be a little warmer. The customer was already shopping a window unit for those rooms.

What flips it

Neither of those needed a sale to be answered — they needed a question. Fair enough, I'm not here to sell you a unit. Let me ask this though: if those two back rooms could actually hold temperature without a window unit in them, would that be worth having somebody look at? The customer has already decided to spend money on the problem. The only question is whether it is spent at Home Depot or with us.

5

What Juan did right — real strength, build on it

  • He asked the bill question and it changed the whole call. How are your power? → last month was 300 and normally it's 18. On a solar house that is a seventeen-fold jump, and he is one of the few technicians who had a number to work with.
  • He turned the customer’s theory into a test. She thought the thermostat was misreading; he put a thermometer next to it and showed her the gap — It's picking up about 84 degrees against a thermostat reading 80. Nothing persuades like a measurement the customer watches.
  • His expectations line is most of the way there and it sounds natural. I don't do any work without your approval, so you don't have to worry about me doing any surprise work.
  • He named the next diagnostic instead of guessing. I'll run a couple of tests and it'll tell me the. What the pressure is in the duct system. That is the correct answer to a room-imbalance complaint.
  • He captured the customer’s actual priority. my concern is the wear and tear on the unit — that sentence is the key to this whole call, and he got her to say it out loud.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Never agree a problem is unsolvable. Any “there's nothing you can do about that” gets a question and a test, not a nod.
Proof it worked: On every call where a customer writes off a comfort problem, the recording contains a proposed test or a named path.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: The pre-emptive “we're not buying” gets acknowledged without conceding. Respect the statement, keep the evaluation open, report honestly.
Proof it worked: No recording contains a flat “no” to a path before the system has been evaluated.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Open every report with the customer's own priority sentence, quoted back. Add the today line and the five-star seed to the expectations he already gives.
Proof it worked: Manager can hear the customer's priority repeated at the start of the presentation on every call.

Bottom line

Juan ran the best discovery of any single call this day. He got the bill, the setting, the run-time, the onset and — hardest of all — the customer's real reason for caring, in her own words. Then he spent the rest of the recording agreeing with her: no new unit, nothing to be done about the hot rooms, that is just the way south-facing houses are. She was mid-decision on a window unit and he helped her make it. Nothing about his questioning needs to change; the one habit to break is agreeing that a problem cannot be solved before he has finished finding out whether it can.
