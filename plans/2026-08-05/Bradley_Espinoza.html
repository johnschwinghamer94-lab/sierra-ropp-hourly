The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

The best opening habit on the board, and the objection was already on the table before he sat down.

What actually happened

On the Hill call the customer told him twice, inside the first two minutes, how this would end: I'm not getting myself way out in front till I see what's what and I don't do nothing off the spur of the moment… me and my wife will decide… She sleep. Bradley answered with credibility — I'm very transparent with my evaluation. I always show everything. All of my work, all of my photos — and then went to work.

What flips it

The transparency answer is good and it is only half of it. The wife was asleep in the house. That is a plan to make, not a fact to accept: Completely understood — you shouldn't decide anything on the spot. So let's do it this way: I'll photograph everything, and when I'm done I'll leave you two a written page with the options on it so you can go through it together tonight. Would it help if I walked her through it as well while I'm here? On the Maciel call he did exactly this, with the husband, and it is the strongest moment of his day.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Every objection gets a question back before any reassurance. Rehearsed with a manager until it is automatic — acknowledge, ask, then help.
Proof it worked: Every recording with an objection has an audible question back, not just a credibility statement.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Name the finding at the check-in. No “a couple things” — one named fault, tied to the customer's complaint, before he goes to write.
Proof it worked: On every call the customer can state the problem back before the report is written.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Use the Maciel decision-maker plan on every call where somebody is absent — a phone call or a written page for that evening. And collect the five-star he already seeds three times out of three.
Proof it worked: No call ends with an unaddressed absent decision-maker, and the ask is audible at the door.
