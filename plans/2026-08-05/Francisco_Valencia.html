The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

He knows exactly how to make the hand-off. He only makes it when the customer has already given up.

What actually happened

On the Peggy call the process was textbook: route the exact number to the specialist — To get the exact number, I would have to get the other guy to come out and just size up that system… He would be the one that determines that — then get him moving: So they're sending someone right now for you… he should be here in the next 20 to 30 minutes. On Casillas, a customer with a five-year-old system and a $20,000 quote from somebody else got it doesn't sound like you guys want to go either route… So it's up to you guys what you guys want to do.

What flips it

The Casillas customer asked him directly: Do you want to see if there's a repair? That is an invitation to work, and the answer was a membership pitch and a brand lecture. The same sentence that worked on Peggy works here: Let's find out properly instead of guessing. I'll evaluate it, and I'll have our specialist give you a real second number at no cost so you can compare it to that $20,000 — then you're deciding with facts instead of somebody's word.

What Francisco did right — real strength, build on it

  • He made the hand-off properly, and he made it fast. Specialist dispatched during the visit, he should be here in the next 20 to 30 minutes, with a personal introduction and a tour of the equipment. That is the Option C flip executed the way it is supposed to work.
  • He routed the exact number to the specialist instead of guessing. To get the exact number, I would have to get the other guy to come out and just size up that system… He would be the one that determines that. Because they're specialized. Exactly right.
  • He listened to a customer’s real constraint and answered it. next month I'll be 78, so I need one to last → the ten-year warranty. Her age was not an obstacle to work around; it was the reason for the recommendation.
  • He explained a genuinely dangerous shortcut another company had taken. The bypassed high-pressure switch, why it exists, and what it protects — when that compressor goes out, you guys are looking at a big expense — without calling anyone a crook.
  • He knows the refrigerant rules and states them plainly. with the refrigerant change, we can't replace the condenser outside by itself. That saves a customer from a false expectation later.
  • He offered a low-commitment path when a customer was not ready. The maintenance programme framed as tracking — the maintenance program will keep a track of how things are progressing… when the next person comes, if something's off, then they can compare it to readings from the past visit — is a good instinct that needs an evaluation behind it.

Customer, Casillas"Do you want to see if there's a repair?"
Francisco"So that's. I guess what I'd like to see is. So we know that the outdoor unit is running high on amperage. So the other thing that I would suggest, if you guys don't want, you know, to get to go into details at the moment, would be to sign up for the maintenance program."

The fix These people called for a second opinion after being told to spend $20,000 on a five-year-old system. The one thing they wanted was somebody to actually look. Never tell a customer what they do not want — ask, then do the work: Let me put my gauges on it and give you the real condition in writing. That way, whatever you decide, you're deciding from facts and not from two different companies' opinions. The evaluation is the option-builder; without it there is nothing to present.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Always evaluate. No visit ends without gauges on the system and a written condition report, regardless of what the customer says they want at the door.
Proof it worked: Every gradeable call has readings and a report on the ticket.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Talk about their unit, not about brands. No brand hierarchies, no commentary on manufacturers or other companies — measurements from the system in front of him instead.
Proof it worked: Zero brand-ranking passages on any recording; every claim traced to a reading.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Make the Peggy hand-off the default, not the exception. Any customer who mentions replacement, a competitor quote or a long wait for parts gets the specialist offered and dispatched in the same conversation. Add the approval, today and five-star beats to his expectations frame.
Proof it worked: Every recording where replacement comes up contains a specialist offer with a time.

Bottom line

Francisco already owns the move the whole team is being coached toward — he made a real Option C hand-off, same day, warm introduction included, for the hardest customer anybody had this week. The problem is that he only reaches for it once the customer has already surrendered. On the two calls where somebody was still deciding, he agreed with the objection, told them what they did not want, and filled the silence with brand rankings instead of putting gauges on the unit. His close rate is one call away from doubling.
