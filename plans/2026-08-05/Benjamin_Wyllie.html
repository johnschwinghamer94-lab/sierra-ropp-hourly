The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

Two customers with failing parts. One got a path; the other got an education.

What actually happened

On the Nelson call the customer volunteered everything: a previous technician had told her the blower motor was flooded, it's probably going to go sometime soon, and it's like $1,200, and to let it go until it fails. When Ben asked whether she wanted to hear about concerns she said bring it to my attention so that I know what to expect… know how much I have to start saving. He then found a dead start capacitor and a compressor well over its limit — and the options action was flagged as not passed.

What flips it

Know how much I have to start saving is not a comment, it is a request for a number and a plan. Then let's get you that number properly. Two things: I'll price the capacitor and the motor so you know the repair path — and I'll have our advisor come out at no cost so you also know what a new system looks like. Then you're saving toward a decision instead of toward a surprise. Can I get him out this week?

5

What Ben did right — real strength, build on it

  • He reads specifications out loud and shows the arithmetic. rated for 45 plus 10, plus or minus 5%… the lowest the number should ever be is 9.5. This is reading 7.74. A customer cannot argue with that and does not need to trust him to believe it.
  • He gave away the leak search. Usually that's like an additional charge, but I'm going to do that for you for free so we can get you taken care of. On a customer who had just lost thousands to another company, that was the right call.
  • He asks the horizon question. Is this a long term home or are you planning on moving soon? That single answer determines whether repair or replacement is the honest recommendation, and hardly anyone on this board asks it.
  • He explained why topping off a leak is not a fix. the federal government says that we can't just add Freon into a system where there's a leak going on… giving you a temporary fix for a very short amount of time, not giving you an actual solution. That protects the customer and it protects Sierra's recommendation.
  • The floor-protector answer. I want to respect your home the way my wife makes me respect ours. Warm, specific, and it lands.
  • He promises visual proof up front. I'm gonna show you pictures and videos… so that you see it and it's not just me telling you.

Week 1

Drill: Every measured failure gets a named path in the same conversation — repair priced, plus the no-cost advisor visit offered to a day.
Proof it worked: Every recording with a failed component contains a stated repair path and an advisor offer.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Answer inherited advice. Whenever a customer repeats another technician's “run it till it fails,” respond with the cost-of-failure reframe rather than general lifespan facts.
Proof it worked: Manager can point to the reframe on every call where prior advice is quoted.

Week 3
Drill: One sentence on the past, then forward. Cap commentary about other companies and brands, and spend that time on the two paths instead. Collect the five-star he already seeds on every call.
Proof it worked: No extended competitor passages on any recording; the review ask audible at the door.
