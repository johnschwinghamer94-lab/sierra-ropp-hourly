The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

A perfect front door, and both objections were already on the mat before he stepped over it.

What actually happened

Before he had touched anything, the customer told him twice how this would end. First: I have a favor to ask you. Don't find a lot of stuff wrong because they already gave me a list… When I say they gave me a list, it means dollars. Try not to find a lot of stuff. He answered I'll do my best. Then: I have to discuss it. Everything with my husband… if you're telling me something, I can't go with it right away.

What flips it

Both of those are gifts if they get answered at the door instead of at the report. I hear you — and I'm not here to add to a list. My job is to tell you the truth about what's urgent and what can wait, and I'll separate the two for you. Is your husband reachable while I'm here, or should I put it in writing so you two can look at it together tonight? Fifteen seconds, and the presentation stops being an ambush.

5

What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • He announces the expectation instead of sliding into it. I just want to make sure I'm setting a clear expectation of my visit for today. The customer knows a frame is being set and that it is for her benefit.
  • He asks the goal question, and then asks it again a different way. any expectations that you have of me… Is there anything that you're hoping to accomplish with me being here today? Nobody can build an option without that answer.
  • He asked the rooms question properly and followed the answer. When she said the front room runs cold and her son had explained it away, he pushed one step further: Does the back room feel a little warmer than others?
  • He offered to do work he was not sent for. if you have any extras, I'm happy to just get that done for you while I'm here. Just while I have my ladder and such.
  • He protects the customer’s home without being asked twice. Attic access in a clothes closet, and the answer was Thousand percent before she had finished the sentence.
  • He asked who decides, at the door. How involved is your husband when it comes to things around the house, maintenance wise? Asking early is exactly right; the follow-through is the part to build.

The gaps

Moderate

Gap 1 — “Don’t find a lot of stuff wrong” was accepted as an instruction

Customer, at the door"So I have a favor to ask you. Don't find a lot of stuff wrong because they already gave me a list… When I say they gave me a list, it means dollars. Yeah, I understand. Try not to find a lot of stuff."
Andrew"I'll do my best."

The fix She is not asking him to hide problems — she is telling him she is overwhelmed by a list she has already been handed today. Answer the fear, not the request: I understand completely, and I'm not here to hand you another list. What I will do is separate anything I find into what actually needs attention now and what's just worth watching — so you're not staring at a wall of numbers. Fair? That protects his own findings without arguing with her.

Moderate

Customer"Of course you'll tell me, but I have to discuss it. Everything with my husband, and I understand. You know the drill."
Customer, again"if you're telling me something, I can't go with it right away."
Andrew’s follow-up"How involved is your husband when it comes to things around the house, maintenance wise?" — answered "No, he's good. Yeah," and left there.

The fix He asked the right question and then accepted a non-answer. When a customer says twice that somebody else decides, get that person into the call before the report exists: That makes sense — would it be easier if I called him with you when I'm done, or should I put two clear options in writing so you can go through it with him tonight? An absent decision-maker is a reason to plan the hand-off, not a reason to accept a delay.
