Gap 1 — He named a price he had not earned and then disowned it

Joe"not really sure how much those cost off the top of my head. It could be anywhere between $300 and $600."
Customer"Or just a float. They have them like online at supply places for like $30."

The fix Two separate errors in thirty seconds. First, a guessed range before any evaluation — the price has to come from the report, not from memory, and the honest answer is I won't know until I've verified it. Give me twenty minutes and I'll bring you a real number. Second, and worse, distancing himself from his own company's pricing. That tells the customer the number is indefensible. The FSG answer to a markup objection is empathy and a question: Totally fair — you can absolutely buy the part cheaper than we can sell it installed. Can I ask what happened last time somebody worked on this for you? He already knew the answer to that: the customer had just told him a long story about being charged twice and argued with on the phone. That was the opening, and it was walked straight past.

6

This week's drill — never answer an objection with a fact

Empathy → question → helping. In that order, every time.

Trigger: any pushback on price, timing, or "I can't right now."

Response — and nothing before it:

"That's completely fair. Can I ask you one thing — is it the number itself, or the timing?"

Then stop and listen. No defence of the price, no "I don't make the prices," no cancelling the call. Two objections came up on this day and both were answered with information instead of a question. Practise this out loud with a manager five times before the next demand day.

3-week training plan

Week 1
The objection reflex
Empathy plus one curious question on every piece of pushback. Never quote a price that has not come from the report, and never distance yourself from a price you have quoted.
Week 2
The power-bill question
Added after every thermostat answer, on maintenance and demand calls alike, and reused during the check-in.
Week 3
Five-star seed and ask
Planted at the end of the expectations block, asked for at every close — starting with the calls that go well.
