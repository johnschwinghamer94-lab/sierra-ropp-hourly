1

The one-line verdict

Why it closed

He showed her the reading, then checked her warranty before he quoted her.

What actually happened

He checked in from the outdoor unit exactly as promised, then put the meter in front of the customer: you see right here where it says 45.5? … yours is reading F 3.4 and then nothing. Then, before naming a price: let me just double check first, because I want to make sure it's not in warranty.

Why that mattered

The customer had already told him what she was afraid of — I hope to God you can fix it. We don't have the money that we get a new one. He answered it by repairing rather than escalating, priced it plainly, took approval before the work, and only afterwards showed her the fan and voltage components that are wearing, framed as something to keep in mind rather than something to buy today.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He checked the warranty before he quoted. let me just double check first, because I want to make sure it's not in warranty. On a customer who had already told him money was tight.
  • He refused to fit a part that might have caused the failure. it needs what it needs… I don't want to do any more harm. That conversation cost him time and made him nothing.
  • He built the visit around two people who had just left hospital. Please don't, I don't want you to move around. I'll be all set. I'll come to you — and then closed the garage and left by the front door so neither of them had to stand up.
  • The full expectations block on UPHAM, named beat by beat. Thorough evaluation, permission to share findings, report and options, approval, today, five stars.
  • He priced the total, not the part. it'd be a little less than 600 for everything — before asking for a signature.

Gap 3 — One option, when the same visit found two more components failing

After the repair"this is your voltage, which that is starting to get burned… this is showing signs of failure. So if something else does happen, those two components could be what it is."
What was never builtAny path from "keep that in mind" to a plan — no bundled repair, no protection option, no specialist visit for a household that has just said it cannot absorb a replacement.

The fix He found the fan and the voltage component wearing and handed the customer a warning instead of a choice. The FSG move is to make it a real option while she is already sitting there with the meter in front of her: fix today's fault, or fix it and address the two parts that are visibly going, so the next failure is not a 110-degree emergency in a house that cannot afford one. Same honesty, one more path.

6

This week's drill — collect the review you already earned

Trigger and response

Trigger: the customer thanks you in their own words — "you saved us", "thanks for being honest", "I appreciate you."

Response, immediately:

"That really means a lot. If you felt that way about today, would you mind putting it in a five-star review? It's the biggest thing that helps me and the team."

Then stop talking. He earned two of these on one call and asked for neither.

7

3-week training plan

Week 1
The five-star ask
Seeded in the expectations block on every call, asked for at every close, starting with the ones that go well.
Week 2
The block on demand calls
All six beats on evening and emergency calls, not just maintenance visits. Manager spot-checks the first five minutes.
Week 3
Two options, not one
Whenever a second component is found wearing, it becomes a priced path — not a warning delivered after the card is run.
