What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He pre-framed the repair-versus-replace handoff at the front door. we'll have someone come and give you the quote so you know the pricing of what it'd be to repair versus replace. Agreed to before there was anything to disagree about — this is the cheapest possible time to set up Option C.
  • Permission asked, and then asked again. And is it okay for me to just come in and out as needed? Almost nobody asks this, and it removes every awkward moment for the rest of the visit.
  • Four diagnostic questions before a tool came out. Duration, noises, prior breakdowns, prior repairs — on a fault the customer had already been given a theory about.
  • He made the member feel like a member. Looks like you've been a member of this for some time now, man. I appreciate you having me.

Moderate

Gap 2 — Three of the four required questions never get asked

What he asked"Would you say you've changed those pretty recently?"
What he did not askWhich rooms run hotter or colder, what the thermostat is set at in summer and winter, and what a standard power bill looks like at that setting.

The fix His diagnostic questions are better than most people's required questions — but they are not the same thing. The four required questions are what build the replacement case later; the diagnostic ones only solve today's fault. On a call where the customer has already asked to compare repair against replacement, the bill question is the single most valuable sentence available and it was never said.
