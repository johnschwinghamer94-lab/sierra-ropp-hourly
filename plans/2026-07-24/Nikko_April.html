1

The one-line verdict

Why they converted

He explains the dead end and then hands over the only door out.

What actually happened

The coils are no longer made for R22 refrigerant. The gas that your system takes — can't get coils anymore, or you can't get outdoor units in that gas. Then the honest limit: I don't have a price. That's not my department. I service them. And then the handover — with the warranty terms explained so the customer knew what the specialist was bringing: three years full, ten years parts.

Why that works

Constraint, honest limit, handover. The customer cannot solve an obsolete-refrigerant problem with a repair, Nikko cannot price the alternative, and the specialist can. Stating all three plainly makes the second visit the obvious next step rather than a sales attempt.

5

What Nikko did right — real strength, build on it

  • Two specialists dispatched the same day. Both handovers happened during the visit rather than being promised for later.
  • He explains the dead end and his own limit in the same breath. I don't have a price. That's not my department is precisely why the specialist is credible.
  • He named the ceiling-damage risk as protection rather than pressure, and the customer heard it that way.
  • He asked the power-bill question and used it as a diagnostic signal rather than a sales prompt.
  • He explained the home-warranty route honestly — including what it should cover — instead of talking the customer out of it.

Nikko's method has settled into something repeatable: explain why the repair path is closed, admit what he cannot price, and hand the customer to someone who can — with the warranty terms stated so they know what is coming.
