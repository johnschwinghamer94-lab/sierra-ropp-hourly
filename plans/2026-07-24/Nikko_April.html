1

The one-line verdict

Why both flips landed

He never let "that's not my department" be the last thing he said.

The finding

Twenty-six-year-old equipment, a rusted coil clogging the drain line, and an emergency pan at the brim above the living-room ceiling: Hey Terry, I gotta shut this off asap. You guys are on the verge of having flooding come through your ceiling. Then the economics, in the customer's favour: instead of putting 8, 900 in cleanings, you can put that money towards payments on a new system, being under a 10 year warranty.

And then the appointment

Asked what a replacement costs, he gave the same answer other reps gave — and did not stop there: I don't have a price, that's not my department… I could have them come out, take their measurements while I'm on site. There's no charge for that. I'm not charging you for coming out because you're a member. Then he made the call: I'm gonna call Ryan. He's the one that sets up the appointment… I'm gonna get that ball rolling just so you guys aren't sitting in the heat.

Check-ins

Draw the Picture
Strong
Best sustained teaching on the board. The contactor: when the power goes through that switch, when it connects, it does a little static shock. It's normal. But over years of doing that, it burns the switch up… so every other time when it goes to turn on, it's not making that connection. Closed vents: that's kind of like clogging off your exhaust on your car. The drain system: one of these is the primary drain, you should see water dripping out of… that pipe is your emergency drain — that's where, when water's not coming out of the primary, you have a secondary so it doesn't flood your ceiling.

Strong

An easier read would have been a compressor sale.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He shut a system down before it flooded a ceiling. You guys are on the verge of having flooding come through your ceiling — spotted, escalated and stopped inside a minute.
  • He apologised for another technician's mistake, unprompted. It looks like the previous technician poked a hole in your AC coil. I apologize for that. Nothing rebuilds trust faster.
  • Two specialists dispatched during the visit, not promised for later. Both flips exist because he made the call while he was still standing there.
  • He refused to sell a compressor he was not sure about. The pressures looked like a failure; he checked with a manager and reported the truth instead.
  • He hunted a warranty through two surnames, a serial number and the parts department for a customer he had never met — and then told her honestly it was not registered.
  • He taught a tenant to change his own filters, step by step, on a house he will never sell anything into: I'll show you how to change those, just so you know.
  • He is the only rep who explained why the power-bill question exists. if there's something going on with the outdoor unit and you have a two, three hundred dollar equal pay electric bill, there could be something going on underlying.

The fix He prevented ceiling damage, waived a fee, and got thanked in the customer's own words — and left without asking. Moderate

Gap 2 — He knows exactly why the bill question matters and asked it once

On the one call he asked it"In the summertime, what are your energy bills like?" … "we ask these questions — if there's something going on with the outdoor unit and you have like a two, three hundred dollar equal pay electric bill, there could be something going on underlying."
On the two replacement calls, where it would have decided thingsNever asked — even while explaining to one customer that a struggling twenty-six-year-old system costs money to run, and to another that my energy bills were 300 a month, and now I replaced the unit, now it's cheaper.

The fix He used another homeowner's bill as evidence on a call where he could have used the customer's own. Ask it after every thermostat answer, on every call, and the replacement case stops being a story about someone else and becomes their arithmetic. He already has the best explanation of it on the board — it just needs to be said four times a day instead of once.

Moderate

Gap 3 — He kept teaching after the customer had already said yes

Customer, decided"All right, well, give me an estimate on the pricing" … and on the other call: "so what are we looking at for replacement with replacement quotes and options?"
What followedLine-set flushing, nitrogen, P-traps, foam thickness, permit video inspections, drywall crews, three-year and ten-year warranty structures.

The fix All of it is true and most of it is genuinely reassuring — but it arrives after the decision, when it can only introduce new questions. Confirm the appointment, then stop: Perfect. He'll be here in about half an hour, and he'll walk you through all of it. Save the depth for the moments it moves someone, and let the specialist own the rest.

6

This week's drill — one sentence at each end of the call

Two habits, both one line long

At the end of the expectations block, every door:

"And my goal today is a five-star experience — if I'm falling short of that at any point, stop me."

After every thermostat answer:

"And what does a standard power bill look like for you, setting it there in the summer?"

Then, at the close on the calls that go well — and on this day two of them went very well — ask for the review. He has already earned it twice over.
