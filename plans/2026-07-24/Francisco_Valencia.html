1

The one-line verdict

What the tape shows

He got the customer to say the diagnosis back to him.

How he did it

Three findings, each with a reading and an image. The start device: it's like your car battery, when it starts losing its juice, it doesn't send the proper power to that component. The compressor: I can't open it and see how it's doing from inside, so we rely on our tools… it's lost a significant amount of that wiring. The contactor: it's supposed to be all copper, but if you see here you start having a lot of charcoal and a lot of burn marks.

And then the customer took over

Todd, unprompted: So the compressor is just not running efficiently … And then when it tries to kick on again, it may not. Francisco: Correct. That is the hardest behavior in the entire rubric, executed properly — the customer owns the problem before anyone mentions money.

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • The customer diagnosed his own system out loud. So basically a bad compressor or a compressor that's going bad … And then when it tries to kick on again, it may not. That is the hardest behavior in the rubric and he got it three times in one conversation.
  • Every finding came with a reading and an image. Rated against actual, tolerance explained, then the car battery — no jargon left standing.
  • He checked comprehension instead of assuming it. Any questions? Did I confuse you anywhere?
  • Honest about what today could and could not deliver. if it's something that we need to order, we wouldn't be able to fix it for you tonight because all our warehouses are closed. Nobody was set up to be disappointed.
  • He caught what the setpoint answer meant. That it's really struggling then — the ECKLUND system was losing ground every afternoon and nobody had told them.

Gap 2 — He proved the system was struggling and never asked what it was costing

ECKLUND"What temperature do you guys keep it at?" — "Usually 79." … "It'll go up to 80 and then it comes back down." — "Till the sun drops down. Wow. That it's really struggling then."
What was never asked"And what does a standard power bill look like for you setting it there in the summer?"

The fix He was one sentence from the strongest argument available to him. A system that cannot hold 79 through an afternoon is being paid for twice — once in comfort and once on the bill — but without her number it stays his opinion. The follow-up is mandatory for exactly this reason: ask it every time the setpoint question gets answered, on maintenance calls most of all.

Weak

Gap 3 — No approval promise, on any call

What he says"Then I'll write up a report for you guys… and then I'll go over it and if it makes any sense, we can talk about it."
What is missing"I won't do anything without your approval." Not said on ECKLUND, LOPEZ or MANNING.

The fix His version — you just let me know, hey, this makes sense, this doesn't make sense — is close, and it is why customers relax around him. But it is a promise about the conversation, not about the work. The approval sentence is what makes the option conversation feel safe rather than sold, and it costs him four seconds.

6

This week's drill — two sentences and a running recorder

Bolt these onto what he already does well

At the intake, after the setpoint answer:

"And what does a standard power bill look like for you setting it there in the summer?"

At the end of the expectations block:

"And I won't do anything without your approval first."

Then let the tape run to the end of the call. His teaching is already the standard; nobody has ever seen him close.

Bottom line

Francisco does the thing everyone else on the board is trying to learn: he explains a failure so clearly that the customer explains it back to him. Three findings, three numbers, three images, and a homeowner who understood his own system by the end of it. Two habits are missing — the power-bill question and the approval promise — and one recorder is quitting on him right at the moment the call turns into a decision. Fix those and there is very little left to coach.
