What went right — real strength, build on it

  • Four doors, four complete expectations blocks, five stars included every time. Nobody else on the board did this on more than one call. This is the training standard and it belongs on tape for the team.
  • He told a customer his system was fine. Everything is within specs… the fan was within specs, wasn't loose. The easiest money on the board this day was a manufactured noise problem, and he did not take it.
  • He did a chargeable leak search for free because the pressures warranted it — and it found the actual fault.
  • He asked the pain question nobody asks. What was that experience like? → Oh, that was rough → and then he named it back: so that's why you kind of have PTSD from that. Of like, we don't want to do that again. That is the whole reason that customer will buy.
  • He handed the conclusion to the customer. What do you think we should do about this part? — and then waited.
  • Booties, and a reason for them. I want to respect your home the way my wife makes me respect mine.

Customer"If I had even four to six months just to save ten grand, then we circle back and do that." … "I don't have ten grand to fork up right this second."
Ben"Exactly. No, totally understand that. So we'll go take a look."

The fix That is not a no — it is a customer handing over her exact requirements. Six people in a house at eighty-five degrees, a thousand a week eating out, and a competitor's quote she cannot afford. The FSG move is empathy, then a question, then help: Totally fair, and I'd feel the same. Can I ask — if the monthly worked, would the timing change? Because the whole reason our specialist comes out free is to show you what it actually looks like month to month, and there's no obligation in it. Her own arithmetic on takeaway alone may close that gap. Agreeing with the objection ends the conversation; asking one question keeps it alive.

6

This week's drill — attach an appointment to the recommendation

Trigger and response

Trigger: you have just said any version of "I'd rather you do a long-term solution," or the customer has asked what a replacement costs.

Response, in the same breath — never "different department" first:

"I can't price a system myself, but our specialist can and it costs you nothing. I can get him out here — does this evening work, or is tomorrow morning better?"

Then stop talking. Everything Ben builds before that moment is already the standard; the recommendation just needs a date attached to it.

3-week training plan

Week 1
Capture the whole call
Recorder check before every knock; stops only in the van. Target: one complete tape through options, price and close — his first real close-rate read.
Week 2
Recommendation plus appointment
Every replacement recommendation converts to an offered day and time for a specialist, on the spot.
Week 3
One question after every objection
Never agree and move on. Empathy, then a curious question — especially on affordability, where he already has the customer's trust.

Bottom line

Ben is the only rep on the board who set clear expectations at every door, five stars included — and he backed it up by telling one customer his system was fine, running a free leak search for another, and handing a third the conclusion to reach herself. The front half of his work is the standard. What is missing is the last ten minutes: an appointment attached to every replacement recommendation, one curious question after every objection, and a recorder that runs long enough for anyone to see what he actually does with all of it.
