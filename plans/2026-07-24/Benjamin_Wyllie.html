1

The one-line verdict

Why they converted

He explains what cannot be fixed, and why patching it is the expensive option.

What actually happened

On the coil call: parts unavailable out of warranty, refrigerant loss continuing, and around seventy-five hundred dollars even for those who can get the parts, because the outside coil has to match the inside coil. Then the recommendation, unprompted: this is a long term home. I'd rather you guys do a long term solution than just trying to Band-Aid.

Why that works

He is not selling a system — he is explaining why the repair path has a dead end. A customer who understands the constraint reaches the replacement conclusion without being pushed there, and on both flips that is exactly what happened.

5

What Benjamin did right — real strength, build on it

  • He says what cannot be fixed. Telling a customer the part no longer exists is harder and more useful than quoting a repair that will fail.
  • He gives a recommendation. I'd rather you guys do a long term solution — most reps on this board stop at information.
  • He raised resale value unprompted for a customer weighing whether to sell, which reframed the whole decision.
  • He asks the customer for the judgement rather than delivering it: what do you think we should do about this part?
  • He warns up front that one fault usually means several, so later findings feel expected rather than invented.

8

What we owe Benjamin Wyllie

  • Look at van stock. A contactor and a whip should not stop a no-cool call in July.
  • Credit the honesty about obsolete parts. It is the reason both of those calls converted.
  • The review ask — fifth graded day. This needs to become a checklist item rather than a coaching note.
  • Team morale is worth a check-in. Self-deprecating remarks on one recording, and a conversation about how a drive-by visit gets coded, are both worth a manager's attention separately from this plan.
Bottom line

Benjamin converted two calls by telling customers what could not be fixed and why patching it is the expensive route — then giving an actual recommendation rather than a menu. That combination is rare on this board. Two things hold him back and both are old: he still never asks for the review he promises in every welcome, and he still never asks what the customer pays for power. A third, outside his control, cost him a call: the part wasn't on the van.
