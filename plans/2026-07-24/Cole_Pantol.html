The one-line verdict

The day in two calls

He knows exactly how to flip one — and let an identical one go to email.

BALTERA — textbook

Immediates, futures and a third path, all named: I did give you that third option we talked about, which would be speaking to one of our comfort advisors about what it would take to update the system. That way you have a repair price, you have a replacement price, and you can see what works best for you and your wife. Then he protected the customer from himself: I hate to see you guys light $3,000 on fire and then go for a permanent solution. So I can always get someone out. The customer booked it: I'll go ahead and give a call and we'll get them out here.

JENSEN — the same signal, twice, unconverted

Two failing compressors on banned refrigerant, a rusting coil and a leaking blower motor. The customer asked How much is the whole unit going for nowadays? Cole: I just work on the repair side, so I'm not entirely sure on what replacements cost, but that's always information we can get for you guys if you wanted it. Asked again, same answer. No advisor called, no appointment offered — the report went out by email and he collected the tune-up fee and left.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He talked a customer out of spending three thousand dollars. I hate to see you guys light $3,000 on fire and then go for a permanent solution. And that is precisely why the flip happened.
  • He tied a failing compressor to a solar bill. it's going to be running up the amount of electricity used by about 10%. Eat up those credits you build up through the year. Nobody else on the board linked a finding to the customer's own economics that cleanly.

6

This week's drill — the replacement question is always a booking

Trigger and response

Trigger: any version of "how much is a whole new one?" — from anyone in the house, at any point in the visit.

Response, within ten seconds, and never "if you wanted it":

"Great question, and I want you to have the real number rather than my guess. Our comfort advisor does that at no cost — he's out this way this afternoon, or is tomorrow morning easier?"

Then stop talking and let them pick. He already runs this perfectly on one call out of four. The drill is running it every time the question is asked, not only when he senses the customer is ready.
