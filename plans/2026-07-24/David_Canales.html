The one-line verdict

The day in two calls

The same sentence closed one call and lost the other.

Flor — flipped, properly

He named the immediate, the future, and then the third path by name: So I did an option C, which is a system update. So that's just talking with one of our guys, our equipment specialist. When the customer said her husband finished at half past six, he did not leave it there — Do you want him to be part of this conversation? Because I can ask the manager to see if he can sit someone here when you both are here so you both can hear. Signed, at no cost, with both decision-makers scheduled.

The unnamed call — lost to a competitor

The same customer asked the same question and got the wall instead: I don't deal with that. That's a different department. Her reply was the warning: Why would they send out someone who can't do this? Why do they do that? Twenty minutes later: I'll just pay you the fee, and then I'll just wait and get the other people to come by, and then they can do the same thing.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He taught a customer her whole system because nobody ever had. Has anybody ever explained to you how an air conditioner works? — then twenty minutes of genuine education, unpaid and unasked for. Nothing on the board this day beats it.
  • The most complete expectations block anyone delivered, including the line that makes the approval promise real: let's say I do find something, I put in a part, and then I just hit you with the bill. We don't do that.
  • He solved the decision-maker problem instead of working around it. I can ask the manager to see if he can sit someone here when you both are here so you both can hear. That is why it closed.
  • He tuned the analogy to the customer's own profession. Heart, blood, circulation — for an occupational therapist. She got it immediately.
  • He was honest about the limits of what he knew. my worst fear is that I can put this stuff in but I couldn't turn on the compressor. No overselling a repair he could not guarantee.

Gap 2 — Two long calls, and not one of the four required questions

What he askedOwnership history, install year, prior contractors, the fault timeline. All good, all diagnostic.
What he never asked, on either callHow often the filters are changed. Which rooms run hotter or colder. What the thermostat is set at in summer and winter. What a standard power bill looks like at that setting.

Four questions, before the tools come out, every call.

Gap 3 — The rapport ran long enough to become the call

What the tape showsOn the unnamed call, extended stretches of conversation about politics, family history, career advice and business plans, well beyond the point where rapport had been established.
What did not happen in that timeAny required question, any five-star seed, and — until late — any expectations block.

The fix His warmth is a genuine asset and the Flor call proves it converts. Rapport that costs the customer two hours and ends with her calling somebody else has not served her.

6

This week's drill — never say "different department" first

Trigger and response

Trigger: any replacement question — "how much for a new one", "should I just replace it", "is it worth fixing".

Response, leading with the solve:

"Great question — I want you to have the real number, not my guess. Our equipment specialist does that at no cost and I can get him out today. Does this afternoon work, or is the evening better for you?"

The department detail comes after they have a time, if it comes at all. He proved on the Flor call that this exact move closes; on the other call the same fact, delivered as a limit, cost the customer.

7

3-week training plan

Week 1
The four required questions
Filter, rooms, thermostat setting, power bill — asked on every call before the tools come out, and the bill number reused at the presentation.
Week 2
Lead with the solve
Every replacement question converts to an offered day and time. No "that's a different department" before an appointment exists.
Week 3
The block on every door, and the five-star ask
The Flor expectations block run on all calls, and the review asked for at every close.

  • The Flor system-education sequence recorded and used as the team's Be A Nerd training standard. It is the best teaching on the board.

Bottom line

David gave a customer something almost nobody in this trade gives away: twenty unhurried minutes explaining how her own home works, with no sale attached, and then earned the flip by solving her real problem — getting her husband in the room. That is the job done properly. The same day, the same question got "that's a different department" and the customer went to a competitor. Lead with the solve, ask the four questions that make the recommendation hers instead of yours, and this becomes a very strong board.
