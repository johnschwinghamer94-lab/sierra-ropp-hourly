The one-line verdict

The difference between the two

One customer got a structure. The other got a decision she wasn't ready to make.

What actually happened

On the call that converted, the structure was explicit — immediate repair at a stated figure, the compressor as future work, and then Option C named as a third path with the specialist explained. On the other, the customer had a competing technician arriving and said I can't deal with all this. I'm talking to some other guy coming out, and it's too much for me. It's just too much today.

What the second one needed

She had already told him what she wanted: it would have been one thing, just get it patched up today and then deal with it when you come back. That is a repair request, not a refusal. The answer was the small fix today and the bigger conversation later — instead the whole option set arrived at once and overwhelmed her.

What David did right — real strength, build on it

  • He names Option C explicitly. Saying the words — so I did an option C, which is a system update — is what makes it a real choice rather than an aside.
  • He teaches without being asked. I just want to show you how everything works if nobody explained is a genuinely generous instinct.
  • He checks what has already been replaced before diagnosing, which stops him quoting for work someone else already did.
  • He never pressures. Two households, including one clearly at capacity, and no pushing on either.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Match the size of the yes
Drill: Any customer signalling overwhelm is offered the smallest useful step, immediately. Ten reps.
Proof it worked: No tape where an overwhelmed customer leaves with nothing.
Week 2
Open properly
Drill: Five-star seed and goal question added to every welcome.
Proof it worked: Both beats on every recording.
Week 3
Beat the competitor
Drill: Any customer with another company attending is offered same-day work before the visit ends.
Proof it worked: An offer on every competitive call.
8

What we owe David Canales

  • Credit the Option C structure. He names it, prices around it and hands off cleanly. Several longer-tenured reps on this board still do not.
  • A smallest-useful-step script. Overwhelmed customers are common and there is no standard way to shrink the ask.
  • The welcome card. Two beats missing, both cheap to add.
  • Confirm the specialist ticket typed out. The handoff is on tape; the lead should be verified.
Bottom line

David names Option C out loud, prices around it and hands off cleanly — that call converted and the structure is right. The other one is the lesson: a customer with a competitor arriving told him twice that she just wanted it patched today, and she got the full option conversation instead and then an email. Ask what would make today a win, and when someone says they are at capacity, shrink the ask instead of finishing the presentation.
