1

The one-line verdict

Why the flip landed

He came back the next day for the person who wasn't home.

What actually happened

The evaluation happened one day; the other decision-maker was out. Rather than emailing a report into that gap, he returned and set the frame himself: I just wanted to take a few moments before AJ arrived… Patty just wants to be prepared, see what everything could look like, because in the case that systems are original to the home, we're already identifying issues. Then he walked the husband through every finding, every price, and the live warranty lookup — before the specialist ever opened his folder.

And he refused to guess the number

I wouldn't want to give you a number, because I would be doing you a disservice — because we're going in with this expectation of, hey, you told me it was this much. AJ has full access to the price books and everything that's up to date. He held the value and handed the pricing to the person who owns it. The specialist arrived to a customer who already understood the problem.

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He went back for the decision-maker who wasn't there. Not a phone call, not an email — a scheduled return with the specialist. That is why it converted, and it is the single best piece of sales judgement on the board this day.
  • He is the only rep who asked the mandatory power-bill follow-up. How does that translate over for your energy bills? — and got a real before-and-after number back.
  • He refused to guess a replacement price. I wouldn't want to give you a number, because I would be doing you a disservice. The correct answer, and the opposite of how a call got lost elsewhere on the board this day.
  • He researched the warranty live and told the truth about it. Registration, expiry, and the part customers never hear: the warranty wouldn't come into play until the part has completely failed… you would still be paying for labor.
  • The open-heart-surgery analogy. Best teaching image of the day, and it made the repair-versus-replace maths obvious without a single scare tactic.
  • He asked the customer's own time horizon — this is how many more years I want out of you? — and then built the recommendation on that answer.

6

This week's drill — stop talking after the yes

Two habits, both about restraint

When the customer accepts the specialist: confirm the time, thank them, and stop. No margin explanations, no ranges, no price history.

When asked what a replacement costs:

"Honestly, I'd be guessing, and a guess would only set the wrong expectation. That's exactly what our specialist brings — the real number for your home."

Then nothing. He already knows this is right; he said it out loud. The drill is holding the line for the next sixty seconds.

What we owe Andrew

  • Recognition for the reconnect. Returning the next day, with the specialist scheduled, so both decision-makers could hear it at once is the standard the whole board should be working to.
  • Recorders that survive a full call — a 35-minute visit produced only a handful of usable lines and could not be graded at all.
  • Clear guidance on what ranges, if any, a technician may quote, so he is never caught between the right principle and the customer's direct question.
Bottom line

Andrew asked the questions almost nobody asks, researched a warranty live in front of the customer, told the truth about what it would and would not cover, and then did the thing that actually decides these calls: he came back the next day so the person who makes the decision could be in the room. The gaps left are small and entirely about restraint — plant and collect the five-star review, and once the customer says yes, stop talking. Do those two things and this is the strongest all-round board on the team.
