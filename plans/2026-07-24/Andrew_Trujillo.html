1

The one-line verdict

Why it converted

He treated the follow-up as a job, not a phone call.

What actually happened

He arrived ahead of the specialist and re-presented the whole case: the pending repair total, the voltage switch, the fan and motor assembly with pricing and a two-year membership warranty, the compressor running on excess power across two technicians' reports, and the warranty position pulled from the original install date. I just wanted to take a few moments just before AJ arrived… we just want to touch base, see how everything is.

Why that works

A next-day reconnect gives the customer time to absorb the numbers and gives us a second, warmer conversation — with the specialist arriving into a briefed customer rather than a cold one. This is the most deliberate handoff anyone ran this week, and it is a repeatable process, not a personality trait.

5

What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • The reconnect is the best-designed visit of the week. Come back the next day, re-present, and bring the specialist into a briefed customer.
  • He arrives with the file. Previous report, emailed estimate, warranty dates and another technician's notes, all assembled before he knocks.
  • He used two technicians' observations as corroboration — that turns a finding into a pattern the customer can trust.
  • He asks how the household wants him to move through it, every time.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Record the close
Drill: Recorder running through the report and presentation on every call.
Proof it worked: A presentation on tape for every gradeable call.
Week 2
Use their own comparison
Drill: Any customer who cites a before-and-after bill has it quoted back in the findings. Five reps.
Proof it worked: Customer's comparison referenced on every recording where it appears.
Week 3
Ask for the review
Drill: Review requested at the door on every visit.
Proof it worked: Review asked on every call.

Bottom line

Andrew went back to a customer the day after her evaluation, brought the whole file with him, re-presented every finding, and had the specialist walk into a briefed and comfortable household. That is the most deliberate handoff of the week and it should be written down as a process. The other call assembled a perfect value case — a customer whose bill dropped by a third after her last replacement — and then the tape ran out before anyone used it.
