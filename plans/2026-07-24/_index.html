What the whole board has in common

The five-star ask
Weak
Eleven of twelve reps never asked a single customer for a review. Several were thanked warmly, unprompted, and left anyway. It is the single cheapest fix on the board.
The power-bill follow-up
Weak
The mandatory follow-up to the thermostat question was asked on two calls out of thirty-five — on a day when most of the replacement conversations would have been decided by it.
Recordings that stop at the decision
Weak
Five reps have no tape that reaches an option or a price. Their close rates are unknowable and they are being coached entirely on their introductions. This is a hardware and habit problem before it is a rep problem.
"That's a different department"
Moderate
Four reps met a direct replacement question with the limit rather than the next step. Where a specialist was offered with a day and a time instead, it converted almost every time.
Welcome and expectations
Strong
The real strength of this board. Introductions, parking, permission and thorough-evaluation promises are near-universal, and several blocks are complete enough to be training standards.
Bottom line

This board opens better than it closes. Expectations, rapport and teaching are genuinely strong across almost every rep — and then, again and again, the tape stops, the review goes unasked, and a replacement question gets answered with a department instead of an appointment. Three habits would move the whole team at once: run the recorder to the end of the call, attach a specialist and a time to every replacement question, and ask for the five-star review you have already earned.
