The one-line verdict

What the tape shows

He turns a documentation problem into an advocacy job — and does it well.

What is on tape

Per Nevada, when a new install comes in, you're supposed to pull permits and you're supposed to register — followed by a promise to put everything in writing and photograph the labelling discrepancies: I'll put everything on paper… I'll give you guys all the information. I'll take pictures. I'm here to help you guys out.

What is missing

The advocacy is genuinely valuable and the customer is now better armed. But a household this dissatisfied with its previous installer is also a household in the market. No membership, no replacement number and no offer of our own work appears anywhere on tape — and this is the fifth graded day with no recorded presentation.

What Mario did right — real strength, build on it

  • He built a customer's case for them. Permits, registration, labelling — documented and photographed so a household could pursue a claim.
  • He knows the state requirements. Being able to explain what an installer was legally obliged to do is rare and genuinely valuable.
  • He likes being followed around and says why. That invitation is why customers understand what he finds.
  • He documented another company's failures without stoking the anger — factual, useful and professional.
