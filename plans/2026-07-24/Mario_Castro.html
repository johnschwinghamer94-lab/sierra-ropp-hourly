1

The one-line verdict

What the tape shows

He reads the room better than anyone — and the tape still quits on him.

What actually happened

The LECLERC customers were a week into a nightmare with another company — no permits, no registration, five years of warranty gone, a no-show the day before. Mario led with the one thing they needed: I'm sorry you guys are going through all that and I'm sorry. It's been over a week now. Then he made himself useful instead of selling: Per Nevada when a new install comes in you're supposed to pull permits and you're supposed to register everything… I'll put everything on paper, I'll do my research on my end, I'll give you guys all the information. I'm here to help you guys out.

What we cannot see

That is a customer who would have signed anything he recommended. The recording ends at the moment he asks where the indoor units are. On both calls we have the intake and nothing else — no findings, no options, no price, no close.

6

This week's drill — finish the block, finish the tape

Two habits, both one sentence long

At the end of the expectations block, every call, either language:

"I won't do anything without your approval, and my goal today is a five-star experience — if I'm falling short, stop me."

Before you knock: check the recorder. Before you drive off: check it is still running. Five tapes in a row have stopped at the intake — his intake is not the problem, and it is the only thing we can grade.

Bottom line

Mario walked into a house where another company had taken the money, skipped the permits and stopped answering the phone — and the first thing out of his mouth was an apology and an offer to put it all on paper. That instinct cannot be taught. What can be fixed is smaller than it looks: two questions back into the sequence, one sentence about five stars, and a recorder that runs to the end of the call. Do those three things and we will finally get to coach the part of his job we have never seen.
