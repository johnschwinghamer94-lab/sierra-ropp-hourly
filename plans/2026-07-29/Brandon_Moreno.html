The one-line verdict

Why the flip stayed small

Priced, not linked.

What actually happened

After an hour of careful diagnosis the customer asked what cost are we looking at? and got a clean, honest list — cleaning, compressor, cooling coil, member and non-member pricing. He picked the cheapest one: Yeah, probably a basic cleaning.

What it means

Every number was correct and none of them was connected to the shutdown that brought Brandon out. The blower cleaning is the quickest win — it takes the strain off that compressor while you decide on the bigger work turns a price list into a reason, and the customer chooses on purpose instead of by cost.

5

What Brandon did right — real strength, build on it

  • <b>He refuses to conclude early.</b> I don't want to jump the gun... let me take a look at the full thing is exactly the discipline that makes the later findings credible.
  • <b>He separates urgent from eventual honestly.</b> Nothing was failing today; the compressor is wearing and the rust is developing — said plainly, without inflating either.
  • <b>The teaching is genuinely elite.</b> Heart, radiator, blood work — three images that make a complicated system legible in under a minute.
  • <b>He removes friction at the door.</b> Shoe covers, early appointments in the heat, I'm not the filter police — customers relax around him immediately.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Link every option
Drill: Each option gets one sentence tying it to the customer&#x27;s own stated problem before any price is named.
Proof it worked: A link sentence audible ahead of every price on tape.
Week 2
Offer on every maintenance
Drill: Every tune-up ends with at least one named option, even on a healthy system.
Proof it worked: An offer on tape on every maintenance recording.
Week 3
Work the constraint
Drill: Any time constraint or hesitation gets one question before any reassurance.
Proof it worked: A question following every constraint the customer raises.
