What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He refused to falsify a document. Asked to backdate a report for an insurance claim, he said I don't believe I can backdate anything for you. It would have to have today's date as I'm here today — then called his manager rather than quietly deciding. This is the single most important thing anyone did on this board all day.
  • He handled that refusal without embarrassing the customer. I don't mind doing what you're asking me to, but just how they have protocol — firm on the answer, gentle on the person, and the customer stayed cooperative.
  • Discovery that actually shapes the diagnosis. He asks about prior companies, previous repairs, and what the customer has already tried — then uses it. Most techs collect that information and ignore it.
  • He fixed a problem for free and told the truth about it. ORTEGA-WHITE's thermostat was a power-outage reset. He reconnected it, verified it, and was straight about his role: I'm the fix it guy versus the sales department.

6

This week’s drill

Never hand a live customer to a department

Trigger: the customer objects to terms, structure or price — anything you cannot personally change.

Response, instead of deferring:

"That's a fair ask, and it's not my call — so let me make it my call. I'll ring the office right now while I'm here and see what we can do."

Phone in hand, in the room. That single move is worth more than any script on this page.

7

3-week training plan

Week 1
Call the office in the room
Every terms objection triggers a live call to dispatch or the manager before you leave. Log every one.
Week 2
Own the evaluation frame
Any mention of competing quotes gets the diagnostic-first response. Rehearse it five times before Monday.
Week 3
Ask at thank-you
Five-star request the moment the customer thanks you, on every call.
