The one-line verdict

The moment

He followed the process exactly right and stopped one sentence before the part that creates the lead.

What actually happened

On the Miguel visit the customer raised a mini-split not cooling one room properly. David held the line correctly — yo nomás soy de reparaciones, I only do repairs — which is exactly what the process asks of him.

What it means

Then he asked the customer to call sales. In the SILO model the tech who hears the problem is the one who books the specialist. Let me get our specialist out to look at that with you is the whole difference.

What David did right — real strength, build on it

  • He knows his lane and stays in it. Refusing to quote the mini-split is correct SILO process and he did it without making it awkward.
  • He removes fear with an explanation. The burning smell had a cause and a timeframe, and the customer stopped worrying about it.
  • He inspects properly. Amp draw, cables, ductwork, motor — the 173-minute call is genuinely thorough, not just long.
  • He meets people where they are. Serving a Spanish-speaking customer in their own language for over an hour is service most techs cannot offer.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Book, don't refer
Drill: Every out-of-scope need gets a specialist appointment offered on the spot instead of a phone number.
Proof it worked: The offer on tape every time a customer raises something outside the ticket.
Week 2
Finish the call
Drill: Every visit ends with findings, a recommendation and an approval request.
Proof it worked: All three on the recording of every call.
Week 3
Spend the rapport
Drill: Use the relationship built during the visit to ask for the next step before leaving.
Proof it worked: An ask on tape on every completed call.
