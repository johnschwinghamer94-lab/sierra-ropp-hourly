1

The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

He scheduled while the customer was still skeptical.

What actually happened

On the Yanoff call he validated the customer's own instinct about the warranty company — They're gonna say, yeah, it just needs new refrigerant. They work cheap and they may find stuff wrong that's not really wrong — and booked the no-cost estimate in the same conversation.

What it means

Timing is the whole lesson. He booked it while the customer was engaged and doubtful, not after the customer had gone quiet and reconvinced himself the system was fine. On the Jeong call the same moment was allowed to pass.

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He books while the doubt is still live. The Yanoff estimate was scheduled during the conversation, not promised for later. That timing is the whole flip.
  • He validated the customer instead of competing with him. A homeowner with his own meter and his own readings was treated as a collaborator, and became one.
  • He turned a bad experience into a decision. now I know the real problem and can make an informed decision is the outcome the model is aiming at.
  • He managed cost expectations up front. Nobody got sticker shock when the estimate landed, because he had already been honest about the range.

6

This week’s drill

A second opinion is not a no — book around it

Trigger: the customer says they want to think about it or get another quote.

Response, immediately:

"Totally fair — get the other quote. Let me also get our specialist out so you've got financing options and a real number to compare against. Tomorrow morning or afternoon?"

You are not competing with the second opinion. You are making sure you are still in the room when it comes back.

7

3-week training plan

Week 1
Book around the second opinion
Every deferral gets an appointment attempt before you leave. Log the ask either way.
Week 2
Financing as the third option
Put a payment path on every option sheet where the total is above a few thousand dollars.
Week 3
Ask when they thank you
The five-star request goes in the moment the customer expresses appreciation, on every call.
8

What we owe Juan

  • Use the Yanoff call as the teaching example for timing. Booking while the customer is still skeptical, rather than after they have calmed down, is the subtlest and most valuable thing on this board.
  • Give him financing collateral he can hand over. He is doing the hardest part already; the payment conversation should not be improvised.
  • Tell him the technical depth is an asset, not a risk. He explains at the customer's level, not below it, and it is why he gets trusted.
Bottom line

Juan does the technical half of this job at a level nobody else on this board matches, and — more importantly — he books the estimate while the customer is still uncertain rather than waiting for them to settle. That is why Yanoff flipped. The one thing missing is the same move on the calls that go sideways: when someone asks for a second opinion, that is not the end of the conversation, it is the moment to put a no-cost specialist on the calendar so you are still in the room when the other quote comes back.
