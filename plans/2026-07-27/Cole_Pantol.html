What went right — real strength, build on it

  • The best-delivered expectations block on the board. Not read — delivered, with the correction invitation intact: If I'm not doing that, let me know so I can change it.
  • He notices what the house is telling him. You guys are camping out down here? named the customer's discomfort out loud before she had to explain it.
  • He asks about the competition without defensiveness. you guys just been getting, like, multiple companies out here? — useful intelligence, gathered warmly.
  • He surfaces the second decision-maker early. On JAGTIANI he established the tenant-versus-homeowner split in the first two minutes, which is exactly when it needs to happen.

6

This week’s drill

Worry is a buying signal — answer it with a plan

Trigger: the customer expresses fear about reliability, a repeat failure, or stressing the system.

Response, within ten seconds:

"That's exactly what our maintenance plan is for — we catch it before it becomes another emergency call. Want me to get you set up while I'm here?"

Worry is not an objection. It is the customer telling you what they want to buy.

What we owe Cole

  • Record his opening and use it for onboarding. It is the cleanest delivery of the expectations block on this board.
  • Give him a presentation to practise, not a lecture about closing. His problem is not willingness; he has never been drilled on the back half.
  • Check whether his recordings are cutting early. Two of three ended during the walkthrough. That may not be him.
Bottom line

Cole's opening is the best-delivered version of the SILO script on this board — he promises a thorough evaluation, a report, an approval boundary and a five-star visit, and he means all four. The problem is that none of the recordings show him keeping the second half of that promise. A customer told him she was afraid her system would fail again and he reassured her instead of enrolling her. The skill is there. The back half of the call is missing.
