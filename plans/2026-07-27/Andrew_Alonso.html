1

The one-line verdict

Why the day split

He closes when he asks. He asks about half the time.

What actually happened

On MORALES he ran the whole play — authorization, discovery, teaching, then a signed no-cost specialist ticket with a two-hour arrival window. On the 14-minute evaluation he explained what he found, said he would get this together for the Comfort Advisor, and stopped there.

What it means

Same tech, same skill, two different endings. The difference is a single sentence: naming a day and a time and waiting for the customer to answer. Every call that reaches a finding gets an appointment attempt, not just the long ones.

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He protected the customer and the company at once. Refusing to discuss the system until the deed-holder authorized it, on a recorded line, is the kind of discipline that never shows up on a scoreboard and always shows up when something goes wrong.
  • Empathy that led somewhere. No, I'm genuinely sorry was followed immediately by I will 1,000% get you guys a solution — sympathy converted into a promise, then into a booked appointment.
  • He handed the advisor a warm room. if you guys got questions, just because you're gonna be in your brains at higher state of mind… write them down, call me up. I got the answers before he gets here. That is what a real handoff sounds like.
  • Teaching that saves the customer money. The MERV-11 recommendation and the extra-return problem both cost the customer nothing and buy years of trust.
5

The three gaps

each one anchored to a real moment on tape
Weak

Gap 1 — The short evaluation ended without an ask

Card evidenceSet the process — I'll get this together for the Comfort Advisor — then the call ended. No options, no appointment, no time named.
What was availableThe findings were already in hand. Everything needed for the ask was done.

The fix Finish every evaluation the same way you finished MORALES: "I want our comfort advisor out here with your options. He's got a one-to-three tomorrow — does that work, or is later better?" Then stop and let them answer.

Moderate

Gap 2 — Two hundred and twenty minutes in one home

DurationThe MORALES visit ran three and a half hours.
What it costEvery hour in one house is an hour not spent on the next no-cool call in a heat wave.

The fix Nothing in the transcript was wasted — but the same result was reachable faster. Set yourself a checkpoint: findings and the appointment ask inside ninety minutes, then teaching for as long as the customer wants it.

What he saidif you refer people to my name and then they can call the office.

The fix Ask for the review first, in the room, while the goodwill is live — then ask for referrals on top of it. You had a family that thanked you four times; that is the moment.

6

This week’s drill

Findings, then a time — every call, not just the long ones

Trigger: you have finished the evaluation and you know what is wrong.

Response, before you pack a tool:

"Here's what I found. I want our comfort advisor to walk you through the options with no cost and no commitment — I've got a one-to-three tomorrow. Does that work, or is later better?"

Say it on the eight-minute calls, not just the three-hour ones. The short visits are where the whole board is losing flips.

7

3-week training plan

Week 1
Appointment ask on every call
Book, or attempt to book, a specialist slot on 100% of calls that reach a finding — including maintenance and short evaluations. Log the ask either way.
Week 2
Time discipline
Findings plus appointment ask inside ninety minutes. Teach as long as you like after the calendar slot is set.
Week 3
Review before referral
Ask for the five-star in the room on every call, then layer the referral ask on top.
8

What we owe Andrew

  • Back him on the fee waiver. He gave away a diagnostic to a family in genuine distress and turned it into a booked lead. Tell him publicly that was the right call so he does not second-guess it.
  • Give him a ride-along on short calls. His long-call craft is not the problem; the eight-to-fifteen-minute visits are. Watch three of those with him.
  • Show him the MORALES outcome. He should see the ticket that came out of that handoff — the loop closing is what makes the ask automatic.
Bottom line

Andrew is one of the few people on this board who will stop a call cold to do the legally right thing, and then still leave with an appointment. The MORALES visit is a template — authorization, empathy, teaching, a named time, a waived fee, and a customer who thanked him four times on the way out. His whole gap is that the short calls do not get the same ending. Same words, shorter call. That is the entire adjustment.
