1

The one-line verdict

Why nothing converted

He accepts the scope the customer hands him.

What actually happened

KIM: I'm selling my condo. The buyer requested to repair the insulation. Mario: Do you want to just do that or do you want me to check the unit out? Customer: Just the repair. Mario: Just the repair? Okay, I'll take a look at it.

What it means

He did ask — and then took the first no. A home sale is a transaction moment, and "while I'm up there, let's check the whole system so you can tell buyers it's clean" is a service to the seller, not a pitch.

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He recovered a broken promise without excuses. Faced with They always say that, but never happens, he did not explain, defend or blame — he committed twice and ordered on the spot. That is how you get a second chance at a commercial account.
  • He asks permission for everything. Are you okay if I take a look inside? — on every call, before every space.
  • He plans for the absent decision-maker. LEE: I'll show you pictures. I'll send them to you. That way you can show your husband later today. Right instinct, executed without being asked.
  • He gave the customer control of the findings. If anything's out of specs, what would you like me to do with that information? is a genuinely good question and very few techs ask it.

6

This week’s drill

Ask twice, with a reason

Trigger: the customer narrows you to a single small job.

Response, after the first no:

"Understood — I'll take care of that. While I'm already up there, let me check the rest of the system at no extra cost. If it's clean, that's good news you can use. If it isn't, better you hear it from me than from a buyer's inspector."

One more ask, with a reason attached to their goal, not yours.

7

3-week training plan

Week 1
Three questions, every call
How long, what is it costing you, what would you fix first — regardless of how narrow the ticket is.
Week 2
Ask twice on narrow scope
Every scope limitation gets a second, reasoned ask. Log both the ask and the answer.
Week 3
Record the presentation
Confirm audio before the write-up on every call for two full weeks.

What we owe Mario

  • Tell the team about the EMMIS recovery. Owning a company failure without defending it is the hardest soft skill in this job and he did it instinctively.
  • Fix the recording coverage. Three for three ending mid-walkthrough is a setup problem, not a performance one.
  • Give him a transaction-moment script. Home sales, new purchases and move-ins come up constantly and he has never been given words for them.
Bottom line

Mario is the person you want walking into a customer who is already angry at Sierra. He owned a broken promise twice and had it ordered before the customer finished complaining. What he does not do is push back on a narrow scope — a man selling his condo got exactly the one repair he asked for, and nothing else. Ask twice, with a reason that serves their goal. That is the whole adjustment, and it is worth thousands.
