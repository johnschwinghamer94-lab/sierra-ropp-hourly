The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

What actually happened

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He books the advisor while the customer is still in the room. No "someone will call you" — a live phone call to dispatch, an OTO ticket, and a confirmed window. Then he re-cut the window when the customer's work schedule changed.
  • He names the options A, B and C. Customers can hold three labelled choices in their head. They cannot hold a paragraph.
  • He fought a home warranty on the customer's behalf. On BOSWELL he took the phone, answered a fifteen-minute interrogation about model numbers and W-9s, and stayed patient throughout — for a job he was probably never going to get.
  • He builds value without attacking the competition's price. TONG: we pull permits. We do everything by code… we charge for the quality. We don't overcharge. Like we're not the most expensive, but we're also not a mom-and-pop shop.

The three gaps

Weak

Gap 1 — CASIS ended on "that's pretty much all I can do"

What he foundA damper motor stuck closed, a bypass almost shut, and a coil frozen solid — roughly eight hundred dollars of parts he had already priced out loud.
How it endedHe sealed the duct, set a two-hour fan timer, and left the customer to call a home warranty.

The fix The diagnosis was excellent and the ending gave it away. "Here's the option sheet either way — if the warranty covers it, great, use it. If they don't, we can be back out tomorrow. And separately, let me get our advisor out at no cost so you know where you stand on the whole system." Warranty or not, the option sheet should always exist.

6

This week’s drill

Warranty or no warranty, the option sheet always exists

Trigger: the customer says a home warranty, insurance or another company is handling it.

Response, before you pack up:

"Use them if they'll cover it — I hope they do. Either way here's your option sheet so you know what it actually costs, and let me get our advisor out at no charge so you know where the whole system stands. Costs you nothing and it means you're never guessing."

You lost two calls this day to warranty companies. Neither had to end with nothing.

7

3-week training plan

Week 1
Option sheet on every call
No call ends without options presented, including warranty and return visits. Zero exceptions.
Week 2
Reset the hot opening
Any call that starts with a complaint gets the expectations block after the vent. Practise the transition line with a manager.
Week 3
Review with the receipt
Attach the five-star ask to the email collection you already do on every call.

  • Back him on the home warranty stance. He spent fifteen minutes on the phone for a customer he was unlikely to bill. Tell him that was right.

Bottom line

Francisco runs the cleanest Option C on this board. He names A, B and C, he calls dispatch with the customer sitting right there, and he adjusts the appointment when their schedule changes instead of leaving it to the office. The gap is at the other end of the day: when a home warranty gets in the way, or the customer opens angry, he stops building. Those calls still deserve an option sheet and a free advisor visit. Same tech, same skill — just do not let anyone else decide when the call is over.
