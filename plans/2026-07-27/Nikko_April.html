The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

He reframed the objection into a choice.

What actually happened

CHUNG: when the customer resisted on price, he explained the coil-only fix as a band-aid and asked Would you rather we tackle the compressor now with financing or just do the coil for now?

What it means

That is the model, executed properly — empathy, reframe, then a question that only has forward answers. The result was a membership and a scheduled advisor visit, and the same move was available on both maintenance calls and never used.

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • The best objection sequence on the board. Empathy, reframe, then a question with only forward answers: Would you rather we tackle the compressor now with financing or just do the coil for now? Nobody else here ran that pattern cleanly.
  • The band-aid frame. Naming the cheap fix honestly rather than talking the customer out of it is what made the expensive option credible.
  • He appointed the lead, not just the interest. The CHUNG visit ended with a scheduled comfort advisor call, which is what turned intent into a lead.
  • Root-cause thinking on GEEB. Diagnosing inadequate shade rather than treating the symptom is exactly the kind of finding customers remember.

6

This week’s drill

Solved is not finished — name the next step

Trigger: you have identified a solution the customer agrees with.

Response, before you write anything down:

"So that's the fix. Should I have our specialist reach out this week to get you a number, or would you rather talk budget first?"

Either answer keeps you in the conversation. "I'll document the findings" ends it.

Bottom line

Nikko handled a price objection better than anyone on this board — he validated it, reframed the cheap fix honestly as a band-aid, and then asked a question with only forward answers.
