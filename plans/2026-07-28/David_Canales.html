1

The one-line verdict

Why it didn't convert

He answered the fear he was told about, not the one underneath it.

What actually happened

The customer's stated worry was specific: I just don't want to spend $30,000 on a new AC unit. David's answer was That's completely fine. That's your house, your money — and then he accepted write it up and send me the quotes.

What it means

The customer was afraid of a catastrophic replacement bill, and David was holding a two-thousand-dollar repair that prevents exactly that. Those two facts never got connected out loud. Nothing needed to be pushed — they just needed to be put in the same sentence.

What David did right — real strength, build on it

  • He defused a hostile opening in one sentence. I'm just a repair guy, I'm here to do a maintenance turned a customer braced for a fight into a customer who let him work for two hours.
  • The best system explanation on the board. Compressor as the heart, fan motor, coil, ductwork, filters — start to finish, in words a homeowner can repeat.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Connect the fear
Drill: Every cost objection gets answered by linking the customer's own worry to the cheapest option that prevents it.
Proof it worked: A reconnect sentence on tape after every cost objection.
Week 2
Rank the work
Drill: Never present tiers without saying which one you would do first and why.
Proof it worked: A stated recommendation on every options presentation.
Week 3
Date the follow-up
Drill: Every emailed quote gets a scheduled call to walk through it, agreed before leaving.
Proof it worked: No quote leaves without a follow-up day and time.
