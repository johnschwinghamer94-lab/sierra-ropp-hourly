The one-line verdict

Why it keeps stalling

He shows them the problem and then agrees with their reason to ignore it.

5

What Robert did right — real strength, build on it

  • He shows rather than tells. I want to make sure I show the homeowner so they see what I see is a stated philosophy, and he lives it — photos, readings, video, every call.
  • He films the evidence for the customer's benefit. Making a video so the customer can explain the fault to their warranty company is generous and smart.
  • "What worked different?" One question that gets the customer to narrate the failure themselves — the best diagnostic opener on the board.
  • He teaches with the right analogies. The heart of the system, the metal that cracks where it bends — plain images that a homeowner repeats correctly later.

The three gaps

each one in Robert's own calls
Weak

Gap 1 — "I'll just let them ride" was ratified

Customer, BALLANTYNEYeah. And I'll just let them ride until there's an issue.
RobertOf course.

The fix Don't argue and don't agree — reframe. Totally fair, and I wouldn't replace a working system either. Let's not talk repair at all. Let's just get you the number so that when it does go, at eleven at night in August, you already know your options. Costs you nothing today. Move them from fix-versus-wait to informed-versus-surprised.

Weak

Gap 2 — The warranty preference was treated as an obstacle

Customer, DEMERit doesn't cost me anything to have them come back out, so.
RobertAll right, so you'd rather not get the information from us, or—

The fix That reads as wounded, and the customer hears it. Validate first: That makes complete sense — they cover you and it's free. Then leave the door open: If the warranty won't cover a full replacement down the road, call us for a second opinion. Most of those customers come back.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Reframe the wait
Drill: When a customer says they'll wait, respond with the informed-versus-surprised reframe and a no-cost number.
Proof it worked: A reframe on tape every time a wait is stated.
Week 2
Validate first
Drill: Any stated preference — warranty, another company, later — gets agreement before anything else.
Proof it worked: An agreement sentence preceding every response to an objection.
Week 3
Ask at the goodbye
Drill: Attach the review ask to the warm close he already gives.
Proof it worked: A review ask audible on every recording.

Bottom line

Robert does the part most technicians skip: he takes the reading, photographs the damage, films the fault and puts it in the customer's hands. Every one of these customers understood their system by the time he left. What none of them got was a reason to act, because the moment they gave him a reason to wait, he agreed with it. Two rehearsed lines — validate, then reframe to informed-versus-surprised — would change his day more than anything technical.
