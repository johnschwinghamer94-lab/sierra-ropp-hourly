What Nikko did right — real strength, build on it

  • He makes a hot house bearable before he fixes it. That's hot. That's not good. That's miserable is thirty seconds of being on the customer's side, and it changes the rest of the visit.
  • He puts the customer inside the diagnosis. Asking someone to look out the window and report what the outdoor unit is doing turns a spectator into a participant.
  • He finds what nobody told him about. The R22 coil paired to a 410A condenser on the MEADE job was invisible to the homeowner and explains everything they had been experiencing.
  • When he presents options in the room, he wins. Options A, B and C on HENNAGIR produced agreement to the biggest package of his day.

The three gaps

Weak

Gap 1 — "I'll write up my report and give him a call"

CHUNGI'm gonna write up my report, give him a call and see what else to do.
CHOcoil rust connected to the energy bill, then straight to I'll show pictures with no path proposed.

The fix The moment the customer says yeah, that's what the other guy said, they are agreeing with you and ready to act. Two options, out loud, right there: repair path with the timeline, or a specialist to price a replacement. A callback gives doubt a night to work.

Moderate

Gap 2 — Three options and no trial close

MEADEleak search, temporary repair and full replacement all presented clearly and priced.
CloseI'll write up a report — the customer never asked which one she preferred.

The fix After the third option, ask: Of those three, which one feels closest to what you want to do? Even I don't know yet tells you where she is. Leaving without asking means the whole presentation goes home unresolved.

Moderate

Gap 3 — The urgency he heard never came back

MEISthe customer had landed from Seattle to a 96-degree house and a household at the end of its patience.
Laterno reference back to it when setting up the next step.

The fix Their own words are the strongest reason to act: You told me you're not staying here at 110 — so here's how we get you cold this week. Use the urgency they gave you rather than manufacturing new pressure.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Options in the room
Drill: No diagnostic ends with a callback — two options minimum, presented before leaving.
Proof it worked: Options audible on every recording.
Week 2
Trial close
Drill: After presenting options, always ask which one they lean toward.
Proof it worked: A preference question on tape after every options presentation.
Week 3
Give them their words back
Drill: Repeat the customer's own stated urgency when proposing the next step.
Proof it worked: A customer quote reused at the decision step on every call.
