The one-line verdict

The moment on GERBER

He asked the closing question, got one word back, and repeated it.

What actually happened

Nikko: Do you want to do anything today? Customer: No, I'd rather wait, see what happens. Nikko: Waiting. Wait. I want to try and get the most use out of it that I can.

What it means

I'd rather wait is three different objections wearing the same coat — it is too expensive, it is not broken yet, or I want to think about it. Each has a different answer, and none of them can be given until he asks which one it is. He had already done 172 minutes of excellent work to earn the right to ask.

The three gaps

each one in Nikko's own calls
Weak

Gap 1 — 'I'd rather wait' was never asked about

NikkoDo you want to do anything today?
GERBER customerNo, I'd rather wait, see what happens.
NikkoWaiting. Wait. I want to try and get the most use out of it that I can.

The fix Ask which kind of wait it is. Totally fair — is that a money thing, or does it just feel too early? Then answer the real one. If it is timing: If the kitchen-side unit goes mid-summer, this one carries the whole house in 120-degree heat, and you're waiting days for parts. Can we at least get you a firm number so you know what you're deciding against?

The fix When a customer raises money, stop explaining and ask. Help me understand — is this something you'd want to plan for soon, or are we just keeping it running for now? The answer decides whether this is a repair conversation or an Option C conversation.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
One question after every no
Drill: Every objection gets a clarifying question before any further explanation. No exceptions.
Proof it worked: A clarifying question audible after every customer objection.
Week 2
Frame every call
Drill: The full expectations script runs at the top of every visit, including the routine maintenance ones.
Proof it worked: Name, scope, timeline and approval audible in the first two minutes of every recording.
Week 3
Ask the planning question
Drill: Every customer with aging equipment is asked how long they intend to stay and whether they want to plan for replacement.
Proof it worked: A planning question audible on every call with a system past ten years.
