The one-line verdict

The moment on BLAIS

He heard a budget, and treated it as a door closing.

What actually happened

The customer said it outright: I can't afford to replace the system right now. Not for the way the prices are going. Andrew answered with one thin follow-up — Are you looking at other company prices or ours? — then said he would build out multiple options and never built one. The call ended on I'll text you and see if you're interested.

What it means

That sentence is not a rejection. It is a customer telling you they want the system and cannot write the cheque today — which is the exact customer the specialist visit exists for. On the same day, two houses over, Andrew turned a much softer signal into a booked appointment. He knows how to do this.

5

What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • He makes the customer feel smart about the decision. On SUN the customer went from asking whether to do maintenance now to instructing him to do it after the motor — driven by When I test the power of the motor, it'll be the power of the new motor, not the old one, not by pressure.
  • He puts the customer's money first, out loud. That's why I wanted to make sure. That way, I could get you cheaper pricing. Offering the labour-only structure before the customer asked is why the price never became a fight.
  • He asks the question that opens the sale. Have you ever had it maintenanced? is one line and it created the entire second half of that call.
  • Honesty as a trust move. It's not easy replacing those fan motors. I got to be honest with you. Naming the hard part builds more credibility than smoothing it over.
