1

The one-line verdict

Why nothing flipped

He won back a customer's trust and then didn't use it.

What actually happened

Brandon inherited a distrustful homeowner who had caught a previous tech misreporting a water-pressure reading and had already tried to cancel his membership. He answered it perfectly — no upsell, honest findings, as far as the components in here, nothing to worry about — and earned as long as I can call and ask for you. When the customer went further and said the compressor day was coming and asked what gets replaced, Brandon explained the economics and let it end at let me keep it for the time being.

What it was

That is a customer pre-qualifying himself out loud. He asked what replacement involves, absorbed the compressor cost, and concluded most people replace instead. The only missing move was a time. "Let's get you the real number now while nothing is broken and you're not deciding under pressure — no cost, no obligation." Trust was never the obstacle here; the ask simply never happened.

What Brandon did right — real strength, build on it

  • He repaired a relationship the company had damaged. A customer who had caught a previous tech in a false reading and tried to cancel his membership ended the visit asking for Brandon by name. Nothing on this board is worth more.
  • Honesty that costs him money in the moment. Right now, all these components are working to a customer who would have believed anything he said. That is why the trust came back.
  • The customer diagnosed it himself. The day's going to come. I'm going to have to call you. It obviously sounds like a new compressor. Brandon's teaching produced that sentence.
  • The clearest zone-system explanation on the board. Priority logic, cycling behaviour and the practical fix, in plain language.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Ask for the day
Drill: Every call where the customer acknowledges a future replacement ends with a proposed advisor time. Manager role-plays five customers who agree in principle.
Proof it worked: No tape where a customer names a future purchase and no time is offered.
Week 2
Three options, written
Drill: A written immediate-future-advisor set on every gradeable call, including maintenance with no immediate findings.
Proof it worked: A report exists on every call.
Week 3
The bill question
Drill: Power bill asked in the first ten minutes on every call, summer and winter, written on the report.
Proof it worked: Customer's own figure on every recording.
8

What we owe Brandon Moreno

  • Recognition for the recovery. He turned a customer who believed the company had cheated him into someone requesting him by name. That should be said out loud, publicly.
  • Permission to ask. Brandon appears to treat asking as pressure and honesty as its opposite. A manager needs to tell him plainly that booking a free advisor visit is a service, not a pitch — otherwise he will keep stopping one sentence early.
  • A pocket option template. Immediate, future, advisor — the same three lines every time, so building the set stops depending on how the conversation went.
  • Follow up that Plunkett lead by phone. The trust is there and the customer expects to call. We should not wait for him to.
Bottom line

Brandon does the hardest thing in this job better than anyone on the board: he takes a customer who has been lied to and makes them trust the company again. Three calls, zero asks. The compressor customer walked himself all the way to the decision — named the part, absorbed the cost, concluded most people replace — and left with nothing on the calendar. Brandon does not need to be more persuasive. He needs to say one more sentence.
