Scored on strength bands, never numbers — the only figure anywhere is the close rate. Two calls are credited on ServiceTitan ground truth; both recordings end before the presentation. The third recording ends during the welcome and is counted as a gradeable call with no flip.

1

The one-line verdict

Why they flipped

He names the whole picture and lets the arithmetic speak.

What actually happened

On a twenty-year-old system with a frozen coil, a failed fan motor and an overheated compressor, Joe refused to hide behind either extreme: It's fixable. Just want to give you that peace of mind that we can fix it, get you back up online, and kind of go from there. Just know that there are going to be repairs that will be coming down the line, especially since we're still in the middle of July. Then: it's going to be one of those things of what makes the most sense to you and your family.

Why that works

He gives the customer both a genuine repair path and an honest forecast, and then hands the decision over without weighting it. Customers who feel unmanaged trust the numbers. Both flips came from that posture, not from urgency.

5

What Joe did right — real strength, build on it

  • Two leads off two hard diagnoses. Twenty-year-old equipment and a dead compressor, both converted without a single pressure line on tape.
  • The best permission question here. Would you like me to bring it to your attention or just file it away for you guys? gives the customer real control and gets a real yes.
  • He recovers other people's calls. A customer who felt the last visit skipped inspection points got we trust, but we verify and a complete re-check, with no criticism of the previous tech.
  • The alternator analogy. One sentence that makes a capacitor failure obvious and makes the compressor risk follow logically.
  • He presses filter questions to an actual answer instead of accepting we could have been doing better — that is how a maintenance conversation becomes evidence.

The three gaps

each one in Joe's own calls
Moderate

Gap 1 — the tape stops where his weakest step begins

Joe, twiceGive me 10, 15 minutes to write everything up. Let me give you some concrete numbers, and then we'll go from there.
What the recording contains after thatNothing. Both flips formed off-tape.

The fix Two leads confirm the presentations went well, but the decision step is the part of his call we cannot coach because it is never captured. Keep the recorder running through the report and the presentation. The half of his call that produces leads is the half we can't see.

Moderate

Gap 2 — Option C is never named

Joe, on a twenty-year-old systemIt's very expensive repairs. The unit is original to the house, which is from 2006.
JoeIt's going to be one of those things of what makes the most sense to you and your family.
What is not on tapeAny mention of a comfort advisor, a no-cost replacement quote, or an appointment.

The fix His framing does the work of Option C without ever offering it, which means the customer has to invent the next step themselves. Make it explicit: "So you've got three paths — fix what's stopping it today, fix everything, or have our advisor out at no cost to price the alternative so you're comparing real numbers. I'd take the third either way."

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Record the close
Drill: Recorder stays on through the report and presentation on every call, so the decision step becomes coachable.
Proof it worked: A presentation is on tape for every gradeable call.
Week 2
Name the third option
Drill: Advisor visit stated as one of three options on every call with major findings, priced at zero, with a time attached.
Proof it worked: Option C spoken on every recording with a significant finding.
Week 3
Ask for the review
Drill: Review request at the door on every completed visit.
Proof it worked: Review asked on every call.
8

What we owe Joe Mendoza

  • Credit both leads. Neither presentation is on tape; on transcript alone this looked like a day with no outcomes. It was a two-lead day.
  • Fix the recording coverage. His strongest work is happening in the part of the call we never hear.
  • Steal his permission question for the team. It is better phrased than the version in our own script.
  • Back him on the recovery call. He rebuilt confidence after a visit the customer felt was incomplete, without throwing a colleague under the bus.
Bottom line

Joe converts by being unmanaged about it — he lays out the whole situation, separates what has to happen now from what is coming, and asks what makes sense for the family. Two of three calls became leads on that alone. The two things missing are both small: name the advisor as an actual third option instead of leaving the customer to infer it, and keep the recorder running so the best part of his call stops being invisible.
