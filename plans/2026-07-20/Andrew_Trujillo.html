1

The one-line verdict

Why both flipped

He made the advisor part of the diagnosis, not an afterthought.

What actually happened

On both calls the replacement conversation arrived inside the technical explanation rather than after the prices. Sealed compressors that we don't have any way of opening up to do repairs on the inside, warranty terms that only trigger on total failure, and then the offer: someone can be here within the hour, free, while the report is being written. One customer signed for it on the spot; the other asked for it the next morning.

Why that works

He never asks the customer to choose between him and the advisor. The advisor is framed as the missing half of the same answer — Andrew prices the repair, someone else prices the alternative, the customer compares. That framing is why I would like a bid to what it would take to replace the whole system came from the customer both times rather than from him.

5

What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • Two calls, two handoffs. Nobody else on this board converted everything they touched. The method is repeatable and it is on tape twice.
  • He books while the customer is warm. I can make a call and have someone come on by while I'm writing my report… they can be here within the hour. The advisor visit is offered as something already in motion, not a favour to be requested.
  • Honest about warranty limits. Explaining that extended coverage only triggers on total failure costs him an easy reassurance and buys him complete credibility — which is exactly why the replacement question came from the customer.
  • He works with the second opinion instead of against it. A customer announcing another company is coming is usually where techs get defensive. He kept scheduling.
  • Real teaching under pressure. Two systems, three hours, a shocked homeowner, and the explanations never got shorter or sharper.

The three gaps

each one in Andrew's own calls
Moderate

Gap 1 — the welcome stops two beats short

Andrew's openingLet me just run through what I can in terms of diagnosing, looking through the system… I'll have all the reports built up so that way we can go over prices.
What's missingNo five-star framing and no goal question on either call.

The fix Everything else in his open is strong, which makes the omission cheap to fix. "Above all I'm here to give you a five-star visit — if I'm not, tell me. And what would make today a win for you?" The goal question in particular would have surfaced the replacement interest an hour earlier on both calls.

Moderate

Gap 2 — the price range arrived very wide, very fast

AndrewBetween 17 to 20,000 is what they're going to range from start at… upwards of 25 to 30… between 30 to 35 per system. So you could be anywhere from as low as 34,000 all the way up to 70,000.
Customer, immediatelySorry, I…

The fix A range that wide reads as nobody knows, and it landed on a customer already absorbing bad news. Since the advisor owns exact pricing anyway, hand the number over with the appointment: "Real numbers are his job, not mine — that's exactly why I want him here. What I can tell you is the honest range starts around the cost of the repairs you're already looking at."

Moderate

Gap 3 — no review ask on two calls that clearly earned one

Customer, at the closeFeel free to reach out if you have any concerns or if anything else is going on. — Thank you. Of course. You, too.
What was never saidAny request for a review, on either visit.

The fix Both households liked him and both are getting a second visit within a day. That is the easiest review ask in the business: "If today was a five-star visit, the survey text will come through in a bit — mentioning my name really helps." Ten seconds, at the door.

Bottom line

Andrew converted everything he touched, and not by pushing. He diagnoses thoroughly, tells the truth about warranties even when the truth is inconvenient, and then puts the advisor in motion while the customer is still standing there. Both homeowners asked for replacement pricing themselves — that is what a well-run assessment produces. Add the five-star ask and the goal question and there is very little left to coach.
