1

The one-line verdict

Why they flipped

He shows the failure rather than describing it.

What actually happened

On the frozen unit Mario didn't lead with readings — he led with ice: the whole unit outside is frozen… big chunk of ice. And then we take a look inside. The whole thing is frozen. Only then the mechanics: that compressor, which works like the heart, is megging bad. It's just a matter of days that thing goes bad, and the welded contactor that keeps it running without pushing anything.

Why that works

The customer sees the problem before hearing the diagnosis, so the diagnosis is confirming something they already believe rather than asking them to take it on faith. Evidence first, explanation second, decision third. Both of his calls follow that order and both converted.

5

What Mario did right — real strength, build on it

  • Two for two. A failing compressor and a routine maintenance visit, both converted into real leads.
  • He asks the motive question. Is that for comfort or to help out with utilities? — the single most useful discovery question on this board, and almost nobody else asks it.
  • Evidence before explanation. Showing the ice first and the readings second means the customer is already convinced before the technical part starts.
  • He got the full bill picture from customers who were careful with money. Summer, winter and the reason behind the thermostat setting, without it feeling like an interrogation.
  • Respectful with vulnerable customers. A couple on a fixed income who volunteered as much were treated with care, not opportunity.
