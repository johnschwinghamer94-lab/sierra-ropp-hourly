1

The one-line verdict

The day in one line

He found the problem and never named the ask.

What actually happened

On the tune-up, Andrew documented real findings — a fan motor spraying oil in a perfect line and one of the most burnt, burnt power switches I think I've ever seen — and explained the refrigerant phase-out clearly. Then the conversation turned to pensions, police work and the customer's own AC contact, and the visit ended without a written option set. The customer's last word on the equipment was I don't even know if there's anything wrong.

What it means

The evidence he gathers is presentation-grade. The gap is that it never becomes a presentation. A burnt disconnect and a leaking fan motor on a ten-year-old system is a three-option conversation — repair now, repair soon, or a no-cost advisor visit to price the alternative — and it needs to be written down and handed over, not left inside a friendly chat.

What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • A confirmed flip on the day's longest visit. ServiceTitan shows a lead created off that job. The recorder failed, not the technician.
  • Photo evidence is a real asset. He documents everything and then physically brings both decision-makers to the images rather than describing them.
  • He went back for the switch. Realising he had shown the wife one thing and the husband another, he corrected it: I'm so sorry, I did not show your wife this. That instinct for keeping both people level is rare.
  • Genuine, disarming rapport. Two homeowners who plainly enjoyed the visit and invited him back for next year.
