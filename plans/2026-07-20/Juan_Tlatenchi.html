1

The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

He taught the system until the customer asked for the replacement number.

What actually happened

Juan sketched the whole system, walked the R22-to-A2L history, showed video of oxidation eating the evaporator, and named the honest risk of chasing the leak: on that copper line, there's always that risk of creating another leak just because you start to heat up that metal… and it just creates more leaks. The customer's own conclusion followed: so it's almost like every piece of it is pretty much… and then we'll get an estimate for both parts.

What moves it

That is the flip, earned without a single push. The gap is what happened next: let me step outside, I'll build you guys a report with different pricing. No specialist named, no appointment offered, no time. A replacement estimate that lives in a report is a lead with nobody's name on it. The ask was already granted — it just needed a calendar slot.

5

What Juan did right — real strength, build on it

  • He let the customer flip himself (Get Customer to Diagnose — Strong). I think what we need is probably two estimates — one for fixing, one for complete removal. That sentence came from the homeowner, not from Juan. This is the exact behaviour that was missing twenty-four hours earlier.
  • The leak-search honesty (Spin It — Strong). Telling a customer that fixing the leak might create the next one is the opposite of an easy sale, and it is the single argument that made replacement worth comparing.
  • Floor paper on a floor the customer laid himself. He put it down before he was asked, on a house mid-renovation, and the homeowner noticed.
  • A fair answer on home warranties. No sneering, no scare — dispatch timelines, the failed-only trigger, and inconsistent subcontractors. Credible enough that the customer kept going with the estimate.

The three gaps

each one in Juan's own calls
Moderate

Gap 1 — The flip has no name and no time on it

Customer"All right, so we'll get an estimate for both parts."
Juan"Let me step outside, I'll build you guys a report with different pricing, and that way you guys know what you're up against."
What was never saidWho is bringing the replacement number, and when they are coming.

The fix The customer asked for a replacement estimate and got a promise of paperwork. Repair pricing Juan can write himself; a replacement number needs a specialist in the house. Close the loop while the customer is still asking: "Perfect — the repair number I'll write up right now. For the replacement I'll get our specialist out here, no cost and no obligation. He's got afternoons open this week — does Thursday work, or is Friday better?" A booked slot is a flip. A report is a maybe.

Moderate

Gap 2 — The power-bill question came after the presentation

Juan, after the options conversation was over"And you guys haven't seen a power bill for this for the month? Have you guys seen it?"
What was never askedHow often are you changing your filters? · Which rooms run hotter or colder than the rest?

The fix The whole diagnosis was that an undercharged system runs constantly and never satisfies — which is a bill problem before it is a comfort problem. Asked at minute five, that number becomes the strongest line in the presentation. Asked at the door, it is trivia. Setting, then bill, before the tools come out — and add the filter and hot-room questions to the same breath.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Book the flip
Drill: Any time a customer asks for a replacement number, it ends with a named specialist and two time options before you leave the room. Manager role-plays five estimate requests.
Proof it worked: No tape where a requested replacement estimate ends without an appointment offer.
Week 2
Questions first
Drill: Thermostat setting, power bill, filters and hot/cold rooms — all four before tools come out, never after the presentation.
Proof it worked: All four answers appear in the assessment portion of every tape.
Week 3
Harvest the five
Drill: Review ask at every close, tied back to the promise from the welcome.
Proof it worked: A review ask on every completed visit on tape.

What we owe Juan Tlatenchi

  • Name what changed. Yesterday he documented a deferral; today he stated a constraint, went quiet, and the customer asked for the replacement estimate himself. That is the coaching landing in one day and he should hear it.
  • A specialist he can name and book on the spot. His flip stalled at the report because he had no appointment to offer. That is a process gap, not a skill gap.
  • Credit for the R22 explanation. The phase-out timeline, the reclaimed-refrigerant pricing, and the flammability classification, delivered clearly to a technical customer. Worth capturing as a script.
  • A discovery card. Four questions, front of the call. His assessment work is otherwise the strongest on this board and the missing questions are the only thing keeping it from complete.
Bottom line

Juan turned a twenty-five-year-old R22 system with a leak into a customer asking for a replacement number — and he did it by being honest about the risk of the repair, then saying nothing. That is the whole model working. One thing is missing at each end: the discovery questions at the front, and an actual appointment at the back. Book the specialist before you leave the room and this flip stops being a maybe.
