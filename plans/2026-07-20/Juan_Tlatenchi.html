1

The one-line verdict

Why it didn't flip

The customer asked for the quote. Nobody was sent.

What actually happened

Juan diagnosed a refrigerant leak on a thirty-year-old R22 system in a house the customer had bought at auction and rebuilt himself. He explained the phase-out chain accurately, discussed the home insurance policy the customer holds, and when the customer concluded we'll get an estimate for both parts, answered let me step outside. I'll build you guys a report with different pricing, and that way you guys know what you're up against.

What it was

A written price list is not a replacement estimate, and the customer had just told him which one he wanted. A replacement quote requires a person, and that person can be dispatched from the driveway. "Let me get our advisor out — he does the sizing and gives you the real replacement number, no cost. He can be here in an hour, or first thing tomorrow." One sentence separates this call from a lead.

5

What Juan did right — real strength, build on it

  • The refrigerant explanation is the best on the board. Two phase-outs, the flammability classification and why reclaimed R22 costs what it does — accurate, sequential and jargon-free.
  • Complete expectations, every time. Report, options, approval, five-star and a genuine is that fair? at the end of it.
  • He got the customer to reach the conclusion himself. Replace all those pieces or a new piece altogether? came unprompted.
  • Honest about warranty companies without being cynical. Real dispatch delays, real denial patterns, delivered as information rather than as a scare.
  • Real respect for the customer's work. A homeowner who rebuilt the house himself was treated as a peer, and it showed in how openly he talked.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Order the estimate
Drill: Any customer request for replacement pricing triggers an advisor call from inside the house, before the van. Ten reps.
Proof it worked: No tape where a replacement request is answered with a document.
Week 2
Use the payer
Drill: Insurance, warranty or any third-party payer becomes a stated deadline and a booked appointment.
Proof it worked: Every mention of a payer followed by a proposed date.
Week 3
Book, then talk
Drill: The appointment happens before the long conversation, not after it.
Proof it worked: Decision step reached before the last quarter of every recording.

Bottom line

Juan taught this customer more about his own system than most technicians manage in a week, and the customer responded by asking — twice — for a replacement estimate. He got a promise of a report. There is no lead in ServiceTitan for this job, so the earlier hundred-percent reading was wrong. The fix is the same as yesterday's and it is one sentence long: when a customer asks for a replacement number, you do not write it, you dispatch it.
