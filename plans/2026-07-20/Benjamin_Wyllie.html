The one-line verdict

What the tape shows

Front of the call is a model. The back of it is missing.

What actually happened

On the leak call he set the full expectation sequence, found a dead motor start device and an over-amping compressor, sniffed out the leak in a rusted R22 evaporator, measured the temperature split, and priced the leak search and refrigerant. He then asked the right closing question — with that being said, what are your thoughts on everything? — and got a lot of money. Well, we'll see.

What moves it

That answer is the objection, and it arrives before the presentation rather than after it. The move is to work it there and then, while the compressor explanation is still fresh: "Totally fair. Can I ask — is it the number itself, or is it that you'd be putting it into a system this old? Because those are two different conversations and I'd rather have the right one with you." Nothing after that point is on tape, so the close cannot be graded — but the opening was handed to him and the recording ends with it unworked.

5

What Benjamin did right — real strength, build on it

  • The expectation set, three for three (Setting Expectations — Strong). Every beat, every call, plus the time-respect question and an open invitation for questions. This is the version the rest of the board should be copying.
  • Analogies that do real work (Draw the Picture — Strong). The dying car battery for the start device and the blood-pressure framing for the compressor are both immediately graspable, and they produced the moment that matters: the customer concluding it kind of sounds like we need a new system without being told.
  • Thresholds, not opinions (Check Your Tone — Strong). The manufacturer is saying this isn't working properly as it should. He puts the manufacturer between himself and the bad news every time, which keeps a hard message from sounding like a pitch.
  • He teaches while he works. Ben spent the day training a newer tech and still ran a clean, customer-first call — including handing the homeowner a role in the leak search. Two jobs at once, neither of them dropped.

The three gaps

each one in Benjamin's own calls
Moderate

Gap 1 — "A lot of money… well, we'll see" was heard and left alone

Ben"So with that being said, what are your thoughts on everything?"
Customer"A lot of money is about. Well, we'll see."
What came nextHe moved on to the system's age and refrigerant history, then went to write the report.

The fix Asking the question was right. What follows the answer is the whole job. "We'll see" is the earliest, softest form of "let me think about it" — and it is far easier to work standing next to the equipment than it is after a price sheet appears. "Fair enough. Can I ask — is it the number, or is it putting money into a system this old? Those are two different conversations and I want to have the right one with you." Ask it in the moment, not in the van.

Moderate

Gap 2 — The home warranty was surfaced early and then never addressed

Customer, minute one"Well, you can let me know. I have a home warranty… I gotta call them first anyway and get an approval."
Ben"Okay. Well, what home warranty do you have? … Have they ever done anything with your AC before?" — good questions, and then the subject was dropped.

The fix He did the hard part — he found out about the warranty in the first two minutes and learned they had never used it on the AC. That is a gift. The follow-through is one more question and one fact: "Good to know. Just so you're not surprised — a warranty claim on an AC usually means a dispatch window of several days in July. If we find something today, want me to price it both ways so you can compare before you call them?" Frame yourself as the comparison, not the competitor.

Weak

Gap 3 — The five-star promise is made on every call and collected on none

Ben, every call"I do this all for a five star experience. If at any point I'm not doing that, please correct me on it."
At every recorded closeNo review ask.

The fix He plants the best five-star seed on the board and never harvests it. This is the cheapest fix in the plan — one sentence at the door, tied back to the promise he already made: "I told you at the start I was going for five stars. Did I get there? If I did, the review is the single biggest thing you could do for me."

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Work it in the moment
Drill: Any hesitation — "we'll see," "that's a lot," "I have a warranty" — gets a curious question within ten seconds, standing where you are. Manager role-plays five early-hesitation scenarios.
Proof it worked: Every hesitation on tape is followed by a question, not a topic change.
Week 2
Own the warranty talk
Drill: One rehearsed, non-defensive home-warranty script: acknowledge, name the real dispatch timeline, offer the side-by-side comparison. Ten reps with a manager.
Proof it worked: Warranty mentions on tape are answered with a comparison offer, not a subject change.
Week 3
Collect the five
Drill: Review ask on every completed visit, tied back to the promise from the welcome. Including no-closes.
Proof it worked: A review ask on every completed visit on tape.

What we owe Benjamin Wyllie

  • Recording coverage through the presentation. All three of his recordings stop before the close. He cannot be coached on the part of the call that decides the outcome — this needs fixing before the next review cycle.
  • A warranty script he does not have to improvise. He is running into home warranties repeatedly and handling them by changing the subject. Give him the words.
  • Credit for carrying a trainee. He ran full-quality calls while teaching all day. That costs time and focus, and it should be acknowledged rather than assumed.
Bottom line

Everything visible on Ben's tapes is at or near the top of this board — the expectation set, the analogies, the honesty, the customer reaching the conclusion herself. Two things need attention and both are the same habit: when a customer signals hesitation, he acknowledges it and moves on instead of getting curious. Work the hesitation where it happens. And get the recordings to run through the close, because the part of his call that decides the outcome is currently invisible.
