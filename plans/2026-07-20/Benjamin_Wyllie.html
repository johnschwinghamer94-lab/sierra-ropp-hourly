1

The one-line verdict

The pattern

He sets the frame perfectly. He just doesn't defend it when a warranty shows up.

What actually happened

The welcome is near-flawless every time — tour, evaluation, time check, report, pricing, same-day start, approval and five-star, in one unbroken flow. On the leak call it carried all the way to a real lead, with a customer volunteering it kind of sounds like we need a new system. On the warranty call, the homeowner opened with I have a home warranty… I gotta call them first anyway and get an approval and the conversation moved on without a single question about it.

What to do with it

A home warranty stated up front is an objection arriving early, which is a gift. It gets one curious question and one fact — "How long did they take last time you filed?" — before the evaluation starts. Answered at minute two, it stops shaping the whole visit from the background.

5

What Benjamin did right — real strength, build on it

  • Two flips out of three. Both came off calls where the recording stops early, which means his front half is doing the converting.
  • The welcome is a script other techs should learn. Tour, evaluation, time check, report with pricing, same-day start, approval, five-star — delivered as one natural flow rather than a checklist.
  • He gets the customer to say it first. It kind of sounds like we need a new system came from the homeowner. That is the hardest and most valuable thing in the rubric.
  • Live diagnosis with the customer's hands on it. Having the homeowner hold the coil open during the leak search turns a finding into a shared discovery.
  • He asks permission properly. If I have any concerns, do you want me to bring that to your attention or keep it to myself? — asked as a real question every time.

The three gaps

each one in Benjamin's own calls
Weak

Gap 1 — the home warranty that shaped the whole visit

Customer, in the first two minutesWell, you can let me know. I have a home warranty. Anyway, you know, so I gotta call them first anyway and get an approval.
BenjaminOkay. Well, what home warranty do you have? — American Shield. — Okay. Have they ever done anything with your AC before? — No.
What never came nextHow long the claim takes, what they typically deny, or what it costs to wait.

The fix He asked the first two questions correctly and then stopped one short of the one that matters. "Fair enough — how long did they take the last time you filed on something? Because on AC in July we're usually looking at days without cooling while they dispatch, and they only cover a failed part, not a worn one." Then let the customer weigh it. Asked at minute two, this reframes the entire visit.

Moderate

Gap 2 — the five-star seed never becomes a five-star ask

Benjamin, every welcomeI do this all for a five star experience. If at any point I'm not doing that, please correct me on it.
At the end of every callNo review request.

The fix He has already done the hard part — the customer has been told what standard to hold him to. Collecting it is one sentence at the door: "I promised you a five-star visit at the start — if I hit it, the survey comes by text and my name on it means a lot."

Moderate

Gap 3 — training talk inside a customer's home

On tape during a customer visitExtended discussion with a riding trainee about which calls convert, how work gets routed, and opinions about home warranties.
ContextThis happens inside the customer's house and grounds during the evaluation.

The fix None of it is scored and none of it reflects on the customer conversation — but it fills time that belongs to the visit, and some of it would not read well overheard. Keep the teaching in the van or on the drive. In the home, the trainee watches and the customer gets the whole technician.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Work the warranty
Drill: Any mention of a home warranty gets two questions and one fact within thirty seconds. Ten reps with a manager on American Shield specifically.
Proof it worked: No tape where a warranty is named and left unexplored.
Week 2
Collect the five star
Drill: The review ask at the door on every completed visit, tied back to the promise he already makes in the welcome.
Proof it worked: Review asked on every call.
Week 3
Teach outside the house
Drill: Trainee questions get answered in the van between calls, not on the customer's property.
Proof it worked: No training conversation on tape inside a home.

What we owe Benjamin Wyllie

  • Record his welcome and use it for onboarding. It is the most complete and most natural expectation set on this board and it should not just live in his own calls.
  • A written home-warranty play. Real dispatch timelines, what the big providers actually deny, and two questions he can ask without sounding like he's arguing.
  • Credit both flips. Neither is visible on the tape. He should hear that his opening and discovery are what produced them.
  • Clarity on training ride-alongs. Where the coaching happens and where it doesn't, so he isn't guessing.
Bottom line

Benjamin does the beginning of the call better than anyone here, and the results now show it — two of his three calls became real leads, both of them off recordings that stop before the presentation. The single fix worth drilling is the home warranty: he asked two good questions about it and then let it sit in the background for seventy-five minutes. Work it in the first two minutes and that third call looks like the other two.
