1

The one-line verdict

Why they flipped

He books around the obstacle instead of arguing with it.

What actually happened

The Jones customer had a fifty-four-year-old system, a repair stack approaching ten thousand dollars, and a husband on swing shift she was nervous about committing for. Cole named the third option plainly — I did give you that third option which would be speaking to one of my comfort advisors. I can get them out here when your husband gets home — then made it risk-free and scheduled: I can get you guys on the schedule. That way we can have an appointment and if you need to move it or cancel it, you absolutely can.

Why that is the model

He never asked her to decide anything. He asked her to hold a time she could cancel, at an hour that suited the person she was worried about. The obstacle was the husband's schedule and his temper, so he booked around both. That is what turning an objection into logistics looks like.

5

What Cole did right — real strength, build on it

  • He books around the obstacle. Husband on swing shift and likely to be angry — so the appointment goes after he's home, and it can be cancelled. Two sentences, one lead.
  • Option C is named, not implied. I did give you that third option which would be speaking to one of my comfort advisors — it exists on the page as a choice, which is why it gets chosen.
  • He drew the system on every call, including the one with nothing wrong. That is what makes a maintenance visit feel worth the fee.
  • He didn't manufacture a problem. On a healthy system he said so and left. The credibility that buys is what makes the other two calls land.
  • He handled a written list of a customer's husband's technical questions — load calculations, curb adapters, labour warranty — without deflecting a single one.
6

The three gaps

each one in Cole's own calls
Weak

Gap 1 — the energy analysis landed before the bill question

ColeI also took a few extra minutes. I did an energy analysis for you. Based on age and condition of that system, it's going to be running your electric bill up by 40%.
Then, afterwardsI mean, how much are you guys paying on electricity?
CustomerWell, we've been running our swamp cooler, and our power bill stays about 125, 130.

The fix Right question, wrong order — and this is the second day running. Asked first, the analysis converts into the customer's own money instead of an abstract percentage. Bill question in the first ten minutes, analysis in the presentation. Same work, far more weight.

Moderate

Gap 2 — the numbers stacked before the frame

ColeThose three together come to 3287. For the heat exchanger… if I were to give you a ballpark, four to six thousand dollars… that compressor can be over 8,600 just for the R22 refrigerant and the compressor itself.
CustomerSo we're already up to almost ten grand.
Customer, laterI know we need a new unit and he's going to be p***** at me for saying that.

The fix The stacking worked here, but it worked by overwhelming rather than by guiding, and it left her anxious about her husband. One framing sentence in front of it does the same job more kindly: "I'm going to give you every number, and then I'll tell you honestly which one I'd choose in your position."

Moderate

Gap 3 — three calls, no review asked

Cole, every welcomeAbove all, we're here to give you a five star experience.
Customer, at the close of the maintenance callBut do I owe you anything for today? — No, we're taking care of that for you today.
What followedNo review request.

The fix A customer who has just been told there is nothing wrong and no charge is the single most likely person on the board to leave a five-star review. Ask, at the door, every time.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Bill before analysis
Drill: The power-bill question in the first ten minutes on every call, and the energy analysis expressed in the customer's own dollars.
Proof it worked: Customer's figure on the report before any percentage is spoken.
Week 2
Frame the stack
Drill: One sentence before any sequence of prices, and a stated recommendation after it. Five reps on multi-repair presentations.
Proof it worked: No tape where prices accumulate without a frame.
Week 3
Collect the five star
Drill: Review asked at the door on all three call types, especially no-charge maintenance.
Proof it worked: Review asked on every completed visit.

What we owe Cole Pantol

  • Use the Jones booking as the team's Option C example. Reversible, timed around the absent decision-maker, and asked for once. That is the whole technique in two sentences.
  • The discovery card, again. Same gap as yesterday: excellent close built on a late bill question. This is a sequencing habit, and a prompt fixes it.
  • Back him on the honest maintenance call. He found nothing and sold nothing. Nobody should feel that counts against them.
  • Support on the follow-through. A morning advisor appointment made by a nervous spouse needs a confirmation call, or it quietly disappears.
Bottom line

Cole is closing the way we want it closed — Option C named as a real option, priced at zero, booked to a specific time, and made cancellable so that agreeing costs the customer nothing. Two of three calls turned into leads and the third was honest. The one habit still lagging is discovery: he is running energy analyses before he knows what anyone pays for power. Ask first, and the strongest decision step on this board gets stronger.
