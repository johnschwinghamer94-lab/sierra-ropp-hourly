1

The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

He booked the appointment the customer couldn't ask for.

What actually happened

The homeowner had a cracked heat exchanger, a burst capacitor, a screaming compressor and no cooling — and her real obstacle wasn't money, it was her husband: I'm gonna have a hell of a time repeating it all… I try to talk and he over talks me and then I get really frustrated. She said the safe thing: I better wait until he gets home.

What moves it

Cole didn't argue with the wait — he removed the reason for it. It is the summertime, we are very busy. I can get you guys on the schedule. That way we can have an appointment and if you need to move it or cancel it, you absolutely can. Would that work for you? A no-risk slot, in her name, for the next morning, so she never had to win the argument. That's the flip.

5

What Cole did right — real strength, build on it

  • He fixed the automatic fail in one day (Required Questions — Strong). Filters and frequency, thermostat setting, hotter and colder rooms, and the bill follow-up — asked naturally, on the first call of the day. That is the fastest correction on this board.
  • The flip, under the hardest possible conditions (Overcoming Objections — Strong). A customer who told him outright she could not carry the message to her husband still ended the visit with an advisor booked for the morning. He solved her problem, not his.
  • Letting the customer make the connection (Get Customer to Diagnose — Strong). Do you guys ever get headaches or feel dizzy at all in the winter time? — one question, and the carbon monoxide finding became hers instead of his.
  • Honest on a healthy system. On a four-year-old unit he cleaned the coils and said I don't have any concerns on that system. The willingness to find nothing is exactly what makes the other two calls believable.

The three gaps

each one in Cole's own calls
Moderate

Gap 1 — A carbon-monoxide finding was left with the person who said she couldn't deliver it

Customer"I wish my husband could hear all this. I'm gonna have a hell of a time repeating it all." … "I try to talk and he over talks me and then I get really frustrated with him."
Cole"Is he available by phone?" — asked once, the call didn't connect, and the subject moved on.

The fix Booking the advisor was the right save and it rescued the call. But the finding here was cracked heat-exchanger seams venting into an occupied home — that is a safety message, not a sales message, and it should not depend on a relay the customer told you she can't make. Escalate it: "This one I'd rather not have you carry. Can I text him the photos and my number right now, or call him myself when he clocks out? He can be as annoyed with me as he likes." Take the argument off her plate entirely.

Moderate

Gap 2 — Three prices in under a minute, to a customer already frightened

Cole"Those three together come to $3,287… if we can get one, we're four to six thousand on the heat exchanger… so we're already up to almost ten grand… that compressor can be over $8,600."
Customer"No, me. Sorry." — and then a change of subject.

The fix Every number was honest and every number was necessary. The problem is pacing: they arrived stacked, immediately after a carbon-monoxide finding, to a household that had just described being medically vulnerable to heat. Say one number, stop, and let her respond before the next one. "That's the repair number. I'll pause there — what's your reaction to that before I show you the rest?" Same information, delivered at a speed she can act on.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Reach the decision-maker
Drill: When the person in front of you names someone else as the decider, do not leave without a text, a call, or a booked time with that person. Manager role-plays three absent-spouse scenarios.
Proof it worked: No tape where an absent decision-maker is left to be briefed second-hand on a safety finding.
Week 2
One number at a time
Drill: Present the immediate number, then stop and ask for a reaction before moving to the next tier. Ten reps with a manager on a three-tier presentation.
Proof it worked: Every presentation on tape has a pause and a customer response between tiers.
Week 3
Collect the five
Drill: Review ask on every completed visit, tied back to the promise from the welcome — including no-closes and including free calls.
Proof it worked: A review ask on every completed visit on tape.

What we owe Cole Pantol

  • Name the turnaround out loud. Cole went from zero of three critical questions to all three in a single day. That is coachability, and it should be recognised in front of the team.
  • Back him on the gas shut-off. He turned off gas to a heater on a fifty-four-year-old unit and ate the diagnostic fee. That is the right call and he needs to know the company stands behind it without hesitation.
  • A same-day and next-morning advisor slot he can promise. The cancellable-appointment move is his best close and it only works if the slot is real.
  • A script for reaching the absent decision-maker. One rehearsed sentence for "I'll have to tell my husband" that ends with a phone number, not a hope.
Bottom line

Cole had the best call on the board this day and fixed his biggest gap from the day before while he was at it. The remaining coaching is about pace and reach, not skill: slow the numbers down for a customer who is frightened, and never leave a safety finding to be relayed by someone who has told you they can't relay it. Everything else here is the standard.
