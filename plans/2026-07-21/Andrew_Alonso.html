1

The one-line verdict

Why both flipped

He gets the signature before he writes the report.

What actually happened

On both calls the sequence is identical: name the risk of the repair honestly, offer the advisor as free information, and get it authorised on the spot — so again, zero bucks to speak to him. Let me just get a signature in this little box right here — and only then go to the van to write the repair options. On the flooded-attic call the owners were overseas, so he arranged for the specialist to arrive while the parents joined by phone: I get them out here and then they can get on the phone with you guys.

Why that is the model

Most reps write the report first and mention the advisor at the end, when the customer is already processing numbers. Andrew reverses it. The free visit is authorised while the customer is still absorbing the diagnosis, and the repair options arrive afterwards as the comparison rather than as the ask. Nothing about it is pushy — the signature is explicitly for zero dollars and he says so.

5

What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • He takes the authorisation before the report. Zero bucks to speak to him. Let me just get a signature in this little box right here — the advisor is agreed while the diagnosis is still fresh, not tacked on after the prices.
  • He solved a nine-hour time difference instead of accepting it. Owners asleep in Finland, children unable to authorise — so he arranged the advisor to attend with the parents on the phone at midnight their time.
  • He refused to sell the easy repair. Declining to charge for a fuse that would likely blow again is the most credible thing a technician can do, and it is precisely why the advisor was accepted.
  • He asked for the review. One of very few on the board to do it.
  • He documents water damage properly, with video, which matters when an insurer is going to be involved.

The three gaps

each one in Andrew's own calls
Moderate

Gap 1 — the power bill is never asked, on either call

What was discussed insteadInsurance coverage, refrigerant types, pension plans and retirement.
What was never askedWhat either household pays for power in the summer.

The fix Andrew is already selling the replacement conversation well. The bill is the number that makes it concrete rather than theoretical: "What's July running you on power? Because a system in this condition is charging you for the privilege." Ask it before the advisor arrives so the advisor walks into a value case already built.

Moderate

Gap 2 — the goal question is missing from both welcomes

Andrew's openingI'm going to check out everything on the system. Whatever the issue is, I'll build you out options.
Not asked on either callWhat the customer is hoping to get out of the visit, or when they need him gone by.

The fix On the flooded-attic call the real goal was almost certainly get this documented for the insurer, and it emerged an hour in. "What would make today a win for you?" surfaces that at minute two and shapes everything after it.

Moderate

Gap 3 — the pace outruns the customer

On tapeDiagnosis, refrigerant differences, warranty status and options arriving in rapid succession, with the customer answering mostly in acknowledgements.
Customer, at the closeNo, no, not still can't think of any questions yet. I mean, yeah, maybe later.

The fix Maybe later means it did not land yet. Andrew's content is right and there is a lot of it — so pause and hand it back: "Before I keep going — tell me what you've got so far, so I know I'm making sense." A customer who can restate the problem is a customer the advisor can sell to.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
The bill question
Drill: Power bill asked in the first ten minutes on every call and written on the report before the advisor is dispatched.
Proof it worked: Customer's own figure on every recording.
Week 2
Add the goal question
Drill: Goal question and time-constraint check added to an opening that is otherwise working.
Proof it worked: Both present on every recording.
Week 3
Hand it back
Drill: After each block of findings, one question that makes the customer restate the problem in their own words. Five reps.
Proof it worked: A customer summary on tape at least once per call.
8

What we owe Andrew Alonso

  • Make the zero-dollar signature the team standard. Authorising the advisor before writing the report is the single most transferable behaviour on this board and he did it twice in one day.
  • Confirm both lead tickets typed out. Both advisors were dispatched with a signature on tape; the leads should be verified so his number reflects the work.
  • A one-line insurance script. Water damage plus an insurer came up again and he improvised it well; he should not have to.
  • Recognition for refusing the easy sale. Declining to charge for a fuse he could not stand behind is the behaviour we want rewarded, not just tolerated.
Bottom line

Andrew converted both calls by doing the same thing twice: telling the truth about what a cheap repair would actually buy, then getting the free advisor visit authorised on the spot — before the report, before the prices, with the signature explicitly for nothing. He even solved a nine-hour time difference rather than accepting it as a reason to fail. Add the power bill and the goal question to the front of the call and there is very little else to fix.
