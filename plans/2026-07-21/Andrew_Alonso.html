1

The one-line verdict

Why both flipped

He refuses to sell a repair he can't stand behind — and that is exactly what converts.

What he does

On both calls he named the risk before the price: if I replace that fuse, then I'm charging you, and then it pops again. Because it's something bigger. Then he made the specialist the honest next step rather than the upsell — that's exactly why this estimate is free. We pay these guys salary… if it's 11 o'clock at night, you have to come help people get the idea of a new system — and got the signature: again, zero bucks to speak to him. Let me just get a signature in this little box right here.

What it costs him

The talking. Both recordings run past two hours, and long stretches are Andrew's own biography — skydiving, ADHD, rent, religion, his wife's business plan. The customer stopped being asked questions somewhere in hour one. Rapport this good does not need three hours to build, and the required question set never gets run because there is no room left for it.

  • He turned "I can't drop that kind of money yet" into a signature. That's exactly why this estimate is free… you don't have to say yes or no to anything. That is the model, executed under pressure at eleven at night.
  • He asked the power-bill follow-up on both calls — the single most-skipped question on this board, and he is one of the only people asking it at all.

The three gaps

Gap 1 — He asks the follow-up and skips the question

Andrew, both calls"What is the electric bills in the summer right now?" … "What's your electric bill looking like in the summertime?"
Never asked, either call"What temperature do you keep the thermostat set at in the summer? And what about the winter?"

The fix The bill number is only powerful next to the setting that produced it. $150 with solar means nothing on its own; $150 with solar, holding 78, and it still can't keep the master cool is a replacement conversation that makes itself. Ask the setting first, every time, then the bill — it is two sentences and it turns a data point into an argument the customer builds for you.

Gap 2 — He never stops talking after the ask

The patternSignature obtained on PONDAHARN, specialist on the way — followed by the refrigerant phase-outs, SEER ratings, warranty economics, his rent, skydiving, ADHD, and an indoor dog gym.

The fix Zip It is the one behaviour he does not run at all. Once the customer has agreed, the next voice should be theirs. The risk is not that the customer un-agrees — it is that two-hour calls on a night with a full board cost him the calls he never got to, and the required questions never fit because the airtime is gone. 6

This week's drill — the two-sentence assessment opener

Trigger and response

Trigger: the moment he finishes confirming the reason for the call, before he touches a tool.

Say it in this order, every call:

"Before I get started — how often are you changing the filters? Which rooms get hotter or colder than the rest? And what do you keep the thermostat at in the summer? … And what does a standard power bill look like for you setting it there?"

He already asks the fourth one. Four questions, under a minute, and it front-loads the whole decision conversation with the customer's own numbers instead of his.

Bottom line

Andrew closed everything he was given, on the two hardest calls of his night, by being straight with people about what he could not promise. Run the four questions in order, then stop talking once you have the yes, and this becomes the most complete rep on the board.
