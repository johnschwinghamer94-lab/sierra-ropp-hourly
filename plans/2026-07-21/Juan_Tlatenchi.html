1

The one-line verdict

Why it didn't flip

Third graded day, same ending: the findings never become an ask.

What is on tape

The customer-facing part is good work — the wheels for the motor are completely covered in dirt… it's going to cause the wheel to be imbalanced and make a lot of rattling noise. On top of that, the motors are leaking oil, so they're working a little harder than they need to. They're running unlubricated. After that the recording is almost entirely a colleague conversation.

The pattern

This is the third graded day in a row where Juan's diagnosis is strong and no appointment results. Two days ago it was an insurance deadline; yesterday a customer asked twice for a replacement estimate. Today the presentation does not appear at all. The teaching is not the problem and has never been the problem. The problem is that the visit ends without anyone being asked to do anything.

What Juan did right — real strength, build on it

  • The diagnosis is right and it is well explained. Dirty wheels, imbalance, oil loss and unlubricated running — connected in a chain the customer could follow.
  • He used the physical evidence. The filter she had just pulled became the proof for what he found on the wheels.
  • The customer made the connection herself. That looks like the filter we pulled out is the customer doing the diagnosing.
  • His welcome, on the two days it was captured, was consistently complete. That habit is established and is not in question here.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Never leave without an ask
Drill: Every gradeable call ends with a written option set and a proposed advisor time, regardless of what was found.
Proof it worked: A report and an ask on tape for every call.
Week 2
The house is the customer's
Drill: Colleague conversation moves to the van and between calls; the visit time goes to the presentation.
Proof it worked: No extended colleague conversation on tape during a visit.
Week 3
Record the whole call
Drill: Recorder confirmed at the door so the welcome and the close both exist for coaching.
Proof it worked: Full-length recordings for every visit.
8

What we owe Juan Tlatenchi

  • A direct conversation, not another gap list. Three graded days, three strong diagnoses, zero asks. He needs a manager to name the pattern out loud rather than repeat the coaching note.
  • A mechanical report rule. Take the judgement out of it — every call gets three options, every time.
  • Keep crediting the teaching. His refrigerant and system explanations are among the best here and that should not get lost in the correction.
  • Clarity on colleague conversation during visits. Nobody appears to have told him where the line is.
Bottom line

Juan found two failing motors, explained them so clearly the customer connected the evidence herself, and then the visit dissolved into an hour of conversation with a colleague and ended with nothing offered. That is the third graded day in a row where excellent diagnosis produced no ask. The coaching cannot keep being about technique, because the technique is not the problem. Every call ends with a report and a proposed time. That is the whole assignment.
