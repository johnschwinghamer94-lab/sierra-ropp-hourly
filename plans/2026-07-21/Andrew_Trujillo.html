The one-line verdict

The whole file

The best twenty minutes on the board, and then nothing.

What is on tape

Floor protection offered unprompted — you don't have to put anything on your shoes… I just want to make sure I'm protecting your home — parking confirmed, permission for the back door, and an expectations block that names the one thing most reps forget to ask: if I happen to come across concerns that need addressed, is that information you'd like me to keep to myself, or would you like me to share that with you?

What is not

Four systems, a standing complaint about rooms that never reach temperature, and a customer who says she has been told the explanation before and kind of don't really remember it. That is a maintenance call with a real conversation waiting at the end of it — and the recording stops before a single finding, price or option is delivered. He cannot be credited for what happened next, and he cannot be coached on it either.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He offered floor protection before anyone asked. You don't have to put anything on your shoes… I just want to make sure I'm protecting your home. Nobody else on the board did this.
  • He asked permission to share bad news. Is that information you'd like me to keep to myself, or would you like me to share that with you? That single question converts every later finding from a pitch into something the customer requested.
  • He worked the hot-room complaint to the end instead of accepting the first room named — guest room, daughter's room, the missing thermostat, the time of day, and the door that gets shut.
  • He explained something she had given up on understanding. Sensor interference between thermostats, in one sentence, no jargon.
  • He named his parking spot rather than asking a vague question — a small thing that makes the answer easy.

The three gaps

Weak

Gap 1 — The recording stops before the part of the visit that gets graded

Last words on tape"I'll check with you periodically as best I can… yell for me if need be for anything."
What is missingFour systems evaluated, findings, photos, a written report, options and whatever the customer decided — none of it recorded.

The fix This is a habit and equipment problem before it is a coaching problem, and it costs him the entire record of his best work. Everything the rubric rewards most — building options, Link/Say/Spin/Zip, objection handling, the five-star ask — lives in the last third of a call. Start the recorder at the door and let it run to the truck. Until that happens he is being coached, fairly, on his introduction.

Weak

Gap 2 — He asked the hardest required question and skipped the three easy ones

Asked, thoroughly"Which rooms in particular… any other rooms that are feeling excessively hot or cold?"
Never askedHow often the filters get changed · what the thermostat is set at in summer and winter · what a standard power bill looks like at that setting.

The fix The customer handed him the setup for all three and he walked past it. She said herself that a thermostat says to cool when it's 78 and that setting one to 68 still leaves the room at 73 — that is the thermostat question answering itself, and the mandatory follow-up is one sentence away: and what does a standard power bill look like for you setting it there in the summer? On a four-system home with rooms that never reach temperature, that number is the whole conversation.

Weak

Gap 3 — Three of the six expectation beats are missing, including the five-star seed

What he said"I'll be looking at all systems, inside and out… I'll have a written report based on anything we need to address, and we can go over your options at that point."
What he did not say"I won't do anything without your approval." · "My goal is to take care of everything today." · "Above all I'm here to give you a five-star experience."

The fix He is already the rep who announces that he is setting expectations — the structure is there, it is three sentences short. The approval promise is what makes a customer relax enough to let him find things; the five-star seed at the door is what makes the ask at the end land. Add all three to the block he already runs and it becomes the standard version.

6

This week's drill — finish the block, then finish the tape

Say it in one breath, at every door

"I'll look at the whole system, not just the one thing. I'll do a thorough evaluation, and I'll bring you back everything I find and show you your options. I won't do anything without your approval first, and my goal is to take care of everything today if we can. Above all I'm here to give you a five-star experience — if I'm not doing that, tell me so I can fix it."

Then: recorder on at the door, off at the truck. He already runs the first half better than most of the team; the drill is the second half of the sentence and the second half of the call.
