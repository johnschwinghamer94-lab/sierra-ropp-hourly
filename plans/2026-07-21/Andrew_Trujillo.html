The one-line verdict

What the tape shows

The best twenty minutes on the board, and then nothing.

What is on tape

A complete, unhurried opening: shoe covers declined but offered, parking checked, the full scope named — I'll be looking at all systems, inside and out — the report and options promised, and a customer complaint about previous annual visits taken seriously rather than deflected. Whatever happened here, it did not produce a lead — and the tape cannot tell us whether the ask was made.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Record everything
Drill: Recorder confirmed at the door and checked before leaving; the report and presentation must be on tape.
Proof it worked: Full-length recordings for every visit.
Week 2
Answer the complaint
Drill: Any grievance about past service gets a follow-up question and an explicit commitment for today, referenced again at the close.
Proof it worked: Complaint named and answered on tape.
Week 3
Finish the welcome
Drill: Five-star seed and goal question added to an otherwise excellent opening.
Proof it worked: Both beats on every recording.

What we owe Andrew Trujillo

  • Fix the recording before anything else. His only tape this day is twenty minutes of a much longer visit, and the part that matters is missing.
  • Ask what happened on this call. Four systems and a dissatisfied customer, and no lead. Whether the ask was made is worth knowing.
  • Use his opening as a template. Naming the expectation-setting out loud is a small technique that makes the whole sequence land better.
  • A play for the recurring-tune-up complaint. Customers who feel annual visits are pointless are common and techs need a real answer, not a deflection.
Bottom line

The twenty minutes we have are excellent — the most deliberate expectation-setting on this board, permission asked at every step, and a customer's standing complaint about these visits given room to be said. Then the tape stops, four systems go unreported, and no lead exists. This is not a coaching problem yet; it is a visibility problem.
