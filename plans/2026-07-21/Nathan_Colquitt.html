1

The one-line verdict

The whole file

He built more trust in fifteen minutes than most reps build all day, and then asked nothing with it.

What he earned

A customer who volunteered his entire equipment history unprompted — six systems, which ones run, which ones don't, the panel-versus-wiring dispute between two previous technicians, and the room that is about to matter: we're about to be foster parents. So this one will probably be running more. Nathan heard it and turned it into service on the spot: I'll make sure I do a really good look on that one too, because I'm sure you're gonna want it running really well.

What he did not do with it

Not one of the four required questions. No filter question on a six-system home. No thermostat setting. No power bill. No what were you hoping we could accomplish while I'm here for your summer maintenance? — on a maintenance call where the customer had already told him one unit is dead and another is three weeks old. The information was being handed over faster than he was collecting it.

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He turned a personal detail into a promise about the work. About to be foster parents… So I'll make sure I do a really good look on that one too, because I'm sure you're gonna want it running really well. That is rapport doing a job, not filling silence.
  • He gave the customer something real for free. Several minutes of honest, hard-won advice about trading, aimed at the customer's wife rather than at the customer's wallet.
  • He asked permission before every single access — thermostats, rooms, the ladder, the parking spot.
  • He refused to accept two previous technicians' verdicts and said he would look himself: Once I get in there and get a good look… if that wiring is loose, I can try to give it some tightening.
  • He got the whole property mapped — six systems, which are fenced, where every access is — before touching a tool.

Weak

Gap 1 — Four required questions, none asked, on a six-system home

What he did instead"Would you mind showing me where the thermostats are?" · "I'm going to drop this one in temp so I can get the readings."
What was never askedHow often the filters get changed · which rooms run hotter or colder · what the thermostat is set at in summer and winter · what a standard power bill looks like at that setting.

The fix Locating a filter is not the same as asking about it, and adjusting a thermostat is not the same as asking what it lives at. On six systems those four questions are the difference between a tune-up and a plan — a household about to add foster children, running rooms they currently keep switched off, is going to have a comfort conversation and a bill conversation whether or not he starts it. Ask them at the kitchen counter before the ladder comes out.

What he never said"I'll bring you back everything I find and show you your options." · "I won't do anything without your approval." · "My goal is to take care of everything today." · "Above all I'm here to give you a five-star experience."

The four he is missing take about fifteen seconds and they are what turn a findings review into an expected event rather than a surprise sales conversation — particularly on a property with a system the customer has already written off.

6

This week's drill — the counter conversation

Before the ladder comes out, every maintenance call

"Before I get started — what were you hoping we could accomplish while I'm here for your summer maintenance? How often are the filters getting changed? Which rooms run hotter or colder than the rest? And what do you keep the thermostats set at in the summer — and what does a standard power bill look like for you setting them there?"

Ninety seconds, standing still, before any equipment is touched. This customer would have answered all five in detail — he was already volunteering more than he was asked.

Bottom line

Nathan is easy to trust and people tell him things — six systems, a dead unit, a botched repair and a house about to change — without being asked. That is the hard half of this job and he has it. What is missing is the deliberate half: four questions at the counter, four more sentences at the door, and a recorder that runs past the walkthrough. He is being graded on an introduction because that is all that exists on tape, and it is a good one.
