1

The one-line verdict

Why it didn't convert

He built the entire case and then took the two numbers out of the room.

The case

Sixteen-year-old system, fan motor leaking a ton of oil where it's seizing up, and it's causing that compressor to overheat, a temperature split of 15 where it should be at least a 20 degree split, and a rusting drain pan already holding water: if that pan reaches, all that water is sinking into your guys's ceiling. Then the honest verdict — if it was just a fan motor that was bad, I'd be like, no brainer, just get that thing repaired. But the way that it is now… — and Option C offered properly.

What he did with it

I can offer you guys your repair side, which I'm gonna write up a report and then send it to you guys with the actual prices of everything. No repair price said out loud. And the specialist: if you guys want to schedule an appointment with the other guy after three when you guys get back, that'd be great. No call made, no time named. The customer had already offered him a window — would you guys be able to potentially come back at like 3ish? — and it was never turned into an appointment.

Draw the Picture
Strong
Cause chained to consequence, every time. The split, in numbers the customer can hold: you're blowing about 65 inside the house and about 80 coming in… it should be at least a 20 degree split, and yours is only at 15. The coil, followed to the ceiling: you can see all the rust here and all the rust on the pan… that's when this unit starts to leak refrigerant, and then you start losing even more capacity of cooling.

Strong

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He read the account history before he asked the question. We actually quoted you for something, but it doesn't look like you went with us… we came out here in 2016. Nobody else on this board arrived knowing the customer's own file.
  • He refused to work from second-hand information. With the owner absent, he got her on the phone, confirmed which system was faulty and explained the scope himself rather than relying on the housekeeper's account.
  • He gave the split in numbers the customer could hold. It should be at least a 20 degree split… and yours is only at 15.
  • He named the case where he would have said don't spend. If it was just a fan motor that was bad, I'd be like, no brainer, just get that thing repaired. That is what makes the rest of the recommendation believable.
  • He asked the membership question at the exact moment it made sense — when a customer explained their existing provider had stopped doing summer maintenance.
  • He handled a hard personal disclosure well. A toddler's epilepsy diagnosis, met with attention rather than a pivot back to the sale.

6

This week's drill — the number and the day, before anything else

At the end of every findings review

"So the repair is [number]. The replacement path I can't price — that's what our equipment guy does, and it costs you nothing. You said three works for you; I'll call him from the driveway right now so you've got both numbers today rather than one of them tomorrow."

Then stop talking. He already builds the best case on the board — what is missing is a figure spoken out loud and a specialist attached to a time the customer has already offered him.

7

3-week training plan

Week 1
Say the number
Every repair price stated out loud in the room. The emailed report is the copy, not the conversation.
Week 2
Book the specialist from the driveway
Every Option C offer converts to a named time before he leaves the property. No "if you guys want to."
Week 3
The full expectations block and the missing questions
Approval and the five-star standard at every door; the rooms question and the power-bill follow-up added to the thermostat and filter questions he already asks.

Bottom line

Francisco taught a trainee all day and still explained a failing system better than anyone else on this board — cause to consequence, rust to ceiling, split to compressor, with the honest version of when he would have told her to just repair it. What he did not do is put a number in the room or a specialist on the calendar. The customer told him she was afraid of a replacement and then offered him a three o'clock appointment; both were left on the table. Say the price out loud and make the call from the driveway, and this day converts.
