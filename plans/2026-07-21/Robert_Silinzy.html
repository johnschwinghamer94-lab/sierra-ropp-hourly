1

The one-line verdict

The day in one pattern

Three clean openings, three tapes that stop before anything is decided.

What is recorded

The whole block, in one breath: The visit today I'm going to perform a thorough evaluation — outdoor and indoor, safety, function, overall effectiveness of the unit. If I do happen to notice anything, would you like me to share that with you? From there we'll build a report, list of options. Any work that needs to be done won't be done without your approval, and if you do approve anything, the work could be done today. Goal's to deliver a five star experience — so if I'm not meeting that, you stop me. All six beats, plus the membership question and the thermostat motive.

What is not

A leaking rooftop unit six years old and badly sealed, an occupant who told him plainly that he and his partner are the only earners in a three-person household, and a second property with a leak the occupant cannot explain and a homeowner who is not there. Not one of the three files contains a finding, a photograph shown, a price, an option or an answer.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • Six expectation beats in under a minute. Scope, permission to share, options, approval, today, and the five-star standard framed as an invitation: if I'm not meeting that, you stop me.
  • He asked why the thermostat sits where it does. Is it set normally like that for comfort, or is it more to keep the utilities in check? — one of only two people on this board who asked for the motive rather than just the number.
  • He asked the maintenance opener word for word. As far as the maintenance today, what are you hoping to accomplish?
  • He established the decision-maker before doing the work. I guess the homeowner, or your parents? Are they available today?
  • He let a customer finish. A long, angry account of a previous contractor was heard out in full and answered with I'm sorry to hear that rather than a sales pivot.
  • He documents before he starts — thermostats, attic entry, both units photographed before a tool comes out.

6

This week's drill — name the structure before you leave the room

Trigger and response

Trigger: any customer who raises money before the evaluation — "can you work with me", "just a temporary fix", "I can't afford much."

"Understood, and I'm going to build this so it's easy to choose. I'll show you the smallest thing that gets you cooling, then what a permanent fix looks like, then what financing does to the monthly. You'll see all three and you pick. Nothing happens without your say-so."

Then run the evaluation, come back in, and present it — in the room, on tape. He already promises a list of options at the door; the drill is delivering it face to face.
