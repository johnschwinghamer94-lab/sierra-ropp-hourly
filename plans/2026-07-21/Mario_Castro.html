The one-line verdict

What the tape shows

He asks the questions that make a sale possible, then the tape stops.

What is on tape

The discovery is consistently strong: how are you guys, utility bills? answered with a real seasonal range, damper behaviour worked out against the customer's own description, a home-warranty estimate examined rather than dismissed, and a tenant's landlord-and-insurance situation carefully mapped — usually homeowners insurance will have you pay a deductible, and then submit the rest of the invoice.

What is missing

Every one of those threads is the beginning of a decision conversation, and none of them is on tape. This is the second graded day where Mario converts without a single presentation captured. The part of his call that produces leads has now been invisible twice. That is a coverage problem before it is a coaching one.

5

What Mario did right — real strength, build on it

  • He asks the utility-bill question, consistently. Two graded days, asked both times, answered with real seasonal numbers. Almost nobody else on this board does that reliably.
  • He examined a competitor's estimate instead of attacking it. The customer had already decided it looked wrong; Mario let the estimate speak for itself and kept his credibility.
  • He handled a tenant-and-landlord situation properly. Confirmed what could be disclosed, mapped the insurance path, and arranged to report back before leaving.
  • He got a customer to state the damper diagnosis himself. It just seems to be frozen in place — Mario's questions produced that.
  • He protects the home. Laid down covering before working in the attic so insulation wouldn't fall, unprompted.

Mario keeps doing the two hardest parts well — he asks what the power actually costs, and he handles competitors, tenants and landlords with care instead of defensiveness.
