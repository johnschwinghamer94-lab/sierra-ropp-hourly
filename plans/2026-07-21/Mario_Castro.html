1

The one-line verdict

The day in one pattern

He collects everything the model asks for and then never uses any of it.

What he gathers

Filters, rooms, thermostat setting and the bill, on the same call: You guys normally keep it at 74?… When you guys turn it down, is that for comfort or to help out with utilities?… How are your utility bills when you crank it down?… Do you notice any hotter or cold spots around the house?… And you change them how often? He also asks about allergies, filter thickness and dust — questions nobody else on the board asked at all.

What he does with it

Nothing that reaches a customer. Two dampers he says should be replaced, a 1993 system with rusting drain lines and R22 he calls liquid gold, and a home-warranty estimate the customer already thinks is inflated — and no number is ever said, no option sheet is ever presented, and no specialist is ever offered. One customer said it out loud: we should have just called you first.

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He asked the power-bill follow-up on all three calls. Nobody else on this board asked it more than twice all day, and most never asked it at all.
  • He asked why the thermostat sits where it does. Is that for comfort or to help out with utilities? — one extra sentence that turns a number into a motive.
  • He protected the home before he was in it. I put something on the floor too, that way all the insulation doesn't fall… I'll make sure it's all clean by the time I leave.
  • He named exactly what he was blocking in the driveway rather than asking a vague parking question.
  • He held a hard delivery calmly. A tenant told him it felt like a bereavement; he kept going, plainly and without pressure.
  • He does small things nobody asked for — the stuck robot vacuum, the phone help, the attic cleaned up behind him.

Customer, FRANCO"The estimate seemed kind of high and kind of off… once we saw the number on here, we're like, oh, we should have just called them first."
Mario"If they keep causing you problems and you still feel like it's not [cooling], then I'd say go ahead and take care of both the dampers." No price, no written option, no next step.

The fix That customer told him, in his own words, that he had a competing quote he did not trust and wished he had called Sierra first. That is the easiest option presentation of the day and it never happened. Build the sheet, name the immediate number, name the future number, and put it in his hand: Here's what the two dampers cost, here's what I'd hold off on, and here's what it looks like if you'd rather compare against replacing. He had already earned the right to be believed.

6

This week's drill — spend the answers

At the findings review, every call

"You told me you keep it at [setting] and your summer bill runs [number], and that [room] never keeps up. Here's what I found, here's the immediate number, here's what I'd leave for later — and if you'd rather see what starting fresh looks like next to that, I can have our specialist out with a real figure at no cost."

He already has every blank in that sentence filled in before he opens the van. The drill is saying it out loud, with a number in it, to the person who has to decide.

Mario asks the questions this whole board skips — the setting, the motive, the rooms, the filter thickness, and the power bill on every single call — and he asks them warmly enough that people answer honestly. Then he puts the answers down and walks away without a number.
