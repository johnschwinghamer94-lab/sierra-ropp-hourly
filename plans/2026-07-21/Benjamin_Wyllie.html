1

The one-line verdict

Why nothing flipped

He builds the case perfectly and then hands the customer the pen instead of the calendar.

The case he built

Immediates, futures and a third path, all named and priced: we're looking at four thousand one hundred dollars… I want to give you a heads up, this number is liquid, then that compressor on top of that… we're looking around $8,000, then I did give you the option to speak to one of our install specialists about updating the system — starting over with a new working system that has a longevity to it and not a short-term Band-Aid. The customer understood it completely and repeated it back to her family.

What he did with it

Customer: our next move is to get to this — whoever is going to take care of it. Benjamin: they can even help you out at that point. Then: So when do y'all want me to follow up with you? → I will let you know. No specialist called, no day named, no appointment. The visit ended with a signature confirming he had not punched a hole in the wall.

6

This week's drill — "think about it" is a question, not an ending

Trigger and response

Trigger: any version of "we'll think about it" / "we need to talk to our son" / "this is overwhelming."

Response — acknowledge, then move the free step, not the paid one:

"Completely fair, and you should think about it — that's a lot of money. Let's take the thinking off your plate where we can: the specialist costs you nothing and just gets you the real replacement number so you're comparing two actual figures instead of one. He's out this way tomorrow afternoon — or is Thursday morning easier? You can still say no to everything after that."

He already asks the perfect opener — what things do you have to think about that I can answer for you? The drill is what comes after the answer: never end a call by asking the customer when to be contacted.
