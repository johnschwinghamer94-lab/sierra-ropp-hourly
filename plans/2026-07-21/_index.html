What the whole board has in common

The five-star ask
Weak
Eleven of twelve reps seeded the five-star standard somewhere and exactly one asked a customer for a review all day. Several were thanked warmly and unprompted and left anyway. It remains the cheapest fix on the board.
Recordings that stop at the decision
Weak
Seven reps have no tape that reaches an option or a price. Their close rates are unknowable and they are being coached almost entirely on their introductions. This is an equipment and habit problem before it is a rep problem.
The power-bill follow-up
Weak
The mandatory follow-up to the thermostat question was asked by three reps out of twelve — and one of them asked it on every call. On a day where most conversations were about whether an old system is worth keeping, that number was the argument.
Option C offered without a day attached
Moderate
Four reps offered a comfort specialist and left the booking to the customer — "if you guys want to", "when do you want me to follow up". Both calls that converted did the opposite: the call was made before the rep left the property.
Welcome and expectations
Strong
The genuine strength of this board. Introductions, parking, permission and thorough-evaluation promises are near-universal, and four complete six-beat blocks on this day are good enough to be training standards.
Bottom line

This board opens better than any board should and closes worse. Four reps ran the complete expectations block word for word, one stopped at a doorway because of what was on his boots, and another asked the power-bill question on every single call. Then thirty-seven recordings produced two wins, seven reps whose tape never reaches a price, and a single review request across the entire day. Three habits would move the whole team at once: run the recorder to the end of the call, attach a named day to every specialist offer, and ask for the five-star review you have already earned.
