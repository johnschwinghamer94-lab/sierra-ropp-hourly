The one-line verdict

The day in one pattern

Textbook openings, real findings, and no tape of what any of it was worth.

What is recorded

The full six-beat block, twice: I'll step into my thorough evaluation looking for safety, cleanliness, proper operations. If anything is out of the ordinary, do you want me to let you guys know about that?… I'll step out to the van, create a report for you. We don't do any work without your approval. We get that approval, get started as soon as today. Above all, we're here to give you a five star experience — if I'm not doing that, let me know so I can change it. And on RUNDELL, a compressor side reading of zero and a rusting coil, both shown and explained.

What is not

A capacitor getting zeros on the compressor side, rust eating a cooling coil, and an eight-year-old system in a neighbourhood where, in the customer's own words, a lot of people here been replacing theirs already. Not one of these three files contains a price, an option, a specialist offer or a customer's answer.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He followed rust all the way to the thermostat. It eats through metal… once it eats through that coil it's going to cause a leak… and the system can't keep it above 32 degrees anymore. Cause to consequence, in four sentences, no jargon.
  • He asked permission to deliver bad news. If anything is out of the ordinary, do you want me to let you guys know about that? — and got an emphatic yes that made every later finding welcome.
  • He gave the shape of the whole visit in advance — outside, review, attic, full picture — and then did exactly that.
  • He handled a tenant call correctly. With the homeowner off site: I'll call the homeowner, let them know about that. We don't do any work without his approval.
  • He named the readings and the ratings together so the customer could see the gap rather than take his word for it.
  • He looked after the household, not just the equipment — the dog let in out of the heat, the customer's dentist appointment worked around, the departure time confirmed.

Gap 3 — The teaching ends in a yes/no instead of a conclusion

Cole, after the walkthrough"Any questions on that? Did you learn anything from it?"
CustomerA short acknowledgement, and on to the next area.

The fix Did you learn anything? can be answered with a nod; the model asks for the customer to state the problem themselves. He has already done the hard part — the rust, the metal, the leak, the freezing point are all laid out. Close it with a question that requires an answer: So with that coil rusting through — what do you think happens the first time it springs a leak in August? When the customer says it, the urgency is theirs.

6

This week's drill — end every explanation with a question

Trigger and response

Trigger: the moment he finishes explaining a finding — the capacitor, the rust, the amp draw.

Response — replace "did you learn anything from it?" with:

"So knowing that — what do you think happens on the first 115-degree day?"

Then stop talking and let them answer. He is one sentence away from the hardest behavior in the rubric on every single call.
