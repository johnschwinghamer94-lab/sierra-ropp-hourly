1

The one-line verdict

Why they flip

He teaches the same chain every time, and customers follow it every time.

What actually happened

Rust eats metal, metal leaks, the system can't hold temperature, the coil ices. Cole delivers that sequence in the same order on call after call, showing the rust on video rather than describing it, and then asks the question that matters: do you have any questions or anything I can explain better?… did you learn anything from it?

Why it works

Consistency is the technique. Because the explanation is the same every time, it is tight, and because it ends in a comprehension check rather than a pitch, the customer arrives at the conclusion themselves. Two leads came off calls where the recorder stopped before any presentation — the teaching had already done the work.

5

What Cole did right — real strength, build on it

  • Two more leads, and a repeatable method behind them. The rust chain is delivered the same way every time, which is why it is tight and why customers follow it.
  • He shows rather than tells. Filming the coil closed and then open turns a claim into evidence the customer verified themselves.
  • He checks comprehension. Did you learn anything from it? is a better closing question than most reps' closing lines.
  • He adapts the script correctly for a tenant. Same sequence, homeowner substituted as the approver, nothing skipped.
  • He establishes neighbourhood context early — knowing that neighbours have already replaced systems gives every later finding somewhere to land.

The fix This has now moved from an oversight to a habit. Cole's teaching is the best-drilled on this board precisely because he says the same things in the same order — so add one line to the order: "What's your power bill running in the summer, and what was it in winter?" right after the tenure question.

Moderate

Gap 2 — the comprehension check stops one question short

ColeDo you have any questions or anything I can explain better? … Did you learn anything from it?
CustomerNo, no, it's not looking good. There's a lot going on.

The fix He is one question away from the hardest behaviour in the rubric. Instead of asking whether they learned it, ask them to use it: "So with that coil rusting the way it is — what do you think happens on the first hundred-and-ten-degree day?" When the customer says it, the urgency is theirs.

Moderate

Gap 3 — no review asked, on three calls that earned it

Cole, every welcomeAbove all, we're here to give you a five star experience. I'm not doing that, let me know so I can change it.
At the end of every recordingNo review request.

The fix He promises the standard on every call and never collects. On a preventative maintenance visit with a happy customer this is the easiest ask in the business: "I promised five stars at the start — if I got there, the survey text comes through and my name on it helps a lot."
