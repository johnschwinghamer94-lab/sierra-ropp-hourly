1

The one-line verdict

The day in one pattern

He is the most trustworthy voice on the board and he never asks for anything.

What he gives away

To a customer already committed to replacing: how to compare quotes honestly — make sure that you're using our quote as the standard for what they're changing out. There's companies a lot cheaper than us, but they won't pull permits, the city doesn't inspect it, so they don't make everything up to code — plus which tier to skip (don't go fancy, you're not staying here, don't do the multi stage), when to buy, and why. He refused to charge her: I'm not gonna charge you anything, I'm not looking at anything.

What he never does

Ask. No priced option reaches any customer on any of the three calls. No specialist is offered to the household who has decided to replace and is actively collecting quotes. And the mandatory power-bill follow-up — on a customer who told him she runs 65 at night and 72 all day — is never asked.

Draw the Picture
Strong

And he built a physical theory for a smell the customer had lived with for months: the lower you put your AC, the more the compressor outside works, and your coil gets colder and colder, so it'll start sweating more and more… all that dirt and debris, as it gets wet and then dries and wets, it starts getting that musty smell.

6

This week's drill — advice ends in an appointment

Trigger and response

Trigger: any customer who mentions replacing, comparing quotes, or "when it goes."

"Everything I just told you about permits and tiers only helps if you've got our number to measure the others against. Our specialist does that at no cost and no commitment — he's out this way Thursday, or is Friday easier?"

Then stop. He has already done the part most reps cannot do: he has made the customer trust him. The drill is finishing the sentence with a day in it.

Nikko protects customers better than anyone on this board — floor covers on at the door, work talked down rather than up, a free visit that ended with a household knowing how to spot a contractor who skips permits. The customer told him she was collecting quotes. All he had to do was make sure one of them was ours.
