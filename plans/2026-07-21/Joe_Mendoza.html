1

The one-line verdict

The day in one line

He gets the advisor moving when he asks — and he doesn't always ask.

What actually happened

On the three-system call he did it exactly right: named the compressor cost against a whole unit, then let me get Jordan out here while I write everything up. That way he gives you the information of what a new system costs, and then you'll have the information on the repairs, and then you can compare and contrast what makes the most sense. On the low-funds call he asked what the customer wanted and accepted I think we'll probably leave it alone for now without offering anything that costs nothing.

What it means

The Jordan handoff is the template and Joe already owns it. The gap is that he treats a customer's financial worry as a reason to stop rather than as a reason to send the free visit. "No funds" is the best possible argument for a no-cost quote and a payment plan, not against it.

5

What Joe did right — real strength, build on it

  • Two leads and a paid repair across eight visits. The volume is real and so is the conversion on the calls where he asks.
  • The Jordan handoff is textbook. Advisor dispatched while he writes the report, with the comparison framed before the customer has to ask for it.
  • He sold the membership by making it arithmetic. Warranty part, discounted labour, membership saving — laid out until the cheaper path was obviously the membership one.
  • The most humane call on the board. A customer a year into cancer treatment got patience, honesty about his own loss, and a technician who told him to sit down in the shade.
  • He refuses to sell where he isn't wanted. I don't want to be a financial burden on you — the instinct is right even where the execution stopped short.

The three gaps

each one in Joe's own calls
Weak

Gap 1 — the customer with no funds got nothing, including the free thing

CustomerI don't really have the funds for like a full replacement or anything like that.
JoeBy all means. And I'm not here to have that conversation by any means.
CustomerI think we'll probably leave it alone for now. — Joe: You want to leave it alone? Yeah. Okay. Yeah, no problem.

The fix Respecting a budget is right. Leaving without offering the one thing that costs nothing is not. "Totally — nothing today. Two things that don't cost you anything: our advisor can get you the real number so you're not guessing when it fails, and we have payment programmes that put it in the low hundreds a month. Want me to set that up for a day that suits you?" The customer with the least money is the one who most needs a plan.

Moderate

CustomerQuoted me about 20, 45, $30,000 for the new unit. Now I have a 2006 York unit.
CustomerI may need some R22. I know it's expensive, but I figured that that may be a better option than trying to go with the R438A or some substitute.
What the recording showsCareful, accurate discussion of the leak and the refrigerant — and no advisor offered before the tape ends.

The fix A customer who has already been quoted by a competitor and is researching refrigerants is shopping, not stalling. "Let's make sure you're comparing like for like — I'll get you our number at no cost so that quote you're holding has something to sit next to." That is the easiest advisor booking of the day.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
The free option always
Drill: Every customer who cites money hears the no-cost advisor and the payment programme before the visit ends. Ten reps.
Proof it worked: No tape where a funds objection ends without both offers.
Week 2
Match the competitor
Drill: Any customer holding another company's quote is offered our number at no cost, same visit.
Proof it worked: Advisor offered on every second-opinion call.
Week 3
Record the close
Drill: Recorder running through the report and presentation on every call.
Proof it worked: A presentation on tape for every gradeable call.

What we owe Joe Mendoza

  • Credit all three outcomes. Two leads and a paid repair across eight visits, almost none of it visible on tape.
  • Financing training, specifically. Joe's instinct is to protect people from cost; he needs to know that a payment plan is how you protect them.
  • Recognition for the cancer-recovery call. How he handled that conversation is worth more to this company than the ticket would have been.
  • Recording coverage. Six of eight tapes end before the outcome.
Bottom line

Eight visits, two leads, a repair sold and paid, and the most humane call anyone on this board ran. Joe's handoff — advisor out while he writes the report so the customer can compare — is the template, and he owns it. The gap is who he doesn't offer it to: the customer with no money and the customer already holding a competitor's quote both left with nothing, and both should have left with a free appointment. The free option is for exactly those two people.
