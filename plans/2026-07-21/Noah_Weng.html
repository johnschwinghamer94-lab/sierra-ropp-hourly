The one-line verdict

The day in one pattern

The most careful entry on the board, and no exit.

What is on tape

The complete six-beat block, run properly: I'll do my thorough evaluation. If I notice anything wrong, go ahead and build options… I'm going to my van to write my reports, then I'll go over everything with you guys. I don't do anything without your approval. If you guys do approve something, we can at least get the ball rolling today. Overall, I'm here for five star service, so if there's something you don't want me to do — please make me aware of it. Plus booties used as gloves to protect a new remodel, and a power-bill question almost nobody else asked.

What is not

A system that will not hold temperature and is getting worse — the hours that it won't hold temperature are getting longer and longer — on a house where the customer had already told him she is afraid of the price. Four systems on another property whose owner said he might move every property he manages over to Sierra. And no findings review, no option, no price, no specialist on any of the three recordings.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He stopped at the door because of what was on his boots. Last night I was on a roof with a bunch of pigeons, and I don't want to bring that in your house. Nobody else on this board thought about the house before the equipment.
  • He improvised protection for a customer's new finishes — booties on his hands — on a remodel where the owner's stated worry was exactly that.
  • He asked what the power bill actually costs. One of only two people on this board to ask the mandatory follow-up at all.
  • He gave an honest time estimate — it could take me up to four hours — rather than a comfortable one, to a customer who had asked him to be quick.
  • He corrected the owner's mental model of his own building before starting: one furnace per outdoor unit, four of each, all mapped.
  • He priced the filters plainly and explained the interval rather than dodging a number he knew sounded high.

6

This week's drill — the review is a conversation, not a report

After every evaluation, before the van

"Before I write anything up — let me show you what I found. Here's the immediate, here's what it costs, here's what I'd leave for later, and here's the option if you'd rather compare against a new system. Then I'll put it all in the report."

He already promises this at the door on two calls out of three. The drill is doing it in the room, on tape, before the paperwork — and asking for the review on the way out.

Noah is the most considerate technician on this board — he stopped at a threshold over pigeon dust, wore booties on his hands to protect someone's new finishes, gave an honest four-hour estimate to a customer who wanted him gone, and asked the power-bill question almost nobody asks. What is entirely missing is the second half of every visit. A customer said she was afraid of the price; a property manager offered him every building he owns. Neither got an answer on tape, and both were his to take.
