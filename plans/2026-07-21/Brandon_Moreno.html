The one-line verdict

The pattern

He names the risk before it fails. Then the tape runs out.

What actually happened

On the tune-up he measured a compressor within a fraction of its maximum and told the customer immediately: this thing is literally on the edge of going out. Want to make sure you guys are fully aware of this before we continue the rest of the maintenance. On the zone call he checked warranty status unprompted and organised a colleague to follow up. On the third, the recording ends during the walk-through.

What it means

Brandon's instinct — flag it before it breaks, while the customer can still choose — is exactly right, and it produced a lead on one of three. What is missing on the others is the second half of the sentence. A compressor riding on its limit is an Option C conversation, today, at no cost, not a warning to be remembered later.

5

What Brandon did right — real strength, build on it

  • A confirmed lead off the zone call. Warranty checked, full assessment promised, colleague lined up — and a real opportunity on the board.
  • He says the dangerous thing early. Interrupting a routine maintenance to flag a compressor at its limit is the behaviour that separates a tune-up from a service call.
  • He reads the numbers out loud against the data plate. The customer can check the arithmetic, which makes the conclusion theirs as much as his.
  • Complete expectation-setting on every call, including the time-constraint question most reps skip.
  • He audits our own previous work. Asking whether a repair held and how the service was is how a company finds out what's actually happening.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Attach the offer
Drill: Every serious finding is followed within a minute by a no-cost advisor offer and a proposed time. Ten reps on compressor-at-limit scenarios.
Proof it worked: No tape where a major finding is stated with no route attached.
Week 2
The bill question
Drill: Power bill asked in the first ten minutes on every call and written on the report.
Proof it worked: Customer's own figure on every recording.
Week 3
Record the close
Drill: Recorder confirmed running through the report and presentation on every visit.
Proof it worked: A presentation on tape for every gradeable call.

What we owe Brandon Moreno

  • Credit the lead. One of three converted and none of it is on tape. He should know it counted.
  • Permission to offer, again. Same note as his previous graded day: he treats naming the risk as the whole job. Somebody needs to tell him the offer is part of the service.
  • Recording coverage. Three fragments against three full visits.

Bottom line

Brandon caught a compressor a hair from its maximum and told the customer straight away, before finishing the maintenance — that is exactly the instinct we want. One of his three calls produced a real lead. What is still missing is the second sentence: the free advisor visit, offered with a day attached, so that a warning becomes a plan. Add the power-bill question and get the recorder running to the end of the call, and the coaching gets much sharper.
