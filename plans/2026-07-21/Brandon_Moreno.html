The one-line verdict

The day in one pattern

The front door is a training standard. Nothing after it exists on tape.

What is recorded

A complete expectations block at two of three doors, delivered naturally: we're gonna check on everything — cleanliness, performance, see how everything's running. If we see anything that looks out of the norm, we'd like to let you know… I won't do any work without your approval… and we do all this for a five star experience. If you feel from like it, let me know. Plus one genuinely excellent check-in: the run-load amps read against the plate, out loud, before the wash.

What is not

A compressor literally riding right on the edge on a maintenance call, a failing zone in a master bedroom, two systems on another property — and not one recording contains a finding delivered, a price stated, an option built or a customer's answer. His close rate reads zero because there is no tape of anything that could have closed.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He stopped the maintenance to tell the customer what he had found. I want to make sure you guys are fully aware of this before we continue. That is the check-in behaving exactly as the model intends, and it is rare.
  • He read the meter against the plate out loud — 19.5 against a 20.8 maximum — so the customer could see the gap rather than take his word for it.
  • He checked the warranty before promising anything. I did look briefly as far as the warranties on this guy. It is out of warranty.
  • He asks about time constraints at every door. Nobody else on the board does this consistently, and it is why none of his visits felt like an imposition.
  • He identified the second decision-maker before leaving the first one — the son who would be handling the rest of the visit.
  • He brought floor covers and offered them. One of two reps on this board for whom floor protection appears on tape at all.

6

This week's drill — finish the check-in with a question

Trigger and response

Trigger: any moment he shows a customer a reading, a photo or a part.

Response — the last thing out of his mouth is a question, not a verdict:

"Rated maximum is 20.8. Today it's pulling 19.5. What do you make of that?"

Then stop and let them answer. He already earns the moment — the meter, the plate, the pause before continuing. The drill is refusing to supply the conclusion himself.

3-week training plan

Week 1
Record the whole call
Knock to truck, every visit. Nothing in the Decision step can be coached until findings, options and answers exist on tape.
Week 2
The four required questions
Filters, rooms, thermostat setting and the power-bill follow-up, asked at the counter before any equipment is touched.
Week 3
Hand over the diagnosis
Every check-in ends with a question to the customer, and the five-star ask is made at the end of every visit he has already seeded.
