The one-line verdict

Why two didn't convert

He agrees that the customer should know the number, then doesn't get it for them.

What actually happened

The nine-year-old-system customer volunteered his whole history — a company that jacked me around, a unit replaced under pressure in 2017, and a lingering worry: I sure don't need a unit or anything, but that's going to be in my mind too, down the road. Benjamin's answer was right — it's always good to get the number at least what you're up against — and then the conversation moved to filters and the roof.

What it was

A customer who says the future replacement is on his mind is asking to be prepared. Agreeing with him costs nothing and delivers nothing. "Then let's get you that number — our advisor comes at no cost and you'll know exactly where you stand." The sentence he needed was one clause longer than the one he said.

What Benjamin did right — real strength, build on it

  • He listens through a long grievance. Nine years of history about another company, uninterrupted, and no defensiveness.

  • The membership pitch is complete and honest — two visits, plumbing, emergency, repair discount, monthly price — and delivered without pressure.

Benjamin's opening and his instinct to show customers their own equipment remain the best on this board, and one call converted on exactly that. He does not need to persuade anyone. He needs to finish the sentence he is already saying.
