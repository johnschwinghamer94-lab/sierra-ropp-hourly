The one-line verdict

Why nothing flipped

He was asked to compete and he endorsed the other quote instead.

What actually happened

The customer had fallen, her regular technician was gone, and another company had charged her for a thermostat and quoted around a thousand dollars for a warranty repair. She asked Sierra for a view. Juan offered to evaluate — I can show you what our prices are, but I would have to run the evaluation. Is that something you want me to do? — and when she hesitated, closed it out: our prices are kind of similar to these… like I said, pretty fairly priced.

What it needed

Being honest about a fair competitor price is right. Leaving without running an evaluation is not — because without one we have no number, no findings and no relationship. "Let me do the full evaluation at no charge — then you'll have two real numbers instead of my opinion of theirs." The fee was waived anyway; the evaluation should have been the thing we gave away.

What Juan did right — real strength, build on it

  • He was honest about a competitor's price. Telling a customer another company is fairly priced is integrity, and she trusted him for it.
  • He was gentle with a customer recovering from a fall who had lost the technician she relied on.
  • He read the warranty position correctly — parts covered, labour charged — and explained it clearly.
  • He waived the fee without being asked, and refused the customer's offer to pay anyway.

The fix The pattern is not about technique — the diagnostics and teaching are consistently good. It is a single missing habit at a single moment. Every call ends with an evaluation performed, a written option set, and a proposed time. That sentence needs to be a rule, not a coaching note.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Always evaluate
Drill: No visit ends without a full evaluation, even a price check or second opinion. The fee is waived, not the work.
Proof it worked: An evaluation performed on every visit.
Week 2
Answer the purchase request
Drill: Any stated want — a thermostat, a filter upgrade — is priced on the spot.
Proof it worked: No tape where a purchase intent goes unpriced.
Week 3
Never leave without an ask
Drill: Written option set and a proposed time on every gradeable call.
Proof it worked: A report and an ask on every call.
8

What we owe Juan Tlatenchi

  • A direct conversation, not a fifth gap list. Five graded days, strong diagnostics, zero leads. This needs a manager and a plan, not another note.
  • A second-opinion policy. When a customer asks us to compare, we should always run the evaluation free — that has to be a rule he can lean on.
  • Credit the integrity. He refused to trash a competitor and refused to charge for a visit he felt he hadn't earned. That instinct is worth keeping.
  • Clarity on colleague conversation during visits. Second graded day with substantial off-topic recording.
Bottom line

A customer with another company's quote in her hand asked Sierra to help her, and Juan told her the other quote was fair, declined to run an evaluation, waived our fee and left. She also said she wanted a programmable thermostat and nobody priced one. The honesty is real and it is not the problem. Five graded days of strong diagnostics and no leads is now a pattern that needs a manager, a rule and a follow-up call to that customer.
