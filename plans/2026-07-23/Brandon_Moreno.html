The one-line verdict

Why both flipped

He put the numbers on paper and then put the appointment in a window.

ARDIZZONE — the full model

Immediates priced with an honest warning attached: I'm not confident in these as far as 100% solving the concerns… you're already at $2,030 for that, and that's everything all in, no hidden fees. Futures named: when this does fail, cost on a compressor replace could be around $6,000. Then the third path, framed as comparison: this is to speak with an equipment specialist… that one would be a no cost option. Customer: I would like to try number three. Booked to a four-to-six window around the wife's return, signed, and confirmed twice.

What is still missing

The filter question and the rooms question, absent from all four calls again. He asked the power bill on the call that mattered — what's your usual power bill during the summer? → I think right now our bill is like 109 — and then, correctly, did not use it, because that bill was low. The other two required questions would have told him more.

Draw the Picture
Strong
The best teaching on this board this day, and he draws it out on paper first: have they ever drawn out the system for you guys? Then the compressor against its plate, with a video: this component is rated at 79. We're pulling 88… it's going to operate, but it does have the potential to lock up. Then the control board, in a picture the customer can check herself: it's supposed to have a nice tan color, and this thing's been getting roasted. And the voltage switch, with both failure modes named: it could either get welded stuck on where the unit runs continuously, or it'll just not even turn on. On the two-system house he made it a comparison: 67 is the max on your downstairs, we're at 63. Upstairs, 123 — when our max is 113.
Check Your Tone
Strong
Relentlessly honest in both directions. He refused to condemn a working part — I don't want to jump to conclusions and say something's bad right away if it hasn't failed there and then — and refused to oversell a fix: I don't want to blow smoke and say this thing's gonna be a trooper after that. He also told a customer plainly that Sierra does not work with home warranties and left without pushing anything.

This week's drill — the two questions before the ladder

Add to the setting question he already asks

"And how often are the filters getting changed? Which rooms give you the most trouble when it's struggling?"

Fifteen seconds. On a two-system house they tell him which unit the household actually depends on, and on a failing one they tell him what the customer loses when it stops. Everything else in his evaluation is already best on the board.
