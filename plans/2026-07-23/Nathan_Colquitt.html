1

The one-line verdict

Why it didn't flip

The most demanding customer of the week trusted him completely, and he offered her nothing to say yes to.

What actually happened

She set the terms — treat me like your great grandmother. Not one ounce of BS — and pushed for detail on everything: the compressor price, the filter tiers, whether waiting until failure changes the cost. Nathan answered all of it straight, including the unhelpful truth: if I wait until it fails, is there any difference in the price? Honestly, no. Then the visit ended with the numbers on the page and nothing proposed.

What it needed

A customer that engaged, that demanding and that satisfied is the easiest close on this board. She asked for tier pricing on filters — an immediate, small, willing purchase — and for the compressor number, which is the classic Option C trigger. Either one, asked for plainly, converts. Neither was.

5

What Nathan did right — real strength, build on it

  • His first complete presentation on tape, and it is good. Priced, forecast, explained and honest — the content is not the problem.
  • He honoured "not one ounce of BS" completely, including telling her that waiting costs the same, which was true and unhelpful to him.
  • He brought the filter down so she could look at it. Physical evidence with a sceptical customer beats any photograph.
  • He answered the filter-effectiveness challenge with real cases rather than a claim, which is why she asked for tier pricing.
  • He moved his truck for the bin collection before doing anything else. Small, and it set the tone for a hundred minutes.

The fix That is a customer asking to buy something small and immediate on a visit where the big number was never going to close today. "The charcoal one is the tier I'd put in your house — want me to fit it now?" A hundred-minute visit with a delighted customer should not end with zero transactions.

Weak

Gap 2 — the honest answer became the last word

CustomerIf I wait until it fails, the compressor fails — is there any difference in the price?
NathanHonestly, no.
What was not addedAnything about being without cooling in August, about parts availability, or about getting a replacement number now at no cost.

The fix The honesty is right and it must not be the end of the sentence. "Honestly, no — the price is the same. What changes is that you'd be deciding in a heatwave with no cooling. So let's get you the replacement number now, at no cost, and then whenever it goes you're just making a phone call." Same truth, with a door left open.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Close the small thing
Drill: Any customer asking for pricing on a small item gets a recommendation and an offer to fit it today. Ten reps.
Proof it worked: No tape where a purchase request goes unsold.
Week 2
Never end on the honest no
Drill: Every truthful answer that removes urgency is followed by the free option. Five reps.
Proof it worked: An offer follows every neutral answer on tape.
Week 3
Option C on big forecasts
Drill: Any forecast repair total above the cost of a system triggers a no-cost replacement quote offer.
Proof it worked: Option C offered on every qualifying call.

What we owe Nathan Colquitt

  • Credit the recording. His first full presentation on tape across four graded days — that alone makes this the most useful call we have from him.
  • Credit the honesty and then fix the ending. He told a demanding customer the truth at his own expense and deserves to be told that was right.
  • A follow-up on the filter. She asked for prices. Someone should get them to her.
  • An Option C trigger tied to forecast totals. He builds the case perfectly and has nothing to attach to it.
Bottom line

Nathan finally got a full presentation on tape, and the content is genuinely strong: a compressor priced honestly, future components forecast, filter tiers explained, and a customer who demanded complete honesty and received it — including the answer that waiting costs no more, which was true and cost him the sale. She asked for filter prices and nobody sold her a filter. Fourteen thousand dollars of forecast repairs and nobody offered a free replacement number. He has earned the right to ask. He just has to ask.
