1

The one-line verdict

The call that defines the day

Told to his face that a cheaper competitor was on the way, he answered with a slogan instead of a strategy.

What he did

The diagnosis was excellent and shown live, not just written. He walked the customer through the compressor amp draw — you can see that it's not barely running… the amps on startup are 82. That one was reading at 96, so it's above the amount of power it should be getting — the dead start capacitor — this one reads that it should be 45 plus 5, and it's reading 1 and then 0 on the other side. So that one is pretty much completely out — and then the contactor, explained as a light switch with two ways to fail: it can weld shut… so basically your unit will just run and run and run. The other sign is a coil behind it — if that coil goes out, it just won't close at all. He also ran the full expectations block at every door of the day, five-star standard included.

What he did not do

Compete. The customer told him plainly what he was walking into: the guy that's coming has installed three units for me in my other houses… he's a low-value guy as far as cost. Your company will be competing against a good number. The whole answer was we do things the right way, so we're gonna pull permits and everything — and the customer immediately closed that door too: he does the same thing. He installs them in new homes all over the place. Nothing followed. No difference named, no timeline advantage, no offer to be there when the other quote landed, no request for a chance to respond to it.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • The complete expectations block at every door, five-star standard and approval promise included, almost word for word. Nobody else on this board runs it this consistently.
  • He gave honest, uncomfortable time estimates — three hours on a two-system maintenance — instead of a comfortable one.
  • He taught thermostat staggering unprompted on a house he was not selling anything to, and stayed with it until the customer said it made sense.
  • He stopped a customer closing vents and explained the damage it does.
  • He read the meters out loud with the customer rather than reporting conclusions from the truck.
  • He explained the contactor's two failure modes in language a homeowner can repeat back.
  • He refused to knock the competitor even when the customer opened the door to it.

This week's drill — when a competitor is already booked

"Good — you should get two numbers. Here's what I'll do: I've got the readings in front of me right now, so before I leave you'll have a repair option and a replacement option in writing. When his quote comes in, call me and I'll tell you honestly which is the better deal for you. That way you're comparing like for like."
