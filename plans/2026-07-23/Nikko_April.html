1

The one-line verdict

What changed

He stopped explaining and started offering.

What actually happened

On the frozen-system call he offered rather than described: if you're worried about it causing ceiling damage, I could watch it thaw out, then the membership laid out concretely — coil wash, heating check, gas-leak inspection, and an emergency visit at no charge instead of the diagnostic fee. It was signed and paid before he left.

Why that matters

On his previous graded days the recurring note was that customers received information and no recommendation. This day he made three offers and two landed. The diagnostics were never the problem — and this is what it looks like when the offer is attached to them.

5

What Nikko did right — real strength, build on it

  • A membership sold and paid on site. The recurring note across two graded days was that he never asks. This day he asked and got a yes.
  • He caught a warranty contractor cutting corners — a burnt disconnect box left on a unit replaced within the year — and documented it so the family can force a return.
  • He told the customer to move fast before the contractor could argue the work was out of period. That is advocacy, not selling.
  • He offered to stay while a coil thawed to protect the customer's ceiling. Nobody asked him to.
  • He apologised for a missed courtesy call that was not his fault, and logged it with the office.

The three gaps

each one in Nikko's own calls
Weak

Gap 1 — the warranty call produced advocacy and no opportunity

NikkoThey would have to replace the whole box. It's burned up inside there too.
NikkoI wouldn't start getting the ball rolling because they're going to try to push that appointment out. So then you're out of coverage.
What was not offeredA price to do the work ourselves if the warranty company refuses, or a membership on a household clearly ill-served by its current arrangement.

The fix Sending her back to the warranty company was right. Leaving without a fallback was not. "If they stall or refuse, here's what it costs for us to just do it — and here's the membership, so this doesn't keep happening." Advocacy and an offer are not in conflict.

Moderate

Gap 3 — the welcome still has no approval promise

PresentThe warranty check, the report and the whole-system policy.
Still missing across three graded daysThe approval promise and the goal question.

The fix The approval promise is the one line that reassures a customer nothing will be done or charged without them, and he is selling memberships without it. "I won't do anything without your approval, and if you approve it we can start today. What would make today a win for you?"

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Advocacy plus an offer
Drill: Every warranty or third-party recommendation is paired with our own price and the membership. Five reps.
Proof it worked: An offer accompanies every advocacy recommendation.
Week 2
Bill question fixed
Drill: Power bill asked immediately after the original-owner question, every call.
Proof it worked: Customer's own figure on every recording.
Week 3
Approval promise
Drill: The approval line and goal question added to every welcome.
Proof it worked: Both beats on every recording.
