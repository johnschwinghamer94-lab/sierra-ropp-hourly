1

The one-line verdict

The call that defines the day

He could not fix the system, so he sold the relationship instead — and got the signature on tape.

What he did

A frozen coil on a first-time customer means no repair today and a return visit — the easiest call on the board to leave flat. He did the opposite. He taped and angled the drip so it would not damage the ceiling while it thawed, photographed the coil so the return visit could compare, took the unit's power down so it would not refreeze, and arranged the afternoon return before he left. Then he built the membership on top of the education he had already given: this is on our membership. It's not a bad idea to wash those coils out once a year… it's nice having that peace of mind. And it's only $16 a month. You don't pay anything else for our visits when we come out. He then made the arithmetic unmissable: you can either pay the 39 service fee, or if you wanted to sign up you could pay the 16 → if you sign up, you get the 39 waived. He said the quiet part honestly — I'm not trying to get you to sign up for it — confirmed consent cleanly (you want to do the membership, right? → Yes), disclosed the twelve-month term twice, took the signature, and closed the loop: let me remove this service fee and put the free service fee, because you're signed up.

What he did not do

Build a repair path. The airflow fault is still undiagnosed, and there is no priced option, no good-better-best, and no expectation set with the customer about what the afternoon visit might cost. On the second call he uncovered a burnt disconnect box that will kill a compressor — it's all black in there… you're lucky this thing didn't catch fire — and quoted nothing at all, sending it entirely to a home-warranty company. No review was asked on either call.

This week's drill — always leave a Sierra option next to the free one

When the right answer is a warranty claim, a return visit, or someone else's job

"Do that first — it should cost you nothing. But so you're not stuck waiting on them, here's what it costs to have us handle it and here's how fast we can be here. If they come through, great. If they stall, you've already got a plan."

He is already the most trusted voice in the room on both these calls. This one sentence turns that trust into an option the customer can actually take.
