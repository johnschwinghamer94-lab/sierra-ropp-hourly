The one-line verdict

The pattern

He measures the failure precisely and stops one option short.

What actually happened

On the maintenance call: your compressor is rated for 134 amps max… yours is currently drawing in 164.3. So it's way over that threshold. That lets me know that that compressor is on its last leg. He then explained honestly that parts are hard to source since the refrigerant change — and the visit ended without an advisor, an option set or an appointment.

What that call needed

A compressor thirty amps over its maximum on a system whose parts are getting scarce is the textbook Option C. "Parts are getting hard to find, so let's get you a replacement number now while nothing is broken — no cost, no obligation." The measurement did all the work; the offer never came.

5

What Joe did right — real strength, build on it

  • He fixed our own mistake without blaming anyone. Install crew rebooked the same day, apology given, extra request captured — that is how a company keeps a customer it has already let down.
  • He asks the motive question and the bill question. Comfort or cost, and what it actually costs — two questions, and the whole value case follows from them.
  • The shorted-winding explanation makes a dead system make sense in two sentences.
  • He pre-empts the obvious objection by explaining that compressor cost tracks refrigerant volume, not system size.
  • He captured an upsell request properly — an additional thermostat — and routed it rather than losing it.

Gap 1 — a compressor thirty amps over, and nothing offered

JoeYour compressor is rated for 134 amps max… yours is currently drawing in 164.3. That lets me know that that compressor is on its last leg.
JoeBecause of the refrigerant change that happened two years ago, getting parts for these units gets really difficult.
What was not offeredAn option set, an advisor, or any appointment.

The fix He built the entire case — failing part, scarce replacement, unknown timing — and stopped. "So while it's still running, let's get you the replacement number at no cost. Then when it goes, you're choosing instead of scrambling." Parts scarcity is the strongest Option C argument available and it was left on the floor.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Scarcity is Option C
Drill: Any parts-availability problem is converted into a no-cost replacement quote offer. Ten reps.
Proof it worked: An offer follows every parts-scarcity statement on tape.
Week 2
Price what they ask for
Drill: Any customer request for additional work is priced or scheduled the same visit.
Proof it worked: No tape where a purchase request ends with a referral and no date.
Week 3
Ask for the review
Drill: Review requested at the door on every completed visit.
Proof it worked: Review asked on every call.

  • An Option C trigger for parts scarcity. He explains it accurately and has no next move attached to it.

Bottom line

Joe took a customer our own install team had let down, apologised, rebooked the crew and captured an extra request — that call is worth more than a lead. Two of his four visits converted. The one that stings is the maintenance call where he measured a compressor thirty amps over its rated draw, explained that parts are now scarce, and offered nothing at all. He built the whole argument and never asked the question at the end of it.
