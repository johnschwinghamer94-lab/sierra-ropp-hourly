1

The one-line verdict

What changed

He finally asked what they pay. Now he needs to use the answer.

What actually happened

On two calls the question appeared for the first time: what are your guys's electric bills looking like in the summertime? and, on the long visit, the power bill established at a couple of hundred a month. On that same call he sold the membership properly — these repairs, the cost that I quoted you is 15% less than everybody else's… when it does fail, we come to you first — and the customer agreed to bring the paperwork the next day.

What's still missing

The bill figures were collected and never spent. On a three-and-a-half-hour visit with a manufacturer warranty, a home warranty and a two-hundred-dollar monthly bill in play, no advisor was offered and no replacement number was ever put on the table. The discovery gap is closing; the ask gap is not.

5

What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • He asked the power-bill question. Three graded days of the same coaching note, and it appeared on two calls this day.
  • He sold the membership on arithmetic, not sentiment. Discounted repairs, priority scheduling, a monthly figure named plainly — and the customer agreed.
  • He teaches first-time homeowners properly. Promising to explain how the system works and why filters matter is how a nervous customer becomes a long-term one.
  • He untangled two warranties — manufacturer versus home — for a customer who had been confusing them for a year.
6

The three gaps

each one in Andrew's own calls
Weak

Gap 1 — three and a half hours, two warranties, and no replacement number

On tapeA two-system home, a manufacturer parts warranty, a separate home warranty, and a monthly power bill in the low hundreds.
What was offeredThe membership.
What was not offeredAny repair-versus-replacement comparison or advisor visit.

The fix The longest visit of his week produced a sixteen-dollar-a-month agreement. With warranties expiring and a bill already established, the second half of the conversation was available and never happened: "Let's also get you a replacement number at no cost, so when that parts warranty runs out you already know where you stand."

Moderate

Gap 2 — the bill answers were collected and never used

AndrewWhat are your guys's electric bills looking like in the summertime?
CustomerI think it's only 200. Two hundred every month, for the whole year — the equal pay.
What followedThe maintenance continued; the figure was never mentioned again.

The fix He has closed the discovery gap and opened a usage gap. Say the number back inside the findings: you're on equal pay at two hundred a month, and part of that is this system working harder than it should. A number gathered and unused is the same as a number never asked for.

Moderate

Gap 3 — no review asked on a visit that ran three and a half hours

Customer, at the closeThank you. Thank you again for your service, man.
AndrewI look forward to seeing you next week.
Not saidAny request for a review.

The fix A customer thanking him twice at the door is the review ask writing itself. "If today was five stars, the survey text comes through shortly — my name on it means a lot."

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Spend the number
Drill: Every bill figure gathered is quoted back inside the findings conversation.
Proof it worked: Customer's own figure referenced on every recording where it was asked.
Week 2
Always the third option
Drill: Repair, future and advisor on every call with equipment concerns, regardless of warranty status.
Proof it worked: Option C offered on every gradeable call.
Week 3
Ask for the review
Drill: Review requested at the door on every completed visit.
Proof it worked: Review asked on every call.
8

What we owe Andrew Alonso

  • Credit the bill question. It was the standing note for three graded days and he closed it.
  • A warranty-expiry play. Customers with parts warranties running out are the easiest advisor bookings we have, and he had one and did not use it.
  • Recognition for the membership sale. He explained it in terms of money rather than loyalty and it worked.
  • Confirm the membership paperwork arrived. The customer promised to bring it the next day.
Bottom line

Andrew finally asked what these households pay for power — the note he has carried for three graded days — and sold a membership by making the discount arithmetic obvious. What he has not yet done is turn any of it into a replacement conversation. Three and a half hours in a house with two warranties, an aging system and a known monthly bill, and nobody was offered a number. The discovery is fixed. The ask is next.
