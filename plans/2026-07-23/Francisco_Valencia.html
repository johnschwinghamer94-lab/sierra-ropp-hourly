1

The one-line verdict

The day in one call

He did everything right except put a date on it.

What he did

Established in three minutes that a system which has not run for weeks cannot be making a noise: I think in your case we're better off sending a plumber than an HVAC [tech]. If your system is not running, there's nothing that can make that noise. Then he checked anyway, for free — let me go up there and just see if I can hear the noise… that way I don't have to charge you a service fee — proposed the right fix (an early appointment when the noise happens, and a recording from the daughter), and corrected the customer's belief that she had bought a new system rather than repairs, using her own invoice history.

What happened next

Well, I'm gonna wait on that. Let me wait on that… I'm not ready for that yet. He accepted it, noted it and left. A customer who has been woken by the same noise every morning for months, and who was told once already by the office to wait and see, was allowed to wait again.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He diagnosed the call correctly in three minutes by working the timeline rather than the equipment: a system that has not run cannot be making the noise.
  • He refused to charge a service fee to check something he suspected was not his department.
  • He corrected a false belief using her own records. You did not buy a new air conditioner. You bought the repairs.
  • He contradicted a customer without dismissing her. I'm not saying I don't believe you.
  • He praised the other customer's own judgement for shutting a failing unit down: that's the smartest thing to do.
  • He arranged the absent decision-maker's availability before starting work, and took her number so he could call from the property.

Gap 1 — He documented the delay instead of ending it

Customer, who has been told to wait once already"I called some time ago and she told me, well, just wait and see. Maybe it'll stop. It never stopped… I woke up this morning, I said, I can't take it anymore."
Francisco, at the end"So I'll put that on the notes and I'll tell the girls, that way they're not bugging you with trying to reschedule it."

The fix This customer is not undecided — she is exhausted and has already been let down by a wait-and-see. The right move is to make the next step happen without her having to initiate it again: Let's not leave this to another phone call. I'll book you first thing Thursday. If the noise has stopped by then, ring me and I'll cancel it — but you shouldn't have to be the one chasing this a third time. Making it easy to keep waiting is the one thing she did not need.

6

This week's drill — book it, then let them cancel

When a customer says "let me wait"

"Understood — but let's not make you chase this again. I'll put you on the books for Thursday morning. If it's stopped by then, call me and I'll cancel it, no charge either way. That way the worst case is you get a free visit you didn't need."

The default becomes the appointment rather than the delay, and the customer keeps every bit of control she had before.
