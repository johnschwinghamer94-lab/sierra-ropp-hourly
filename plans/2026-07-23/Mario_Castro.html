1

The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

Two failing compressors, one at a time, and a plan the customer could hold.

What actually happened

He named both problems and then refused to overwhelm: they're both kind of dying, essentially. But my thing is one fire at a time. Let's see what it takes to take care of the upstairs first, and then we'll put our attention after to the other unit. The measurements were read out against the data plates — one rated at seventy-nine drawing ninety-five, the other rated at sixty-seven drawing seventy.

Why that works

A household with two young children and two failing compressors could easily hear that as a catastrophe. Sequencing it — one fire at a time — turns an overwhelming diagnosis into a plan, and a plan is something a customer can say yes to.

5

What Mario did right — real strength, build on it

  • The heart-and-blood-pressure image. One analogy that carries two compressors and their measured excess draw without a single technical term.
  • One fire at a time. Sequencing two failures into a plan is why a household with two toddlers and two dying compressors did not simply freeze.
  • He reads both numbers aloud. Rated and measured, on each unit, so the customer does the comparison themselves.
  • He connected parts scarcity to warranty status, which is the fact that actually determines this customer's options as a second owner.
  • He refused to guess at pricing and said he would check the price book instead.
