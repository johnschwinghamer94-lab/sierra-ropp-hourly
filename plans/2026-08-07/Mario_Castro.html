The one-line verdict

Why the options phase stays thin

He books the conversation into a phone call.

What actually happened

On the Miller job the homeowner was standing in front of him and he still framed the ending as a call: Once I'm done, I'll let her know. I'll get in touch with her. I'll let her know. I'll let her decide what she wants to do. And we'll just go from there. On Gibbs the expectations sequence stops before approval, same-day and the five-star seed.

What the FSG model calls for

A phone call is where options go to get shortened. Set the ending up as a sit-down from the start: when I'm done I'll write it up and then I'd like ten minutes at the table with you to walk through what I found and what your choices are. That sentence, said at minute two, is what makes the options conversation possible at minute forty.

What he did right — real strength, build on it

  • He makes the customer choose the check-in. If anything's out of specs, what would you like me to do with that information? → Tell me. That single question converts a check-in from something he imposes into something they requested, and it is the cleanest version of the behaviour on the board.
  • He finds the personal connection fast and it is genuine. Two questions in and he had the customer's father and grandfather, both Army, both World Wars. On a six-minute call.
  • The filter finding. Free, immediate, and it fixed a real airflow restriction on a house the customer thought had been fully inspected: You don't want filters on the vents and on the unit... because it restricts airflow.
  • He checks whether the setpoint is actually working. Not just what do you keep it at but Are you comfortable? Is that going to help out what you put? That second question is where the real answers live.
  • He verifies the contact details out loud. I'll just verify right now... Is it 805-45-11914? Small, and it is why the follow-up actually reaches the decision-maker.

6

This week’s drill — word for word

Book the table, not the phone call

Trigger: minute two of every visit, while setting expectations.

Put the options conversation on the calendar before you start:

“Here's how today ends: I'll finish the evaluation, step out and write it up, and then I'd like about ten minutes with you at the table so I can show you what I found and walk you through your options. Nothing gets done without your approval, and anything you do approve we can start today. Does ten minutes at the end work?”

Ask for the time up front and the presentation happens. Leave it to the end and it becomes a phone call.
