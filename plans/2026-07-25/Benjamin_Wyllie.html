1

The one-line verdict

The line that costs him

"I'm just the repair guy. I don't know what new systems cost."

What actually happened

He says a version of this on three separate calls, and on one he goes further: they hide all that information from the repair guys. That's a whole different department. It arrives at the exact moment a customer has asked him what a replacement costs — the moment his authority matters most.

What the FSG model calls for

The routing is right; the framing is not. The install specialist does not price the job because the information is withheld from Ben — he prices it because the system has to be measured for that specific home. Ben actually says this correctly once: they have to get that pricing and the sizing to make sure it's correct so it fits your home specifically. That version keeps his authority intact and sells the handoff. The other version tells the customer we do not trust our own technicians with a number.

4

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • <b>The most complete expectations block on the board, and the only consistent five-star seed.</b> I do this all for a five star experience. If at any point I'm not doing that, please correct me on it.
  • <b>He draws the system out and asks permission first.</b> Do you mind if I draw out the system for you to kind of explain how it all works together? Is that okay? That reconnect is why his prices land instead of shocking.
  • <b>Prices itemised, never bundled, never softened.</b> $1,277 for the fan motor, $509 for that voltage switch — and when a customer said it was a lot, he agreed instead of arguing.
  • <b>Clear on compliance, without preaching.</b> Refused a refrigerant top-up on a leaking system and explained why in plain terms: I'd lose my license. The company would get fined. Also shut down a side-deal a previous contractor had offered.
  • <b>Checks who actually decides.</b> Do I need to talk to anybody else about that? and, from the account notes, the homeowner's son is in Florida but you'll be in charge of everything, plus payments — is that right?

Gap 1 — He tells customers the company keeps prices from him

Customer"How much would it be just to replace the whole system?"
Ben"I'm just a repair guy. I don't know what new systems cost."
Ben, on another call"They hide all that information from the repair guys. That's a whole different department."

The fix Route to the specialist without discrediting yourself. Ben already has the right sentence and used it once: they have to get that pricing and the sizing to make sure it's correct so it fits your home specifically. Say that version every time — the specialist prices it because the equipment has to be measured for the house, not because we hide numbers from our own technicians. Then go straight to the ask: he can be out today. Morning or afternoon? Two of seven flipped; this line is sitting on most of the other five.

Weak

Gap 2 — An overpayment percentage on three calls, and the bill question on none

Ben"I did an energy analysis of the system... you are overpaying every single month on your utility bills by 15% just to keep the system running."
Ben, another call"you're actually overpaying by about 45% on your utility bills every single month."
Customer, immediately after"We do have solar." — Ben: "So it just kind of affects the kilowatt per hour at that point. So if you didn't have solar..."

The fix That number walked itself back in front of the customer, and it did so because the question that should have come first was never asked. What do you keep it set at in the summer — and what does a standard power bill look like for you at that? takes ten seconds, tells you whether an efficiency argument even applies, and puts the figure in the customer's mouth instead of yours. Ask it on every HVAC call before the analysis is ever mentioned.

Weak

Gap 3 — Seven five-star promises, zero five-star asks

Ben, in every expectations block"I do this all for a five star experience. If at any point I'm not doing that, please correct me on it."
One customer, unprompted"Great team. Great experience. Need to do a review on that." — and no review was requested.

The fix He is the only rep on this board who reliably makes the promise, and the only thing he never does is collect on it. On the call where the customer volunteered that she owed us a review, the ask was a single sentence away. Say it while you are packing up: I told you at the start I was going for five stars — did I get there today? It genuinely helps me if you'd say so in a review. Seven calls a day makes this the fastest-compounding habit on the page.

This week's drill — own the number you do have

Replace one sentence

<b>Trigger:</b> how much for a whole new system?

<b>Never:</b> I'm just the repair guy, I don't know.

"Great question — and I don't want to guess at your money. Our install specialist measures the space and sizes it to your home so the number is actually right. He can be out today at no cost. Morning or afternoon?"

Same routing, none of the self-demotion, and it ends in a time instead of a shrug.
