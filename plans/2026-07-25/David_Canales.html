1

The one-line verdict

Why neither flipped

He offers Option C. He never books it.

What actually happened

On both calls David built the third option correctly and named it out loud — I put this option just if you want to speak with the equipment specialist so we can get them out here and I can call him out and he can go over other options with you too. Both customers said a version of no, and on both calls he accepted it and moved to signatures. On the James call he offered twice and got I'll skip that for now then I got another company in mind.

What the FSG model calls for

An offer is not a booking. The specialist visit is free and carries no commitment, so it should never be presented as a choice the customer has to weigh — it should be presented as the next step, with a day and a time attached. He's out this way this afternoon and it costs you nothing — does 4 or 6 work better? That single change is the difference between David's teaching and David's close rate.

6

This week's drill — the free step, not the extra option

Reframe Option C

<b>Trigger:</b> any hesitation on price — that's a lot, I need to think, I want another quote.

<b>Response:</b>

"Getting another quote is the smart move — let's make sure you have a real number from us to compare it to. Our specialist comes out free, zero commitment. He's in this area this afternoon — does 4 or 6 work better?"

Never present the specialist as a fourth thing to say yes to. Present it as the thing that makes their second opinion worth getting.
