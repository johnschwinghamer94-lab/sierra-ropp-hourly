1

The one-line verdict

Why none of the five flipped

He hands over information and never asks for a decision.

What actually happened

On the LUNGU call the customer asked the question every rep waits for — how long does it take to replace it and how much does it cost? Robert answered that's not my expertise. I test the components... share the repair cost with you and then we can have somebody come out and speak with you about replacement. The specialist was named. No day was proposed, no time was offered, and the call moved on to leak-search pricing.

What the FSG model calls for

Naming the specialist is not booking the specialist. The customer asked what a replacement costs — that is consent. That's exactly what our comfort advisor does, and it costs you nothing. He can be out tomorrow morning — does that work, or is afternoon better? Then silence. Robert reaches this exact moment on multiple calls and stops one sentence short every time.

Gap 1 — Five calls, five visits that end with nothing scheduled

Customer, LUNGU"how long does it take to replace it and how much does it cost?"
Robert"that's not my expertise... we can have somebody come out and speak with you about replacement."
How it endedNo day proposed, no time offered — the conversation moved to leak-search pricing.

The fix He does the evaluation better than almost anyone here and then treats the handoff as an FYI. The specialist is free, and the customer had just asked for exactly what the specialist provides. Propose a specific day and time, every single time — he can be out tomorrow morning, does that work or is afternoon better? — and then say nothing until the customer answers. Five calls, five missed slots, is not a knowledge gap; it is a missing sentence.

Weak

Gap 2 — He argued the leak instead of getting curious about it

Customer"the last time they came here and added Freon was like three years ago for the upstairs and four or five years ago for the downstairs. So even if it's a leak, it's a very low leak."
Robert"right now it's not that low... the temperatures indicate that."
Customer, shortly after"what is it that you can do now that you're here?"

The fix The customer was reasoning, not resisting — and he was working from real history. The FSG move is a question, not a correction: That's a fair read — and it's why I want to show you the number I measured. What do you think it means that the coil is running below freezing? Let him reach the conclusion. Robert had the better data and still lost the room, because he defended it instead of sharing it.

Moderate

Gap 3 — On one call the trade talk replaced the evaluation

The CRAWFORD visitA homeowner's contact who had spent years in HVAC himself — and the visit filled with Schrader valve removers, recovery equipment, Colorado blizzards and on-call stories.
What never happenedNo required questions, no structured check-in, no options — the visit ends heading to the furnace.

The fix Rapport with a fellow tech is easy and it feels productive; it is not the same as running the call. When a customer knows the trade, that is a reason to be more structured, not less — they will notice the discipline. Run the same block: expectations, the four questions, the check-ins, the options. The shop talk fits around it, not instead of it.

This week's drill — the sentence at the end of every call

Nothing leaves unscheduled

<b>Trigger:</b> the customer has heard the findings and the price.

<b>Before you reach for a signature:</b>

"Whatever you decide, let's make sure you have both numbers to decide with. Our advisor comes out free — he can be here tomorrow morning. Does that work, or is afternoon better?"

Then stop talking and wait. Robert's evaluation earns him this ask on nearly every call. He just has to make it.
