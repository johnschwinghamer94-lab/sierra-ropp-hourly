1

The one-line verdict

Why none flipped

Weigh the options out is not an option set.

What he said

The thing that you can do is just weigh the options out. If you decide that you want to go repair, we can take care of that. If you guys choose that you want to go replacement, we can take care of that. All of it true, on a system with a large leak, a compressor over-amping and weakened windings, and a leak search costing about two thousand on its own.

What should follow

That sentence hands the whole decision back to a customer who has just been told his compressor may not survive the summer. "Here's what I'd do: the leak search is two thousand and the compressor may not last anyway. Let me get our advisor here at no cost so you're not spending that blind — what day works?" The bill answer, the leak and the weakened windings are all already in his hands; only the appointment is missing.

5

What Robert did right — real strength, build on it

  • He asked the power bill question and got a number. A hundred dollars more than last month, volunteered — that is the efficiency argument handed to him complete.
  • He explains the pay-twice problem honestly: a two-thousand-dollar leak search on a compressor that may fail anyway.
  • He establishes who approves the work on a tenant call before doing anything, which prevents a whole category of dispute.
  • He refuses to guess outside his expertise, which is right — it simply needs an appointment attached to it.
  • He gives the approval promise reliably, on three of four recordings.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Use the bill figure
Drill: Every bill answer gets repeated inside the options as the cost of doing nothing.
Proof it worked: The customer's own figure in the presentation.
Week 2
Answer the price question with a date
Drill: Any question about replacement cost is answered by booking the person who can answer it.
Proof it worked: An appointment offered every time replacement is raised.
Week 3
Record to the end
Drill: Recorder confirmed at the door and verified before the van door closes.
Proof it worked: Every recording reaches the goodbye.
8

What we owe Robert Silinzy

  • Recognition for the bill question. He asked the team's most-coached question and got a usable answer; the plan should not read as if nothing improved.
  • A one-line booking script for the moment a customer asks what replacement costs.
  • Recorder support. Three of four visits vanish before the findings, and he is being scored on what survives.
Bottom line

Robert asked the question everyone is being coached to ask and got the perfect answer, then offered a choice of directions instead of a date. The customer asked outright what replacement costs. One sentence — free, an hour, what day — is the whole difference between this day and a good one.
