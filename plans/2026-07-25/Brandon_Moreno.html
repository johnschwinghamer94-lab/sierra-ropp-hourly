The one-line verdict

The moment that matters

He earned the question and didn't answer it.

What actually happened

After a clear, honest diagnosis the customer asked the buying question outright: what are typical numbers we're looking at? Brandon replied It varies. I gotta take a look at the attic. I'm not sure what type of equipment's up there, then Like 30% of like one system. The moment passed and he went up the ladder.

What the FSG model calls for

That question is not a request for a number — it is permission to bring the specialist. The FSG move is to name the handoff and put it on the calendar right there: no cost, no commitment, real numbers. Brandon had already described the Option C path minutes earlier; he just never converted the invitation into an appointment.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • <b>Read the person before the equipment.</b> It was my mom's house and I inherited it → so you're still catching up. That is why the customer trusted the bad news when it came.
  • <b>Let the customer reach the conclusion.</b> You're saying I'm gonna be looking into a whole system replacement? came from the customer's mouth, not Brandon's. That is the hardest behavior in the rubric and he did it naturally.
  • <b>Honest about money without flinching.</b> I'm gonna be upfront, the Freon for this stuff is like $500 to $600 a pound.
  • <b>Corrected his own shortcut.</b> He pre-judged the other two systems, caught it, and went and tested them: Let me do my due diligence. That instinct is worth protecting.

6

This week's drill — signal to slot

Trigger and response

<b>Trigger:</b> any version of what would a new one cost? / what are typical numbers? / what am I looking at?

<b>Response, within ten seconds:</b>

"Great question — and I don't want to guess at your money. Our comfort advisor comes out at no cost and gets you the real number. He's in this area today — afternoon or evening?"

Then zip it and let them pick. The question was the yes; all you have to do is take the slot.
