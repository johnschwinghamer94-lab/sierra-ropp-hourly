1

The one-line verdict

Why it did not flip

He built the whole case and then left the customer to chase the number.

What he said

It just depends on your plans of staying in the house. If you say, Brandon, this thing's for sale this week, we have a buyer, I'd tell you something different. And then the route: we have somebody who comes out, they get an estimate… they're through Sierra, so you get everything done the same day.

What was missing

The customer answered by asking for numbers — but what are typical numbers we're looking at? — and got it varies. That is a buying question, asked twice, met with a shrug both times. The correct next sentence is a diary slot, not an estimate range: the specialist visit is free and takes an hour.

5

What Brandon did right — real strength, build on it

  • He asks how long the household plans to stay and lets that answer set the recommendation. That is the right order.
  • He is upfront about refrigerant economics without turning it into a scare.
  • He asks about time constraints and paces a forty-minute attic job around the answer.
  • He reads the second system's future off the physical evidence — sweating copper, the drip line — rather than off a form.
