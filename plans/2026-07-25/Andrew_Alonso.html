What went right — real strength, build on it

  • <b>The best expectations block of the day.</b> Look, thorough evaluation, options, approval and the five-star seed in one natural breath, ending with a consent check — Is that fair?
  • <b>Parking verified before entry.</b> just not on the red curve — a small behavior almost nobody on the board does, and it sets a tone of respect immediately.
  • <b>Teaching pitched to the person in front of him.</b> The customer had just started assembling power transformers, so Andrew taught the compressor through amperage and heat. That is Be A Nerd done right.
  • <b>The customer diagnosed the motor himself.</b> I know something is off when that thing failed once — Andrew created the space for that instead of announcing it.

Moderate

Gap 2 — The thermostat question was assumed, not asked

Customer, at the door"it's on 65 right now, the thermostat"
Andrew, later"What's your guys's power, though? How many square feet is..."

The fix A number the customer volunteers in the driveway is not the required question. Ask it deliberately — What do you keep it set at in the summer, and what about the winter? — and then run the mandatory follow-up on the bill. Andrew got the bill answer here by luck of the conversation; make it the reflex, not the accident.

Weak

Gap 3 — Membership never came up

Customer"We did try the yes one. I don't know about two visits."
Andrew"Okay. Okay. Since now that you guys are gonna replace the fan..."

The fix That was an open door on membership status and it went by. First-time homeowners in a 2018 build are the single best membership conversation we have. Address it in the Welcome — where the customer stands today — so the value is established long before any price is on the table.
