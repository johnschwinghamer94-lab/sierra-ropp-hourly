The one-line verdict

Why neither flipped

He earns the right to make an offer and then does not make one.

What is on tape

The permission question, the approval promise, the five-star seed, the forever-home question, the room-by-room airflow discussion — all of it, inside the first half of a seventy-two-minute visit. The customer volunteers that a previous technician offered a second option and they declined it because the part was still working.

What is missing

That volunteered history is the opening. A household that has already declined once, plans to stay forever, and is now replacing a fan motor is the exact profile for a no-cost replacement number. No advisor was offered, no option C was named, and the tape ends with the fan motor being replaced and nothing else asked for.

What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • The opening is a model. Permission, the approval promise, the multiple-options framing and the five-star seed, delivered naturally rather than recited.
  • He asks the forever-home question — the single most useful piece of qualifying information there is, and most of this board never asks it.
  • He keeps a household comfortable across a very long visit without losing their attention or their goodwill.
  • He works the airflow problem room by room rather than treating the equipment as the whole system.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Offer the number
Drill: Every evaluation ends with a no-cost replacement figure offered out loud, whatever the repair is.
Proof it worked: An advisor offer on every recording.
Week 2
Use the forever-home answer
Drill: When a customer says they are staying, that sentence gets quoted back inside the presentation.
Proof it worked: The customer's own words in the option framing.
Week 3
Collect the fifth star
Drill: The review ask becomes the last item before the ladder goes in the van.
Proof it worked: Review asked on every call.

What we owe Andrew Alonso

  • Use the opening for training. The permission-and-approval sequence is the cleanest on this board.
  • Pair him with a presentation. He has never been recorded delivering one; a ride-along on someone else's close would close most of this plan.
  • Recorder discipline on short calls. A five-minute maintenance recording cannot be graded and cannot be defended.
Bottom line

Andrew earns the right to make an offer better than almost anyone here, and then does not make it.
