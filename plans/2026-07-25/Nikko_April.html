1

The one-line verdict

The moment that matters

The customer opened the replacement door himself.

What actually happened

On the tune-up call, after Nikko explained that a compressor failure on that old refrigerant runs around nine thousand dollars, the customer said: we'll see what happens for this system. But I like to know what I'm up against. Let's say I would do a new unit. Nikko's answer: It's good to have a plan. At least know the pricing. Know what company you want to use. Contracting out here, there's a lot of shady workers.

What the FSG model calls for

That is not a question about planning — it is a customer asking to be sold. The FSG move is to take the words he just used and put a time on them: Let's get you that plan. Our comfort advisor comes out free, no commitment, and gets you the real number so you're not guessing. He's this way Thursday — morning or afternoon? Then stop talking. Under SILO doctrine that booking is the win, and it was one sentence away.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • <b>Floor protectors, both calls.</b> The only rep on the board doing it. It is a small thing that tells a customer everything about how you will treat their home.
  • <b>He refused to make the customer decide at 2 a.m.</b> Asked to diagnose all three systems in one night, he said no for the right reason: for me checking all these and writing my report, it's gonna be probably like 2 AM when I'm finished, and then you're gonna have to make a decision at that time. Then waived the service fee.
  • <b>He would not sell what did not need selling.</b> It's not causing any issues, not writing you an estimate for that, but in the future it will.
  • <b>He caught a live panel and stopped.</b> When you turn that off, you should have no volts down here. You ready? There's 240 volts going through that. Then called for backup instead of improvising. That is exactly right.

The three gaps

each one anchored to a real moment on tape
Weak

Gap 1 — The replacement signal got advice instead of a slot

Customer"I like to know what I'm up against. Let's say I would do a new unit."
Nikko"It's good to have a plan. At least know the pricing. Know what company you want to use."

The fix He agreed with the customer that having a plan is smart, and then left him without one. The specialist visit <i>is</i> the plan, it is free, and the customer had just asked for it in his own words. Say it back to him with a day attached: Let's get you that plan — our advisor comes out at no cost and gets you the real number. He's this way Thursday, morning or afternoon? Then zip it. That booking is the flip.

Weak

Gap 2 — He tells customers he does not know, out loud, mid-explanation

Nikko, on refrigerant history"they have those systems running on ammonia still. Uh, no, that— well, Freon, I'm not sure if R-22 is ammonia-based. Is it? I don't know. I would look into that."
Nikko, on ductwork through a firewall"that's not my department. So I don't know. But I'll find out."

The fix The second one is exactly right — name the limit, own the follow-up. The first is not: thinking out loud about whether a refrigerant is ammonia-based, and then telling the customer to go research it, undercuts everything solid he said before it. If you are not certain, do not narrate the uncertainty — say let me confirm that and get back to you and move on. Same honesty, none of the cost.

Weak

Never askedHow often the filters are changed · what a standard summer power bill looks like.
Asserted anyway"you're going to have a little higher energy bills cuz you're running all 3 of them."

The fix He handled filters physically on the tune-up — grabbed the sizes, changed them — without ever asking how often they get changed, which is where the coaching lives. And on a call built entirely around energy load, he told the customer what his bills would do instead of asking what they were. Ask first; the customer's own number is worth more than your estimate.

6

This week's drill — say the customer's words back with a date on them

Signal to slot

<b>Trigger:</b> let's say I would do a new unit / I like to know what I'm up against / what would that run?

<b>Response, within ten seconds:</b>

"Let's get you exactly that — a real number, no cost, no commitment. Our comfort advisor is out this way [day]. Morning or afternoon?"

Then stop. He does not need convincing — he just told you what he wants. All that is left is the time.
