1

The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

He never took the repair off the table — and still got the handoff.

What actually happened

When the customer pushed back — so for now, just to get the house cool, is there a way where we can just put Freon and see what happens? — Cole did not argue. He explained the EPA constraint, told him he had already called his manager about refrigerant availability, and offered the repair anyway: if you want to go that route, we could be out here first thing in the morning to at least start the leak search. Then handed the decision back: Your home. It's your decision. Just want to make sure you guys have all of your options.

What the FSG model calls for

This is the model working as designed. The customer stayed in control, the repair path stayed real, and the advisor still came out that night — I usually hang out till he gets here, just so when he gets here, I can show him where everything's at, tell him what's going on... that way he's not walking in blind and everyone's on the same page. The gaps below are refinements on a call that already worked.

The three gaps

each one anchored to a real moment on tape
Moderate

Gap 1 — Two required questions never got asked

Asked"you said you keep it around 75" · "Is there any areas in the home that were hotter or colder than others?"
Never askedHow often the filters get changed · what a standard summer power bill looks like at 75.

The fix On a system that had been running five hours straight without shutting off, the bill question was sitting right there — and the answer would have made the replacement conversation the customer's idea rather than Cole's. Add both to the thermostat block: filter frequency, then temperature, then and what does a standard power bill look like for you setting it there in the summer?

Moderate

Gap 2 — He kept talking after the options landed

Cole, after presenting"Because I'd hate to see you guys put $4,000 in. Your compressor goes out and you guys just lit $4,000 on fire." … "when it comes to new systems, I've seen them go as low as like $150 a month."

The fix The options were already strong and the customer was already reasoning out loud. Present the three, name the prices, and then stop — completely — until he speaks. Every additional argument after the ask gives a hesitating customer another thing to push against. Zip It is the only band on this call that cost him anything, and it cost him nothing this time only because the customer stayed engaged.

Moderate

Gap 3 — The five-star seed never became the five-star ask

Cole, in the Welcome"A five star experience. If I'm not doing that, let me know. That way I can change it."
At the closeLogistics for the advisor, the dog, the ladder — and no review request.

The fix He planted it perfectly and then never collected. On a call where the customer thanked him repeatedly, the ask is one sentence while you are packing up: I told you at the start I was going for five stars — did I get there? If so, it genuinely helps me if you'd mention it in a review. Ask before the advisor arrives, while the goodwill is his.

6

This week's drill — present, then stop

Zip It, drilled

<b>After you name the third option and its price:</b> silence. Count to five in your head.

"Take a look. Let me know what works best for you."

Then nothing. No supporting story, no second metaphor, no monthly-payment figure. Cole already says the right closing line — the drill is saying only that line and letting it sit.
