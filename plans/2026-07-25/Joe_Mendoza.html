1

The one-line verdict

Why both flipped

He books the appointment while he's still holding the pen.

What actually happened

On the maintenance call the customer asked so we need to make an appointment with Sean? and Joe did not hesitate: I know the weekend's coming up. We can get Sean out here Monday morning if that works for you guys. When Monday did not work, he offered real windows — we can do 8 to 10, 10 to 12, 1 to 3, or 4 to 6 — then took the signature: this autograph on my phone is just to approve for Sean to come out next Friday.

What the FSG model calls for

That is the model executed exactly. The coaching on this page is not about his closing — it is about what he skipped on the way there. Across two calls he asked one of the four required questions once. He is winning on rapport and option-building; the assessment discipline underneath is the part that will make it repeatable.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • <b>He converts signals into calendar slots.</b> when would you do it? → I can schedule this for someone to come out tomorrow. And we need to make an appointment with Sean? → a day, four time windows, and a signature.
  • <b>He kills the double-charge fear before it is raised.</b> obviously we would credit this to that. I'm not going to double dip.
  • <b>He let the customer diagnose her own ductwork.</b> She named the cause, the mechanism and the consequence in her own words. That is the hardest behavior in the rubric.
  • <b>Membership used as leverage for the customer, not the company.</b> The Costco relationship, the visible discount, and what I can do is I can just schedule him to come out and it would still be like if you walked into Costco. He removed every step between her and the appointment.

The three gaps

Weak

Gap 1 — One required question across two calls

Asked, once"what temperature did you typically keep it at?" — "like 77, 78."
Never asked on either callHow often the filters get changed · what a standard summer power bill looks like · which rooms run hotter or colder · and, on the maintenance visit, what she was hoping to accomplish today.

The fix Joe closed both of these on rapport and option-building, which is the hardest half to teach and the easiest half to lose on a customer who does not warm to you. The four questions are what make the win repeatable when the chemistry is not there. Run them at the thermostat every call — filter, rooms, summer-and-winter setting, then the bill. On the ductwork call in particular, the customer volunteered late that one room runs warm; asking it early would have made the entire duct conversation her idea from the first minute.

Weak

Gap 2 — He offered the reconnect and took it back

Joe"So does anybody explain to you how the system actually works and functions?"
Joe, immediately after"Not gonna bore you with that."

The fix He asked the exact right question and then answered it for the customer. That teach-back is what anchors a $2,911 number to something the customer understands, and he does it beautifully when he lets himself — the ductwork explanation on the other call is proof. Ask it and then wait for the answer. If they say yes, do it in ninety seconds; if they say no, you have their permission to skip it. Do not decide for them.

Gap 3 — Seventy minutes on a maintenance call, most of it not about the house

On tapeExtended stretches on internal scheduling friction, on-call rotations, management, pay history, and opinions about newer technicians — shared with a customer, mid-visit.
Joe, on the same call"this is not my last stop by any means. I still have probably another 3, 4 stops to go."

The fix The personal rapport is genuinely part of why this closed, and none of that should go away. What should is the company-internal material — staffing gripes, management friction and pay complaints belong with your manager, not a customer who is deciding whether to trust us with five figures of ductwork. It also cost him time on a day with several stops left. Keep the warmth, drop the shop politics, and get the appointment booked twenty minutes earlier.

6

This week's drill — the four questions, at the thermostat

Before the tools come out

<b>Trigger:</b> you are standing at the thermostat taking your photo.

"Couple quick things while I'm here — how often are the filters getting changed? Which rooms run hotter or colder than the rest? And what do you keep it set at in the summer — and what does a standard power bill look like for you at that?"

Four questions, thirty seconds. Joe already closes on instinct. This is what makes it close on the days instinct is not enough.
