1

The one-line verdict

Why both landed

He books people by name and by day, not by promise.

What he said

We'll get someone out here on Friday. Well, not someone, we'll get Sean out here on Friday. And on the repair: you're signing for the leak check and repair… it'll be done tomorrow, with the service call settled that night.

Why it works

A named person on a named day is a commitment; somebody will call you is not. Joe converts because his next step always has a name attached to it. The customer's closing question — whether she could request him again tomorrow — is the strongest signal on this day's tape.

5

What Joe did right — real strength, build on it

  • Two calls, two outcomes. One signed and paid, one booked by name for a specific day.
  • He names the person who is coming. Not someone — Sean is a small change with a large effect on whether a customer expects the visit.
  • He reframed ductwork as equipment protection rather than comfort, which is why the conversation survived the price.
  • He offered staging — living area last — so the household could act without doing everything at once.
  • A customer asked to request him again. That is the strongest satisfaction signal recorded this day.
6

The three gaps

each one in Joe's own calls
Weak

Gap 1 — the per-run price landed before anyone knew the budget

CustomerSo how much do they want to put those in there?
JoeEach individual run is fifteen hundred. And you have twenty-three of them.
CustomerOkay.

The fix Multiplying to a five-figure total in the customer's head, before she has said what she can spend, is how a protection argument turns into sticker shock. "Before I price it — what were you thinking of putting into the house this year?" Then stage to that number.

Moderate

Gap 2 — the easiest review of the week, not asked

JoeYeah, you can request me. You can always call into the office.

The fix A customer volunteering that she wants you back has already written the review in her head. "That means a lot — if the survey text comes through, saying exactly that helps me more than anything."

AskedOnset, history, warranty position.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Budget before price
Drill: On any staged or large-ticket job, the spend question comes before the figure.
Proof it worked: A budget question on every multi-item presentation.
Week 2
Ask for the review
Drill: Review asked on every visit, especially the ones customers enjoy.
Proof it worked: Review asked on every recording.
Week 3
Two questions in the welcome
Drill: Goal and monthly bill asked before the tools come out.
Proof it worked: Both on every recording.
8

What we owe Joe Mendoza

  • Use the named-booking habit as a team standard. Naming the person and the day is why his handoffs stick.
  • A budget-question script for large staged work — he has the structure and is missing the opening figure.
  • Recognition for the ductwork reframe. Protection of the equipment is a better argument than comfort and it is his.
Bottom line

Both calls produced something, and both were booked properly with a name and a date. The one thing to change is the order: find out what the household can spend before multiplying a per-run price by twenty-three in front of them.
