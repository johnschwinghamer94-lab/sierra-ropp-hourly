The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

He talked himself out of a $1,500 repair and put a specialist in the driveway instead.

FERGUSON — the second visit

That motor is $1,500. It's a lot of money to put into an aging system that has other issues that we saw yesterday. So that's going to be completely up to you guys. Then both doors held open at once: I can order the motor and see if we can get it by today to at least get you guys by until the equipment specialist swings by tomorrow — or I can get the equipment specialist out here now. → When do you think they could come? → I can get someone out here in the next 30 to 40 minutes.

What is still missing, four calls running

No five-star standard set at any door, and no review requested on any call — the same gap as his last graded day, across eleven graded calls now. And the mandatory power-bill question was asked on this job by the specialist, not by Joe.

Draw the Picture
Strong
The best single explanation anyone gave this day, on why a motor that lost oil cannot simply be topped up: they come sealed from factory. They lubricate everything, and then they put almost like a little sauce packet in them — that's your get out of jail free card. If it overheats once, it'll burst the bubble and re-lubricate everything. But you only get one of those, and if it does it once, it's going to do it again. The unstrapped capacitor got the same treatment: it's running 370 volts through it… it's supposed to be strapped onto the housing so it doesn't move, because it vibrates. It's an air conditioning system.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He talked a customer out of a $1,500 repair he could have sold that morning — and got a specialist there in forty minutes instead.
  • He offered the membership in Spanish, in full, to a household with a two-year-old system nobody had ever offered one to — four visits, the diagnostic and the plumbing inspection, then no pressure.
  • He offered the customer their own language before assuming. ¿Prefieres español?
  • He cancelled a visit rather than billing for one when the system turned out to be running: we'll just cancel out today's visit. You'll still have that complimentary diagnostic.
  • He chased the part in real time and came back with an actual answer from the manufacturer rather than a promise to check.
  • He explained why lost oil cannot be replaced better than anyone on this board.

This week's drill — the four questions, then the standard

At the thermostat, before the ladder

"Before I get started — how often are the filters getting changed? Which rooms run hotter or colder? What do you keep it set at in the summer, and what does a normal bill look like at that setting? … And above all I'm here to give you a five-star visit today. If I'm not, tell me and I'll fix it."

Five sentences. They cost less time than the walkthrough and they close the only two gaps left on his board.
