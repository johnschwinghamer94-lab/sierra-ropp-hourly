1

The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

Three sentences: it costs you money, here's the free way to find out, and you're covered for ten years.

What he said

With this being so old and the issues that we're seeing here, it's clear that it's running on less efficiency than it was back when it was first manufactured. That means that you're paying more than you should to keep this thing running. And with all this in mind, given how much we have to address the system to get it back up to an optimal standpoint, I've given you the option of talking to someone for free… see if it makes more sense to update the system, get it up to a standard for today, get you on a 10 year warranty. That way you don't have to worry about calling someone out year after year.

Why that works

It is one compact argument with three moves — an inefficiency the customer is already paying for, a free way to compare, and an end to the annual repair cycle — and no pressure anywhere in it. The customer's answer was immediate. This is the version of the Option C pitch the rest of the board should learn.

5

What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • The tightest Option C pitch on the board. Efficiency, free comparison, ten-year warranty — three moves, no filler, no pressure.
  • He made the cost personal without a single scare line. You're paying more than you should to keep this thing running does the work that other reps attempt with failure stories.
  • He stopped talking after the offer. The customer's let's do it arrived in the silence he left.
  • He sold the end of the cycle, not the equipment — which is what a customer with repeated repairs actually wants to buy.

Three-week training plan

Drill: Recorder confirmed at the door and checked before leaving, every visit.

Week 2
Bill behind the claim

Proof it worked: Customer's own figure inside every efficiency pitch.
Week 3
Ask for the review
Drill: Review requested at the door on every completed visit.
Proof it worked: Review asked on every call.
