1

The one-line verdict

Why nothing flipped

Two customers asked to buy. Both were told to call us back.

What actually happened

On the two-system call: he was like, I know that this unit is old, I think we just need to replace it, what's the price for it? So I told them that I didn't know the price, but I would recommend having Jordan come back out. The visit ended with if you guys have any questions or you guys wanted to get the information, the other half of the information, you guys can always just reach out to us. On the other call, a customer heading overseas in August said she would think about it on her return in late September.

What it should have been

I would recommend having Jordan come back out is a recommendation, not an appointment — and it puts the work on the customer. Francisco is the rep on this board who best removes the money obstacle; here he left the calendar obstacle entirely intact. "Let me get Jordan out — he can be here this afternoon, or first thing tomorrow. Which is better?" Two calls, two ready buyers, no dispatch.

5

What Francisco did right — real strength, build on it

  • He reads the service history aloud. Telling a customer exactly what was replaced and what was left original is a level of transparency almost nobody offers.
  • The engine-and-transmission analogy makes compressor and coil costs make sense in one sentence.
  • He told the truth about seasonal pricing. Our prices only increase when the manufacturer increases it — that costs him urgency and buys him credibility.
  • The five-star framing has appeared. It was flagged as missing on his previous graded day and it is now in his opening.
  • Both customers reached the replacement conclusion themselves, which is his assessment working exactly as intended.

Gap 2 — a travel date treated as a reason to wait

FranciscoOur prices don't increase in the summer… the only time that we change our prices is when the manufacturer is like, hey guys, we're raising our prices.

The fix The pricing answer was honest and it removed his own urgency. The date is the lever: "Then let's get the number before you fly — the estimate is free and it holds. That way you decide on a beach instead of in a heatwave in October." A departure date is a deadline, not an obstacle.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Dispatch, don't recommend
Drill: Any customer asking about replacement price gets an advisor time proposed in the room. Ten reps.
Proof it worked: No tape where a price request ends with a recommendation.
Week 2
Dates are deadlines
Drill: Travel, moves and seasonal plans converted into a before-then appointment. Five reps.
Proof it worked: Every stated date followed by a proposed appointment.
Week 3
Train in the van
Drill: Trainee instruction moves out of customers' homes; freed time goes to the ask and the review.
Proof it worked: No training on tape indoors.

What we owe Francisco Valencia

  • A direct conversation about the dispatch habit. He is one of the best on this board at building the case and he keeps handing the last step to the customer.
  • Credit the transparency. Reading the service history aloud and refusing to invent seasonal urgency are both exactly right.
  • Protected training time. Second graded day teaching during live calls because there is nowhere else for it.
  • The bill question, third time. He sells payments better than anyone and never asks what they currently pay.
Bottom line

Francisco built two replacement cases so well that both customers reached the conclusion out loud — one of them asked for the price directly — and both were left to call us back. He cannot quote a replacement, which is precisely why the answer is a dispatched advisor rather than a recommendation. Everything before that moment is strong: the history read aloud, the honest pricing explanation, the analogies. The whole gap is the last sentence.
