1

The one-line verdict

Why it flipped

He found what nobody else had, and handed it to someone who could price it.

What actually happened

The diagnosis is the win: they attached an old furnace and only replaced this indoor coil, the outdoor coil. So when they did, there was existing rust in that furnace… and that rust is starting to collect at the bottom of the brand new pan as well. Airflow problems across half the home, ducting torn in several places, and a four-year-old condenser fan motor already leaking oil. The advisor came out and gave the customer a whole-system number.

What the debrief flagged

His manager's note on tape is worth keeping: don't make it sound like that's just a free option — try to build some urgency behind it… you want to get the homeowner's idea. The advisor visit converted anyway, but framed as "so you can compare and contrast repair pricing against replacement and see what makes the most sense" it lands as the customer's decision rather than as a freebie.

5

What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • He found the real problem. A 2022 installation that mated a new coil to an old rusted furnace is the kind of thing three previous visits can miss. He found it in ninety minutes.
  • He got an advisor on site. Diagnosis to quote in a single visit, with the customer still in the room.
  • He criticised bad work without trashing anyone. That restraint is what let the customer hear the rest of it.
  • He connected the risk to the customer's life, not to the equipment: breaking down at the least convenient moment, scrambling for help.

The three gaps

each one in Andrew's own calls
Moderate

Gap 1 — the third option was framed as free rather than as necessary

Manager, on tapeWith stuff like this, when you recommend replacements — don't make it sound like that's just a free option. Try to build some urgency behind it.
ManagerYou want to get the homeowner's idea. Hey, I put this last option here for you guys, that way you can compare and contrast repair pricing to replacement and see what makes the most sense going forward.

The fix The correction is right and it is worth writing down. The advisor visit is not a giveaway, it is the second half of the answer. Frame it as the comparison the customer needs to make a sound decision and it stops sounding like an upsell attached to a repair quote.

Weak

Gap 2 — no power-bill question, third graded day

What was foundA mismatched system with airflow failure across half the house and a fan motor already leaking oil at four years old.
What was never askedWhat the customer pays to run it.

The fix This system is demonstrably inefficient and the customer's own bill would prove it. "What's your July bill compared to January?" — one question, asked early, and the advisor arrives with the value case already made.

Moderate

Gap 3 — the welcome keeps arriving incomplete

On tapeThe approval promise, and little else.
Missing againThe report framing, the five-star seed and the goal question.

The fix Same note as two previous graded days. The full sequence takes forty seconds and it is what makes the presentation feel expected rather than sprung: evaluation, anything else I find, report with options, nothing without approval, we can start today, five-star, and what would make today a win?

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Frame the third option
Drill: Advisor visit presented as the comparison, never as a freebie. Five reps with a manager using his own words from this call.
Proof it worked: No tape where Option C is described only as free.
Week 2
The bill question
Drill: Power bill asked in the first ten minutes, every call, written on the report.
Proof it worked: Customer's own figure on every recording.
Week 3
Finish the welcome
Drill: Report, five-star and goal question added to the approval promise he already gives.
Proof it worked: All beats on every recording.

  • Put the manager's framing note in writing. It was good coaching delivered in a driveway and it should not be lost.

Bottom line

Andrew found the actual problem — a four-year-old installation that was wrong from the start — and got an advisor in front of the customer the same visit. That is the outcome we want. The refinement is in the framing: the free advisor visit is the second half of the answer, not a giveaway, and one question about the power bill would let him prove the case before the advisor even arrives.
