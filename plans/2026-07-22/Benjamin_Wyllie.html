1

The one-line verdict

Why all three converted

He removes the friction before the customer can feel it.

What actually happened

On the R22 call the arithmetic was brutal — a coil that can't be sourced, fifteen pounds of refrigerant at six hundred dollars a pound, a compressor pulling high amps — and the customer asked the right question: is it better to try to replace the unit at this point? Benjamin's answer removed every obstacle at once: it's something I can just get a guy out here real quick and be here in about 30 to 45 minutes, then we're gonna waive that, so no cost today, then I'm gonna be here waiting with you guys because my ladder's already set up.

Why that works

Three frictions — waiting, paying, and being left alone with a stranger — all removed inside a minute. He also used the customer's utility cost as the argument: they are overpaying by about 15% on their utility bills every single month just to keep the system going. Same-day install followed.

5

What Benjamin did right — real strength, build on it

  • Three for three. Two leads and a completed repair, on a day that included one of the hardest diagnoses of the week.
  • He waived the fee and said so. We're gonna waive that. So no cost today — removing the money question before the advisor arrived.
  • He stayed on site with the ladder up. Not leaving an elderly household alone to meet a stranger is why the handoff worked.
  • He used utility overspend as the argument. The only rep this day to put a monthly cost of ownership in front of a replacement decision.
  • He refused to write something he couldn't verify. Insisting the report not describe an untested compressor as working is small, unglamorous integrity.
6

The three gaps

each one in Benjamin's own calls
Moderate

Gap 1 — the utility figure was quoted, not sourced

BenjaminThey are overpaying by about 15% on their utility bills every single month just to keep the system going because of how old it is.
What was not askedWhat the bill actually is.

The fix He is already making the strongest argument available and using a percentage to do it. One question converts it to dollars: "What's the bill running in July?" Fifteen percent of a known number is an amount; fifteen percent of an unknown one is an adjective.

Moderate

Gap 2 — the review ask, still missing

Benjamin, every welcomeI do this all for 5 stars.
At the end of three callsNo review request — including after a completed repair and a same-day install.

The fix Three graded days, promised every time, collected never. On a day this good it is the cheapest possible win: "I promised you five stars — if I hit it, the survey comes by text and my name on it means a lot."

Moderate

Gap 3 — the second call ended in someone else's hands

BenjaminJust see what we can do, see what she says, and then she'll reach out to you.
Customer, earlierThere's certain things that I can do myself… I would refinance or finance something affordable.

The fix The lead came through anyway, but a customer who has raised financing himself should not leave the visit waiting for a callback. "Let's not wait on a call — I'll get our advisor out and he'll bring the financing options with him. This afternoon or tomorrow morning?"

8

What we owe Benjamin Wyllie

  • Recognition for the R22 call. Fee waived, advisor in forty-five minutes, tech stays on site, same-day install. That is the whole playbook executed in one visit.
  • Authority to waive the diagnostic, in writing. He used it well and other techs will hesitate without a clear rule.
  • A financing card. Customers keep raising it and reps keep improvising.
  • The review ask, made mandatory. Three graded days of promising and never collecting.
Bottom line

Three visits, three outcomes, and the R22 call is the model: he told the truth about an unfixable system, waived the fee so cost couldn't stall it, had an advisor there inside the hour, and stayed on site until the handoff was done. He also did the one thing almost nobody else does — put a monthly cost of ownership in front of a replacement decision. Ask for the actual bill instead of quoting a percentage, and ask for the review you promise, and this is close to a complete day.
