1

The one-line verdict

What changed

He found something serious and it turned into an opportunity.

What actually happened

On the call that converted, the customer volunteered the history — I had a guy come out 3 years ago hook something up to this, I don't know what it is — and Nikko's answer was immediate and unambiguous: your compressor, it's scorched. It's roast… apparently his tool was wrong. And these don't have the safety.

Why it matters

Across his previous graded days the pattern was a correct diagnosis followed by a price list and no recommendation. Here the finding was serious enough to speak for itself and a lead followed. The lesson is that clear naming produces outcomes — and the two maintenance calls, where nothing was named, produced nothing.

5

What Nikko did right — real strength, build on it

  • His first recorded lead across two graded days, off a serious finding named in plain words.
  • He asked the power-bill question. It was the standing gap in his previous plan and it appeared this day.
  • He checks every system, every time, and explains why. That policy protects the customer and the company, and he articulates it well.
  • He praises good work by other companies where he finds it. That even-handedness is why customers believe him when he finds damage.
  • "It's scorched. It's roast." Plain speech beats a technical explanation almost every time.

The three gaps

each one in Nikko's own calls
Weak

Gap 1 — the welcome is still missing its core promises

Now presentThe report mention and the warranty check.
Still missing on all three callsThe approval promise, the five-star seed and the goal question.

The fix The approval promise in particular is not optional — it is what makes a customer comfortable that nothing will be done or charged without them. "I won't do anything without your approval, and if you approve it we can start today. Above all I'm here for a five-star visit — what would make today a win?"

Weak

Gap 2 — two maintenance visits ending with nothing

On one callA fan not running and a compressor hot to the touch, at the point the recording stops.
On the otherA recently installed system checked and confirmed good.
What neither producedA written option set, a recommendation or a no-cost advisor offer.

The fix A fan not turning and a hot compressor is not a clean maintenance result. Every call ends with a written report and a stated recommendation, even when the recommendation is that nothing needs doing today — and when something does, the advisor is one of the options.

Moderate

Gap 3 — the bill was asked and then set aside

NikkoWhat are your energy bills in the summertime?
CustomerI paid only $52 a month.
NikkoYou can't beat that. That's not even worth getting solar for.

The fix He asked the question and got a genuinely useful answer — a household with very low running costs, where the value argument has to be reliability rather than efficiency. Use the answer to choose the argument, rather than treating it as small talk. A low bill tells you which case to make, not that there is no case.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Finish the welcome
Drill: Approval promise, five-star seed and goal question added to every opening.
Proof it worked: All beats on every recording.
Week 2
Always a recommendation
Drill: Every call ends with a written report and a stated recommendation, including clean maintenance.
Proof it worked: A report exists for every call.
Week 3
Use the bill answer
Drill: Whatever the bill figure is, name the argument it supports — efficiency if high, reliability if low. Five reps.
Proof it worked: Bill figure referenced in every findings conversation.

What we owe Nikko April

  • Credit the lead and the bill question. Both were the standing notes on his previous plan and both moved.
  • The approval promise, drilled. It is the one part of the welcome that genuinely protects the customer, and it is still missing.
  • Recording coverage. Three recordings, none reaching a presentation, including one that ends with a hot compressor and a stopped fan.
  • A clean-maintenance template. He needs a standard way to end a visit where nothing is wrong.
Bottom line

Nikko found a compressor cooked by another company's work, named it in four plain words, and produced his first recorded lead. He also asked the power-bill question for the first time — the standing note on his last plan. Two things still to close: the welcome is missing the approval promise that protects the customer, and two maintenance visits ended with no recommendation at all, including one where the fan had stopped and the compressor was hot to the touch.
