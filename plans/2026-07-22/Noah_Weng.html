1

The one-line verdict

Why it converted

He turned "we can't touch this" into a specialist standing in the driveway.

What he did

Diagnosed the leak, spotted the code violation and checked before promising anything: I have to call my boss to see if we can even touch this, just because it's not up to code… you need that safety switch in between, which is the disconnect. Then he gave the customer the honest verdict and the honest alternative in one breath: we can't be liable for it, because we can't warranty the work… you can definitely find somebody who'll come out here and touch it, but then you don't really know who you're getting. But if you wanted an estimate for what it would be to just redo the whole thing, I can get one of our equipment specialists out here. Then he waited on site so the specialist would not arrive cold.

What is still missing

The five-star standard is seeded and never collected — a second graded day running. And the rooms question, on a call where the customer described exactly which bedroom the sun cooks and why she runs the thermostat as low as it goes.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He checked before he promised. I have to call my boss to see if we can even touch this — then came back with a real answer instead of a hedge.
  • He refused work he could not warranty and explained why in the customer's terms rather than the company's: we can't be liable for it, because we can't warranty the work.
  • He turned a refusal into a booked specialist and then stayed on site until he arrived, so the customer never had to tell the story twice.
  • He gave advice that had nothing to do with the sale. Whenever you buy anything, not only get it inspected for the electrical side of things — have an HVAC guy come out and check it out for you.
  • He asked the power bill again, second graded day running, alongside the setting and the filters.
  • He was honest about the limits of his own role. I don't even have prices on [these]. I'm just repairs.
