1

The one-line verdict

The day in one pattern

He walked into a billing dispute and handled it like it was nothing. Then the tape ended.

What he did well

The customer opened with a complaint about Sierra: a capacitor replaced, four days of no cooling, a home warranty technician replacing the same part, and a request for a refund, it's like $400 something. He did not argue, did not defend and did not change the subject — he moved to what he could control, and then corrected a filter mistake with a reason: for one inch, I would say don't go above like MERV 8… it just gives the unit air to breathe. You want to trap as much dust and debris as you can, but you want airflow to go in.

What is missing

Both files end at the ladder. No evaluation result, no explanation of why the first capacitor failed, no answer on the refund, and no recorded acknowledgement of the complaint itself. The customer raised a service failure and there is no record that anyone addressed it.

What went right — real strength, build on it

  • He absorbed a complaint about his own company without flinching — a failed repair, a duplicate part and a refund request, all in the customer's first breath — and kept the visit moving.
  • He corrected a bad filter recommendation with a reason. For one inch, don't go above MERV 8… you want to trap as much dust as you can, but you want airflow to go in.
  • He asked the thermostat question by observation rather than interrogation. I saw the temperature on the thermostat — is that typically where you have it?
  • He kept his boots and his tools out of the house without being asked, on both calls.
  • He checked the equipment's age against the record rather than accepting the customer's memory of it.
  • He noticed the customer's schedule and said so: I don't want to go too much longer.

6

This week's drill — acknowledge, then set the block

When a customer opens with a complaint about us

"That shouldn't have happened and I'm sorry — you paid for a repair that didn't hold. Here's what I'm going to do: a thorough evaluation of the whole system so we find out why that part failed, not just what failed. I'll bring you everything I find and show you your options, I won't do a thing without your approval, and if we can fix it we'll do it today. Above all I'm here to give you a five-star visit — if I'm not, stop me."

Acknowledgement first, block second, tools third. He already stays calm under it; this is the sentence that turns calm into confidence.
