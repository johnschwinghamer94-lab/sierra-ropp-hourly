The one-line verdict

The day in one line

He honoured a boundary and still told the truth. The missing piece is what happens next.

What actually happened

The customer set the terms early: she would go to a hotel when it failed, and did not want to discuss replacement until then. Brandon worked inside that — the heat exchanger explained as married at the factory, the carbon-monoxide risk named honestly, the compressor's previous replacement cost recalled — and ended with we can keep everything running as long as you want. It's just a matter of realistic expectations, risk of breakdown versus cost.

What was still available

Respecting the boundary was right and the trust it bought is real. But she also said she would need a hotel and a plan when it fails. That is a free advisor visit with no purchase attached — you told me you'd need a plan when it goes. Let's have the number ready so the hotel is the only thing you have to arrange. That is not the conversation she banned.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
The plan, not the pitch
Drill: Any customer who declines a sales conversation is still offered the no-cost number as preparation. Five reps.
Proof it worked: No tape where a declined conversation ends with nothing offered.
Week 2
Three options, written
Drill: Written immediate-future-advisor set on every call, including no-sale visits.
Proof it worked: A report exists on every call.
Week 3
The bill question
Drill: Power bill asked in the first ten minutes, every call.
Proof it worked: Customer's own figure on every recording.
