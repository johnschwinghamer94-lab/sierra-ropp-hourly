1

The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

The best diagnosis on the board, delivered without a recommendation.

What actually happened

Over 124 minutes on the Phillips call he established: a fan motor wired to the wrong terminals and wearing out early, oil seeping from the motor housing, compressor inrush at 125 against its ceiling, rust on both coils with the downstairs worse, and R22 at around $600 a pound — three thousand dollars just to refill it. Both units are from 2004. Then the call ended.

What flips it

After all that, the customer knows everything that is wrong and nothing about what to do. So here’s what I’d do. Option A, we repair the wiring and recharge the upstairs — but you’re putting three thousand dollars of refrigerant into a twenty-two-year-old unit. Option B, replace the upstairs now. Option C, our specialist prices both units together at no cost, because they’re the same age and they’ll fail the same way. Which of those do you want to look at first?

5

What Rob did right — real strength, build on it

  • He opens on the person, not the equipment. Sorry that you had to go with no ac, Man. That’s hard. Summertime ain’t no joke. The customer trusted him from that sentence onward.
  • The deepest diagnostic work on this board. A fan motor wired to the wrong terminals, oil seepage, compressor inrush at its ceiling, differential rust between two units of the same age — found and documented.
  • He reframes a flat refusal into a set of choices. I don’t want a whole new system became three quotes — compressor, condenser, complete system — because he agreed first: I know, I know.
  • He gives the customer the number that answers the question. R22 at around $600 a pound, five to seven pounds needed. That fact decides the repair-versus-replace argument without him having to make it.
  • He proves it with pictures. Photographs and video of the rust and the oil, so the customer is not being asked to take his word for anything.

What we owe Robert Silinzy

  • Recognition for the diagnostic work. The miswired fan motor is the kind of fault most technicians never find, and he found it while inspecting a different problem.
  • A specialist path he can offer on a two-system replacement without feeling like he is handing away his own call — the Phillips job is exactly what Option C exists for.
  • A quote-presentation slot he can book himself. He should not have to promise three quotes and then hope the office follows up.
Bottom line

Rob does the part of this job that cannot be taught. He found a fan motor wired to the wrong terminals while looking at something else, he knows what rust patterns and oil seepage mean, and he opens a call with more genuine warmth than anyone on this board. What he does not do is finish. After two hours of proving exactly what is wrong with two twenty-two-year-old systems, the customer had no idea what to do next, because nobody told him. Three sentences at the end — here’s what I’d do, here are your three choices, which do you want first — and this becomes the strongest day on the board rather than the most frustrating one.
