The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

He removes the barrier — when he remembers to look for one.

What actually happened

On the Palermo emergency the customer asked the only question that mattered — How much is this gonna run? — and Bradley answered it in one move: We have payments that can be as low as $150 a month, so we can definitely take care of you on that. 5

What Bradley did right — real strength, build on it

  • He answers ‘how much’ with a way to pay, not a discount. We have payments that can be as low as $150 a month, so we can definitely take care of you on that. A customer on Social Security agreed to a specialist visit minutes later.
  • The most complete expectations promise on the board — scope, safety, approval and the five-star standard — and he gives it on every call, including the one that went nowhere else.
  • He invites correction. So if at any given point I’m not meeting that threshold, by all means, come and stop me. That single sentence gives the customer permission to speak up, which is how objections surface early.
  • He acts on the human problem, not just the equipment. Ice for the house, the dogs in an air-conditioned van, a call to the manager to get the specialist moving. That is what earned the yes.
  • He calls it honestly when a repair is not worth it. It’s a 23 year old Ducane. Cooked. It’s done. No hedging, no wasted money.

It front-loads the options conversation so that even a truncated recording contains the customer’s real choice.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Four questions before the ladder. How has it been running, any breakdowns, when was it last serviced, and what would make today a good visit — asked immediately after the expectations promise, before any social conversation.
Proof it worked: Every recording this week has all four questions audible inside the first ten minutes.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Close the five-star loop. If the promise is made in the opening, the ask happens before he leaves.
Proof it worked: Review requested on every completed visit this week; manager spot-checks three recordings for the ask.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Say the pivot out loud. Before climbing or leaving the room, state what he is looking for and what each outcome means for the customer’s choices.
Proof it worked: The sentence is on tape for every call; no call ends with the decision stage unrecorded and unspoken.

Bottom line

Bradley opens better than anyone on this board and, when a customer puts a barrier in front of him, he takes it away in one sentence. The Palermo call is a model: a distressed customer on a fixed income, a dead system, and a specialist in the driveway inside the hour. What he needs is for that instinct to have something to work on — which means the four discovery questions get asked every time, including on the calls where everybody is getting along beautifully. Warmth is not the problem. Warmth without discovery is.
