The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

He answered the question they asked — and then let the answer go.

What actually happened

The customer told him exactly what he wanted from the visit: it’s 10 years old. It’s got a few issues, so. I’m aware of those. Just kind of keep track on how bad it is. Is it getting worse? Cole found the answer — compressor draw up from 127 in April to 130 now, coil rust progressing — and reported it. Then: Just kind of letting it ride. Roll the dice. No reply.

What flips it

That finding is the close, because it is the customer’s own question answered in his favour. You asked me to tell you if it was getting worse. It is — it’s moved in four months. So the real question isn’t if this compressor goes, it’s when. How much longer are you planning to be in this house? That question turns a monitoring visit into a timeline conversation.

5

What Cole did right — real strength, build on it

  • He asks the goal question and the customer tells him everything. Just kind of keep track on how bad it is. Is it getting worse? — that one answer was the map for the whole call.
  • He has the trend, not just the reading. 127 in April, 130 now. Almost nobody compares against their own previous visit; he did, and it is the most persuasive fact anywhere in his day.
  • He gives a straight no. Asked whether the coils alone could be swapped, he explained the refrigerant barrier accurately instead of taking an easier order. It’s like putting diesel in a gas car.
  • The expectations promise is complete and identical every time — scope, approval and the five-star standard, on every recording.
  • Small courtesies that set the tone. Is the van parked okay? before anything else happens.

The fix ‘I’ll talk to my wife’ is almost never the real blocker; it is the polite version of one. Ask for it: Totally fair — before I send it over, what do you think her first question is going to be? That either surfaces the objection or gets you a better-prepared quote in front of the actual decision-maker.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Answer their question, then ask yours. Whenever a finding directly answers something the customer asked for at the start, name that out loud and follow it with a timeline question — how long are you staying, when do you need this handled.
Proof it worked: Manager hears the ‘you asked me to tell you … it is’ callback on every call where a stated goal gets answered.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Never accept the first no. ‘I’ll talk to my wife’, ‘let it ride’ and ‘roll the dice’ each get one question in reply before the conversation moves on.
Proof it worked: Every hesitation on tape this week has an audible follow-up question after it.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Deadlines become the plan. Any customer-supplied date — a tenant, a sale, a trip — gets a ‘let’s work back from that’ and options shaped to it. Add the five-star ask to the end of every completed visit.
Proof it worked: Every dated call this week has a working-back plan in the notes; review requested on all completed visits.
