The one-line verdict

What the day turned on

Diagnosis is the finish line on three calls out of four.

What actually happened

The pattern is almost word for word the same each time. He finds it, he proves it — So the highest it should ever be is 79. Anything past that means it’s really struggling… you’re at 97 — and then: let me go write the report real quick. On the rental call it was Mandy, at that point, can decide which direction she wants to go.

What flips it

On the one call he did flip, he did the opposite — he named the choice out loud: Now, is replacement cost something that you’d like? Want to at least find out about so you can compare and contrast. That sentence is the difference between his best call and his other three. It costs ten seconds and it belongs at the end of every diagnosis he does.

5

What Ben did right — real strength, build on it

  • He reframes price instead of defending it. Sticker shock on a repair quote became that leak is not taken care of. It’s all for a waste… So I just look at all your options so you can see what that looks like. That is the move that produced his flip.
  • He sells the inspection, not himself. Normal inspectors, when they come by, they reach their hand up to see if there’s air coming out… we’re more of a long term inspection, not a short term inspection. Concrete, provable, and it makes the report matter.
  • Measurement is his teaching tool. Rated against actual, every time — 79 versus 97, 109 versus 122.1. Customers do not have to trust him; they can read it.
  • He protects the customer’s position. On the sale-of-home call he volunteered the confidentiality boundary before being asked: everything goes to you first. You have to give me that permission.
  • The approval promise is real. I’m not gonna do anything unless you approve it. Of course. Said plainly, and honoured.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Diagnosis ends with a choice. Nothing goes to the van until at least two named paths have been said out loud — the repair, and the no-cost specialist visit.
Proof it worked: Manager hears two named options at the end of every diagnosis this week; no call ends on ‘let me write the report.’
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Absent decision-maker = written options plus a delivery plan. Whenever the person who decides is not in the room, the options go in writing and a route to them gets agreed before he leaves.
Proof it worked: Every call this week with an off-site owner has two written paths and a stated hand-off method in the notes.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Seed the review in the expectations promise, and collect it before leaving. Add the ‘I’ll ask how I did’ clause to the opening he already gives.
Proof it worked: Review seeded on every call; asked for on every completed visit.
