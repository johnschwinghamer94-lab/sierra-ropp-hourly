What Andrew did right — real strength, build on it

  • The permission check-in. If I come across concerns, anything out of specs, is that information you want? — asked at the door, which earns the right to raise everything later.
  • The most thorough history on the board. He established what was replaced, when, by whom and for how much before touching the equipment.
  • Honest about the hard part. I'm just being extremely transparent and forward with you on this — no softening of the R-22 reality.
  • He invited the customer to watch. Diagnosis with the homeowner present is worth more than the same diagnosis reported afterwards.

The three gaps

each one in Andrew's own calls
Moderate

One path is not options

Andrew"At the very least, if you want to keep running with these, you'll need to get a new compressor."

The fix "At the very least" implies a second road you never named. Say both: So there are two ways to go. One is a new compressor on twenty-year-old refrigerant we can barely buy. The other is having our specialist give you a no-cost number on starting fresh. I'd want both figures before deciding.

Moderate

$12,000 already spent, and it never came back up

Customer"They came in and said they needed the whole unit. My wife paid like $12,000."

The fix That is the customer telling you what they have already invested and how it turned out. Bring it back: You've already put twelve thousand into this system and you're back here again. Let's find out what starting clean actually costs before you spend more on refrigerant we can't replace.

Weak

PatternThe recording ends on a complete, correct, unhappy diagnosis with no next step attached to it.

The fix Bad news needs a door attached to it or the customer walks out through someone else's. Every failed-compressor call ends with a named person and a proposed time.

Bottom line

Andrew's diagnosis was exactly right and his honesty about R-22 was exactly what the customer needed to hear. What is missing is the second half of the sentence. A dead compressor on a refrigerant that is no longer manufactured, on a house that already spent twelve thousand dollars, is the easiest specialist hand-off on this board. Name two roads, not one, and put a time on the second.
