What Joe did right — real strength, build on it

  • He disarms a burned customer. I felt like it was more salesy, so I definitely hear where you're coming from — naming the previous bad experience is why this call flipped.
  • The Vegas mileage frame. equivalent to a car with 480,000 miles on it makes age concrete without fear-selling.
  • He asks the goal question. what is it that you're looking to accomplish with me being here today? — the single most useful question on the board, and he asks it naturally.
  • He asks permission to continue. do you want to have me keep looking at everything? keeps a long visit consensual rather than imposed.

The three gaps

each one in Joe's own calls
Weak

A confirmation call was treated as a diagnosis call

Joe"looks like you met with Jordan yesterday about potentially replacing some of these systems, right?"
Customer"That's what has to be done."
Joe, later"Jordan can talk to you about replacing that unit if you wanted to."

The fix She told you it has to be done and she had already met the specialist. Nothing needed selling — it needed a date. You've already talked it through with Jordan and you know it needs doing. Let me get him back out this week to finish it. Is Thursday or Friday better?

Weak

The objection was the double doors, and it was never worked

Customer"Anytime you open it, you just lose all the airs."
Joe"But do you usually keep the garage door open?" … "Yeah, I get what you're saying."

The fix You had the answer half-formed and let it go. Finish it: You keep it closed, so you're not losing the air — that unit isn't the problem, the fuse is. Let's fix what's actually failing and leave the rest alone. Resolving the objection is what turns a wandering visit into a decision.

Weak

No expectations set on the second visit

Opening of the four-system callStraight into walking the property — no sequence, no timeline, no approval frame, none of the structure he uses everywhere else.

The fix The Skipper opening is your own template — The outdoor units are 40% of your air conditioner. The other 60% is upstairs in the attic. I'm gonna get started in the attic first. Use it every time, especially on the rambling visits where structure matters most.
