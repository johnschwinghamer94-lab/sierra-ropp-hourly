5

What Nathan did right — real strength, build on it

  • He respects a budget without shrinking the truth. The McLins got the full picture — compressor risk included — and a path they could actually afford: if this is too expensive, Nate, just do these repairs and get us going. At least you have the information ahead of you.
  • He offered the replacement comparison three separate times. Including one last check before placing the parts order. That is the process run properly, and the no was theirs, not his.
  • Price goes to the specialist, exactly as it should. I'm not sure on the exact price… they'll know all the pricing, they'll come out really fast and go over the sizing of the home. No guessing, no undercutting the specialist.
  • He says when he doesn't know. That's actually the first time I have seen that. It cost him nothing and bought him the room.
  • The teaching is genuinely elite. The mini-split explanation is worth recording and using in training.
6

The three gaps

each one in Nathan's own calls
Weak

A healthy system is still an Option C — and the customer wrote the pitch himself

Customer"when we paid 11 grand for this, I either want to know if it's working or not working." … "I often wondered if I had an exhaust fan on the garage that would actually do more to blow the heat out."
Nathan"everything that I'm seeing is in order with it. It's a combination of the heat that we're seeing right now as well as the lack of insulation inside of the garage."

The fix He handed Nathan the diagnosis and the upgrade idea. Close the loop out loud: Your unit is doing everything it can — the fight is the heat and the insulation. Let me get our comfort specialist out to price what actually solves it: insulation, ventilation, or more capacity. No cost to look. Are you around Tuesday? Same visit, same diagnosis, one more sentence.

Weak

Ten years old, $23,000 spent, issues flagged in May — and no path offered

Customer"this unit was put in about nine years ago… for $23,000." … "there were some issues that they said there were some issues up there, but nothing that had to be done immediately."
Nathan"I'll go through all that and evaluate what they said because even though it wasn't under immediate, it is possible that with the heat that we're seeing… we're starting to see effects of those things."

The fix The question was right; the follow-through is missing from the tape. A ten-year-old system with deferred items and a thermostat that has fallen off the network is a comfort-specialist conversation, not just a repair quote. Pair the two on the invoice: Here's what fixes it today, and here's the free second opinion on what starting over would cost, so you're choosing with both numbers.

Moderate

Option C was offered as a yes/no question, three times

Nathan"did you guys want to compare the pricing between what it would be to replace?" … "just to confirm, just so I know before I place this order — so you didn't want to compare the pricing between the replacement and these items?"

The fix Persistence is the right instinct; the phrasing invites a no each time. Make it assumptive and free-standing: While I order the motor, I'm going to have our specialist swing by so you have the replacement number on paper — costs nothing and you can throw it away. He's out this way tomorrow morning. Same offer, three times better odds.

Weak

Two hours in a home, an authorised repair, and no five-star ask

PatternFour calls, none seeded and none requested — including the McLin visit, which ended with signatures and genuine warmth.

The fix Attach it to the expectations speech he already gives: …and at the end I'll ask how I did. If it's not five stars, tell me before I leave. One clause inside a sentence he says on every call anyway.
