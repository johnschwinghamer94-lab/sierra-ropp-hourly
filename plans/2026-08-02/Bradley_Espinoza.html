5

What Bradley did right — real strength, build on it

  • He never guessed at a replacement price. Three separate asks, three clean hand-offs to the specialist. This is the SILO process, and here is the receipt: the customer signed because of it.
  • He got the specialist out during the visit, not after it. we can get him out here now while we're here. That way he has all the information. Same-day beats same-week every time.
  • He diagnosed by elimination, out loud. Capacitor tested good on both sides, so the fault had to be the compressor — the customer could follow every step of the reasoning.
  • He flagged the second system before it failed. this compressor is also on its way out at 83.2 against an 82 limit. That is the whole-home picture, not a one-unit fix.
  • The human read was exceptional. A widower in his late wife's attic surrounded by her clothes — Bradley slowed down, listened, and did not rush him. That is why the trust was there when the price question came.

The fix He is unusually good at getting people to talk — that is the hard half. Spend the first ten minutes of it on the system: Before I put a gauge on anything, walk me through it — when did you first notice, and who's been out here before me? The rapport still happens; it just carries the diagnosis with it.

The fix Option C works best next to a written repair figure, not instead of one. Put the compressor-plus-R-22 repair on the invoice with its price, the upcoming second-system risk under it, and the specialist visit as the third line. The customer chooses between three things he can see — and the replacement case makes itself.
