  • He stayed professional through an 87-minute call with a hostile, profane customer. He did not escalate once.

The three gaps

each one in David's own calls
Weak

"Well, we'll see" — asked directly for a commitment, he gave none

Customer"Yeah, I am going to have air tonight, right?"
David"Well, we'll see… it really depends on what's going on. And then also too, it depends on the landlord."
Customer, moments later"Oh, she gonna fix this s***. I don't care what it costs her. I'm living here."

The fix She told him the decision-maker would pay. That is a lead, not an obstacle. Then let's get her the number today — I'll have our specialist out to price the replacement so your landlord can approve it tonight instead of next week. Deferring to the landlord ends the call; getting the landlord a price starts the job.

Weak

The customer offered her own theory and he pressed pause

Customer"So you have an idea. Because it keeps happening, the same issue."
David"I know I have an idea, but I'm gonna have to double check… I just don't want to give you false information right now."

The fix The instinct — don't guess — is right, and it does not require going silent. Trade information instead: What are you thinking it is? Because I already found rust on your indoor coil, and that's usually part of this story. Let me confirm it and I'll show you. The customer stays engaged, and he still verifies before committing.

Weak

101 minutes on tape and not one option built

David, first call"go build up some options for you… and then we don't do anything without your approval."
David, second call"It's just, it's a 36-year-old system, 35-year-old system, and it's, it's old."

The fix A promise to build options is a debt. Every call ends with a written page in front of the customer — immediate, upcoming, and Option C — even when the answer is "there is no repair." Especially then: no parts available on a 35-year-old system is the strongest replacement case there is.

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Week 1
Drill: Every call ends with a written options page. Immediate, upcoming, Option C — no exceptions, including calls with no repairable fault.
Proof it worked: An options page on every invoice this week; manager reviews two.
Week 2
Week 2
Drill: Rentals and absent owners. The moment a landlord or property manager is named, the response is "let's get them a price today," not "it depends on them."
Proof it worked: Two landlord-decision calls this week end with a specialist appointment or a quote sent.
Week 3
Week 3
Drill: Give the expectations speech from the first call — evaluation, report, options, approval, five-star seed — on every visit, word for word.
Proof it worked: Manager hears the full speech on every recording sampled.

Bottom line

David gets the two hardest things right — customers trust him quickly, and he does not bend on safety or on guessing. What is missing is the last twenty minutes of the visit. He promised to build options on one call and ran out of tape; on the other he had a 35-year-old system, a motivated landlord and 87 minutes, and left with nothing on paper. Finish every call the way he opened the first one: here is what I found, here are your choices, and here is what happens next.
