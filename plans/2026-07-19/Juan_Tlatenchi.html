1

The one-line verdict

Why it didn't flip

The customer handed him a deadline. He handed back a form.

What actually happened

On the Guevara call the customer explained his position without being asked: the home insurance will pay if he brings them an estimate, the adjuster's technician cancelled, and yo el problema es que yo ya no tengo en el hotel donde me estoy quedando, tengo nomás hasta el jueves — he is out of his hotel Thursday. Juan quoted the leak repair, mentioned that a replacement quote needs a different person, and when the customer said déjeme pensarlo, took the signature: déjame sacar tu firma y le voy a poner que quieres pensarlo por ahorita.

What it was

That was not a stall — it was a buying window with a hard expiry and someone else paying. The FSG move is to name the deadline back and book against it: the advisor comes today, the estimate goes to the insurer tonight, the decision happens before Thursday. Juan even had the opening — he had already said ellos pueden venir hoy. He offered it as an option and then let the customer opt out of it.

5

What Juan did right — real strength, build on it

  • The system sketch is the best teaching on the board. He announces the intent — lo hago para que sea más fácil seguir — and then walks compressor to line set to coil to blower to filter to thermostat, so every later finding has a place to sit.
  • Full bilingual service with no loss of quality. The technical depth in Spanish matches the English calls exactly: tolerances, amp draws, refrigerant weights, why the leak can't be soldered where it sits.
  • Honest about the limits of the diagnosis. With the blower dead he could not read operating pressures and said so plainly rather than inventing certainty — and the customer respected it enough to ask a sharper follow-up.
  • Complete, calm expectation-setting. Permission, approval, five-star and time constraints, on a seven-o'clock Saturday call after a long day.
  • No fear selling, ever. Two customers in financially exposed positions and not one scare line on tape.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Book the deadline
Drill: Whenever a customer states a date, a claim, a hotel checkout or a payer, Juan repeats it back and proposes a specific time against it before saying anything else. Manager role-plays five deadline scenarios in both languages.
Proof it worked: Every tape containing a customer deadline also contains a proposed day and time.
Week 2
Option C on the page
Drill: The advisor visit is written into the option set on every gradeable call, priced at no cost, presented alongside the repairs — never after them.
Proof it worked: Three options visible on every presentation this week, one of them the advisor.
Week 3
Work the deferral
Drill: Banned: taking a signature on a think-about-it form before asking one question. Ten reps on déjeme pensarlo — every one answered with "¿Qué es lo que quiere pensar, el precio o el momento?"
Proof it worked: No tape where a deferral is documented without a question first.
8

What we owe Juan Tlatenchi

  • Tell him the teaching is elite. The system sketch and the bilingual depth are assets the team should be copying. He needs to hear that the fix is one beat at the end, not a rebuild of how he runs a call.
  • A Spanish-language objection script. He is doing this work in two languages with rebuttals written in one. Déjeme pensarlo, está muy caro and the insurance-claim path all need lines he can say naturally.
  • Clarity on insurance-claim jobs. Who writes the estimate, how fast, and what the insurer needs on it. This will come up again and he should not have to improvise it.
  • A rule for no-decision-maker visits. Techs need explicit permission to stop and reschedule rather than burning an hour on a house where nobody can approve work.
Bottom line

Juan does the hardest ninety percent of the job better than most of this board — the welcome, the questions, the drawing, the honesty, in whichever language the customer needs. He loses calls in the last two minutes, where a documented deferral feels like professionalism and is actually a surrender. One customer told him his insurer would pay and his hotel ran out Thursday. Next time, that sentence gets answered with a day and a time, not a signature line.
