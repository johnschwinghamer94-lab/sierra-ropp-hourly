1

The one-line verdict

Why it didn't close

The customer said yes. Noah asked him to wait.

What actually happened

Noah diagnosed a completely dead motor start device and quoted it on the spot. The customer answered All right. We definitely need that. Noah's reply: Absolutely. But, I mean, I don't wanna charge you something and be like, hey, this is it, without looking at the whole system. Minutes later the customer asked about American Home Shield, and Noah said If that's what you want to do, I'll go ahead and pack up. No report was written and no options were presented.

What moves it

The integrity instinct is right — he doesn't want to sell a part and leave. But the customer had already bought it. Take the yes, then finish the evaluation: Done — I'll get that on the invoice now. Give me twenty minutes to check the rest so we're not back here in a week, and then I'll show you everything in one report. Yes first, thoroughness second. The order is the whole difference between this call and a close.

5

What Noah did right — real strength, build on it

  • The most complete welcome on the board (Setting Expectations — Strong). All six LTOAT5 beats plus the goal question, four calls out of four. Nobody else on this day was consistent across every call.
  • Discovery that doesn't feel like an interview (Required Questions — Strong). In the summertime, how high is the power bill spiking? and what's the highest bill you guys have had? came out mid-conversation and got real numbers back. That is the hardest required question in the rubric and he asks it naturally.
  • Physical proof before photos (Show Pictures — Strong). I brought you out here because I just wanted you to see everything before I show you a bunch of pictures. Deliberate sequencing — the customer sees it with their own eyes first, then the images reinforce it.
  • Buying signals arriving unprompted. On one call: if you tell me that we need new ones, that's honestly not the end of the world. We kind of knew something like that was coming. On another: if we go with the new system, when would it get put in? His teaching is producing these. The gap is entirely in what happens next.

The three gaps

each one in Noah's own calls
Weak

Gap 1 — "We definitely need that" — the yes he argued with

Customer"How much is that?" — Noah: "I think it's like 550 bucks." — Customer: "All right. We definitely need that."
Noah"Absolutely. But, I mean, I don't wanna charge you something and be like, hey, this is it, without looking at the whole system."
Customer, minutes later"Hey, so I just need to get it started. I mean, if you gotta put that part in to get it started." — and again Noah deferred to finishing the evaluation.

The fix The customer said yes twice. The instinct behind the deferral is honourable — Noah doesn't want to charge for a part and leave a broken system — but he expressed it as a reason to wait instead of a reason to be thorough. Reverse the order: "Perfect, I'll get that on the ticket right now. Give me twenty minutes to check the rest so we're not back here in a week — then I'll show you everything in one report." Capture the yes, then earn it. Nothing about that is less honest.

Weak

Gap 2 — "I'll go ahead and pack up" — asking permission to fail

Customer"I got American home shield. If I call them and just have them…" — Noah: "Yeah, but I mean, I don't think they'll be out here today."
Customer"Yeah, I'm probably gonna do that." — Noah: "If that's what you want to do, I'll go ahead and pack up."
Noah, again"You sure you want me to wrap everything up?" — and then: "If we have to come back out here, you'll be charged this time."

The fix He had the right argument and used it once, weakly, as a story about someone else. Make it about this customer, in this house, tonight: "Totally your call. Here's the one thing I'd want you to know — they can't dispatch today, so you're looking at three or four nights in this heat, and the second visit costs you the diagnostic again. I can have you cold in an hour. Want me to just do it?" Then be quiet. And never end with a question that invites the customer to confirm the loss.

Moderate

Gap 3 — "You guys are expensive" met with a defence instead of a question

Customer"You guys are expensive."
Noah"I know, I know we are. But it's done right the first time. Honestly."
Same call, minutes later"Did the handyman get a city inspected?… So who ran the electric to it?… I know you save money at the beginning, I understand it, but at the same time…" — to a customer who had just said "I can't afford another one."

The fix Both moments make the same mistake: defending Sierra instead of getting curious about the customer. Price pushback is information, not an attack — "Fair. Can I ask what you're comparing it to? I'd rather know what you're weighing than guess." And when a customer has already told you money is tight, the past-decisions lecture costs trust and buys nothing. Empathy, question, help — in that order, every time.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Take the yes
Drill: Rule for the week: when a customer agrees to anything, it goes on the ticket in that moment. Thoroughness is the sentence that comes after, never the reason to wait. Manager role-plays five "we definitely need that" scenarios.
Proof it worked: No tape where a stated yes is followed by a deferral.
Week 2
Never pack up
Drill: A written report and presented options on every gradeable call, including the ones the customer seems ready to end. Banned sentences: "I'll go ahead and pack up" and "you sure you want me to wrap everything up?"
Proof it worked: A report exists for every call on tape, no exceptions.
Week 3
Curious, not defensive
Drill: Price objections get a question, never a justification. Ten reps on "you guys are expensive" and "my home warranty covers it." Plus the five-star ask on every completed visit.
Proof it worked: Every objection on tape followed by a question; review asks on all completed visits.
8

What we owe Noah Weng

  • Tell him his front half is the standard. Four calls, four complete expectation sets, the best discovery questioning on the board. He should hear that clearly before he hears anything about the decision step, because the fix depends on him not changing any of it.
  • Explicit permission to take the money. Noah is deferring sales out of integrity, not laziness. A manager needs to say out loud that capturing an approved repair and then finishing the evaluation is the honest sequence — otherwise he will keep protecting the customer from a yes they already gave.
  • A home-warranty rebuttal, written down. Real dispatch timelines, the repeat-diagnostic cost, and one sentence he can say without it sounding like a pitch. This objection will come back this week.
  • Coaching on the customer's past decisions. The handyman lecture needs to be named and stopped — not because he was wrong, but because it costs him the trust his teaching just built.
Bottom line

Noah's problem is not skill and it is not effort — his welcome, his questioning and his teaching are the best on this board, and his calls are generating buying signals other techs never hear. He is losing calls in the one place nobody expects: after the customer has already said yes. Drill one reversal — take the yes first, be thorough second — and this close rate changes without him changing anything else he does.
