What Cole did right — real strength, build on it

  • The flip itself (Overcoming objections — Strong). A homeowner who opened with I don't want to double a ton of money into a new unit ended the visit with a comfort advisor 30 minutes out. That is the primary win, and Cole got it without pressure.
  • Textbook expectations (Setting Expectations — Strong). Look, thorough evaluation, options, approval, today, five-star — all six, out loud, in the first five minutes, plus any time constraints today, anytime you got to kick me out by?
  • Honest pricing under pressure (Say It — Strong). It's R22. It's over 10 grand and four to six weeks… if we can get one. He told the truth even when the truth killed the repair — and that honesty is exactly why the third option landed.
  • He stayed. Ladder still set, walked the advisor through the attic himself: I'll show him where everything's at, and then we'll sit down and give you all your options. The handoff didn't leak.

The three gaps

each one in Cole's own calls
Weak

Gap 1 — The three critical questions never got asked

What he asked"How long have you guys owned the home?" · "Are you guys from Vegas?" · "what's your ultimate goal for today?"
What he never askedHow often are you changing your filters? · Which rooms get hotter or colder than the rest? · What do you keep the thermostat at in the summer — and what does a standard power bill look like at that setting?

The fix This is the automatic fail on an otherwise excellent call. The energy analysis in the report said the system was running the power bill up — but Cole never had the homeowner's own bill number to put next to it. Ask the thermostat question and the bill follow-up before you go to the attic, and by the time you present, the replacement conversation is arithmetic the customer did himself. Same three questions, every call, before tools come out.

Moderate

Gap 2 — The homeowner asked "can you fix it?" four times before he understood the answer

Customer, repeatedly"I mean, something that can be resolved today or no?" … "So, but the repair. You can't do the repairs?" … "which you can do today or no?" … "I guess I'm just confused."
Cole"You can't put the A2L inside that system." … "the bare minimum, if you went to the new stuff, is the whole outside unit and then the coil."

The fix The refrigerant-transition answer is genuinely complicated, and Cole led with the jargon (A2L, R22, coil match) instead of the outcome. Lead with the sentence that answers the question actually being asked: "No — not today, and not this week. The part is four to six weeks out if it exists at all. So your real choice is wait six weeks in the heat, or get a replacement number today." Then explain the refrigerant. Answer first, physics second.

7

Three-week training plan

Week 1
Three questions
Drill: The three critical questions asked out loud on every call before tools come out of the van — filters, hot/cold rooms, thermostat plus the power-bill follow-up. Write them on the back of the tablet if that's what it takes.
Proof it worked: Every report next week contains the homeowner's own bill number next to the energy analysis.
Week 2
Answer first
Drill: When a customer asks a yes/no question, answer it in one sentence before any explanation. Manager role-plays the R22 scenario three times; Cole has to lead with the timeline, not the refrigerant.
Proof it worked: No call where the same question gets asked twice.
Week 3
Close the loop
Drill: The five-star ask, tied back to the promise he already made in the expectations. Ten reps out loud with a manager until it stops feeling like a request and starts sounding like a check-in.
Proof it worked: A review ask on every completed visit, and reviews naming him by name.

What we owe Cole Pantol

  • Fast advisor dispatch, every time. He held the customer on site and handed off in person. That only works if the advisor actually shows in 30 minutes — protect the behavior by protecting the response time.

Cole did the hardest thing in the FSG model and made it look easy: he heard "I'm selling, I'm not spending" and turned it into a booked visit instead of an argument. Nothing in this plan should read as a knock on that. The whole gap is the first five minutes — three questions he didn't ask — and it's the cheapest fix on this board. Ask the three, keep doing everything else exactly as he did it here.
