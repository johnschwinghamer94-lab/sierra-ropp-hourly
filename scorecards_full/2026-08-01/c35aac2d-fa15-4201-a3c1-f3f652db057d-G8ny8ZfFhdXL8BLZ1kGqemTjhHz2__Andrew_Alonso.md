## Expanded Coaching Advice

### Strengths to Lean Into

1. **System Age as Permission Structure**
   - You correctly identified 15 years as the hinge point. Customers aren't upset by repair costs; they're upset by uncertainty. By anchoring to "this system is at end of life," you give permission to spend on replacement.
   - Replicate: Always ask installation year *early*. Frame everything that follows through the lens of remaining useful life.

2. **Photo + Narrative Pairing**
   - Showing the rust on the coil alongside the explanation ("when it's the entire circle, I know it's about to..." line 4727) worked because the customer saw it, not just heard it. Rust becomes destiny.
   - Replicate: Lean hard on "here's what I see" + "here's what that means" + "here's what happens if we ignore it." Three steps, three senses.

3. **Deference to the Sales Role**
   - You stayed in lane as the diagnostician and brought in the sales coordinator. "That's above my pay grade" (5686) sounds humble and builds trust that you're not pushing product. It paradoxically makes the replacement recommendation *more* credible because you're not financially motivated by the upsell.
   - Replicate: Use this posture on bigger decisions. "Here's what I found, here's the risk, here's who helps you optimize the choice."

### Areas for Next Calls

1. **Tighten Explanation Sequence**
   - Front-load the critical context (age, prior repairs, rust risk).
   - Save technical education for moments when it directly supports the decision at hand.
   - Example: Skip EPA refrigerant history unless customer asks "Why not just refill it?"

2. **Shorten Decision Loops**
   - By minute 120, you'd already won. The remaining 35 minutes included excellent asides (career talk, kids, warehouse work stories) that built rapport but didn't move the outcome closer.
   - Coaching: Recognize when the decision is made and offer "Let me get the ball rolling with sales; they'll reach out within the hour." Keeps momentum.

3. **Lead with Worst Case + Best Case**
   - Instead of "it might leak in a year," say: "Worst case, it leaks in 2 months and costs $7,200 to replace the coil. Best case, we swap the motor today for $1,227, and you get a year of peace of mind. Sales will show you the replacement option so you can compare."
   - That frames both paths as rational, reduces anxiety, and gets faster buys-in.
