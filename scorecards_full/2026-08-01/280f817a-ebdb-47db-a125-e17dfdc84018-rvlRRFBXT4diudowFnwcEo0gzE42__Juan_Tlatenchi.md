## Top Gaps & Coaching Advice

### Gap 1: Bundled All Findings Into One Decision (Major)
Juan presented a comprehensive dual-unit assessment but never segmented urgency or size of repair options. It's like saying "your car needs an oil change, new tires, and the transmission might need rebuilding" without separating the $50 from the $2,000.

**Coaching:**
After the assessment, articulate the decision hierarchy:
1. **Tonight's win:** "The upstairs capacitor is reading low and should be replaced—that's about $200-$300 and takes 30 minutes. We can do that right now while I'm here."
2. **Discussion point:** "The outdoor unit has a minor oil stain that suggests a potential leak in the future. That's something to discuss with your spouse, but it's not urgent tonight."
3. **Follow-up:** "Once you two talk it through, give me a call and we can schedule any larger work. Here's my direct line."

### Gap 2: No Reassurance on the "Morning Light" Decision (Moderate)
The customer explicitly said they wanted to review in the morning and with family present. Juan honored that but didn't de-risk it. He could have said: "That's smart. One thing I'll note—the capacitor issue on the upstairs doesn't get worse overnight, so you've got time to decide. The oil stain is something we want to monitor, but it's stable."

**Coaching:**
When customers defer to "morning" or "with family," use that as a segmentation moment:
- Confirm what IS urgent (nothing, in this case).
- Confirm what CAN wait (the bigger decisions).
- Set up the callback: "I'll have you get back to me by [time] tomorrow, and we'll finalize the plan."

## Summary

Juan demonstrated strong technical mastery and excellent customer rapport, but missed the segmentation moment that could have converted a "FIX" into a "WIN." You gave the customer *all* the information but didn't guide them to *a* decision. The gap is in bundling findings into a single decision node instead of breaking them into "do tonight, discuss tomorrow, monitor for now."

The coaching here is not about being more pushy—it's about being more *organized*. When you lead with the smallest, highest-confidence repair first, you build momentum and frame the bigger questions as secondary. Next time, isolate the capacitor (or equivalent "quick win") and close that. Let the harder decisions cascade naturally from there.
