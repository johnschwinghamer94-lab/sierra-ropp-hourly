## Top Gaps & Coaching

**Primary Gap:** Missing the lead-creation moment  
When customer mentioned "the split system not cooling one room well," this was the door opening for an upgrade conversation. David correctly knew not to quote the job himself (SILO repair tech), but instead of asking customer to call, he should have: (1) expressed empathy for the cooling issue, (2) offered to schedule a Comfort Advisor appointment right then, (3) set expectation that the advisor would come alongside him or soon after to assess and quote the main splits. This keeps the customer engaged and in control, and creates a TGL.

**Secondary Gap:** No 5-star review request  
After completing the tune-up and payment, David did not ask for a review or referral.

## Expanded Coaching Notes

David showed solid technical competence—thorough inspection, good diagnosis, calm demeanor, and correct protocol adherence (declined to quote outside his scope). The call's length (76 min) reflects genuine engagement, though much time was spent on personal conversation (Mexico, family, career history) that, while building rapport, diluted focus on the customer's service needs.

The critical miss: David heard a customer pain point (main splits not cooling) but treated it as "call sales" rather than "let me set you up for an expert visit." In SILO's model, the tech who triages the upsell opportunity is the one who creates it. A one-sentence Comfort Advisor appointment ("Let me schedule our specialist to come look at that system with you—they can give you a real quote") would have flipped this call and validated the customer's concern in one move.

**Next call:** Practice the warm handoff. When a customer mentions an upgrade or expansion need, use it to lock in an appointment, not to deflect.
