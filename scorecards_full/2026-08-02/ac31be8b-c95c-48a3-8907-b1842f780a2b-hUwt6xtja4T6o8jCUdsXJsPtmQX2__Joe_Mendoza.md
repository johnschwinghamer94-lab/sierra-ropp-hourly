## Expanded Coaching

**The Turn: Price Objection → TGL**

Around 3,800 lines into the call, customer said: "Seems like a lot though for a motor and a cap. I think that should be like $800 at the most for you guys' time."

This was the critical moment. Joe could have:
1. Defended the pricing (risk: customer walks)
2. Pushed the repair despite objection (risk: customer agrees, calls back unhappy when compressor fails)
3. Pivoted to a better option

Joe chose #3. Instead of explaining why the price was fair, he immediately acknowledged ("No, I hear you") and reframed the entire decision: "However, we're looking at almost $2,000 into this thing to try to get you... but it's gonna keep having issues. Your end goal is to move out here full-time, and your goal was just to get it up and running and deal with this later, which I completely understand that mindset. However... the system's down."

He then offered the specialist quote, which solved the sticking point and moved the customer from "no" to "that might be worth doing."

**Next call:** Watch for this pattern on price objections—the pivot to information/expert consultation often succeeds where price-justification fails.
