## Coaching Notes

### The Flip Opportunity (The Single Moment That Mattered)

The customer created an opening when they expressed frustration about having no AC: **"And you're gonna tell me there's no other way you can get this working tonight?"**

This was the moment to pivot from "I can't fix it tonight" to "Here's how we solve this tomorrow." Instead, David responded with "I can't do nothing tonight" and moved toward putting the breaker panel back.

**What should have happened:** David should have said something like: "I understand you're without air on a hot day. I can't get parts tonight, but what I *can* do is schedule you with our specialist tomorrow to look at your options—whether a repair makes sense or if replacement is the better path given the system's age. When does tomorrow work for you?"

This would have:
1. Acknowledged the customer's urgency (empathy)
2. Clarified David's constraint (honesty)
3. Offered a concrete next step (option C)
4. Created a TGL and kept the conversation moving toward resolution

### Secondary Gap

David identified that the system is 35-36 years old with an ancient mercury thermostat. He said "I don't have the parts for that"—but he never explained *why* that matters or what needs to happen next. A stronger approach would have been to educate: "This system is too old for me to get parts fast. Given its age, we should have a specialist look at whether repair or replacement makes more sense for your situation."
