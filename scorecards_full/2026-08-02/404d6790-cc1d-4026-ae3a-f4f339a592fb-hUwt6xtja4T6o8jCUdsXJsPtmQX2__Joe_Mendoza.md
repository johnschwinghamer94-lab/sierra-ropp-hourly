## Expanded Coaching

**The Turn: Uncertainty → Home-Inspection Framing**

Around the midpoint of the call, after presenting Option A ($1,784) and Option B ($8,100 future repair), the customer revealed the real concern: "Because we're not sure what we're going to do with the house anyway."

This was the critical objection. Many techs would accept this as a walk: "Okay, well when you know, give us a call." Joe did something smarter.

He reframed the uncertainty as an inspection liability: "This is most likely going to come up during inspection with the sale of the home. So just keep that in mind." This was the RSA-level reconnection moment. He transformed the objection from "we don't know" into "you will need to know by inspection time."

Then he offered Option C (equipment specialist consultation) as the escape hatch: "That's why I have option C, which would be speaking with an equipment specialist about updating the comfort system before we start throwing in good money over bad."

This three-part move—acknowledge uncertainty, reframe as inspection risk, offer expert consultation—kept the conversation forward and secured authorization on Option A.

**Next call:** When a customer says "we might sell," "we might renovate," or "we're not sure what we're doing," test this pattern: name the future trigger point (inspection, closing, etc.) and offer the specialist path. Uncertainty often converts when you name the moment it becomes certain.
