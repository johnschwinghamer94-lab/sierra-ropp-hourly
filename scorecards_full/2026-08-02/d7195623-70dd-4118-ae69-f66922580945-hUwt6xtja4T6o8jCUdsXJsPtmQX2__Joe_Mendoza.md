## Coaching Tip (Expanded)

**WIN — Overcame customer skepticism about plumbing by using real-world examples and technical reasoning to earn agreement to schedule inspection.**

**The Specific Moment:**
When Frank expressed doubt ("I don't see it being a leak or anything like that"), he was showing the classic "dismissive objection"—the customer had made up their mind it wasn't a plumbing issue. Joe's move here was textbook and teachable:

1. **Empathy first:** "No, no, no, no, no, no, no. I, I understand that."
2. **Reframe:** "It's just stagnant water doesn't—"
3. **Specific, relatable example:** "In the Asian communities... they leave for 3, 4 months at a time. They'll completely drain out the toilets to make sure that there's no water in them so it's not just sitting there."

This example does three things:
- It de-stigmatizes the issue (not a broken home, not a leak, just normal preventative thinking)
- It makes the diagnosis credible (Joe clearly has seen this pattern many times)
- It makes it relatable (customer recognizes the logic immediately)

Frank's response was immediate agreement—he stops objecting and starts understanding. By the call's end, he's relieved: "We can now sleep in peace, right?"

**What to Repeat:**
When a customer dismisses a recommendation, don't restate the recommendation louder. Educate with a real-world example that makes them the hero (not the problem). Joe's Asian community example works because it implies "smart homeowners do this," not "your home is broken."
