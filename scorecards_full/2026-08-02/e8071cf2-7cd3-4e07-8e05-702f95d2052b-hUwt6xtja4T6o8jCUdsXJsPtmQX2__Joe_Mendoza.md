## Top Gaps

### Gap 1: Missed the Strategic Question When Emotions Were High
**The moment:** When she said "I just paid you about almost four grand less than a couple months ago. And now they're trying to charge me another five grand," this was the critical opportunity to flip the call.

**What was missing:** A question to separate diagnosis skepticism from price skepticism. Should have asked: "What's most important to you in getting a second opinion—understanding what's really broken, or knowing what options exist if it's the compressor?"

**Why it matters:** She may not trust the diagnosis (wants to verify the actual problem), or she may trust it but can't afford $5k (wants less expensive solutions). Without asking, Joe can't tailor the inspection to her real concern.

---

### Gap 2: No Conditional Option-Building
**What should have happened:** Joe should have framed the inspection path with conditional outcomes: "Depending on what I find—whether it's the compressor, one of those earlier parts acting up, or something else—we'll have different options to discuss. Let me nail down exactly what it is, and then we'll talk through what makes sense for you."

**Why it matters:** This builds anticipation and sets the stage for multiple paths forward, not just "I'll let you know."
