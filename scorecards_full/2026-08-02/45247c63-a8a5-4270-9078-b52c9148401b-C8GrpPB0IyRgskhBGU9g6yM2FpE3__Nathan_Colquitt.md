## Coaching

**What Nathan Did Well:**  
Exceptional diagnostic discovery. He didn't skip steps, didn't make the customer feel blamed, and connected the current thermostat issue to a deeper system risk (rust on the coil, summer heat stress). The customer trusts him to investigate fully.

**The Missed Moment (Why No Close):**  
After the diagnostics and education, Nathan never shifted to the decision frame. He should have said something like: "Okay, so here's what I'm seeing. We've got a thermostat that can't stay connected—which might be fixable—but we also have some rust on the indoor coil from your May service that could be getting worse in this heat. Let me run a couple quick tests outside and in the attic so we know exactly what we're dealing with. Once I do, I'll have 2–3 options for you: we can do A, B, or C. Should I call you as soon as I know, or do you want to sit down and talk through them together?" That moves the customer from passive ("tell me what's wrong") to active ("I'm choosing my path").

**Action for Next Call:**  
Once you've got your findings, lead with: "Here's what I found. Here are your options—here's why I'd recommend Option [X]—what feels right to you?" instead of "I found some issues, now I'm going to present options later."

## Summary

Nathan excels at building trust and doing thorough diagnostic work. The gap isn't in capability—it's in the sales funnel. He didn't move the customer from "interested in learning what's wrong" to "committed to fixing it now." Solid foundation; needs to close the loop by presenting options and asking for a decision while he still has the customer's attention and momentum.
