## Coaching Notes

**What Matthew did well:**
- **Rapport & curiosity:** The Vegas conversation wasn't filler—it built trust that made the customer receptive to a long, technical discussion.
- **Transparency:** Matthew was refreshingly honest about what he didn't know ("I don't 100% know... I've never experienced redoing ductwork") and about probabilities ("It's a solid 50/50").
- **Technical depth:** Explained complex concepts (voltage distribution, filtration, airflow, ABC rule) in ways the customer could understand and build confidence.
- **Customer-centric curiosity:** Listened when the customer articulated their real goal ("better flow to the beds") and educated toward that need.

**Where the call fell short:**
1. **Didn't segment the sale.** The $2k decision felt binary (yes or no). The customer wanted to solve airflow *soon* but couldn't commit to $1.8k–$2.2k. A quick win (filters for ~$180) + deferred decision (voltage switch) would have closed something and built momentum.
2. **Didn't lean into urgency/consequence.** Matthew's "it's 50/50" was honest but not motivating. He could have said: "If that pad completely fails, you're down to emergency calls in 115°F heat—that's a $10k decision with no time to shop."
3. **Missed the Comfort Advisor close.** Customer asked about new units; Matthew deflected to a specialist *without booking*. That should have been: *"I can get a comfort advisor out here this week—takes 30 minutes, and it's free. You'll know what replacement looks like, and then you can decide on maintenance vs. replacement."*

**30-second huddle version:**
"Matthew, your technical knowledge and honesty won the customer's trust. Next time, when they say 'I don't have the money,' lead with the filter upgrade as an immediate win and defer the big decision. And if they ask about newer units, book the Comfort Advisor *before* they leave."
