1. **Missing Options Framework:** Cole explained three separate failures but bundled them as pricing discussion rather than positioning strategic options: (A) individual repairs with timeline risk, (B) replace downstairs unit, (C) specialist assessment of both units for efficiency/warranty strategy. 2. **Lost Reconnection:** When customer asked "expensive one?" Cole confirmed expense but didn't reconnect to their original goal ("everything runs better") or urgency ("How long can these units actually keep running?"). This would have positioned specialist referral as necessary, not optional.

## Full Summary

Cole delivered expert technical diagnosis with excellent use of analogies (heart/blood pressure, car engine) that made complex issues understandable. His assessment phase was methodical and thorough. However, the decision conversation lacked structure—three major component failures are presented, but Cole moved directly to "I'll get you prices" without positioning strategic options or reconnecting to customer urgency. The customer will likely flip to a specialist estimate given the complexity, but Cole missed an opportunity to explicitly frame it as "This is beyond quick fixes—let's get our specialist out to look at both units and give you a long-term strategy" before ending the call.

**Coaching Tip:** After diagnosing multiple major components, pause and say: "What I'm seeing here are three big issues that need specialist attention to get you a real game plan. Let me get our comfort advisor out here to evaluate both units together so you understand your options—does tomorrow work?" This frames the specialist handoff as essential strategy conversation, not cost avoidance.
