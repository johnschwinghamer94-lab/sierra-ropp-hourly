## Expanded Coaching Notes

**What Cole Did Exceptionally Well:**

1. **Built unshakeable credibility through validation.** Cole didn't just talk—he brought the husband into the inspection, showed him the evidence (the blower motor buildup, the zero readings on the capacitor), and let him see the problem. When the customer started to worry about throwing money away on a starter, the husband's own experience ("One time it might have just been a capacitor, but then we had to change the evaporator oil and then we had to change the condenser") made Cole's recommendation feel inevitable rather than pushy.

2. **Explained the "snowball effect" clearly.** The most dangerous moment in an old-system scenario is when a tech says "We need a new compressor" without context. Cole's metaphor—"It's kind of like a snowball effect... there's still 17 other components inside of the system that are going to be 17 years of age"—made it visceral. The customer understood why $500 for a starter could become $5,000+ in follow-up failures.

3. **Pivoted to a "yes" instead of defending.** When the customer said "That's too much, I don't have that much money," Cole didn't push the repair or defend the cost. He said, "We have financing." That one shift moved the customer from "I'm stuck" to "I have options." The specialist handoff took the pressure off Cole and made the customer feel they were getting proper guidance, not being upsold.

**To Take to Your Next Call:**

- **Validate first, explain second.** When a customer expresses a concern (like cost), mirror it back ("I don't blame you, that's a lot of money") before diving into why it's necessary. It defuses defensiveness.
- **Bring the decision-maker into the inspection.** Cole got the husband upstairs, showed him the evidence, and let him draw his own conclusion. That's worth more than any sales pitch.
- **Have a financing story ready.** The moment you mention a price above $1,000, have a clear handoff plan. Cole's "equipment specialist comes out the same day, handles financing, and sits down with you" is a great template.
