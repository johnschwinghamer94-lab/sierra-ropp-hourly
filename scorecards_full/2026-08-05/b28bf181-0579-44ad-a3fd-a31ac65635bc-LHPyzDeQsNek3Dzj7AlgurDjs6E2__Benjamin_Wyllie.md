## Top Gaps & Coaching

**Primary Miss:** Options presentation. After the diagnostic education and pressure reading explanation, Ben needed to transition explicitly to options. He could have said: "Based on what I've found, we have a leak causing refrigerant loss. Once I complete the leak search upstairs, I'll know whether we're looking at a repair or component replacement. But let me ask you: if it turns out the coil they installed isn't compatible and a full system makes more sense long-term, is that something you'd consider?" This opens the door to Option C / Comfort Advisor conversation before the call ends.

**Why it matters:** Rita is clearly willing to invest in a real solution ("I just want to know what's wrong. Keep the house cool"). She never got to hear the actual options or next steps. The call ends mid-diagnosis, leaving her hanging.

---

## Summary

Ben did excellent diagnostic and educational work, built real empathy with a frustrated customer, and established trust through transparency. The gap is structural: he never shifted from "I'm investigating" to "Here's what I found, and here are your paths forward." 

**Coaching Tip:** Front-load the decision stage. After diagnosing and educating, always frame at least two paths (repair if accessible vs. full replacement discussion) before or immediately after the diagnostic work, so customers know what to expect and feel ownership of the path forward.
