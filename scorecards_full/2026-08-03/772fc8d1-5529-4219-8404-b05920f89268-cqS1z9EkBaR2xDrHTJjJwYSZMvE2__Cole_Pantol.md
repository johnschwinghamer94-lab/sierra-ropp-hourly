### Decision: **Moderate**

Cole presented findings and options with good technical clarity, but failed to reconnect the deteriorating compressor trend to the customer's stated goal of tracking system degradation. When hesitation surfaced, no empathy-driven check-in question was deployed to overcome it.

**Strengths:**
- **Explained Technical Reality:** Clearly walked through why refrigerants can't be mixed ("It's like putting diesel in a gas car"), why new A2L parts won't work in old R22 systems, and why this caps the available repair path
- **Presented Multiple Paths:** Option A (no immediate repairs, future only), Option C (replace compressor + coils with matching R22 parts, $8,600+), and implied Option B (monitor and wait)
- **Fielded Technical Objections:** When customer asked "Can we just replace the coils?" Cole accurately explained the refrigerant incompatibility barrier

**Critical Gaps:**
- **No Reconnection at Decision Moment:** Customer's stated goal was to track degradation and know if it's getting worse. Cole confirmed it IS getting worse (compressor amps: 127 → 130 in 4 months, coil rust progressing). **This was the moment to say:** "I know you mentioned wanting to keep an eye on how bad this gets—I'm seeing it get worse. That changes the timeline. Would you feel better having a repair plan in place, or do you want to keep monitoring?"
- **No Empathy-Driven Check-In on Hesitation:** When customer said "I'll show the wife and we'll decide," Cole moved directly to "Want to schedule?" without first saying something like "I know this is a bigger investment than you expected—what's making you hesitate most: the cost, the timing, or something else?" That question would have revealed the real blocker and opened a path to address it.
- **Cost Objection Not Overcome:** Customer mentioned they're on solar ("We're on solar, so we pay virtually nothing") to justify not caring about the utility overpayment. Cole acknowledged but didn't reconnect: "I hear you—and since you won't see it on the utility bill, the real question is how much longer you want to keep this compressor limping along."

**Supporting Quotes (Missed Moment):**

### Missed Moment (In Detail)

After customer said "I'll show the wife and we'll decide," Cole had an open door to turn hesitation into a decision driver. The conversation should have gone:

**What Cole said:** [Moved to wrapping up; offered to send quote]  
**What Cole should have said:**
> "I get it—this is a decision to make together. But before she sees the quote, I want to flag something important: the compressor's power draw has been climbing since April—it was at 127, now it's at 130. That trend tells me it's getting weaker. Here's what I'd ask: How much longer are you planning to stay in this house? Because if it's several years, the real question isn't if this compressor fails, but when. And right now, parts are still available at these prices. Once this pops, we're in an emergency replacement with fewer options. What do you think?"

This move does two things:
1. **Reconnects** to the customer's stated goal ("keep track on how bad it is; is it getting worse?") — YES, it IS getting worse
2. **Asks a check-in question** that reveals the timeline (a key decision factor the customer hasn't surfaced yet)

## Summary

Cole crushed the Welcome—genuine rapport, clear expectations, and a credible expert presence. His Assessment was solid, with good discovery and thorough inspection work. But the Decision phase is where the call stalled: he presented the options and cost clearly, but failed to reconnect the deteriorating compressor to the customer's fear ("Is it getting worse?"), and when hesitation surfaced, he asked for a close without first understanding and addressing the real blocker.

A 20-second reconnection + timeline question at the moment of hesitation likely flips this to a scheduled estimate appointment (even if the wife says "let's wait"). Instead, the customer left with a quote, a "we'll talk and call you back" vibe, and no urgency.

**Key Lesson:** When a finding directly answers a customer's stated concern (compressor IS getting worse), pause and ask a timeline check-in before moving to the pitch. It keeps you in discovery mode, shows empathy, and often reveals why they're hesitating.
