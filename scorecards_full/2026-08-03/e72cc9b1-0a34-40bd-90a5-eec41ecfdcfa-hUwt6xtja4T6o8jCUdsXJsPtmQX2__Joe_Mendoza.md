## Coaching Advice (Full)

**FIX — The missed moment was when the customer said the system is still working. That was your opening to reconnect to the power bills with a check-in question.** When he said "it's working, I don't need to replace it," you should have asked: "I hear you—it's running now. With those power bills running $300-350 a month, what would happen to your budget if it breaks down during peak summer when you need it most?"

That's not a sales pitch. It's a genuine check-in that helps the customer *feel* the cost gap between paying high power bills indefinitely and taking a proactive replacement approach. Once they feel it, they either lean in to explore a replacement option, or they consciously re-commit to maintenance-only mode with full awareness. Either way, you've given them real information to make a choice.

The key pattern: whenever you see multiple cancelled appointments + an aging system + high utility costs, that's three signals pointing to the same pain. Connect them explicitly with a question, not just an explanation.

## Pattern Notes
This customer has a clear pattern: multiple replacement consultations offered over years, all cancelled. The reason: "it's working." This is a classic case where the customer isn't resisting replacement because of the solution—they're resisting because the pain doesn't feel *acute* enough yet. Your job isn't to pressure; it's to help them see the real trade-off (high bills + breakdown risk vs. one-time replacement cost). The check-in question does that.
