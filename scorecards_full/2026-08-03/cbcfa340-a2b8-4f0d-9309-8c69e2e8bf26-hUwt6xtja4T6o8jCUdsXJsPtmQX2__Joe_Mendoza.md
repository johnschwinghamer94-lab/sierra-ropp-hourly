### Assessment: STRONG
Joe conducted a thorough inspection and asked key diagnostic questions:

**Questions asked:**
- "How long have you owned the home, Kathy?" - context on equipment age
- "When did the AC [stop cooling]?" - problem timeline
- "Did your husband replace the outdoor unit in 2022?" - history and decision-making context
- "Any particular reason why only that was replaced and not the coil?" - understanding the previous HVAC contractor's work

**Diagnosis quality:**
Joe identified a complex problem: mismatched equipment (2002 indoor coil with 2022 outdoor compressor), a refrigerant restriction inside the indoor coil causing freezing on half the coil and bone-dry on the other half, and compressor overload (running at exactly 25 amps, at the rated limit).

He explained the technical root cause: the 2022 condenser installation likely didn't use nitrogen purging, creating the restriction. He also educated about refrigerant history (R-22 → 410A → 454B) and the future cost implications.

**Check-in quality:**
Joe engaged the decision-maker (the son, Brett) midway through the call to ensure understanding and buy-in. He re-explained the problem to Brett and verified he understood the two paths forward.

### Decision: STRONG
Joe presented two clear options and positioned them strategically:

**Option 1 (Repair):** $3,791
- Remove all refrigerant, replace filter dryer, replace metering device, weld system, pull vacuum, recharge with new refrigerant
- Addresses the immediate issue but leaves the underlying aged equipment

**Option 2 (Replace):** Equipment specialist quote pending (within 30 minutes)
- Brand new system, complete warranty, peace of mind
- Avoids future cascading repairs (rusted coil, fan motor leaks, etc.)

**Option positioning (the winning move):**
Joe didn't just present Option C—he justified it by articulating the future repair risk: "There are other components on the way out, like a rusted coil, fan motor, leaking oil, things of that nature, which right now are still working. I mean, by all means, but these are repairs that are going to come down the line, which I. I would hate for her to be in a situation where we fix it now. And before summer ends, she has to call me back out because something else happened."

Brett validated this logic: "Yeah, like fixing something now and they keep fixing stuff will probably cost more than what a whole new unit is." Joe confirmed: "Exactly."

**Reconnection:**
Joe connected back to the customer's core need: "I can't believe you need air. Of course. For sure." And he emphasized the peace-of-mind aspect of replacement: "She would have peace of mind of knowing that when she's in her home watching her show, she's going to be comfortable."

**Seal:**
Joe guaranteed fast specialist availability ("I can get one of my guys out here in the next 30 minutes") and positioned it as a compare-and-contrast decision with the son.
