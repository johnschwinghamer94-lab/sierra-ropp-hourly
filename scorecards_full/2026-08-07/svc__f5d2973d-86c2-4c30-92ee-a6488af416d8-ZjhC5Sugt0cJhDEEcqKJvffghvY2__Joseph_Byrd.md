### Diagnosis – **Strong**
Methodical troubleshooting and education:

**Initial problem isolation:**
- Customer reports AC inside won't blow air
- Tech resets circuit breaker, thermostat to test
- Narrows down: *"The FAU, you mean?"* – confirms forced air unit vs AC unit when customer confused about terms

**Filter education:**
- Identifies MERV 14 filters: *"You want 8 or 9 on that number. That's way too high filtration."*
- Explains mechanism: *"so they're high filtration, but to get that higher filtration, they gotta compact them really tightly... air can't as easily get through there"*
- Flags as potential contributing factor but doesn't definitively blame

**Root cause diagnosis:**
- Tests fan in multiple modes (fan mode, cooling mode)
- Describes behavior: *"it started to rev up, but then stopped, and then started to rev up again and then stopped completely"*
- Concludes: *"My assumption is blower motors, essentially the fan that blows all this, is going bad."*
- Later confirms: *"Your fan, it's... it's out. The one that's up there, it's an ECM fan, electronic fan. Something in the electronics burnt up and we need to order a new one."*

**Impact explanation:**
- Warned customer: *"make sure you're not running the AC because it can ruin the compressor... the fan's not turning on, so that won't shut off, so it'll wear it out and break it"* (risk of $4k+ additional damage)
- Explained why OEM ECM required: different from standard fans due to motherboard integration

**Strength:** Excellent teaching – took time to explain FAU terminology, filter MERV ratings, why workarounds won't work, why they can't install an off-the-shelf fan.

## Gaps & Coaching

### Top Gaps
1. **Options breadth:** Call was constrained by emergency circumstances (failed part, no substitute), so limited options were realistic. Tech did what was possible.

2. **Membership miss:** With a 3-year-old home and a failed component in extreme heat, this customer is a prime candidate for SAM (twice-yearly maintenance visits to catch issues early). Not pitched.

3. **Softer close:** "Sign here" vs. "Given the emergency situation, let's get you on our list for immediate callback as soon as the part ships – sound good?" – more assumptive closing would have reinforced urgency.

### What Worked
- **Crisis management:** Stayed calm and professional when delivering bad news (no same-day AC in 100-degree heat with elderly and 4 kids in home).
- **Workaround creativity:** Portable AC rental-return strategy bought customer comfort alternatives and showed empathy.
- **Technical credibility:** Detailed explanation of why it's a burnt ECM, not a generic fan, prevented customer from trying DIY fixes or cheaper alternatives.
- **Warranty clarity:** Explained parts are free, labor is negotiable, building trust.
- **Negotiation:** Secured $100 off even though parts were fixed by warranty, showing good faith.
