## Gaps & Coaching

**Primary Gap:** All-or-nothing pricing.  
When a customer hears $500 and hesitates, the most expensive move is to stand firm with one price. Always pre-stage options:
- *"The noise is the fan bearing. We can rebuild it for $300..."*
- *"...or replace the whole fan assembly for $500 with a 5-year warranty..."*
- *"...or I can temporarily adjust the fan speed to reduce noise today ($0) and you can decide on a full fix later."*

Devyn was saying no to $500, not no to fixing the noise. A $300 option might have closed on the spot.

**Secondary Gap:** No diagnosis→options bridge.  
The 70 minutes should have been used to dig into the noise root cause and build a tiered response. Instead, Chris seems to have jumped to a single recommendation. Diagnosis is only half the job; options-building is the other half.

---

## Coaching Tip

**FIX — When a customer hesitates on a $500 price, respond with a lower-cost option that addresses the primary concern first (e.g., start with just the noise-reduction work), rather than leaving them with an all-or-nothing choice.**
