## Outcome

## Coaching Notes

**What's Working:** Chris has excellent bedside manner and diagnostic skills. He's thorough, systematic, and friendly. Customers clearly trust him.

**The Miss:** When the customer mentioned the $500 budget ceiling, Chris had an opportunity to:
1. Ask questions about prioritization ("Which cabinet is most urgent?")
2. Present tiered options ("We could start with one cabinet now for less, then do the second later")
3. Explore financing ("We have payment plans that keep your monthly cost under $X")
4. Justify the investment ("This prevents bigger failures down the road")

Instead, he accepted the objection and handed it back to the customer ("just call us").

**Next Time:** When you hear a budget objection, present 2-3 options at different investment levels rather than walking away. Move the conversation from "yes or no to my full recommendation" to "which option works best for you now?"
