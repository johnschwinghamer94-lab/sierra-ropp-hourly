## Gaps & Coaching

**Primary Gap:** One-option trap.  
Chris diagnosed brilliantly, but offered only a single solution. When a customer says "no" to $1,350, they're not saying no to the fix—they're saying no to *that* price. Always have tiers ready:
- *"Good news: it's just the capacitor. We can replace it for $300, and that'll get you back in business today."*
- *"Better option: add a soft-start device ($150 more) so this can't happen again."*
- *"Best: full dual protection ($600 total) so you're safe for years."*

Give the customer a choice. The $300 option might get approved where $1,350 gets declined.

**Secondary Gap:** No objection handling.  
When customer hesitated on price, Chris didn't pivot. A better move: *"I hear you on the budget. Tell you what: let's get the capacitor replaced today for $300 so the unit runs. Then, when you're ready, the soft-start retrofit is $150 more and locks in the price."* Break it into phases if needed.
