## Bands

### Welcome: Solid
- Greeting: "Morning... nice to meet you. Here for maintenance for you guys today."
- Sets basic expectations: "I just need to see thermostats, attic access, and then just the filter."
- Professional tone, customer-focused ("Any more questions for me at all? Let me know.")
- **Gap**: Could have been more explicit about what findings might lead to recommendations and approximate visit duration.

### Diagnosis: Strong
- Thorough field inspection:
  - Went to attic to check filters
  - Inspected capacitor (found it out of spec)
  - Checked outdoor unit thoroughly
  - Took readings and photos
- **Excellent technical explanations**:
  - Explained LRA (inrush) readings: "It's the amount of electricity that's going to your compressor... there's a manufacturer rating they want you to be below or at."
  - On the smaller unit: 134 vs. rated 127; larger unit: 155 vs. rated 151
  - Explained consequence: "if you keep letting it go... you are under warranty. So the compressor would be covered, but... it's a pain. It's a special order part... you'd be out of AC for a couple days."
  - Explained root cause: "Manufacturers aren't in Vegas... their systems are built to run between 90, 95°... when exposed to extreme heat, stuff starts to fail a lot quicker."
  - Explained why capacitor failed: "That's probably why the capacitor is over overcharged as well."
  - Discussed filter status: "They go make it to the fall? Probably."

### Options: Moderate
Presented options but structure could be clearer:
- **Option 1**: Capacitor replacement (covered under warranty, no cost to customer)
- **Option 2**: Filter replacement (customer opted to wait until fall)
- **Option 3**: Soft start kit ($1,350 per piece)
  - Explained benefit: "It'll start your compressor at about 40, 50% of that number, and then gradually build it up... you're not redlining it every time the system turns on. It'll also get rid of that noise for you."
  - Offered financing
  - Mentioned "we could probably work out a little deal with that"

**Gap**: Did not present a clear 4-5 option matrix (good/better/best). Presented individual items but not as "packages." For example, could have offered:
- Option A: Just replace capacitor (free)
- Option B: Capacitor + filters + soft start on smaller unit ($1,350)
- Option C: Capacitor + filters + soft start on both units ($2,700)

### Close: Weak

- Call ended with: "I'm going to go pop up there just finish up in the attic... then I'll come down"
- **No confirmation** of what customer wanted to do about soft start

## Coaching Notes

### TOP GAP: Missing the close moment
**The moment it went wrong**: 
Customer said: "There something you can give me a quote on?" (opened the door to buying)  
Tech said: "Yeah, I can get you a quote on it for sure... They run about... 1350 a piece."

**What should have happened**:
When customer asked for a quote, tech should have built 3-4 bundles and asked which fits their budget/needs:
- "So we have a couple ways to handle this. We could do just the smaller unit for $1,350, or both units for $2,700. We also offer financing if that helps. Plus we could bundle the soft start with a filter refresh and give you a discount. What sounds best to you?"

Then: "Let me write that up and we can get started today."

**Why this matters**: Customer showed buying signal. Tech had the inventory, the authority to offer a deal, and 2+ hours left in a 77-minute call. Missed revenue on a tech-identified problem.

### Secondary gap: Options structure
Tech presented individual items (capacitor, filters, soft start) but not as strategic packages. Should frame as:
- **Good**: Capacitor replacement only ($0)
- **Better**: Capacitor + soft start smaller unit ($1,350)  
- **Best**: Capacitor + soft start both units + filter + financing ($2,700, $150/mo)

## WIN/FIX Summary
**FIX** — When the customer asked for a quote on the soft start kit, build 3 bundle options (smaller unit only, both units, soft start + expedited filter replacement) and ask which works best, then close the sale instead of quoting one price and ending the visit.
