## Gaps & Coaching

**Primary Gap:** Diagnosis without structure.  
Technical depth is valuable, but customers don't buy jargon—they buy solutions. After diagnosing the zone-sensor issue, translate it: *"Your system has four zones, and the sensor on zone 2 isn't sending the temperature back to the thermostat. That's why those rooms stay cold. We can [replace the sensor at $X], [upgrade to a wireless sensor at $Y], or [install a redundant sensor backup at $Z]."* Every finding should end with an option.

**Secondary Gap:** No options framework.  
Even in a 53-minute call, if you diagnose deeply but present no tiered options, you've wasted the time. Always exit with at least three distinct price points tied to risk/benefit.

---

## Coaching Tip

**FIX — Organize diagnostic findings clearly before presenting to the customer: identify the specific problem, explain what caused it, quantify the risk, then present distinct repair tiers with costs—avoid mixing technical jargon with diagnosis.**
