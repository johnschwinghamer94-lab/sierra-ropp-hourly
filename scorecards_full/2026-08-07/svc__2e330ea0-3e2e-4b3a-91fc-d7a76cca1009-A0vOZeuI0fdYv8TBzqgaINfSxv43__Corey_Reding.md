## Coaching Notes

This is a masterclass in service diagnosis and selling. Corey didn't rush. He spent time understanding the system, tested methodically, and presented his findings as a menu, not a mandate.

The close is the moment other techs often fumble—after spending an hour diagnosing, they present options and then pause, waiting for the customer to say yes. Corey didn't wait. The instant the customer said "bare minimum," he moved to signature and payment. That's the difference between a customer deciding later (often deciding no) and a customer saying yes on the spot.

One detail to note: Corey mentioned soft starts as a prevention measure ($6,785 option) even though the immediate issue (failed starter) doesn't require them. He framed it as "investment protection"—systems will fail faster if over-amping continues. That's value-add thinking, not pressure-selling.

For Devin in training: watch how Corey listens to customer cues ("Do you feel sticker shock?"), observes body language, and pivots. He reads the room.
