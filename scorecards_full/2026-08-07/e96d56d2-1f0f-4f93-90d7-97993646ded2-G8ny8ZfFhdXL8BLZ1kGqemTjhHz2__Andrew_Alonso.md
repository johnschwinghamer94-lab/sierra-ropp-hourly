By understanding the constraint and framing the specialist visit as the necessary next step (not a hard sell), he earned immediate agreement. The customer felt heard ("I know it's a landlord-tenant situation") and the path forward felt logical, not pushy.

### Where It Could Have Been Stronger (FIX)
1. **Decision clarity:** The explanation of the landlord-tenant process, permission requirements, and what the specialist would do could have been presented more linearly. The customer understood the "what" (specialist coming) but the "why" and "how" were buried in details.

2. **Deliver explanation:** Andrew never clearly explained what the maintenance work accomplished or why it matters. "Be a nerd" — showing your work and its value — would have reinforced confidence in the company before the specialist visit.

3. **Objection muscle:** While the customer was cooperative, Andrew didn't ask a follow-up question after the customer's filter complaint. A deeper probe ("What's your biggest concern with how often these need cleaning?" or "If we could solve that, would that change how you think about your systems?") might have created a stronger emotional bridge for the specialist visit.
