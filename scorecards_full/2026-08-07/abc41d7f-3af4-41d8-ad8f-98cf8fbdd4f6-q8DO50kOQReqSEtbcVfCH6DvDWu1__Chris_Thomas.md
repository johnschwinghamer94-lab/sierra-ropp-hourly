**Welcome: Solid**
- Professional greeting and introduction ("Hey. Morning, Chris. Mike from Sierra. Nice to meet you.")
- Sets expectations for the visit (will check thermostats, attic access, filter)
- Friendly rapport-building conversation

**Diagnosis: Strong**
- Thorough inspection of both outdoor units
- Takes pictures and shows findings to customer
- Identifies specific technical issues: capacitor out of spec, compressor over-amping (LRA readings of 134 and 155 vs. manufacturer specs of 127 and 151)
- Explains findings clearly in plain language: "That compressor is getting really overheated so it's causing more power to draw to it over time. That will cause damage to it."

**Options: Moderate**
- Presents soft start kit as main option (~$1350/piece)
- Mentions financing options available
- Offers capacitor replacement at no cost (warranty coverage)
- Mentions possibility of "working out a deal" for today
- Does not present tiered options (3-4 distinct repair levels)

**Close: Weak**
- Says "if you wanted to take care of that today" but doesn't get commitment
- No direct ask: "Should we go ahead and schedule this?"
- Customer response is non-committal; transcript ends before decision

## Critical Actions

- **expectations** ✓ Yes: Clear about what he needs to check and what the findings mean
- **options** ✗ No: Soft start kit presented, but not multiple distinct options with varying price/scope
- **membership** ✗ No: No mention of SAM maintenance agreement or membership program
- **close** ✗ No: No direct ask for the sale; defers to estimate follow-up

## Outcome

**Unknown** - Customer hasn't approved work; tech says he'll get a separate estimate for soft start kit and will "bring them down" to show customer.

## Gaps & Coaching

**Gap 1: Weak option presentation** - Only one real upgrade path (soft start). Customer gets confused about whether this is "needed" vs. "nice to have."

**Gap 2: No direct close** - After solid diagnosis, tech should ask "Which option would you like to move forward with today?" instead of "let me get you an estimate."

**Gap 3: Missed membership moment** - When discussing compressor reliability and future maintenance, this was the ideal opening for SAM pitch ("This is exactly why preventive maintenance matters...").

## Expanded Coaching

The diagnosis was excellent and the customer was clearly engaged (asking follow-up questions). But Chris missed the window to close. After presenting the soft start kit option and financing, he should have built a simple menu: "We can do the smaller unit today for $1,350, both units for $2,700, or add the capacitor work to that and get full package pricing." Then ask directly which makes sense. The "let me get an estimate" close gives the customer time to shop and object later.

**Coach Notes:** Strong diagnostician who explains well; needs to present multiple distinct options and ask for yes/no decision on-site rather than deferring.
