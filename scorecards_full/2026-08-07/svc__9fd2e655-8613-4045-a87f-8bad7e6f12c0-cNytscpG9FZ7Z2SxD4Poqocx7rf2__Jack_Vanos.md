## Coaching Notes

### TOP GAP: Options after exhaustive diagnosis
**The problem**:
Tech spent 2+ hours and narrowed the issue down to 2-3 most-likely causes. But instead of saying to customer:

"Here's what I found. Your system IS cooling the air properly (20° split is perfect). But the thermostats aren't sensing the cool air reaching them. That's either:
1. **Most likely: TXV issue** (~$400-600 to replace) — keeps refrigerant from flowing right
2. **Possible: Thermostat sensor** (~$200-300) — not detecting temperatures
3. **Less likely: Ductwork blockage** (we'd have to inspect further)

Supervisor tomorrow will focus on option 1 first since it's most common with your symptoms. Here's the cost range so you know what to expect."

**What tech actually said**:
"I don't know. I really just don't... It doesn't make any sense to me... Anyways, you guys can listen to that recording. Catch ya."

**Why this matters**: Customer is upset, wants a refund, house is getting hotter. Offering structured options (with cost ranges) shows you have a plan and respect their time.

### Secondary gap: Escalation clarity
When deferring to supervisor, tech should clarify:
- "Supervisor will spend 1-2 hours checking X, Y, Z"
- "If it's the TXV, we're looking at $400-600 and we can do it tomorrow"
- "If it's the thermostat, $250 and same timeline"
- "You'll get a call by 10 AM with findings and options"

Instead: "We're gonna have a supervisor come out" — vague, customer left hanging.

### Tertiary gap: Refund discussion
Customer mentioned requesting refund; tech punted: "I'm not sure. It's out of my control."

Better approach:
"I understand you're frustrated. This isn't a quick fix, but I've narrowed it down to the most likely cause. Let me get the supervisor here tomorrow and we'll have a clear plan and pricing. Then you and the office can discuss any credit based on your stay."

Shows ownership instead of helplessness.
