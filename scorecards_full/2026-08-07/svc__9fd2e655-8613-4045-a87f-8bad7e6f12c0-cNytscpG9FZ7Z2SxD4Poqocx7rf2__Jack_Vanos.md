## Expanded Coaching Notes

Jack's diagnostic work was outstanding. His systematic approach—checking pressures, taking temperature splits, measuring surface temps, adding refrigerant, and testing again—showed deep technical skill. The customer clearly trusted his competence by the end of the call.

However, competence on the technical side doesn't convert to a close on the sales side. The customer needed help fast, and Jack's response was "a supervisor will come check." In a service call, that's when you step into the sale:

"Based on what I've found, the TXV is the likely culprit. My supervisor can replace that tomorrow morning. Given that you've got guests coming, would 8 AM work, or do you need earlier? And just so you know, a TXV replacement typically runs around $X and takes about Y hours. Should I get them scheduled?"

That specific ask, with time and price transparency, is what closes the call. Right now, you left the customer hoping someone would call them tomorrow—and if they don't hear from the supervisor team by 9 AM, they're calling back frustrated, or worse, calling a competitor.

Next time, always follow diagnosis with: "Here's what I found, here's what I recommend, here's when we can do it, and here's the cost. Sound good?"
