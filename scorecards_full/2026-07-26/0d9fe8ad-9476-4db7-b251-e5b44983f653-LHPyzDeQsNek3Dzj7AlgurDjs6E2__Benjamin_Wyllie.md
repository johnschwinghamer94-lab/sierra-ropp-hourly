## Coaching Notes

### What Happened (FIX)
Ben did an excellent job diagnosing (extended troubleshooting, multiple check-ins, honest about limitations). The technical assessment was thorough. But when the customer said "we'll run it until it fails," Ben explained the proactive vs. reactive options clearly, but didn't use the emotional hook he already had.

**The missed moment:**
- **Customer said:** "I've got a baby coming on the way, so I'm not letting my kid have no air conditioning in their house."
- **What Ben did:** Acknowledged this and said proactive maintenance matters, but when customer chose reactive, Ben didn't push back
- **What Ben should have said:** "I hear you—you want AC when the baby arrives. So here's what I'm thinking: you run it until it fails, but let's get the install team to quote a full replacement now. That way if the compressor quits mid-summer, you already know the cost and timeline instead of scrambling and being without AC for weeks."
- **Missing FSG move:** Reconnecting to unstated concern + offering a compromise that honors customer's wait-and-see preference while creating next-step urgency

This would likely have at least moved them from "no appointment" to "install team quote appointment."

### Next Call
When you hear an emotional concern (especially about family), write it down. Circle back to it when the customer shows hesitation. Connect the dots: "Earlier you said you don't want your kids in a hot house. That's why I'm suggesting we at least get a quote for the replacement so you're prepared."
