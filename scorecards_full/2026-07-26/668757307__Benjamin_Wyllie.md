## Key Gaps

### Gap 1: No Commercial Value Proposition
Benjamin treated this like a residential call, but this is a commercial customer (MEGA ENTERPRISES L L C). He should have asked about: production impact of downtime, preferred maintenance schedule, budget cycles, and multi-unit service plans.

### Gap 2: Weak Closing on Commercial Opportunity
For a commercial customer, Benjamin should have proposed a preventive maintenance agreement or service contract, not just one-off repairs. This represents recurring revenue potential that was missed.

## Coaching Advice

**FIX — When you walk into a commercial account, flip your mindset from "repair this broken thing" to "how do we ensure this equipment never fails and disrupts your business?" Instead of just presenting inspection findings, ask: "Would you prefer to budget for scheduled maintenance quarterly, or would you rather we respond to emergencies as they happen?" That conversation sets you up to offer a service plan that commercial customers actually want. Right now, you're competing on price instead of value. Commercial accounts pay premium prices for reliability and peace of mind—sell that, not just the repair.**
