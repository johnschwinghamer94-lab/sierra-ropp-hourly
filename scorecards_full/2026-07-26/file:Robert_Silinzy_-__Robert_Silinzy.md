## Key Gap (Critical)

### Gap 1: Left Customer in Limbo with No Decision Framework
**What happened:** "the windings are weakened to the point where like it may cut on, it may not... I don't do that. I just see the signs, and I share that with the homeowner."
**What he said:** Told them it's unpredictable but didn't frame decision.
**What he should have said:** "Here's the situation: Your compressor is getting weak. It might start next time you need it, it might not. Do you want to wait for it to fail on a 115-degree day when we're booked out 3 weeks? Or do you want to plan now while we control the timeline? I can get you a compressor price and we can schedule when it works for you."
**FSG Move Missing:** Decision/Reconnecting (Framing the choice as risk management, not just diagnosis)

---

## Coaching Advice (Expanded)

**FIX — Told customer windings are weakened and "may or may not kick on" — you left them in uncertainty limbo. Ask the hard question: "Do you want to wait for failure in 110-degree heat, or plan now while you control the timing?"**

You did excellent diagnostic work here—the over-amped hard start, the weakened windings, the supply chain reality. But you delivered the diagnosis without the decision framework.

Next call: When you find a component showing end-of-life signs, move into decision mode immediately after diagnosis. Frame it as a choice between controlled timing (plan now) vs. emergency timing (fails in summer). That's what converts "unknown" outcomes to "closed" or "flipped."
