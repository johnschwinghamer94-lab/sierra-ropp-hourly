## Key Gaps & Coaching

### Gap 1: Missed the Urgency Window
When customer expressed concern about cost/timeline ("I said I'm a prop. Before Monday... yeah, pretty much everybody's pretty booked"), Francisco did NOT pivot to position the capacitor fix as an immediate win. 

**What could have happened:** "If I can swap out just the capacitor today—I may have it on the truck—you're back up and running within hours. That buys you time to think about the fan motor and compressor. We can schedule those for next week. Sound good?"

### Gap 2: No Oral Options Presentation
Francisco promised to "write up a report" with three options but never explained them verbally or created a hierarchy of choices (immediate vs. future, cheap vs. permanent).

**What could have happened:** Three clear paths:
- **Option A (Immediate):** Capacitor only = ~$300, buys 1-2 weeks of cool air
- **Option B (Soon):** + Fan motor = ~$800 total, more stability
- **Option C (Long-term):** + Compressor replacement = $2000+, permanent fix

## Coaching Tip

**FIX —** When customer showed hesitation about cost and timeline ("pretty much everybody's pretty booked"), you missed the chance to anchor the capacitor fix as an immediate, low-cost option that could be done *today*. Instead of promising to write it up later, position it now: "Here's what I can do today for under $400, and here's what we schedule for next week. Both make sense for different reasons. Which do you want to tackle first?" This creates forward momentum instead of postponement.
