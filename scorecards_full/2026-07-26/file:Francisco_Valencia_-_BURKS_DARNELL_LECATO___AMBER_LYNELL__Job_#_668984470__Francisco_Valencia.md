## Key Gaps

### Gap 1: Cost Deflection Instead of Confrontation
When the customer asked "You'll be able to fix it?", the underlying question was "Will this be expensive and take forever?" Francisco answered by explaining what parts needed ordering—he explained the problem instead of addressing the customer's real concern about cost and timeline.

### Gap 2: No Bridge Between Diagnosis and Decision
Francisco did excellent diagnostic work but didn't transition smoothly to "Here's what I recommend and why it makes sense for you." The customer was left wondering whether the repair is worth doing.

---

## Coaching Advice

**FIX — When the customer asked "You'll be able to fix it?" they weren't asking a technical question—they were asking "Is this going to cost too much and take too long?" Instead of explaining parts availability, say: "Yes, I can fix it. It'll cost about $1,800 and we can have you cooled down by tomorrow afternoon. Or, if you want to think long-term, I can get you a replacement quote that might make sense given the age of your system." That directly addresses their real concern and gives them permission to decide. Right now, you're explaining the problem when the customer wants permission to solve it.**
