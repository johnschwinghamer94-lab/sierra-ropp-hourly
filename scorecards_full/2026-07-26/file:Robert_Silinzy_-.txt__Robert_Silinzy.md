## Key Moments & Gaps

**Strongest moment:** The thorough diagnosis and clear technical explanation. Robert tested the compressor windings, identified specific failure modes, and explained what oil on the blower motor means.

**Biggest gap:** When the customer reacted to the $5,500 compressor cost, Robert should have reconnected that finding to the blower motor failure he just found. The oil leak is the real urgency — compressor _might_ keep running, but the blower _will_ fail soon, meaning no AC this summer.

## Coaching Notes

This is a classic evaluation call where the tech did solid diagnostic work but didn't drive urgency in the decision phase. The customer was worried about cost (remembering the $1,400 bill), and instead of using the blower motor oil as the key to urgency, Robert let the conversation drift to technical specs and future pricing lookup.

Next time a customer expresses sticker shock: pause, acknowledge it, then reframe around what you found that creates immediate risk. "Yeah, I know that's a big number. But look, we've got another problem that's more urgent — that oil on the blower means it could quit on us in the next week or two. Let's make sure we have that motor in stock before it fails, because without that, you're in the dark."
