## Key Gaps & Coaching

### Gap 1: Missed the Objection-Handling Moment
Customer said: "He charged me too much for... Way too much for."

This was a door opening. The customer was signaling price sensitivity and past negative experience. Robert should have said:

**What he could have said:** "I totally understand—that's frustrating. Here's what I do different: I show you the parts, the labor, why each one costs what it does. When I send you the report, you'll see everything itemized. No surprises. Sound good?"

**What actually happened:** Robert moved on to checking old invoices without acknowledging or reassuring the customer. This left the objection hanging and unaddressed.

### Gap 2: No Options Framework
Robert identified three components to potentially repair:
- Hard start (microprocessor)
- Compressor
- Blower motor

But never presented these as options (e.g., "I can replace the hard start for $X, or add the blower motor for $Y, or do both"). Instead, he said he'd "put the prices" in the report and the customer can decide later. This postpones the conversation and loses momentum.

**What could have happened:** "So here's what I'm seeing: the hard start device is over-amped, and the compressor windings are weak. We have two paths: One, I can replace the hard start and see if that buys us some time. Two, we replace the hard start AND the compressor, more permanent. The hard start alone is around $400-500. The compressor adds another $3,000-3,500. Which one makes sense for you—quick fix or permanent fix?"

### Gap 3: No Urgency
Robert ended with "Give me about 20 minutes... then I'll come and share it with you guys." This is processional, not compelling. No urgency, no decision-making frame, no reason to act today.

---

## Coaching Tip

**FIX —** When the customer said he'd been overcharged before, you missed the objection signal. Say: "I hear you—that's exactly why I show you the parts, labor, everything itemized. When I send this over, you'll see it all broken down, no surprises." Then don't just promise a report—propose a choice: "Here's what I'm thinking: hard start is $450, compressor is another $3K. Which makes sense for right now? I can schedule whichever you pick for tomorrow." Action kills objections way better than promises of future reports.
