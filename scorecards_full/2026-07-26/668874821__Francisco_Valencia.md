## Key Wins & Coaching

### Win 1: Powerful Reframe
When customer learned the compressor was "cooked" and repairs would be expensive, Francisco didn't defend the repair cost. Instead, he reframed the decision: "it's hard for me to offer repair options on a 30+ unit system... it's better off me telling you guys just let it be until it dies, **or let's look at replacing that system before it happens**."

This pivot from "defend the $8,500 repair" to "consider the replacement path" took pressure off the pricing objection and moved the conversation forward. Customer immediately engaged: "If possible, I'd like to do both."

### Win 2: Payment + Timeline Flexibility
Customer's concern: "That's too much money." and "But it's such a small place... 700 square feet."  
Francisco's response: "payment programs... you're probably going to be looking at about $150 to maybe the highest, if you guys go with the higher end stuff, $250... the interest rate... another Sean question."

By deferring the details to the specialist but confirming monthly payment is possible (and quick qualification), Francisco removed a major barrier and kept the option open.

### Win 3: Built Urgency Without Pressure
Customer had mentioned the system might fail anytime ("It's going to fail when it's hot like this, when it's under the most strain"). Francisco didn't say "You need to replace it now." Instead, he offered both paths (repair + specialist quote) same-day, which creates legitimate urgency: "I can get someone out today... get you guys a quote... if you guys want to get replacement options."

Result: Customer said yes to both, setting up either a repair win or a replacement sale.

## Coaching Tip

**WIN —** When the customer said "That's too much money" about the $8,500 repair, you reframed the entire conversation: "it's better off me telling you guys just let it be until it dies, **or let's look at replacing that system before it happens**."
