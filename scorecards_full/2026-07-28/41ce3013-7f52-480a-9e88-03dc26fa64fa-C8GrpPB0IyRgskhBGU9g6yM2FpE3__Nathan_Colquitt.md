## Critical Gap: The Decision Handoff
**What happened:** Nathan diagnosed the problem well, explained complexity, then said "I'll call you back"
**What customer likely thought:** "He's going to call me with bad news and I'll have to deal with it later"
**Why this kills closing:**

When you identify an expensive problem and don't present options immediately:
1. Customer has time to get second opinions elsewhere
2. They contact competitors and hear different recommendations
3. The decision process extends (kills urgency)
4. They talk themselves into DIY temporary fixes

**The missed moment:**
After saying "very expensive" and "huge deal," Nathan should have asked:
- "Here's what we can do: We can write up a repair quote for the motor, or we can explore a full replacement system. Which direction feels more aligned with your situation—quick repair, or long-term investment?"

This creates **two clear paths**, not "wait for my call."

## Major Miss: Punt on Pricing
**SILO Price Rule Note:** This coach acknowledges the SILO price rule—techs don't quote pricing. However, the issue here isn't pricing transparency; it's **decision framework clarity**.

Nathan should have said: "I can't give you exact pricing today because the motor needs to be programmed by Bryant, but here's what I can do—let me connect you with our service specialist who can walk through repair vs. replacement options with actual numbers so you can decide right now."

This moves customer to **decision** (on call) with specialist, not **waiting**.

## Why This Didn't Flip

- Specialist follow-up call introduces friction and time for second-guessing

## Coaching Notes
Nathan's diagnostic and educational skills are strong. The flip opportunity was missed in the decision phase. Next time:

1. **Identify the real objection:** Cost + complexity + timeline uncertainty
2. **Present options immediately:** "Here's repair path (specs: cost/timeline), here's replacement path (specs: cost/timeline), which aligns with your needs?"
3. **Create urgency:** "That motor will continue to fail in heat, so the sooner we decide, the sooner you're comfortable"
4. **Move to specialist:** "Let me get our service advisor on the phone right now so you can get exact numbers and make the call today"

**Replicable coaching:**
When a customer hears "very expensive" + "huge deal," they need **immediate options**, not "I'll call you." Options + timeframe = decision. Delay = shopping around.
