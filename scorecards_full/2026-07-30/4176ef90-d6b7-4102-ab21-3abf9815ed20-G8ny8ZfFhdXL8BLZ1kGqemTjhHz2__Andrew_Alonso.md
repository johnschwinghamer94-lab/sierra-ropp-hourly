## Gaps & Coaching Advice

**#1 Missed Door: Energy Cost as Value Lever**  
The customer revealed the real pain point: $600/month power bills. This is the opening to reframe the system replacement as an *investment*, not a cost. A high-efficiency system could reduce that bill by 20–30%, which pays for the system in 5–7 years. Andrew should have said something like: "I see the power bill is pretty heavy. A modern high-efficiency system could cut that $600 significantly. Would you be interested in seeing what the payback looks like?" This either closes the deal or flips it to an Option C estimate to show ROI.

**#2 Objection Handling: Reframe, Don't Retreat**  
When the customer said they can't afford replacement at current prices, Andrew didn't acknowledge the concern or offer an alternative path. A skilled move would have been: "I hear you—replacement is a bigger investment. Let me show you a few options: we could focus on maintenance to stretch what you have, or we could explore a financing plan that turns your $600 bill into a payment. Let me get our specialist involved to map that out for you." This leads to a TGL.

**#3 No Comfort Advisor Flip**  
Andrew did the diagnostic work correctly, found real issues (energy overruns, comfort zones), but had no pathway to escalate to specialist pricing/financing advice. A "Let me have our comfort advisor call you with a couple of options" closes the loop and creates a TGL.
