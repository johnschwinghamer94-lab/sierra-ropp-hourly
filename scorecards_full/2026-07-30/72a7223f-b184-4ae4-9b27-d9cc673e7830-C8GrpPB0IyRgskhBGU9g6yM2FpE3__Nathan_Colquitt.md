## Top Gaps & Coaching

1. **Timing objection = TGL opportunity** — The customer just told you they want to keep the system running a couple more years. That's not a "no"—it's a "not right now, but yes eventually." Nathan should have asked: "What if we could give you peace of mind while you're waiting? Have you heard about our Comfort Advisor program?" Option C often wins here because it's lower friction than a full replacement quote.

2. **Evaluation ≠ Decision** — Promising a detailed evaluation is good setup, but the call should end with a clear ask: "After I evaluate it, would you like me to get you some options on how to keep it reliable or prep for replacement?" Otherwise, the evaluation sits in a file and the customer decides on their own.

3. **Missing the window** — By the time the next tech comes out for the full eval, the customer may have called another company or decided to DIY. Close the loop on this call by presenting at least one option now (maintenance plan, future quote, etc.).

## Tip for Next Call
**FIX** — When a customer says "if it keeps working a couple more years," they've given you the timeline. Ask, "What if we could keep it running reliably while you plan?" and present a Comfort Advisor or maintenance plan option instead of just promising an evaluation later.
