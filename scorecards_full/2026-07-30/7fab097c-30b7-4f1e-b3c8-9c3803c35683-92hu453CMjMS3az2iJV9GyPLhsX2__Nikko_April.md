## Coaching Notes

**What Nikko Did Well:**
- Technical depth. Explained dual-filter stress causing coil rust risk and compressor wear clearly and accurately.
- Friendly rapport. Built a comfortable, conversational atmosphere (shared his own plans to add a mini-split).
- Thorough inspection. Checked both units, identified the specific compressor wear concern, changed filters, took photos.
- Professional close. Acknowledged customer frustration ("I know it seems like we always be the bearer of bad news") and validated customer's preference.

**The Missed Moment (circa line 4357–4368):**  
Nikko asked: "Do you want to do anything today?"  
Customer answered: "No, I'd rather wait, see what happens."  
Nikko replied: "Waiting. Wait. I want to try and get the most use out of it that I can."

**What's Missing:**
The customer's true objection was not explored. "I'd rather wait" could mean:
- Cost concern ("I can't afford $X right now")
- Urgency concern ("It's working fine, why fix it now?")
- Trust concern ("I want a second opinion or more time to think")

Nikko should have asked a follow-up question to understand why, then reconnected the recommendation to customer's stated goal ("Just to keep them running good"). A stronger close might have been:

"I hear you want to squeeze more life out of it. Here's my concern: if the kitchen-side unit fails mid-summer—right when we're most backed up—this living room unit will have to run the whole house in 120-degree heat. You could be looking at 3–5 days without AC while we get parts and schedule. Locking in the replacement cost today also protects you from emergency pricing. Can we at least get you a firm quote so you know what you're deciding against?"

This reconnects to his goal (reliability), paints the consequence (multi-day downtime), and presents urgency/value (act while not swamped, lock in pricing).
