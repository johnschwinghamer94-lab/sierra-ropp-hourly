# David Canales - Cracked Condensate Pan Inspection

## FSG Bands
- **welcome**: Strong — Crystal-clear expectations: "So before I get started, want to know what you can expect from me today?... I'm going to go to my bank, write a report, fill out some options for you... We don't do anything without your approval... I'm here for a five star experience."
- **assessment**: Strong — Methodical inspection of condensate system: checks primary pan, secondary pan, metal pan underneath, looks for cracks, checks drain pitch ("you see that bubble?"), traces water flow, and discovers the root cause: "that pan is cracked... So, yeah, that's why I couldn't see anything."
- **decision**: Solid — Explains findings clearly with technical detail (pan material, coil location, manufacturer limitations for 2015 unit). Presents repair options with pricing. Reconnects to customer concern but doesn't create urgency—customer responds "If I decide to go forward" (conditional, not committed).
- **deliver**: Strong — Teaches system (explains condensate flow, why pan location matters, why replacement is complex). Offers workaround: "You can just put [a portable AC unit] in the bedroom with that." Provides written estimate via email. No explicit 5-star review request, but process is professional.

## Critical Actions
- **expectations** (PASS): Set at welcome with clear timeline
- **questions** (PASS): Asked about prior service, confirmed scope ("anything else besides that main issue")
- **options** (PASS): Multiple repair options presented with lead-time discussion
- **objections** (FAIL): Customer's hesitation ("If I decide to go forward") wasn't addressed with urgency or timeline. Offered workaround but didn't ask when they need the system back to drive commitment.

## Outcome
**no-close** — Customer approved evaluation, received options/pricing, but deferred decision ("If I decide to go forward"). No commitment during call; customer has email quote.

## Coaching
David nailed the inspection, diagnosis, and explanation. The gap: he didn't create urgency against the timeline. When he mentioned 113F coming, that's the moment to say "Lead times can be 1-3 weeks depending on parts availability—when do you need cooling restored before the next heat wave?" and tie the decision to immediate need, not abstract "if." The portable AC offer was kind but wasn't framed as a decision-forcing alternative ("or let's get this started today so it's done before then").
