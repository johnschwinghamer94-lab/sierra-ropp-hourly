## Coaching Notes (Expanded)
This was a classic time-constraint kill. Rep arrived around 6 PM with a hard 8 PM deadline, but 1999 systems with multiple unknowns need more than 13 minutes. The rep made the right call to not rush the diagnosis, but should have:

1. **Reset expectations upfront** — "We're a bit short on time. Let me do a quick initial assessment and let you know if we need a follow-up, or if I can wrap it today."
2. **Prioritize high-impact checks** — Focus on compressor operation, refrigerant level, key electrical components; skip deep coil inspection if time is tight.
3. **Reconnect before time runs out** — With 30 min left, come back in and say: "Here's what I found. Because of the system age and these issues, I recommend we talk replacement options. Can I schedule our install team to give you pricing?"
4. **Close even if incomplete** — "I'll get back with you tomorrow, but here's what I'm seeing: This 1999 system needs work. In the meantime, here are some cost-saving tips [filters, thermostat settings]. Our install team will reach out Wednesday to discuss options. Sound good?"

**The miss:** Rep left customer in "unknown" state without any recommendation or pathway forward. At 1999 system age with repair history, a replacement consultation should have been the clear ask, even if detailed diagnosis had to wait.

**Key learning:** Late arrivals still need strong closes. Don't let time pressure prevent you from at least pivoting to the next step (install consultation) with confidence.
