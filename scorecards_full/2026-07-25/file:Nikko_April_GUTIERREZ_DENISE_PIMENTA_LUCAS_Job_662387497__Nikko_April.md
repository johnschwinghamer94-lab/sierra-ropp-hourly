## Key Gaps

### Gap 1: Over-Diagnosis Without Clear Decision Framework
The 58-minute call suggests Nikko was thorough but may have spent more time explaining problems than helping the customer prioritize solutions. Customers need a frame: "Do you want to fix it now or monitor it?" not just "here are all the things that could go wrong."

### Gap 2: No Attempt to Uncover Customer's Real Priority
Nikko diagnosed extensively but never asked: "Of all these issues, which one concerns you most?" or "What would success look like for you—lowest cost, fastest fix, or long-term reliability?" This left the customer without a decision pathway.

---

## Coaching Advice

**FIX — When you have 58 minutes on a call and the customer still walks out undecided, it often means you've asked the customer to make too many decisions at once. Next time, after the inspection, ask: "I found three things that need attention. If you could fix only one to start, which matters most to you—comfort, reliability, or resale value?" That single question focuses the conversation and gives the customer permission to be decisive. Right now, you're giving thorough education but leaving the choice paralyzed.**
