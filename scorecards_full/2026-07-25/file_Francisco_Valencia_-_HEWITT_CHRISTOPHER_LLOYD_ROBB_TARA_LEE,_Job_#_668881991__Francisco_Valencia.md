## Gaps & Coaching

**1. Opening Formality:** While rapport was built effectively, the initial greeting was very casual. Could structure opening with: "I'm Francisco with Sierra. Before we get started, let me walk through what I'll do today so you know what to expect."

**2. No Explicit Close Request:** No 5-star review request at end of call, even after successful installation and customer satisfaction.

**3. Objection Handling:** When customer said the compressor repair is "going to be more" expensive than they expected and that "it's the front that is obsolete," Francisco could have asked a discovery question like "What's most important to you right now—immediate cooling or long-term reliability?" to reconnect rather than just accepting the resistance.

**4. Post-Call Ownership:** Customer had negative comment about previous owner's repairs (Mexicans/"friends of a friend"). While Francisco explained professional standards well, acknowledging and validating that concern directly would strengthen the narrative.

## Wins to Reinforce

- **System expertise demonstration:** The full-system diagram explanation was the turning point. This "nerd" moment earned credibility that moved customer past skepticism.
- **Transparent pricing & options:** Giving all three options with clear pros/cons (rather than pushing replacement) built trust.
- **Empathy with decision-making:** Validating that it's "not an easy decision" and positioning it as their choice, not your pressure, is persuasive.
