## Coaching Notes (Expanded)
This rep proved that a hands-on system diagram beats any verbal explanation. By drawing compressor, condenser, evap coil, and copper lines, the customer could *see* why each failing part cascades into bigger problems. That visualization justified the $10,417 repair price (confirming it was real and necessary), then made replacement feel inevitable (not upsell).

**Master moves:**
1. **Show, don't just tell** — Draw the system; let customer visualize the problem
2. **Quantify hidden costs** — "45% overpayment on utilities every month" is more powerful than "system is old"
3. **Present full repair itemization** — $1,277 motor + $509 switch + $2,001 compressor = $3,787 parts alone, plus labor. Customers see the value in replacement.
4. **Compare timeframes** — "New system = 20 years trouble-free. This one = 2-3 more years barely running"
5. **Offer financing** — Don't make replacement sound expensive in cash; offer monthly alternative

**Growth opportunity:** Could have asked more about long-term vs short-term home status earlier to tailor the message. But overall, strong flip through transparency and visual proof.
