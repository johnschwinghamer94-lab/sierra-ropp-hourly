### Decision: **Strong**

This was Joe's strongest section. The customer initially objected to cost ("It's almost cost of a new unit" and "I don't want to spend 15 grand or 20 grand on a new system"), but Joe's response was masterful.

**Building Options - Three Distinct Paths:**
1. **Leak Search & Repair:** $2,911 (immediate diagnostic to find and fix the leak)
2. **Coil Replacement (if unrepairable):** $7,650 (if the leak is in the coil fins, contingent credit from diagnostic)
3. **Full System Replacement:** $15,000-$20,000 (via equipment specialist for a fresh start)

**Reconnecting to Concerns:**
"I just would hate for you guys to throw good money over bad without at least getting all of your information." This directly addressed their hesitation while positioning the diagnostic work as essential, not wasteful.

**Explaining Options with Technical Depth:**
- Explained the oil pool under the coil and what it signals (insufficient refrigerant circulation)
- Detailed the unrepairable leak risk: "Where the fins meet that pan, we run the risk of the leak being inside those fins. If the leak is inside of those fins, it becomes an unrepairable leak."
- Explained the motor overheating: "That motor overheated at some point. There's little sauce pack inside that bursts when it overheats to keep you running."
- Provided market context: R-22 refrigerant was phased out on 1/1/24; "they only have one system" in stock in Maine, 3-week lead time
- Shared a real-world comparison: "I had a homeowner earlier today, same situation... Goodman found one for me in Maine. It's going to take three weeks to get to me."

**Guiding the Decision:**
"that way you guys can decide, okay, do we spend $2900 to see where the leak is or do we put that money towards a new one and start fresh with new warranties?"

**Overcoming Objections:**
Joe didn't just acknowledge the cost concern—he gave the customer agency and explained why each option made sense. The result: the customer moved from "that's too much" to "Okay, so we want to take care of that" and committed to the $2,911 diagnostic.

### Deliver: **Solid**

**Be A Nerd - Excellent Technical Education:**
Joe provided comprehensive technical explanation without condescension:
- Described the coil condition: "the bottom half of the coil is getting cold, the other half isn't. That lets me know there's not enough refrigerant to blow through it because there's oil at the bottom of the pan."
- Explained the leak mechanism: Builders solder pipes; soldered joints fail 1-2 years after construction. As the system ages, coil points weaken and leaks develop.
- Motor issue: "it's leaking a lot of oil... that motor overheated at some point. There's little sauce pack inside that bursts when it overheats to keep you running. So it looks like you already used your get out of free card."
- Refrigerant context: Explained why old R-22 systems are problematic post-2024 and why parts are becoming scarce.
- Set realistic expectations: Builder-grade systems may only last another year or so, but the repair keeps the system functional.

This was genuinely educational and appropriate for a homeowner.
