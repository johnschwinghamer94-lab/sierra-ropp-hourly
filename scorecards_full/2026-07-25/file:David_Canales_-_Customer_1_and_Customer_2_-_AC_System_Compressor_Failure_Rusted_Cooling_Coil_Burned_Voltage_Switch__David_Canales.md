## FSG Band Scores

### Welcome: **Strong**

David opened strong with clear empathy and expertise:
- "I'll do a thorough evaluation. I'll go check out Outside unit, go check up in the attic, check everything out."
- Set explicit expectations about approval: "we don't do anything without your approval"
- Explained report process: "I'm gonna go to my van, take about 15-20 minutes to write up a report"
- Committed to partnership: "And then overall, I'm here for a 5-star experience. So if you're not meeting that expectation, please let me know so I can fix that."

The tech demonstrated expertise by immediately asking about Nevada Energy settings and setting expectations that he would only present findings without acting until approved.

### Assessment: **Solid**

David asked some qualifying questions and conducted a thorough inspection:
- Asked about filter cleaning frequency and what customer noticed
- Inquired about previous service history ("Who checked it out, if you don't mind me asking?")
- Asked clarifying questions about which unit had the problem (upstairs vs. downstairs)
- Conducted comprehensive inspection: checked power draw, compressor windings, fan motor, voltage switch, cooling coil

However, the questions were more system-focused than customer-concern focused. Missing deeper discovery about customer's priorities, timeline, budget comfort, or previous bad experiences with repair costs.

### Decision: **Solid**

David presented clear options and handled some objections but didn't strongly pursue the sale:
- Educated extensively about the system: explained compressor function, refrigerant cycle, why rust happens, why replacement is sealed system
- Presented a clear option: Replace compressor and refrigerant for $7,091 (with breakdown of why: 12 lbs of refrigerant required)
- Mentioned equipment specialist option for broader discussion
- Answered customer's technical questions about valve failure and compressor cause
- Acknowledged cost objection: "Yeah, that's a lot of money"

Missed reconnection moment: When customer said "$7,091 is a lot of money," tech pivoted to specialist option rather than reconnecting to the urgent original problem (AC not cooling during summer heat).

### Deliver: **Solid**

David delivered solid "be a nerd" education but work wasn't approved:

- Explained technical failure modes: damaged winding insulation, valve stuck open, oil leaks, voltage switch burn marks
- Explained consequence of rust: "once the rust starts, you can't stop it... The damage has been done"
- Explained why replacement is necessary: sealed system means can't repair internally

Did not ask for 5-star review at end since no work was performed.

## Key Gaps

**1. Missed Reconnection During Objection**  
When customer said "$7,091 is a lot of money" (line ~2556), tech should have reconnected to the original urgent problem rather than offering specialist option. The customer's AC system wasn't cooling at all during summer—that's an urgent comfort issue. Should have said something like: "I understand the cost is significant. Your system isn't working right now, and in summer heat, that's uncomfortable. Here's how we can get started this week..."

**2. Weak Discovery Questions**  
Tech asked mostly system-focused questions, not customer-priority questions. Missing: budget comfort level, timeline needs, previous experience with repair costs, preference for repair vs. replacement thinking.

**3. No Close Attempt**  
After presenting the option and customer hesitated, tech didn't attempt to move them toward a decision. Accepted "I'll think about it" without exploring what would help them decide faster or offering next steps (payment plan, schedule tentative appointment, callback from specialist).

## Coaching Notes

David demonstrated strong technical expertise and thorough inspection work. The education phase was excellent—customers left understanding their system and why it failed. However, the sales approach was too passive. When the customer expressed cost concern, tech had a golden moment to reconnect the $7,091 investment back to their original pain (no cooling on a 80-degree summer night) but instead pivoted away to a specialist consult. In a competitive market, that hesitation often means the customer calls three other companies and never calls back.

Next job: Practice reconnecting options to original customer problems during objections. When you hear "that's expensive," respond with "I get it—but your system's not working right now and it's summer. Let me show you how we make this work for you this week."
