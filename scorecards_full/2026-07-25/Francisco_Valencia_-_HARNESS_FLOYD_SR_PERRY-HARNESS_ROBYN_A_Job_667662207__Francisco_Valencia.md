## FSG Bands

### Welcome: Solid
Francisco greeted the customer and quickly established his process: "How Sierra does it is we look at the system first, so that way at least you have an understanding of how it's doing. And then we can give you an estimate on replacement afterwards." He built credibility by mentioning "we've been around for about 40 years" and set clear expectations for the diagnostic visit.

**Supporting Quote:** "I'll take a look at it and then once I'm done with looking at it, then we can talk about the options for you and then just let me know what makes more sense to you."

The greeting was functional but brief and somewhat disjointed due to transcription issues. The process explanation was clear and positioned Sierra favorably.

---

### Assessment: Moderate
Francisco asked about outdoor unit location ("on the side of the house"), whether the customer needed help ("Do you need my help or anything?"), and what they were looking for ("You're trying to buy full system"). He also checked in on the noise complaint ("Going a little too loud for you? The nighttime will be when it kicks on").

However, he conducted minimal consultative discovery. He didn't ask about:
- How long they've had comfort issues
- Budget parameters or timeline for decision
- Whether they're planning to stay in the home long-term (though customer brought this up themselves: "at least the next 20 years")
- Specific pain points beyond noise

**Gap:** Discovery was transactional rather than consultative. More probing on the customer's actual needs and constraints would have strengthened the diagnostic phase.

---

### Decision: Solid
Francisco demonstrated commitment to options and arranged for a second tech: "So at this point, I'm gonna write up the report, but I'm going to also call in so that way the other guy can come out while I'm writing the report. So that way you can get both options."

He emphasized the options approach: "That way you have options. Not just one option and stuff like that."

Francisco provided extensive technical education on system choices:
- Single-stage ("Turns on, turns off")
- Two-stage with low/high speed operation ("it has a low speed and a high speed... it runs a little longer at a lot quieter because it's only running at about 60% of its power")
- Multi-stage systems ("you can't even hear when they're running... they run at 30% and increment 1% at a time")

He reconnected the customer's noise concern to the solution ("Anything that you get is going to be more efficient") and showed confidence in presenting multiple brands (Lennox premium vs. Goodman tier options).

**Score Rationale:** Strong setup for the options phase, but this is a diagnostic call—the actual close and objection handling happen later with the estimate tech.

---

### Deliver: Weak
Francisco provided technical explanations of the findings (compressor amp load at 17.9 of 18.3 rated, capacitor wear-and-tear, fan motor oil seepage, furnace mounting issues) and educated on technology differences, but:

1. No explanation of the work he personally performed during inspection
2. No ask for a 5-star review
3. Call ends before the estimate/recommendation close phase

**Gap:** The call ends at the handoff to the estimate tech, so the "deliver" phase (which includes the review ask and final sales recommendation) is incomplete.

## Outcome: Flipped

Through Francisco's diagnostic findings and option-rich positioning, the appointment evolved into an estimate visit with the second tech coming to provide replacement pricing. This is a successful diagnostic-to-sales-opportunity flip.

---

## Top Gaps & Coaching

1. **Discovery depth (Assessment weakness):** Francisco conducted a technical inspection but didn't uncover the customer's motivations, constraints, or decision timeline early. The customer had to volunteer "at least the next 20 years" (indicating long ownership intent and a stronger value case for premium systems). Ask earlier: "How long are you planning to stay in this home? That'll help me recommend the right option."

2. **Review ask missing (Deliver weakness):** The call ends before Francisco asks for a 5-star review. Even in diagnostic calls, this should happen as the tech leaves or transitions to the estimate tech: "Thanks for letting me inspect your system. If we provide great service over the next week, would you mind leaving us a 5-star review?"

## Expanded Coaching Advice

**Strengths to reinforce:**
- Francisco built credibility without aggression ("40 years," nephew Aaron connection, friends/family pricing mention)
- He explained findings in technical detail without overwhelming the customer
- He set up the estimate tech for success by laying the groundwork for multiple options

**Next steps:**
- Deepen discovery questions during diagnostic calls—understand the customer's pain points and home plans before the technical findings
- Own the close recommendation, even if pricing comes from the second tech
- Always ask for the review at transition points
