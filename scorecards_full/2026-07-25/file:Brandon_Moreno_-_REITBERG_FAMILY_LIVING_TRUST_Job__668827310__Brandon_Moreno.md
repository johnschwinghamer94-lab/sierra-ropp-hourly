## Coaching Insight

**The Win:** When Rudy learned that R-22 repairs would cost thousands and the system was near end-of-life, Brandon didn't lose him to sticker shock. Instead, he:
1. Acknowledged the financial reality without apology
2. Pivoted to Rudy's actual uncertainty (keep or sell the house?)
3. Offered a **due-diligence path** that served both scenarios: complete the attic inspection, bring in an estimator, compare repair vs. replacement costs same-day through Sierra

This is the **decision** FSG step at its best—not pushing a solution, but building a process that lets the customer make an informed decision. Rudy went from "this sounds expensive, why is this happening?" to "okay, I'll wait for the full assessment and options," which is exactly what you want from an inherited-property situation where the customer is still learning the house's condition.

**What to reinforce:** When you hit a cost objection on an old system, don't retreat on the diagnosis. Instead, step back and ask: *"What's actually uncertain here for this customer?"* Rudy's uncertainty wasn't "is this broken?" (Brandon proved that). It was "Do I keep this house or sell it? And either way, what's worth spending?" By tying the options to that real decision, Brandon transformed an objection into a path.
