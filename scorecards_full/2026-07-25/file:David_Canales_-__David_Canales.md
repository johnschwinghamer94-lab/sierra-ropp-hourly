## Expanded Coaching Advice

**On Objection Handling:**  
Price and scope objections are the most common blockers on complex HVAC repairs. When a customer says "this is a lot," your next move should be a diagnostic question, not an explanation. Probe: "Is the total cost the concern, or is it the scope of what needs to be fixed?" Then offer options within their comfort zone (phases, warranty, priority repair). David's "smart shopper" response was kind but didn't move the sale forward.

**On Urgency Building:**  
David found serious issues—melted voltage switch, compressor stress, oil stain indicating refrigerant leak. But he never connected these to a timeline: "In 30 days, when the sun beats down and we hit 115°, this compressor will overheat and stop working. You'll have no AC in peak summer, and an emergency replacement costs 2-3x a planned one." This would have justified the upfront investment and reduced shopping-around impulse.

**On System Education:**  
Your system explanation was excellent—thorough, well-structured, and accessible. This built trust. But tie it back to the options: "The reason we need the coil AND the compressor fix is because the refrigerant leak is damaging the compressor. If we just fix the compressor and leave the leak, we're back here in a year." This frames the total solution as preventive, not just expensive.
