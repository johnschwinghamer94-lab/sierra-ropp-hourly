## Expanded Coaching

**Strengths:** Chris is warm, thorough, and clear in his technical communication. He doesn't push unnecessary repairs on a healthy system—that's ethical. His membership pitch is complete and value-driven.

**What went wrong:**

1. **Closing timing.** Sales close when the customer is "hot"—when they've just said "yes" or shown clear interest. Chris had that moment and let it pass. The rule: **Ask for the sale while the customer is nodding, not after they've mentally moved on.**

2. **Deferral language.** "We'll talk about it later" rarely leads to a close. By then, the customer has shifted context (back to their day, other worries) and the energy is gone. Always say, "Let's get that set up real quick" and make it happen before you leave.

**How to fix it:**

- **When you see interest, act immediately.** If the customer says "I'll take them off for sure," you respond: "Perfect. Let me just grab a tablet and get you signed up real quick. It takes 30 seconds and you save $39 on today's service." **Do it right then.**

- **Treat membership like a close, not an upsell.** It's a recurring revenue product, so it deserves your full attention at the moment of interest. Don't park it for later.

- **Practice two closes for maintenance calls:**
  - **Immediate close** (while you're in the room): "Ready to enroll?"
  - **Backup close** (if deferred): Return to the customer with tablet in hand before you leave. "Okay, I'm all done up there. Let's get you that membership started so you're covered." No waiting, no asking twice.
