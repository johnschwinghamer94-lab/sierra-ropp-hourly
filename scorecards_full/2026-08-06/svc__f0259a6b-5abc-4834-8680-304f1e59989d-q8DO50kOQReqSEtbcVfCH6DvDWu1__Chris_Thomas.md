**Call Date:** 2026-08-06  
**Duration:** 184 min  
**Outcome:** CLOSED ($3,250 compressor + dual soft-start kits + 4 filters)

## Band Ratings

**Welcome: Strong**
- Friendly greeting with names, immediately built rapport ("Good morning", "Nice to meet you guys")
- Set clear expectations upfront for the maintenance visit
- Explained the inspection process and positioned himself as the problem-solver

**Diagnosis: Strong**
- Thorough inspection of both outdoor units and identified specific failures
- Documented findings with photos and audio evidence (the electrical hum video)
- Clear technical explanation: explained the burnt contactor switch, what causes the noise, and the cascade failure risk ($6,000-$8,000 compressor replacement)
- Educated the customer on LRA (locked rotor amperage) being 90.3 vs target of ~78, showing strain on compressor
- Explained filter strategy and why 3-inch filters are superior (30-40% dust capture vs 15%)

**Options: Strong**
- Presented 4-5 distinct options:
  1. Replace just the voltage switch ($450 part-for-part)
  2. Microprocessor upgrade ($515, eliminates mechanical parts, 5x longer lifespan, 2-year warranty)
  3. Soft-start kits for both systems (protect against electrical surge damage)
  4. 3-inch filters with quarterly maintenance
  5. Financing available
- Each option had specific, clear pricing
- Explained the value proposition: soft-start kit prevents the "redline the engine" scenario by gradually ramping up compressor load

**Close: Strong**
- Directly asked for the business after presenting the package
- Handled price objection smoothly: "Looking at like $3,000?" → reduced to "$3,250 with a discount" → met them at "$3,250 for you"
- Secured payment confirmation and signatures
- Confirmed next steps (tech work outside, then attic check)

## Key Strengths

1. **Technical credibility:** The burnt contactor explanation with side-by-side comparison of the damaged vs undamaged switch was highly persuasive. Customer could literally see the problem.

2. **Value framing:** Positioned soft-start kits not as add-ons but as insurance against catastrophic failure. The car analogy ("redline the engine 20-30 times a day") made abstract concepts concrete.

3. **Flexibility:** When customer hesitated at $3,500, reduced to $3,250 without making it seem like a concession — positioned as a manager approval ("He took $2.50 off for you").

4. **Customer engagement:** Long call (184 min) suggests tech spent time walking customers through findings, answering questions, building trust. Customer even wrapped a sandwich for him.

## Gaps

1. **Membership:** A $16/month membership with 2x annual maintenance visits would have been a strong add-on pitch, especially given the high-value repair closed. Should have offered it.

2. **Urgency framing on second unit:** The larger system was even worse (123 vs 109 LRA), but tech didn't emphasize that the second soft-start is equally urgent — could have positioned both as a package deal with higher perceived value.

## Coaching Observations

- This is a WIN call: high-ticket close with strong technical foundation and clear value communication
- Membership offering remains the only consistent gap across service calls
- The "redline engine" analogy and side-by-side visual comparison of the contactor should be replicated in other high-amperage situations
