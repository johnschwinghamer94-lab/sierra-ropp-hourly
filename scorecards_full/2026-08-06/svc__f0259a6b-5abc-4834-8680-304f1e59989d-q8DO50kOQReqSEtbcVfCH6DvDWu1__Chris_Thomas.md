## Coaching Notes

**What Worked:**
1. **Thorough diagnosis builds trust**: The video evidence of the electrical hum, side-by-side comparison of burnt vs. good switches, and concrete cost projection ($6-8k compressor replacement) made the recommendation credible.
2. **Multiple option paths**: Chris didn't force one solution. He offered voltage-switch-only, microprocessor upgrade, and soft start kits as separate decisions, letting the customer build their own ideal package.
3. **Persistence without pushiness**: When customer objected to the $3,000+ price, Chris itemized rather than repeating "you need all of this." He then negotiated down without losing margin—showing he was problem-solving with them, not just selling.
4. **Membership mention with real value**: Soft start pricing came "with membership," but Chris explained the benefit (5-year protection, discount), not just the membership itself.

**One Gap:**
Membership (SAM/service agreement) was not explicitly offered as a standalone product. The call mentioned maintenance discounts and warranty benefits, but Chris never asked "Would you like to enroll in our membership to lock in these discounts?" This could have been an add-on that stacks value.

**Why It Closed:**
The customer kept saying yes because each recommendation solved a real, concrete problem (electrical hum → failing switch; high amp draw → compressor risk; dusty house → better filters). Chris's job wasn't to convince them to buy; it was to help them understand the problem and offer solutions. They bought.
