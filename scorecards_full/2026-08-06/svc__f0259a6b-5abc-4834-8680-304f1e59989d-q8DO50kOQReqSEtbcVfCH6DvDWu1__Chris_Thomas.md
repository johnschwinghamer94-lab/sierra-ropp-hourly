## Expanded Coaching

**Strengths:** Chris is a master of diagnosis, technical storytelling, and value-building. He doesn't oversell; he explains consequences and lets the customer feel the risk. His analogies (car engine redline, electrical hum video) make complex systems tangible. He handles objections with empathy ("Okay, well, I can probably work out a little bit of a discount") rather than dismissal.

**What's working:**

1. **Visual proof.** Chris took a video of the hum and a photo comparison of the burnt vs. good switch. This removes doubt and justifies the upgrade.

2. **Quantified risk.** "$6,000 to $8,000" compressor failure makes the $3,250 repair feel like a bargain. Customers buy when they feel they're preventing a worse loss.

3. **Multiple options.** Four distinct choices mean the customer is choosing what *level* of solution they want, not whether to buy. This is a win-frame.

4. **Membership integration.** Chris frames soft starts and the microprocessor within the membership pricing, so the customer feels the bundle discount and sees longer-term value.

**To sustain or improve:**

1. **Tighten the second-half pacing.** After the customer says "Okay, let's do it," move quickly to payment. The 1.5-hour call could be 90 minutes without losing any closes.

2. **Proactive secondary-system upsell.** When you notice both units are over-amped, offer the microprocessor for both: "For peace of mind and to avoid a future breakdown on the second unit, I'd recommend upgrading that one too—it's the same $515." You left ~$500 on the table here.

3. **Pre-call checklist.** Before arriving, confirm the customer has authority and funds to approve work. Chris had to work around a payment-card expiration mid-call ("Oh, that one's expired"), which disrupts flow.
