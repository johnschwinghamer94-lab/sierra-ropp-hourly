## Band Details

### Welcome: Strong
**Greeting and Expectations Set**

Joe opened with friendliness ("How are you today?") and quickly built rapport. He established clear expectations upfront:

> "It's $39 for the tune-up special. The tune-up special does not include the cleaning of the outdoor unit. It's a full inspection. That's all that it is. We'll check in for safety and performance, making sure that everything is operating as it should."

He signaled transparency about potential findings:
> "I don't expect to notice anything out of the ordinary. However, if I do, I'll bring it to your attention, let you know what's going on."

This set Rod at ease about both cost and scope.

---

### Diagnosis: Strong
**Thorough Assessment with Clear Technical Explanations**

Joe conducted an extensive evaluation:
- Inspected outdoor unit (2019 compressor, 2007 indoor system)
- Identified mismatched components causing stress
- Measured actual vs. rated power draw

**Technical Findings Explained:**
> "Your compressor has a locked rotor amps rating of 127.9. That is how much power that compressor can handle when it turns on. Anything over that lets me know that there's internal damage inside that compressor. You're pulling in 143.4."

> "You're at 143. About 20% over what you're allotted. That lets me know that that compressor is on its last leg."

Joe also identified secondary issues:
- Metal fatigue on heating element with risk of breaches
- Dirty coil causing overwork
- Control board with hot spots
- Estimated failure cost: $6,500 per component ($13,000 if both fail)

Rod acknowledged understanding: "Okay" and accepted the diagnosis. The 20-degree temperature split was working, but the system was overworking because of mismatches.

---

### Options: Solid
**Multiple Paths Offered, but Not as Clear Good/Better/Best**

Joe presented four distinct offerings:

1. **Tune-up Only:** $39 (inspection, no cleaning)
2. **Tune-up with Cleaning:** $149 (outdoor unit cleaning added)
3. **Membership:** $16/month (includes annual summer cleaning, winter service, plumbing inspection, water heater flush, complimentary diagnostics, 15% repair discount, 4 visits/year, 1-year contract)
4. **System Quote:** Option C — schedule specialist to provide replacement system pricing

The membership emerged as the primary recommendation, positioned as preventive maintenance to catch issues before failure. However, these weren't presented as a cohesive good/better/best package. Instead, Joe explained the problems first, then offered the membership, with the system quote as a secondary consideration.

Rod initially hesitated on the system quote ("I'll go get it myself") but committed to the membership.

---

### Close: Solid
**Direct Ask with Objection Handling, Partial Success**

Joe asked directly for commitment:
> "So you want to do the membership?"

Rod agreed: "Yeah, I'll do that."

**Objection Handling:**
When Rod expressed skepticism about cost and pushed back on feeling pressured about the system quote, Joe responded:
> "Listen, you know I get paid to do the repairs. That's how I get paid. I don't want you to do these repairs... I don't want you to do repairs."

And later:
> "I'm on your side here, Rod. I'm gonna get you taken care of, okay?"

However, when Rod explicitly said he hated being pressured, Joe didn't skillfully reframe. Instead, he ended with:
> "You have a wonderful day. Take care."

The membership close was solid (signed contract, payment info collected, confirmation of $16/month charge on 15th of month, explained 1-year lock with annual cancellation option). But the close could have been **strong** if Joe had repositioned the system quote as non-pressure information gathering rather than a sales push.

## Gaps & Opportunities

1. **System Quote Close:** Rod's defensive pushback ("I hate being forced to something") signaled the system quote pitch was feeling transactional. A softer reframe ("Let's just gather the info so you have it when you talk to others") might have kept the specialist visit on the calendar.

2. **Objection Resolution:** When Rod said the way Joe was talking was "pushing" him, Joe ended the call rather than defusing with empathy and a question. Stronger close would have been:
   > "I hear you—you want to stay in control of this. I get that. The system quote is info, no obligation. When works best for you?"

---

## Summary

Joe demonstrated strong diagnostic and technical skills, building credibility through detailed power-draw analysis. His welcome set clear expectations, and his diagnosis was thorough. The membership close was successful. However, the overall close could have been stronger if he'd read Rod's pressure-sensitivity earlier and softened the system quote positioning. The win here is technical credibility unlocking the membership buy.
