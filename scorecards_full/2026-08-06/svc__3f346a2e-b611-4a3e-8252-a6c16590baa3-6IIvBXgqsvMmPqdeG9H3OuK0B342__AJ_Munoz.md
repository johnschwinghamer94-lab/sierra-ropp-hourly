## EXPANDED COACHING ADVICE

### Strengthen the Options Conversation

The transition from diagnosis to outdoor inspection is a key moment to anchor customer confidence. After identifying the problem geography (which rooms are hot), use this script:

**Instead of:** "I'll check the outdoor first, see what's going on."

**Try:** "I've got the picture—these rooms here are getting too warm while the master and Zoe's room are cold. Once I check the outdoor unit, I should be able to see if this is a refrigerant issue, an airflow problem in the ductwork, or a damper setting that's throwing air to the wrong zones. I'll come right back in and walk you through what I find and what your options are. That sound good?"

### Why this Works
- **Educates:** Customers understand possible causes (not scary—just technical)
- **Previews value:** Shows tech will return with actionable options
- **Builds trust:** Customer feels included, not abandoned during the diagnosis phase
- **Prevents outside opinions:** Less likely to call competitors while you're working

### Delivery Technique
Say this **after** you've confirmed the hot/cold rooms but **before** you leave for the outdoor unit. Use a calm, confident tone. The goal is to reassure, not oversell.

### Secondary Focus: Confirm ETA
Always tell customers how long you'll be outside/in attic. Silence creates anxiety. "I'll be about 20 minutes" sets expectations and prevents them from worrying you've found a bigger problem.
