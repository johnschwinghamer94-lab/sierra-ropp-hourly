## Key Findings

### Welcome
Professional greeting ("I'm Chris, by the way... Nice to meet you"). Set expectations about checking the system and explaining findings. Solid rapport with customer.

### Diagnosis
Thorough diagnosis of a recurring problem:
- Customer reported AC not shutting off, temperature reading off by 1 degree
- Chris identified burnt voltage distribution switch ("just a little burnt")
- Explained failure mode: "Once that goes, it can weld together and then your system's just not going to shut off"
- Customer confirmed they'd been experiencing this issue: "Yeah, we've been having that problem already with the thermostat"
- Chris checked warranty status via manufacturer website (had difficulty loading)
- Determined unit was installed 2-3 years ago, likely still in warranty
- Advised customer to contact manufacturer, register the unit, and call back for free replacement

Strong technical diagnosis with clear explanation of the problem's consequences.

### Options
Limited options discussion:
- Option 1: Replace voltage switch ($225 parts, without warranty coverage, potentially free labor if warranty applies)
- Advised calling manufacturer first to confirm warranty registration
- Suggested customer get the unit registered, then call Sierra back for installation

However, no "good/better/best" repair+upgrade options were offered. Should have proposed: (a) basic switch replacement, (b) switch + full system inspection, (c) preventive upgrades like soft start kit or capacitor check.

### Close
No direct ask for the sale. Chris said "everything else is great on it, it's working great. So if you continue to have the problem, I'd say give us a call and we'll come back out." Essentially a referral close rather than a commitment close. Left the decision-making to the customer after they contact the manufacturer.

## Top Gaps

1. **Warranty uncertainty blocked the sale** — Chris couldn't confirm warranty status in real-time, which created friction. Should have said: "Even if warranty is unclear, we can install this today for $225 and you'll know it's fixed" to give customer an immediate option.

2. **No upgrade options** — Should have offered soft start kit, capacitor check, or preventive maintenance package alongside the switch replacement.

3. **No membership pitch** — 40-minute troubleshooting call with zero mention of membership.

4. **Pass-the-buck close** — Handed the problem back to customer ("call the manufacturer") rather than owning the solution path.

## Coaching Advice

**FIX —** "When the customer hesitated because of warranty uncertainty, you should have said: 'Call the manufacturer to register it—that takes 10 minutes. In the meantime, I can replace the switch today for $225 and you'll have it fixed immediately. If the warranty comes through, we'll credit you back.' Then offer a soft start kit or filter upgrade to make the visit profitable today instead of hoping they call back after they sort out the warranty."
