## Coaching: WIN vs. FIX

### WIN: Credibility through Transparency + Family Leverage
Corey executed a textbook service call:

1. **Deep diagnosis first**: Ran amp draw tests, checked refrigeration, explained dual-component failure (fan running, compressor not)
2. **Transparency builds trust**: Acknowledged the problem was compressor overworking, not catastrophic failure
3. **Relationship leverage**: Worked with Harry (cousin/service manager) to apply family discount, showing customer care
4. **Options scaffold**: Started with simple repair ($166), then positioned soft start ($1,066) as protective upgrade
5. **Financing removes objection**: When customer hesitated on price ("I only got a thousand dollars right now"), Corey offered no-interest financing

The membership pitch integrated naturally into both options - it wasn't an add-on, it was the foundation of the pricing. Customer's uncertainty resolved quickly ("It's smart... Sounds like a plan").

### FIX: Soft Start Could Have Been Presented Earlier
While this call closed well, the soft start device (which prevents the exact problem the compressor is experiencing) was only introduced after the base price was quoted. Better positioning:

- After explaining the 20amp overload problem (line 935-946), immediately offer soft start as the "protective upgrade"
- Then offer: "We can do the starter alone ($190), or add the soft start to really protect your system ($1,066)"
- This creates parallel options earlier, before customer mentally anchors to the $166 number

Also, the financing option was mentioned late (line 1983+). Consider bringing it up when you sense price hesitation: "If the $1,066 feels too much today, we have financing - no interest, 12 months to pay."
