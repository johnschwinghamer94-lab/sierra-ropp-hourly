## EXPANDED COACHING ADVICE

### Use Financial Comparison to Justify Membership

Corey's "$83 savings" statement was the key to converting this customer from a one-time repair to an ongoing maintenance plan. Copy this formula:

**Pattern:** "Maintenance program cost → One-time repair cost → Difference (savings) → Added benefits over time"

**Instead of:** "Our maintenance program is $16 a month" (sounds like extra cost)

**Try:** "With the maintenance program, you're at $490 for the capacitor replacement plus the first year of coverage. Paying outright is $557. So you're actually saving $83 today, plus you get two-year warranty and annual maintenance instead of one year. That's the better play."

### Why This Works
- **Reframes maintenance:** From "additional expense" to "smart financial choice"
- **Creates urgency:** Savings available only if they enroll today
- **Justifies higher features:** 2-year warranty is worth it because it's cheaper
- **Builds confidence:** Logical financial reasoning appeals to rational decision-making

### Script for Different Customer Types

**Budget-conscious customers:** "I know cost matters. The maintenance program actually saves you money compared to paying outright today. Plus, next time something breaks—and it will—you've got warranty coverage and emergency visits included."

**Warranty-focused customers:** "You get a 1-year warranty for paying outright, or a 2-year warranty plus annual maintenance for less money on the maintenance program. It's the stronger coverage option."

**Customer skeptical of "programs":** "Look, you're already spending $490 for the repair. Most customers find they break even in year 2 when something else happens and the warranty covers it."
