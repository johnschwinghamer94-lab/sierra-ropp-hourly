## Scoring

Diagnosis is thorough, transparent, and explained in accessible terms.

### Options: **Moderate**

- 2 free premium 3-inch filters
- Dedicated tech (Joseph as the primary)
- Potential for a supervisor to come out if needed
- Implied 15% discount on additional work

No tiered repair/upgrade framework (e.g., "Here's a quick filter change for $X, a mid-tier duct balancing for $Y, or a full system maintenance for $Z"). A missed opportunity to layer additional value.

### Close: **Moderate**
Joseph makes a direct ask: "If you want to do the membership still, talk to my management. We'd be happy to just have me be your only tech that ever comes out. ## Gaps & Coaching Tip

**Top Gap:** When Jenny hesitated on the membership due to cancellation lock-in concerns, Joseph folded rather than reframe. He had a perfect opening to say something like: "You're in control—30-day cancellation anytime, no penalty. The $180 just locks in your annual rate and guarantees me as your tech. Cancel whenever, no questions asked."

**Secondary Gap:** No tiered options. The membership was presented as an all-or-nothing choice. Offering smaller upgrades (premium filters for $25, annual ductwork inspection for $49, etc.) would have created stepping stones to the full membership.

**Coaching Tip:**  
**FIX —** When Jenny said "if I can't cancel next year," you had the chance to reframe value vs. commitment: "You can cancel anytime—I'm not locking you in. The $180 just keeps your rate locked and gets you those premium filters included and me as your tech." Instead, you backed off without clarifying the terms. Next time, lean into the objection with a question: "What would make the membership feel right for you?"
