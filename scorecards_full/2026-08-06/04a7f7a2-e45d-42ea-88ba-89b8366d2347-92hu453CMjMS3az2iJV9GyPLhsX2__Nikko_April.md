## Outcome Analysis

**What happened:** No commitment was made during the call. No Comfort Advisor appointment was scheduled. No agreement to proceed with repair or replacement was obtained.

**Why:** Homeowner (decision-maker) was out of town and unreachable via phone. Nikko appropriately did not pressure the daughters into an unauthorized decision. He committed to writing a report and presenting it to Andy upon contact.

**Coaching perspective:** This call was set up for Nikko to lose. A strong tech in this scenario would have:
1. Completed the diagnosis (Nikko did this well)
2. Written the report (Nikko committed to this)
3. **Scheduled a specific callback time for a Comfort Advisor to present options formally to Andy** (Nikko did NOT do this)

The missed moment came when the customer asked: "And how long will it take to get all of this done so that it's up and working?"

At that point, Nikko had the customer's full attention and a warm opening. Instead of just listing timeline, he could have said:
> "Great question. Here's the timeline: if we do a leak search, that's 2-8 hours depending on how obvious the leak is. If you decide to replace the unit, we can probably get out here as soon as tomorrow. Let me finish up this report, and let's get our Comfort Advisor scheduled to call you and Andy first thing in the morning — I'd like him to walk you through all three options side-by-side so you have a clear picture before making a decision. Does 9 AM work for you?"

That move would have created a firm "Option C" appointment = a flipped lead in ServiceTitan.

## Strengths to Reinforce

1. **Diagnostic thoroughness:** Nikko didn't assume the issue based on the symptom. He inspected both systems, checked electrical, tested capacitor, went into the attic. That's a 9.5/10 diagnostic.

2. **Customer education:** Explaining refrigerant pressure, capacitor function, annual maintenance ROI, and government refrigerant mandates in plain English builds trust and positions Sierra as the expert.

3. **Appropriate caution:** Nikko did NOT pressure absent homeowner into an unauthorized decision. That's good judgment and compliance.

---

## Areas to Develop

1. **Option C lock-in:** Always aim to schedule the specialist callback *during* the diagnostic call, not after. Once the homeowner goes back to his day, inertia sets in.

2. **Framing the three options as a formal choice:** Could say, "I'm going to present you with three scenarios: repair (short-term, higher recurring cost), single-unit replacement, or both-unit replacement (peace of mind, 10-year warranty). Let's schedule time with our Comfort Advisor to compare them side-by-side."

3. **The close in the room:** Even though Andy was absent, Nikko could have asked Tiffany/Susan: "When Andy gets back in town or when he's free to talk, what time would work for him to hear from our specialist? Let me lock that in for him right now so he's expecting the call." This builds urgency and ownership.
