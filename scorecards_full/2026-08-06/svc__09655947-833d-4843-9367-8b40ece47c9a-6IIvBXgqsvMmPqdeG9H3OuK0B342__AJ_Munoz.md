## Outcome
**No-close** — Customer received membership pitch but transcript ends before decision; no repair work or upgrades offered.

## Key Findings

### Welcome
Warm, professional greeting ("Hi... Nice to meet you"). AJ introduces himself, asks how the unit has been running, gets permission to adjust the thermostat. Sets expectation of a maintenance tune-up. Professional but brief.

### Diagnosis
AJ mentions he'll do a tune-up and check both indoor and outdoor units. "Just make sure everything's working well." Limited technical explanation or assessment shown in this segment. Focused on execution rather than education. 

### Options
AJ offers only the membership during the visit—no repair or upgrade options are discussed. The pitch is comprehensive ($16/month covers: summer AC maintenance, winter heating maintenance, plumbing inspection, one free emergency visit, chemical coil cleaning, gas pressure adjustment, priority service, same-day parts, 15% discount on repairs, 2-year labor and parts warranty). However, this is a membership pitch, not repair/upgrade options. A true SERVICE call should offer good/better/best on the repair or upgrade in front of them after diagnosis.

The customer says "If you want to explain, go ahead" and listens to the full membership benefit list, then says "You can just hold on to that if you want to think about it while [I work]." No decision reached.

### Close
No direct ask for the membership or repair sale. AJ says "I'll get started. I'll do the attic first" and moves to work. Ended the pitch without a commitment from the customer—implied decision-deferral ("think about it").

## Top Gaps

1. **No repair options** — Maintenance-only visit. AJ should have identified potential upgrades or additional services based on system inspection and offered 2-3 pricing tiers (basic maintenance, better with coil cleaning, best with membership).

2. **Soft close on membership** — Handed materials and said "think about it" rather than asking for a decision. Should have used assumptive close: "Great, let me get you set up with the membership" or direct ask: "Want to move forward with that $16/month?"

3. **Short interaction** — 6 minutes is very brief. Likely a quicker preventative maintenance where the tech rushed through the membership pitch without building urgency around what could break.

## Coaching Advice

**FIX —** "After walking them through the membership benefits, they were ready to hear a yes/no question. Instead of 'think about it while I work,' ask directly: 'Want me to get that $16/month started today?' A decision right then beats hoping they'll say yes when you're done. Also, check the unit *first*, then pitch options—'I noticed the coil's dusty, we can include a cleaning for $X with the membership' gives them a reason to say yes *today*."
