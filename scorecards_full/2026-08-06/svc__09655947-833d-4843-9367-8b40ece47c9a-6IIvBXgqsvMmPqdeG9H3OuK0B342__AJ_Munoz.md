## EXPANDED COACHING ADVICE

### Convert "Think About It" Into a Decision

After presenting membership benefits (which AJ did well), use a direct close that removes the decision friction.

**Instead of:** "You can just hold on to that if you want to think about it while."

**Try:** "Based on everything I've shown you, here's the thing—most customers sign up because it saves them money on repairs and gives them peace of mind with two annual tune-ups. Can we get you signed up today? We'll get you started at $16 a month."

### Why This Works
- **Removes indefinite delay:** "Think about it" often means "no" (customer won't call back)
- **Creates urgency:** Asks for decision while customer is engaged
- **Acknowledges interest:** "Most customers sign up" normalizes the decision
- **Specific ask:** "Today" moves the conversation forward

### Script by Customer Response Type

**If customer hesitates:** "Tell me what concerns you about the membership. Let's address it right now."

**If customer wants to discuss with spouse:** "Absolutely. Quick call to your wife right now? Easier than calling back later." (Only if appropriate)

**If customer says 'maybe later':** "Here's the thing—if you don't enroll today, you might forget. And honestly, the value really shows when you need an emergency visit and the fee is waived. Can we lock this in right now? Takes two minutes."

### Closing Sequence (After Full Benefit Explanation)

1. **Validate understanding:** "So you've got the picture—two tune-ups a year, emergency coverage, 15% discount..."
2. **State the decision:** "This is a no-brainer for your situation."
3. **Ask the close:** "Can we get you signed up today?"
4. **Handle response immediately:** Don't hand off to another process—get commitment or reason right then

### Delivery Technique
- Use **confident tone**, not questioning
- Say "Can we get you signed up" not "Would you like to"
- Get **immediate yes/no**, not indefinite "maybe"
- If yes: Process it right there before finishing the job
- If no: Ask why and address the objection on the spot

**Key insight:** AJ has excellent people skills and product knowledge. The only gap is confidence in asking for the sale when the moment is right. This customer was ready; AJ just needed to ask.
