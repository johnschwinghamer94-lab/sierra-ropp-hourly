## Key Gaps

### Gap 1: Weak Close Despite Strong Offer
Tech explained membership value clearly and customer showed interest, but tech said "hold onto this and think about it" instead of asking for commitment. This passive language kills urgency. A trial close ("Can I get you set up today?") or a constraint-based close ("We fill up fast; should I reserve your first service?") would have converted interest into action.

### Gap 2: No Tiered Repair Path
Membership was presented in isolation. Should have been bundled with repair options—e.g., "$150 repair + $40/month membership" vs. "$150 repair alone." Giving the customer a choice between two solutions (one with membership, one without) creates urgency; asking them to think about a standalone membership creates hesitation.

## Coaching Advice

**FIX — Tech explained membership value clearly, but said 'hold onto this and think about it' instead of asking directly for commitment; no trial close or urgency.**

You nailed the pitch on why membership saves money. The close is where you lost it. When someone's listening and engaged, that's your moment—don't punt. Instead of "think about it," try: "Let me get you set up today; first service is included with new memberships." Make it a yes/no decision right now, not a homework assignment. Passive language sends customers to your competitor. Go for the close.
