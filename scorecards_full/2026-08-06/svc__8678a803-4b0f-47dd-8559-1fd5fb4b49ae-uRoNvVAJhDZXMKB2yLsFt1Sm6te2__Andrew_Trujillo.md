## GAPS & COACHING

### Strength to Reinforce
Andrew's diagnostic thoroughness was excellent. His 271-minute inspection covered all systems, identified multiple issues, and communicated urgency about timeline ("we could need our systems...at least October"). He built trust through patient rapport-building with a customer new to the home.

### Critical Gap: Options & Close
This was a classic "diagnostic handoff" call, not a sales call. Andrew had two opportunities to shift this from diagnostic to sales:

1. **At options point:** Instead of "it's hard for me to give you a rough ballpark," Andrew could have said: "Deborah, based on what I found, I want to walk you through your actual choices here. We can do Lennox two-stage at [price], Lennox single-stage at [price], or Goodman at [price]. Which resonates with you?" This would have moved to options on the spot.

2. **At close point:** Andrew could have asked: "What makes sense for you—would you like to move forward with a replacement, or would you like to wait for the quote from my colleague?" This direct ask is missing.

### Why It Matters
A 271-minute inspection builds enormous credibility and trust. That relationship is the prime moment to present options and close. By handing off to someone else, Andrew lost the sale momentum and gave the customer time to shop competitors or reconsider. The next tech will have to rebuild trust from scratch.
