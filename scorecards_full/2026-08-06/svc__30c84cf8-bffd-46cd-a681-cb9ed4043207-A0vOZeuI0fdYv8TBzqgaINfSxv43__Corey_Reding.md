## Critical Actions

**Expectations** ✓ TRUE  
Set clear expectations: explained cost to diagnose, what would happen if motor failed (compressor damage), mentioned warranty status

**Options** ✗ FALSE  
Only one repair option presented (motor replacement at stated price points). No upgrade paths, no preventive options, no filter/maintenance upsells

**Membership** ✗ FALSE  
No membership or maintenance agreement offered despite customer stress about cost. Would have been ideal opportunity to discuss ongoing maintenance to catch similar issues

**Close** ✗ FALSE  
Did not secure the sale. Customer politely declined, and Corey accepted deferral without re-engagement

## Outcome Evidence

Customer statement: "I'm going to say put it back on. I'll live with the sound and I'll pay attention to it. If it goes, then it goes. And then it's you're going to replace it."

This is a clear no-close. Customer acknowledged the risk but chose to delay investment.

---

## Gaps & Opportunities

1. **No membership pitch** – Customer just paid $2,500 on spa repairs and was clearly budget-conscious. Should have offered SAM membership to spread cost and add value
2. **Limited options** – Could have presented premium motor replacement for better longevity, or a phased approach (monitor now, commit to replacement in 30 days with financing)
3. **Missed follow-up** – Should have locked in a callback commitment or given customer a phone number to call proactively if noise worsened

---

## Coaching Tip

**FIX —** When customer hesitates on price after hearing repair cost, offer membership immediately (16/month for 15% off repairs + future maintenance savings) rather than just asking manager for additional discount—gives customer control of the budget and binds you to future service visits.
