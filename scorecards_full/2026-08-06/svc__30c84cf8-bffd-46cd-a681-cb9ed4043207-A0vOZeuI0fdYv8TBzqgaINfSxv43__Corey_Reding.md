## COACHING NOTES

**This tech did the diagnosis perfectly—showed the damage, explained the risk—but lost the sale at the objection.**  

Customer was ready to listen after you found the insulation damage and showed the oil residue. That's when you present OPTIONS, not just ONE solution. Next time after a solid diagnosis like this:

1. **Offer the ideal solution** (replace motor today, $1,044).
2. **Offer a monitoring plan** (weekly checks, if oil loss or noise accelerates, we replace; if stable, we monitor).
3. **Offer financing** (e.g., $260/month for 4 months, no interest if paid in 90 days).
4. **Lock in a membership** (15% discount forever + free emergency trip could've bridged the $1k objection).

When the customer says "I don't have $1,000," the answer is "Great—here's what we CAN do today: [option 2 or 3]." Don't retreat; advance with alternatives. The customer just told you the problem: it's the cash constraint, not the urgency. Address the constraint, not the objection.
