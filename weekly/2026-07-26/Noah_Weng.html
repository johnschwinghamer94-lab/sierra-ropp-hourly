Strengths

1. The Comfort Advisor handoff executed exactly as the guide describes it (Strong)

Call 7 is the best handoff in the window from any technician. Noah tells the customer the truth about what Sierra can and cannot touch, offers the replacement path, and then works the mechanics: So I'll go ahead and call the office and get somebody out here, then We're going to send our guy Rob down here… I'm going to wait until he gets here, so. Just so I can show him everything. He stays at the table and keeps the conversation off HVAC while they wait — asking about the lake house, the wife, the realtors — and he does not leave until Rob dismisses him on the customer's behalf. The customer notices: you could have left earlier, man, but I appreciate you staying around. Coaching value: this is the primary win of the SILO role performed to standard with no prompting; it should be recorded and used as the team's reference example.

2. The Agenda Card said out loud, the same way, every time (Strong)

Four separate recordings contain a near-identical expectations block, which means it is a habit rather than a performance. Call 2 is the fullest version: I'll go ahead and do a thorough evaluation… If I happen to notice anything else other than the main issue, do you guys want me to let you know about it?… I'll go ahead and write my report in my van. It'll take me about 20 minutes, get your pricing on everything… I don't do anything without your guys's approval, so no unexpected bills. And then if you do approve something… we can get the ball rolling. On Call 4 he adapts it to a four-system remodel — Might take me about a half an hour to write all these reports — without dropping a single box, and on Call 6 he adds the check criteria: Check for safetiness or safety, operation and cleanliness… and then just anything that could happen in the future. Coaching value: he has already automated the hardest habit on the card; protect it and use it as proof to him that the Decision-step script will automate the same way.

3. Naming the upgrade path before the customer reaches for it (Strong)

On both of his scoreable calls the words "replace" and "specialist" come out of Noah's mouth first. Call 1 opens with it: I'll take a look and give you an evaluation of the unit and just, like, let you know what condition it's in. And then somebody will come out later today and go ahead and give you an estimate on what it would be to replace. Call 7 he offers it as the constructive alternative to a refusal: If we can't do the leak search and repair, I can talk to them about. Just starting over with new stuff and getting it installed the right way with new warranties. That's up to you, though. Coaching value: his 100% handoff rate is not luck — it is this sentence, and it should be the language other techs are taught.

4. Escalating for authority instead of guessing at it (Strong)

Facing a system that could not be legally repaired, Noah did not improvise and did not stall — he left the customer, called his manager, and came back with a decision and a reason. Call 7: I'm gonna call my manager real quick… what he'll let us do, then I talked to my boss and said we can't put hands on it. The speed limit all the way up to code, and that means full replacement. He framed the constraint as Sierra's standard rather than the customer's problem — if it's not up to code, we're too. Too big of a business. We won't — and gave the customer an honest out: If you want to try to find somebody else, by all means. We just. We can't be liable. Coaching value: he converted a refusal into the week's cleanest turnover lead, which is exactly the judgment the flip doctrine depends on.

Consistent Gaps

(patterns across many calls)

Gap 1 — Telling customers he is not the person who prices things (Weak)

Twice this week Noah verbally removed himself from the Decision step. Call 2, to a customer whose compressor he had just diagnosed as damaged: You either replace the compressor or you can replace the unit. So I'm just a repair technician. I don't have calls on unit repair. Call 7, again: And I'm just. I'm just repairs, so… I don't even have prices on. The SILO role is precisely to build the four-option sheet, and by disclaiming it he leaves the customer with a diagnosis and nothing to decide between. Impact: it is the most likely single reason his Repair-Won rate is 0% — on Call 2 a damaged compressor, a burnt smell and a tripping breaker produced no Option A at all.

Gap 2 — No option sheet, no price stated, no close on any recording (Weak)

Across eight recordings the only price Noah says inside a customer's home all week is for filters: they are $90 a piece, depending on how many filters you need (Call 4). Nowhere does he hand a sheet over, state Immediate and Future repairs, or ask which option works best today. On Call 3 the customer pre-empts him with her own number — sometimes it's like $2,000 to fix your machine — and his entire answer is Yeah. I mean, depending on what it is. Impact: six of eight recordings end with the customer holding a diagnosis and no priced choice, which is why his week has two flips and nothing else.

Gap 3 — He plants the five-star seed and never harvests it (Weak)

The Agenda Card seed is in four recordings — overall, I'm here for five star service, so if you see me doing something you don't want me to do… let me know (Call 6) — and there is not one review ask anywhere in the window. Call 6 ends Hopefully not for long. Okay, thanks; Call 7, after a handoff the customer explicitly thanked him for, ends Good luck on your trip. Impact: he does the difficult half of the review process on every call and skips the ten-second half, on calls where the customer has already volunteered that they appreciate him.

Gap 4 — Rapport that consumes the recording (Moderate)

Noah's warmth is real and customers reciprocate, but on several calls it is where the Assessment went. Call 5 runs through a house fire, a rebuild, a lawsuit and an $8,000-a-month rental — You'd think for $8,000 a month, though, this place would come with a butler or something — and contains no presented findings at all. Contrast Call 1, where the same instinct is used productively: he connects over Pennsylvania and West Virginia early, then moves straight into the airflow walkthrough. Impact: on Calls 2, 5 and 6 the customer's attention was spent before the options conversation could start.

Gap 5 — Price Transparency deferred to the van (Moderate)

His Agenda Card promises pricing later by design — I'll go ahead and write my report in my van. It'll take me about 20 minutes, get your pricing on everything (Call 2), and half an hour on Call 4 — and no ballpark is ever given before he goes. On Call 6 the customer tells him up front that money is the constraint (I know they're really expensive to replace, so if you have some sort of cheaper solution, that'd be great) and gets no range in response. Impact: the customers who most need a scale to think against — Calls 3 and 6, both of whom raised affordability themselves — spend the whole visit without one.

Noah has no no-move calls this week — he has two flips and six recordings that stop before anything is decided, so the real contrast is between the calls where he took the Decision step at all and the calls where the recording ends with him walking out to the van: on Call 7 he made a decision happen by calling the office, and on Call 2 an equally serious diagnosis simply dissolved into conversation.

Decisive Gap — the Decision

Decisive gap — the Decision. Same tech, same Agenda Card, same quality of technical explanation — but two very different endings. On Call 1 he named the specialist visit in his opening sentence and the customer was asking about new-unit timelines by the end (The time frame that if you need to put the new unit in, how long it'll take); on Call 7 he called the office, stayed at the table and handed the customer to Rob in person. On Call 2 he diagnosed a damaged compressor and a burning smell and the recording ends with When was the last time you guys changed your filters; on Call 6 a 17-year-old 5.5-ton system on a no-cool call ends with tattoo artists; on Call 5 a full hour produces no presented finding at all. The variable is not warmth, expectations or diagnosis — Noah has all three on every call — it is whether he takes the Decision step or walks to the van instead.

Beyond the Guide

He treats a compliance refusal as a sales opening

Call 7 could have ended with "we can't help you." Instead Noah told the customer why (if it's not up to code, we're too. Too big of a business), gave him permission to go elsewhere (If you want to try to find somebody else, by all means), and then offered the alternative that actually solved the problem (Just starting over with new stuff and getting it installed the right way with new warranties). Coaching implication: this reflex — the honest no followed immediately by the constructive option — is transferable to every price objection he currently has no answer for.

Non-customer audio is landing inside customer recordings

Call 7 opens mid-personal-call with I literally just pulled up to a customer's house. I'm about to get out of my van and includes the line I'll be honest with you. Customers keep telling him money is the constraint and he never picks it up

Noah answers Okay. That's awesome and moves on. Coaching implication: this is a multi-property account signal that belongs in a note to management the same day, and Noah is the reason Sierra heard it at all.

Top 3 Coaching Priorities

(ranked by impact)

  • Stop disclaiming the options role and start building the sheet. Noah currently tells customers So I'm just a repair technician. I don't have calls on unit repair (Call 2). He should instead present the full sheet — Immediate Repairs, Future Repairs with the efficiency statement, System Update — state the prices once, hand it over and go quiet. On Call 2 that alone would have turned a damaged compressor, a tripped breaker and a burning smell into an Option A instead of a conversation about the Sopranos.
  • Give a ballpark in the home before walking to the van. His own Agenda Card defers pricing by twenty to thirty minutes, and on Call 6 a customer who had already said replacement was really expensive got no range at all. A spoken range at the unit keeps the number attached to the failure he just demonstrated and removes the cold reveal his current sequence guarantees.
  • Harvest the five-star seed he already plants. He says I'm here for five star service on four calls and asks for a review on none. On Call 7 the customer said I appreciate you staying around unprompted — that is the moment to reference the seed and ask, and it costs him ten seconds on calls where the goodwill already exists.

Pre-call parts prep is worth a quick consistency reminder, but it is a distant fourth — the missing filters on Call 4 cost one same-day job, whereas the absent option sheet is why the whole week produced zero repair revenue.
