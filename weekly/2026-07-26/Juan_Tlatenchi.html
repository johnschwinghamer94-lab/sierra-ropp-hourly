Strengths

1. The Agenda Card delivered in full, in the customer's own language (Strong)

Juan's expectations block is the most complete on his team and it survives translation. Call 3: I'll do the outside, come inside and do the inside portion, and then I'll come and touch bases with you… I will have to step outside and just write a report for you, and I can include some options, depending what it is I find. And I don't really work without your approval so… you don't have to worry about any surprise Bill. Call 1 closes it with a commitment check — at any point in time you feel like I'm not meeting that expectation. Is that fair? — and Call 7 rebuilds the same structure in Spanish: lo que voy a hacer una inspección total, Voy a ver qué está pasando con el sistema… y ya, pues si me doy cuenta que hay algo más aparte del problema inmediato que te diga. Coaching value: he can already open a call in two languages to the same standard, which makes him the natural person to model Welcome for the rest of the team.

2. Asking what the customer actually wants out of the visit (Strong)

He does not assume the job is the work order. Call 3: what would you say is your overall goal just for this visit?, and the customer answers with a priority Juan can work against — whatever the normal maintenance is… and then yeah if there's any big issues let's talk about it. Call 4 he asks the same question of a customer with an 18-year-old system, and the answer is the whole call: just to have it work now. Now if… if what you propose is… have to buy a whole new unit. Well, that's a different matter because you don't have to time big expenses like that. Coaching value: that second answer is a customer pre-authorising an Option C conversation; he already knows how to elicit it, so the coaching is entirely about what he does with the answer.

3. Putting money on the table before the customer has to raise it (Strong on wins)

On his one flip, financing is offered unprompted and framed around the customer's month, not the invoice. Call 7: todo se puede hacer en pagos ya pues te da chance de como arreglarlo, arreglar tu financial, said directly after the customer worried about the first of the month — ya se viene el primero, ya ves la luz, agua. He then separates the immediate repair from the bigger question: sé que nomás me cuesta 2200 el motor pero en el futuro. Coaching value: this is the exact sequence the guide asks for — immediate repair, affordability, then the upgrade conversation — and it is the only call in his week that produced an outcome.

4. Explaining a fault so the cause is obvious (Solid)

Call 2's blower diagnosis is genuinely well-built: he shows the caked wheel against the filter the customer had just pulled out — That looks like the filter we pulled out / See how bad it is? — then links it forward: it's going to cause the wheel to be imbalanced and it's just make a lot of rattling noise. On top of that, the motors are leaking oil… They're running unlubricated. Coaching value: the customer's own noise complaint is answered with a visible cause, which is the strongest position to present options from — and on that call he never presented any.

What Separates Positive Outcomes From No-Moves

The single difference between Juan's flip and his no-move is who set the agenda: on Call 7 he ran his own evaluation and built from his own findings, and on Call 5 he worked entirely from a piece of paper another company had already filled in.

  • Ready: Moderate on both. Neither call started from a reviewed history — on Call 5 the only history in the room was the competitor's, which is precisely how the call got framed against him.
  • Welcome: Strong on the flip, largely bypassed on the no-move. Call 7 carries the full expectations block in Spanish; on Call 5 there is no agenda at all because he stepped straight into reviewing someone else's work.
  • Assessment: Present on the flip, absent on the no-move. Call 5's I would have to run the evaluation. So is that something you want me to do? is the pivot point of his entire week — one soft ask, one decline, no evaluation.
  • Decision: Strong on wins versus nothing. Call 7 produced an immediate repair, a payment plan and an offer to book the pricing conversation; Call 5 produced a signature waiver.
  • Deliver: Weak on both, with no review ask or membership conversation on any recording in the window.
Decisive Gap — the Assessment into the Decision

Decisive gap — the Assessment. Same tech, same warmth, same fluent explanation of what a failing part does — but two very different endings. On Call 7 he ran the inspection himself, then priced the fan motor, offered payments (todo se puede hacer en pagos) and offered to schedule the pricing conversation (puedo hacer el schedule para que venga y te hable alguien del precio), and the call flipped. On Call 5 he offered the evaluation once, took It's already been done for an answer, endorsed a competitor's price twice (pretty fairly priced) and waived the fee. Call 1 and Call 4 sit in between: customers who raised replacement themselves (replace all those pieces or a new piece all together) and got a report promised from the van rather than an option on the table. The variable is not language, rapport or technical ability — it is whether Juan generates his own findings before he starts responding to someone else's.

Beyond the Guide

Call 2's recording captures shop talk in which flip rates, close rates and month-end revenue are discussed in detail — he has like an 80 flip rate or more like 90 flip rate. So he's like flipping almost all his calls — alongside I got the highest of the actual selling rate for when the CAs get there. The vocabulary of the doctrine is completely internalised. Coaching implication: he does not need the flip explained to him; he needs a trigger that fires in the moment, because on Calls 1 and 4 the same person let two customer-raised upgrade conversations pass.

Recorded audio is carrying material that should never be near a customer's home

Across Calls 2 and 4 the recordings contain a private medical matter, pay and commission figures, disparaging remarks about customers, competitor criticism and a handgun purchase — all captured on Sierra's job recordings. Coaching implication: this belongs in a conversation with him before it belongs in a coaching plan; separately, it is why two of his longest jobs produced no assessable Decision step at all.

Spanish-language calls are where his best process shows up

Call 7 is his shortest scoreable recording and his most complete one — inspection framed, immediate item isolated (lo que es inmediato es el fan motor), affordability handled, upgrade conversation offered. Nothing in his English-language recordings this week reaches that structure. Coaching implication: Sierra is getting demonstrably better SILO execution from Juan in Spanish; the fastest coaching route is to have him narrate a Spanish call back in English rather than teach him the process from scratch.

He is trusted with other techs' problems mid-shift

Call 4's recording includes him working through another technician's diagnosis in real time — I would… take the M draw while it's running… you see the high amp draw. Show that to them and be like, look, I can replace this thing, but it's likely that this compressor is going to go out, followed by Don't say, don't say nothing about the pressure switch because you'll just confuse. Coaching implication: he coaches other people to present findings the way he does not consistently present his own; a peer-teaching role would likely tighten his own Decision step faster than being coached at.

Customers keep telling him the timing problem, not the money problem

Call 4: you don't have to time big expenses like that, Right? Call 7: ya se viene el primero, ya ves la luz, agua. Call 1's customer had insurance that might cover a replacement. None of these are refusals; all three are scheduling and funding constraints. Coaching implication: Option B with the efficiency statement and a financing conversation are built for exactly this objection, and he used them once out of three opportunities.

Top 3 Coaching Priorities

(ranked by impact)

  • Never leave without running Sierra's own evaluation. On Call 5 he offered once — I would have to run the evaluation. So is that something you want me to do? — took a no, and endorsed the competitor instead. The evaluation is not optional and it is not a favour; it is what he was dispatched to do, and on a system with a failing capacitor and an oily fan motor it was the only path to an option sheet.
  • When a customer says "new unit," call the office before leaving the room. Call 1's replace all those pieces or a new piece all together and Call 4's buy a whole new unit both went unanswered. The rule should be mechanical: the customer names replacement, he names the Comfort Advisor and books the visit on the spot — exactly as he already does in Spanish on Call 7 (puedo hacer el schedule para que venga y te hable alguien del precio).
  • Build the sheet before walking to the van, not after. Four recordings contain a promise of options — I can include some options, depending what it is I find (Call 3) — and one contains actual options. Immediate, Future with the efficiency statement, System Update, said out loud in the home, then handed over and silence.

Reviewing prior service history before knocking is worth a quick consistency reminder, but it is a distant fourth — the missed history on Call 4 was embarrassing rather than decisive, whereas walking out of Call 5 without an evaluation is the whole reason his week has a no-move on it.
