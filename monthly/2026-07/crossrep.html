Bottom Line

Bottom Line

Across four weekly rollups, 832 recordings and 539 scoreable calls, the SILO team closed 60% of graded calls in July: 287 verified Option C handoffs and 43 repairs won. By the doctrine this system grades on, that is a good month — the handoff is the primary win and the team produced them at better than one call in two. The month’s central finding is the same one every week reached independently, and it is worth stating as a settled result rather than a hypothesis: diagnostic quality does not separate the technicians on this roster. Assessment banded Strong for every technician in the weeks ending 12, 19 and 26 July without a single exception. What separates a close rate of 85% from one of 39% happens in the last two minutes of the call, and it comes down to whether the technician converts the customer’s agreement into a dispatched, named person while still standing at the equipment — or describes a service the customer could arrange later.

Top Team-Wide Recurring Gaps

  1. The offer is spoken and then the paperwork starts. Present in every technician’s month under a different heading — “booking himself instead of the advisor”, “deferring the lead to a phone call”, “the specialist promised rather than booked”, “handing the next step to the customer’s calendar”, “‘down the line’ said without a specialist attached”. It is one behaviour with eleven names, and it is the largest single recoverable loss on the roster.
  2. The five-star seed planted and never harvested. Named for eleven of the fourteen technicians, in most cases in every week they appear. One technician harvests it reliably (Dustin Romine, week ending 5 July), which is the proof it converts.
  3. Recordings that never reach the Decision step. Roughly a third of all recordings in July stopped before an option existed — 832 recordings produced 539 scoreable calls. For two technicians this is the binding constraint on being coached at all.
  4. The first objection ends the conversation. A budget objection, a home warranty, or a plain “let me think about it” is treated as an answer rather than as the argument for a free comparison. Named for at least seven technicians.
  5. Non-customer audio inside customers’ homes. Named for nine technicians across the month, ranging from ordinary open-mic hygiene to material that needs a manager rather than a coach.

What To Do In August

  1. One habit, whole team: dispatch before paperwork. When the customer agrees to see a specialist, the next action is the phone call, not the signature pad. Every technician on the roster already does this correctly on their best calls; none does it reliably. It is the only change on this page that would move the team close rate by itself.
  2. Make the objection the trigger. A budget objection, a home warranty or a “let me think” is the argument for a free concrete comparison, not against it.
  3. Harvest the five-star seed. Eleven technicians plant it and do not return to it. One harvests it reliably; borrow his sentence.
  4. Fix the two measurement problems — job numbers on Juan Tlatenchi’s recordings, and the recording coverage that turned a third of July’s recordings into ungradeable fragments.
  5. Establish whether Andrew Trujillo’s final week is repeatable. It is the largest single improvement on the roster and nobody yet knows what caused it.

Method and Coverage

Fourteen technicians, 832 recordings, 539 scoreable calls, July 2026. This is a rollup of the four July weekly rollups — weeks ending 5, 12, 19 and 26 July — not a re-grade of raw transcripts; bands and findings are carried forward from those reports. Assessed against the SILO Field Strategy Guide using the five-band key, with no numeric scoring of any step; close rate is the only percentage in this report and every other figure is a plain count. The Option C handoff is the primary win and a won repair is a secondary positive outcome. Handoffs are taken from tgl_truth/<date>.json keyed by source job number including next-day lead creation, and the ServiceTitan record overrides transcript impressions in both directions. Declining to quote replacement pricing and routing it to a Comfort Advisor is the correct move under this process, is credited as a strength throughout, and is never recorded as a gap.

Coverage gaps, stated honestly. The week ending 5 July covers 29 June to 5 July and straddles the month boundary; it is counted toward July per the rollup spec. 27 to 31 July is covered by no weekly rollup — the week ending 2 August had not been generated when this report was built — so the last five days of the month are absent from every count on this page. tgl_truth day files are present for 1 to 28 July; 29, 30 and 31 July are missing and are not treated as zero. tgl_truth/ytd_sources.json is absent from this checkout. scorecards_full/ covers only 14 July and 25 to 30 July. plans/ exists for 19 to 29 July only. The weeks ending 5, 12 and 19 July were backfilled after the fact by the July catch-up job; only the week ending 26 July was produced by the live weekly run.
