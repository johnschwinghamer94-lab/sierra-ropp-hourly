Bottom Line

Bottom Line

Nikko had the roster’s lowest close rate in July — seventeen handoffs and three repair wins from forty-nine scoreable calls, with thirty no-moves — and that sits oddly alongside teaching that is among the best-argued in the corpus. He proves decline with his own photographs from previous visits, he pre-empts the “my last one lasted twenty years” objection before the customer raises it, and he understands home warranty coverage better than anyone on the roster. The month’s pattern is consistent and costly: he reaches the right conclusion, states it clearly, and then argues the customer out of acting on it — sometimes by offering them the do-it-yourself route, sometimes by handing them back to a warranty company, sometimes by leaving the conclusion as an opinion.

Top Recurring Gaps

Gaps that appeared in more than one week this month, merged rather than repeated. Each is cited back to the week that recorded it.

  • He argues the customer out of the decision he just won. All four weeks: “he argues the customer out of the decision he just won”, “talking himself out of the handoff on the customer’s behalf”, “offering the customer the do-it-yourself route”, “coaching the customer to buy elsewhere”. This is the defining gap of his month and it is the one thing that would move his numbers most.
  • Naming a repair as likely futile and then offering only that repair. Week ending 19 July. He tells a customer plainly there is a very big chance the compressor fails hours after he leaves, and then offers the fan motor anyway.
  • The first no is the last word. Weeks ending 5 and 12 July.
  • No review ask, and no five-star seed. All four weeks.
  • Long calls that end without a decision. Weeks ending 5, 19 and 26 July, including the longest call of the week ending 19 July producing neither outcome.

What Stuck

Coaching given earlier in the month that visibly changed a later week’s calls.

  • Decision climbed off Moderate after the first week and has held at Strong on wins since — the offer is now reliably built, which was not true at the start of the month.
  • Declining to guess at a replacement price became consistent from the week ending 12 July onward.

Next Month’s Top 3 Priorities

  1. Stop arguing the customer out of the decision. Four weeks, four headings, one behaviour. Nothing else in his month is worth this much.
  2. When a repair is likely futile, that sentence opens Option C — it does not close the call.
  3. Treat the first no as an objection, not an answer.

Appendix — Method and Data Coverage

  • Sources. This is a rollup of the July weekly rollups, not a re-grade of raw transcripts. Bands, strengths and gaps are carried forward from the weekly reports under weekly/2026-07-05, 2026-07-12, 2026-07-19 and 2026-07-26; no call was re-scored for this report.
  • Weeks contributing. week ending 5 Jul, week ending 12 Jul, week ending 19 Jul, week ending 26 Jul. All four July weekly rollups contain a report for this technician.
  • Backfilled weeks. The weeks ending 5, 12 and 19 July were generated after the fact from raw transcripts by the July catch-up job; only the week ending 26 July was produced by the live weekly run. Backfilled weeks used the same rubric and the same ground truth.
  • Month boundary. The week ending 5 July covers 29 June to 5 July, so it straddles the boundary and is counted toward both June and July, per the rollup spec. 27 to 31 July is covered by no weekly rollup — the week ending 2 August had not been generated when this report was built — so the last five days of the month are absent from every count above.
  • Ground truth. Option C handoffs are taken from tgl_truth/<date>.json keyed by source job number, including next-day lead creation, and the ServiceTitan record overrides transcript impressions in both directions. tgl_truth day files are present for 1 to 28 July; the files for 29, 30 and 31 July are missing and are not treated as zero. tgl_truth/ytd_sources.json is absent from this checkout, so attribution rests on the per-day files alone.
  • Per-call scorecards. scorecards_full/ covers only 14 July and 25 to 30 July, so bands for most of the month derive from the transcripts by way of the weekly reports.
  • Daily plans. plans/ exists for 19 to 29 July only, so the “what stuck” check against assigned coaching is corroborated by daily plans for the last third of the month and by the weekly reports’ own priority lists for the rest.
  • Price rule. Declining to quote or break down replacement pricing, and routing the question to a Comfort Advisor, is the correct move under this process. It is credited as a strength throughout and is never recorded as a gap. No priority on this page asks any technician to be more transparent about price.
