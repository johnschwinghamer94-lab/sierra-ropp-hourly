Top Recurring Gaps

Gaps that appeared in more than one week of July, merged rather than repeated per week. Each is cited back to the week that recorded it.

1. Calls that end with a goodbye where a question belonged

The opening week flagged eleven such calls. Every later week found the same shape — taking the first no as the final answer, then down the line said without a specialist attached in the closing week, where twenty-four of twenty-eight recordings never reached a Decision step.

2. Booking himself when the customer needs someone booked

Flagged in the opening week and again in the week ending Jul 12, where the finding was sharper: the advisor gets booked only when he books it in the room. The closing week flagged a promised follow-up that lives in his own head.

3. No five-star seed and no review ask

Flagged in all four weeks — no Agenda Card and no seed anywhere in the opening week, the seed never planted and the review asked once in the second, no review ask anywhere in fifteen recordings in the third, and the seed missing from the opening entirely in the fourth.

4. Conceding to warranty and future-work conversations

The week ending Jul 19 flagged that the warranty made the argument disappear; the closing week flagged future work explained and never priced in the room. Both leave the customer with information and no decision.

5. Frustration recorded immediately after a customer goodbye

Flagged in the opening week. Worth confirming it has not recurred, given how consistently it shows up elsewhere on the roster.

What Stuck

The Welcome

Solid in the opening week with no Agenda Card anywhere. Strong from the week ending Jul 12 onward and held for the rest of the month — the clearest coaching win on his page.

Booking the advisor in the room

Raised in the opening week and explicitly credited in the week ending Jul 12 as working when he does it, which coincided with his best Decision band of the month. It did not become habit, but the mechanism was proven on his own calls.

Appendix — Method and Data Coverage

  • Weekly reports contributing: 4 of 4 (week ending Jul 5, week ending Jul 12, week ending Jul 19, week ending Jul 26).
  • Bands: merged from the weekly reports, not re-derived from raw transcripts. The month band for each step is the band that recurred most often across the graded weeks; the trend column shows the week-by-week sequence in full so a modal band never hides a moving one.
  • Close rate and flips: taken from ServiceTitan via tgl_truth, aggregated across every available day of July for this technician (31 leads created off 56 calls run). ServiceTitan overrides the weekly reports’ own tallies in both directions.
  • ServiceTitan data gap: tgl_truth files exist for Jul 1 through 28 July only. Jul 29, 30 and 31 have no ServiceTitan truth data at all and are not counted as zero — they are simply outside the close-rate window. This close rate covers 28 of July’s 31 days.
  • Weeks not covered by any weekly report: Jul 27–31 falls after the last weekly rollup (week ending Jul 26) and no weekly report exists for it. Daily plans exist through Jul 30 and transcripts through Jul 31, but no banded grading was produced for those days and none has been invented here.
  • Week straddling the month boundary: the week ending Jul 5 runs from Jun 29, so its graded-call count includes two June days. This follows the spec rule that a straddling week counts for both months it touches.
  • Price rule: under the locked SILO price rule, no technician is banded down or given a gap for declining to quote or break down replacement pricing. Routing a price question to the Comfort Advisor is credited as correct process. Where this report flags pricing behaviour it is the opposite failure — a technician doing the advisor’s pricing work himself instead of handing it over.
