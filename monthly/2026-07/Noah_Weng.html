Top Recurring Gaps

Gaps that appeared in more than one week of July, merged rather than repeated per week. Each is cited back to the week that recorded it.

1. Telling customers he is not the person who prices things

Present in the weeks ending Jul 12 and Jul 26. In the closing week he says it twice on two different calls — So I'm just a repair technician. I don't have calls on unit repair and I'm just repairs, so… I don't even have prices on. The week ending Jul 12 flagged the same reflex as answering the customer's opening price question with a repair estimate. Routing a replacement price to the specialist is correct; disclaiming the options role entirely is not the same thing, and it leaves the customer with a diagnosis and nothing to choose between.

2. No option sheet built on any call, in any week

Every week of July flagged the absence of an Immediate/Future/System Update sheet. The week ending Jul 12 recorded him handing the customer two numbers and asking them to choose; the closing week recorded no sheet handed over on any of eight recordings. This is the single reason his repair conversion sat at zero all month against the roster's best handoff rate.

3. The five-star seed planted and never harvested

Flagged in the weeks ending Jul 5, Jul 19 and Jul 26 — three of four. He says overall, I'm here for five star service in the Welcome on call after call, and there is not one review ask anywhere in the month, including on a call where the customer volunteered I appreciate you staying around.

4. Recordings that stop before the Decision step

The week ending Jul 5 flagged four such recordings; the closing week flagged six of eight. Where the call does not become a handoff it simply stops, which is why his month has a high flip rate and no second outcome.

5. Non-customer audio on customer recordings

Raised in the weeks ending Jul 5 and Jul 19, and still present in the closing week — personal calls, van talk and commission conversation landing inside customer recordings. This is a professionalism and privacy exposure, and it is also consuming the recording time that should be capturing his Decision step.

What Stuck

Naming the specialist path before the customer reaches for it

Coached after the week ending Jul 5 and visibly automatic by the week ending Jul 19, where his handoff rate reached its month high on his largest sample. The words replace and specialist come out of his mouth first on both of his closing-week scoreable calls.

Escalating for authority instead of guessing at it

In the closing week he faced a system that could not legally be repaired, left the customer, called his manager, and returned with a decision and a reason — then stayed at the table until the advisor arrived in person. That is the judgment the flip doctrine depends on, and it was not present in the earlier weeks.

Next Month’s Top 3 Priorities

  • 1. Build and hand over the Immediate / Future / System Update sheet on every call. The handoff is already automatic; the only thing standing between him and repair revenue is putting the first two options on paper. Flagged in all four weekly reports and unaddressed in every one.
  • 2. Stop disclaiming the options role out loud. Route replacement pricing to the specialist — that is correct process — but never follow it with I'm just repairs. Recurring in the weeks ending Jul 12 and Jul 26.
  • 3. Harvest the five-star seed he already plants. He promises five-star service in the Welcome on almost every call and asks for the review on none, across three of four weeks.
