Bottom Line

Bottom Line

Cole finished July as the roster’s highest-volume technician and its most reliable repair closer, and his month reads as one long demonstration of a single mechanic: he does not offer a Comfort Advisor, he sends for one from the driveway, names them, gives an arrival window, and stays on site to walk them in. Twenty-eight verified handoffs and seven repair wins across sixty scoreable calls. His Assessment band climbed from Solid in the first week to Strong and held there, and his Ready and Welcome followed it. What did not move is the thing that separates his good calls from his flat ones, and it is visible in every week’s report under a different heading: when the customer agrees, he either reaches for the phone or he reaches for the signature pad, and the month’s no-moves are almost entirely the second group.

Month Snapshot

Calls graded: 60 scoreable service calls across 87 recordings, from 4 weekly rollups (week ending 5 Jul, week ending 12 Jul, week ending 19 Jul, week ending 26 Jul).

Close rate: 57%. Option C handoffs: 28. Repairs won: 7. No-moves: 26.

Strongest steps this month: Assessment (Strong) and Decision (Strong on wins).

Weakest step this month: Deliver (Solid) — the step that decides whether the offer becomes a ticket.

Trend Across the Weeks

Improving, decisively, on the front half of the call. Assessment moved Solid → Strong → Strong → Strong; Ready and Welcome moved Solid → Solid → Strong. The week ending 19 July is where the front of his call stopped being the weak part of it — the LRA-against-the-data-tag routine, the megohmmeter escalation and the oil-carpet demonstration became standard rather than occasional. Decision is flat at Strong on wins for all four weeks, which is the honest reading: the offer is excellent and identical on the calls that convert and the calls that do not. Deliver declined from Strong in the first week to Solid thereafter, tracking his rising call volume.

Top Recurring Gaps

Gaps that appeared in more than one week this month, merged rather than repeated. Each is cited back to the week that recorded it.

  • The offer is spoken and then the paperwork starts. Named in three of four weeks under three headings — “he books himself for tomorrow instead of the advisor for today” (week ending 5 Jul), “converting a booked visit into a callback when the customer pushes on price” (12 Jul), “on the no-moves, the option is spoken and then the paperwork starts” (19 Jul). Same behaviour, three descriptions. On the week ending 19 July his eight no-moves and ten flips carried the same diagnosis, the same energy analysis and the same Option C sentence; the only variable was the phone call.
  • The routing sentence that stops halfway. Flagged in the week ending 12 July as Gap 1. Substantially fixed by 19 July — see What Stuck — but the final week logged “describing himself out of the replacement conversation”, which is the same sentence losing its second half again on a thinner corpus.
  • No review ask, all four weeks. Twelve times across the month a customer volunteered unprompted thanks. In the first week’s report he is recorded asking for a review once, and it worked instantly. It has not recurred since. His two-signature ritual is a fixed pause on nearly every recording and is the obvious place to attach it.
  • Recording coverage that eats a third of his month. Six of twenty-two recordings never became a service call in the first week; fourteen of eighteen never reached a Decision step in the last. Some of that is genuine dispatch mix, but it makes his measured rates rest on a much smaller base than his recording count implies.

What Stuck

Coaching given earlier in the month that visibly changed a later week’s calls.

  • The routing sentence got its second half. The week ending 12 July recorded the gap as “I just work on the repair side is correct and should never change; it needs and I can have the person who does know here in forty minutes attached to it.” By the week ending 19 July the completed sentence appears on Calls 13, 19 and 25 — I just work on the repair side. I don’t quote or give options as far as what a new system costs, but I did put one of them, an option down there for you at the bottom — and his repair conversion rose while the handoff volume held.
  • Assessment stopped being his weak step. The first week’s report named it explicitly as “thinner than his peers’” and the weakest of his five. It banded Strong for the remaining three weeks and is now among the strongest on the roster.
  • The warm introduction became a standard, not a mood. First recorded in the week ending 12 July as “the Comfort Advisor arrives before he leaves”. In the week ending 19 July it appears near-verbatim on six separate recordings and is the single most repeatable behaviour in the month’s corpus.

Next Month’s Top 3 Priorities

  1. Dispatch before paperwork. When the customer agrees, the next action is the call to dispatch, not the signature pad. This is a sequencing change, not a new skill — he already does it correctly twenty-eight times this month.
  2. Treat a promised callback as a no-move in progress. “I’ll give you a call around noon” and “wait for Harry to call you” produced nothing in July. Book the specialist and cancel it if the customer changes their mind.
  3. Attach one review sentence to the two signatures. The ritual already exists on nearly every visit and he has proved once that the ask converts on the spot.

  • Per-call scorecards. scorecards_full/ covers only 14 July and 25 to 30 July, so bands for most of the month derive from the transcripts by way of the weekly reports.
  • Daily plans. plans/ exists for 19 to 29 July only, so the “what stuck” check against assigned coaching is corroborated by daily plans for the last third of the month and by the weekly reports’ own priority lists for the rest.
  • Price rule. Declining to quote or break down replacement pricing, and routing the question to a Comfort Advisor, is the correct move under this process. It is credited as a strength throughout and is never recorded as a gap. No priority on this page asks any technician to be more transparent about price.
