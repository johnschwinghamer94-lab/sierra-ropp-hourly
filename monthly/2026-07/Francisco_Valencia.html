Bottom Line

Bottom Line

Francisco ran the month’s second-largest corpus and its second-best close rate: thirty-seven verified handoffs and five repair wins from fifty-eight scoreable calls, with handoffs on more than half his scoreable calls in every week of the month. His handoff mechanic is the most consistently closed on the roster — where others describe a specialist, he leaves with a window or a signature. He also spent much of July training another technician on live customer calls, which shows up in his reports as both a strength and a cost. The gap that survives all four weeks is at the front of the call, not the back: he is the only technician on the roster whose expectations block is repeatedly absent, and it was named in three separate weeks before the month ended.

Top Recurring Gaps

Gaps that appeared in more than one week this month, merged rather than repeated. Each is cited back to the week that recorded it.

  • The expectations block is missing from the front of the call. Named in three consecutive weeks (5, 12 and 19 July) and again in the last week as “the opening arriving after the work has started”. Everything downstream of it is strong, which is precisely why this is the cheapest available fix.
  • Quoting replacement scope, tiers and figures himself. Weeks ending 5, 12 and 19 July. The routing is correct; the problem is the material he supplies before the specialist arrives. The week ending 19 July also logged a compressor figure — $45,000 — an order of magnitude above every comparable number in the corpus and still unverified against the written report.
  • The review ask that did not carry over. Present in the week ending 12 July, gone by 19 July, and by the last week the review count is used as proof and never as an ask.
  • Training and internal talk consuming the customer’s recording. Weeks ending 19 and 26 July, including commission structure discussed on a customer’s line.

What Stuck

Coaching given earlier in the month that visibly changed a later week’s calls.

  • The handoff became a signature rather than an intention. The first week’s report named “the specialist is promised rather than booked” as Gap 1. From the week ending 12 July onward the same behaviour is logged as a strength — “turning the handoff into a signature, not an intention”, then “closing the handoff with a window or a signature”. That is the clearest single instance of coaching landing anywhere in the month.
  • Anchoring the recommendation to how long the customer is staying appears from the week ending 12 July onward and is now a standing part of his options conversation.

Next Month’s Top 3 Priorities

  1. Put the expectations block back at the front. Three weeks named it, the fourth named its consequence. Nothing else in his month is this cheap to fix.
  2. Stop supplying replacement scope ahead of the specialist — and reconcile the $45,000 compressor figure against the written report before it recurs.
  3. Reinstate the review ask he was already making in the week ending 12 July.

Appendix — Method and Data Coverage

  • Sources. This is a rollup of the July weekly rollups, not a re-grade of raw transcripts. Bands, strengths and gaps are carried forward from the weekly reports under weekly/2026-07-05, 2026-07-12, 2026-07-19 and 2026-07-26; no call was re-scored for this report.
  • Weeks contributing. week ending 5 Jul, week ending 12 Jul, week ending 19 Jul, week ending 26 Jul. All four July weekly rollups contain a report for this technician.
  • Backfilled weeks. The weeks ending 5, 12 and 19 July were generated after the fact from raw transcripts by the July catch-up job; only the week ending 26 July was produced by the live weekly run. Backfilled weeks used the same rubric and the same ground truth.
  • Month boundary. The week ending 5 July covers 29 June to 5 July, so it straddles the boundary and is counted toward both June and July, per the rollup spec. 27 to 31 July is covered by no weekly rollup — the week ending 2 August had not been generated when this report was built — so the last five days of the month are absent from every count above.
  • Ground truth. Option C handoffs are taken from tgl_truth/<date>.json keyed by source job number, including next-day lead creation, and the ServiceTitan record overrides transcript impressions in both directions. tgl_truth day files are present for 1 to 28 July; the files for 29, 30 and 31 July are missing and are not treated as zero. tgl_truth/ytd_sources.json is absent from this checkout, so attribution rests on the per-day files alone.
  • Per-call scorecards. scorecards_full/ covers only 14 July and 25 to 30 July, so bands for most of the month derive from the transcripts by way of the weekly reports.
  • Daily plans. plans/ exists for 19 to 29 July only, so the “what stuck” check against assigned coaching is corroborated by daily plans for the last third of the month and by the weekly reports’ own priority lists for the rest.
  • Price rule. Declining to quote or break down replacement pricing, and routing the question to a Comfort Advisor, is the correct move under this process. It is credited as a strength throughout and is never recorded as a gap. No priority on this page asks any technician to be more transparent about price.
  • Notes specific to this technician. The $45,000 compressor figure on the week ending 19 July, Call 17, is flagged as requiring verification and is not treated as established. It is likely a transcription artefact.
